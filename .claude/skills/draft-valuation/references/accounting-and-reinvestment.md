# Keep profit, capital and cash flow on the same basis

Read when preparing base-year facts and reviewing material accounting choices. The evidence note carries a short applicability screen and detailed reconciliations only for material items. Follow [§18.4](assumptions-spec.md#section-18-4) rule 1 and [§18.11](model-spec.md#section-18-11).

## Primary teachings

Damodaran treats expenditure that produces multi-year benefits as investment, including R&D. Capitalization requires a supported useful life and historical spending; it changes operating profit and the capital base together. His discussion also distinguishes existing equity compensation claims from future compensation. A simple earnings add-back or diluted-share adjustment can omit important effects. See [intangibles.txt](../../../../tools/valuation/damodaran-notes/sources/intangibles.txt), PDF pp.8–12 and sections on equity options. The paper's tax/accounting examples are dated September 2009; verify current company treatment.

Growth needs reinvestment, and the value of additional growth depends on the return on new investment relative to its cost. That marginal return is distinct from the return measured on accumulated historical capital. See [growth-and-value.txt](../../../../tools/valuation/damodaran-notes/sources/growth-and-value.txt), “Paying for Growth” and “The Excess Return Effect”.

## Required working reconciliation

| Item | Show when material | Consistency check |
|---|---|---|
| One-time items and cyclicality | Reported profit, named adjustments and evidence of recurrence/nonrecurrence. | A recurring charge is not removed merely because management excludes it. Cycle normalization belongs in a supported forecast transition, not an invented reported base year. |
| R&D or other growth spending | Reported spending, useful-life rationale, asset/amortization schedule if used, and reported versus adjusted profit/capital. | Do not improve margin without recognizing investment. Keep future margins, reinvestment, ROIC and peer comparisons on the chosen basis. |
| Acquired intangibles/goodwill | Reported capital and profit, any supplementary normalization, acquisition spending and amortization roll-off. | Do not remove goodwill solely to raise ROIC; do not add amortization twice or omit recurring acquisition costs. |
| Leases | Disclosed lease balances, expense/cash treatment and valuation bridge. | Match the operating-profit/reinvestment treatment to debt-like claims; a liability in the balance sheet alone does not establish a coherent adjustment. |
| Stock compensation and dilution | Future compensation expense plus separately identified existing options, restricted awards, convertibles and other claims. | Leave SBC expensed under the house rule. Identify whether claims are already represented in shares or the bridge before subtracting them again. |
| Taxes | Current effective rate, mature rate, geographic mix, material losses/credits and expected cash-tax timing. | Do not assume an immediate cash tax benefit on operating losses. The engine has no explicit tax-loss carryforward ledger. |
| Cash and non-operating holdings | Operating cash needs, excess/restricted cash, excluded operating income and separately valued holdings. | Assets and their income cannot be counted both in operating cash flows and the equity bridge. Follow the specified carrying-value convention and disclose material limitations. |
| Reinvestment | Net capex, acquisitions, noncash working capital and any capitalized growth spending, with spending-to-revenue lag. | Reconcile gross and net amounts and cash-flow signs. The marginal history and the stock sales-to-capital benchmark are different measures, not interchangeable observations. |

The engine's R&D switch adjusts starting profit and capital; it does not forecast the entire future research schedule, tax treatment or comparable basis for the analyst. Verify supported behavior before using any switch. On an initial or explicitly authorized redraft, select a documented, supported treatment; preserve existing owner choices outside that scope. If a complete material adjustment cannot be represented, record the limitation and affected computation instead of making only its favorable half.

Finish by comparing growth, after-tax margins, capital additions, implied ROIC and cash flows together. A ratio that generates implausible returns may indicate an error in profit, capital definitions or reinvestment; do not force convergence by changing whichever input is easiest.
