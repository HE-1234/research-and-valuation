# Express uncertainty and keep the answer independent

Read before assigning scenario inputs or probabilities, and during review. The record belongs in the evidence note and the affected input's `reason`/`detail`; no new YAML schema keys are needed.

## Primary teaching

Damodaran recommends identifying different sources of uncertainty, keeping models focused on important drivers, making one's best estimates and showing uncertainty explicitly. He distinguishes operating/discrete risks from market risk and warns against false precision and hindsight. See [uncertainty.txt](../../../../tools/valuation/damodaran-notes/sources/uncertainty.txt), “Dealing with Uncertainty”, especially points 2–6. The extracted article marks two image diagrams as unavailable; rely on the surrounding prose or inspect the originals rather than inferring their contents.

He connects narratives and numbers and calls for feedback when evidence changes the story. See [narrative-and-numbers.txt](../../../../tools/valuation/damodaran-notes/sources/narrative-and-numbers.txt), the five-step valuation process and feedback discussion.

## Apply to this repository

**Define the cases before assigning their weights.** Base is the best-supported central operating outlook, seeded by usable consensus under [rule 10](assumptions-spec.md#section-18-4). A modest, slightly conservative continuation is allowed when a named business judgment supports it; it is not a blanket percentage discount. Test possible analyst optimism with company evidence, without assuming every provider is too optimistic or any company will permanently beat estimates. Bear and bull are realistically adverse and favorable outcomes around that outlook; they may describe the same business with different adoption, pricing, execution or recovery paths. Distinct mechanisms matter, not dramatic prose or a minimum distance between numbers. Bear may retain a moat and substantial growth. Permanent impairment or moat loss requires company-specific support; do not bundle every disclosed risk or manufacture a catastrophe merely to distinguish it from base.

Published analyst high/low estimates describe disagreement among forecasts, not automatic bull/bear scenarios or probabilities. Use an available median, range and analyst count as context for the starting mean and for questions to investigate. Select linked revenue, margin and reinvestment outcomes from their business mechanisms, then assess weights separately; no additional broker-by-broker research is required.

For each case, explain the main change, its timing and persistence, which adverse or favorable developments share a cause, and management's feasible response. Check whether the combined outcome is credible at the proposed weight, not just whether each event is individually possible. Cite contrary evidence and a less severe or more severe credible alternative when it materially changes the choice. Disclosed risk is evidence of possibility, not a measured probability. If the evidence cannot calibrate probabilities, say that weights remain provisional judgments; preserve saved owner weights and identify any material mismatch for owner review. A separate severe stress can be described when relevant, but do not silently add a weighted case or a full valuation during drafting.

1. **Distinguish unknown facts from future judgments.** A missing balance-sheet fact requires a source or an explicit gap. A future conversion rate can be a labelled judgment supported by a range, operating evidence and alternatives; no source needs to predict the exact percentage. More decimal places do not make it better supported.
2. **Map material risks once.** Use a compact table: uncertainty | mechanism | input/outcome affected | overlap with other treatment. Lost customers can reduce revenue and margin if both mechanisms are explained. Do not also add an arbitrary discount-rate penalty for the same expected loss. Failure probabilities and recoveries require a defined horizon and consistent asset/equity basis; avoid counting the same failure both inside a scenario and again in the bridge.
3. **Vary connected assumptions together.** A pricing strategy changes revenue, margins and sometimes working capital. Capacity spending can delay cash flow before it produces revenue. Bear/base/bull should be coherent alternative business outcomes. A sensitivity grid changing one input answers a narrower question; it is not a probability distribution.
4. **Label probability assumptions.** The house 25/50/25 weights are starting conventions. Preserve owner values and explain evidence supporting proposed alternatives on an authorized redraft. A weighted average is only as meaningful as the scenarios and weights; three cases do not establish statistical confidence bounds. The base case describes central assumptions and need not equal the expected valuation because the calculation is nonlinear.
5. **Keep price from becoming the target.** The runner can fetch market inputs for the financing build. The analyst does not use present or prior value-per-share results to choose operating assumptions. Reverse DCF is a subsequent comparison with market expectations, not a source for the analyst's preferred growth path. Do not choose inputs just to make the stock appear cheap, expensive or safely valued.
6. **Use diagnostics to question economics.** State what a flag means, inspect the linked assumptions, and retain a justified warning when appropriate. Never select a terminal premium or capital ratio solely to pass a threshold. Evaluate errors in either direction.

After computation is authorized, sensitivities can show which uncertainties matter most. During drafting use only restricted operating diagnostics. Do not run a full valuation or simulation to choose the story. Refer to [forecasts and learning](forecasts-and-learning.md) for recording and revisiting judgments without hindsight edits.
