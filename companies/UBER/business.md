# Uber Technologies, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Uber runs an app that matches people who want something moved with independent people who will move it: a rider with a driver (Mobility), a restaurant or grocery order with a courier (Delivery), and a company's freight with a trucking carrier (Freight) [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It does not own the cars and, in most places, does not employ the drivers, who "provide a vehicle to perform services on our platform" as independent contractors [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. The paying customers are the people doing the work: "We generate substantially all of our revenue from fees paid by Drivers and Merchants for use of our platform" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. In the quarter to June 2026, 208 million people used the app at least monthly, took 3.9 billion trips and paid $58.0 billion in total (Gross Bookings), of which Uber kept $14.2 billion as revenue [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 release, p.2]. On the other side, "a record 10.2 million drivers and couriers earned over $25 billion on the platform this quarter" [Q2 2026 remarks, p.1].

The app works in over 70 countries and 15,000 cities [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Founded in 2009 in San Francisco, it listed on the NYSE in May 2019; Dara Khosrowshahi has been CEO since 2017 [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 5] [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. Operating losses of $3.8 billion in 2021 and $1.8 billion in 2022 became a $5.6 billion profit in 2025, but the accumulated deficit (all past losses net of profits) was still $8.0 billion at June 30, 2026 [10-K FY2023, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A].

## 2. How the money comes in

**The cut.** When a rider pays $20, the whole $20 with taxes and tolls is a Gross Booking; the driver's earnings and bonuses come out of it and the rest is Uber's revenue, which "is net of Driver and Merchant earnings and Driver incentives" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Uber never says "take rate" in its 10-K; its deck and 10-Q call this ratio "Revenue Margin" [Q2 2026 supplemental, p.16] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. The fee is "either a fixed percentage of the end-user fare or the difference between the amount paid by an end-user and the amount earned by Drivers" [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. In Delivery the restaurant pays "a fixed percentage of the meal price" and Uber keeps the delivery fee less the courier's pay [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Driver bonuses reduce revenue; consumer discounts are marketing expense except "market-wide promotions which are recorded as a reduction of revenue"; advertising and Freight are booked gross [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Why revenue grew 12% when Gross Bookings grew 24% in Q2 2026.** Where Uber is legally the transport provider it books the whole fare as revenue and driver pay as cost of revenue, as in the UK from March 2022 [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Note 14]. A UK tax-law change effective January 2, 2026 reversed that: "payments to drivers are recorded as a reduction of revenue instead of cost of revenue" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. That took $1.1 billion off Q2 2026 revenue, $808 million off cost of revenue and about 8 points off revenue growth; adding the $1.1 billion back, revenue would have grown about 21% (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

## 3. The economics

**Where the money goes.** Cost of revenue is the direct cost of trips: driver and courier pay where Uber is the legal provider, "certain insurance costs", freight-carrier payments, card fees and hosting [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Below it sit operations and support (city teams, customer support), sales and marketing (advertising $2.2 billion and consumer promotions $1.6 billion in 2025), research and development, and general and administrative, which carries legal accruals [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Gross margin (revenue minus cost of revenue, over revenue; computed, not reported) excludes depreciation and swings with accounting choices, so it says little about the economics [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1].

**Does cost rise with every trip?** Half does: insurance, card fees and driver pay in gross markets. The four lines below cost of revenue behave like software, falling from $1.71 per trip in 2022 to $1.06 in 2025, and from 11.4% of Gross Bookings to 7.4% (computed) [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. That spread took the operating margin (profit after all running costs, as a share of revenue) from a 22% loss in 2021 to 10.7% in 2025 and 13.3% in Q2 2026 (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1].

Free cash flow was $9.8 billion in 2025 and passed $10 billion over the twelve months to June 2026, "the first time we have exceeded the $10 billion mark" [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.7]. Two things sit inside it. And stock-based compensation of about $1.8 billion a year is a real cost paid in shares: Adjusted EBITDA added it back, the new Non-GAAP Operating Income does not, and free cash flow after it was $7.9 billion in 2025 (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [8-K 2026-01-12, Item 8.01].

GAAP (official accounting rules) net income is a poor guide. 2024's $9.9 billion included a $6.4 billion benefit from releasing a "valuation allowance", an accounting reserve against past losses that is written back once the company expects to earn enough to use them; 2025's $10.1 billion included a $5.0 billion Netherlands release; without them, roughly $3.5 billion and $5.1 billion (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Note 11]. Stakes in Aurora, Didi, Grab and others are marked to market (revalued at the current share price) through the income statement each quarter: a $1.5 billion loss in Q1 2026, then a $1.6 billion gain in Q2, about half that quarter's pre-tax profit (computed) [Q1 2026 release, p.2] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. That Q2 gain included $1.1 billion on the Delivery Hero shares, booked just before the stake "prospectively transitioned to the equity method of accounting" (Uber now records its share of Delivery Hero's profit or loss, not the share price); only the $1.6 billion of total return swaps on Delivery Hero stock still move with the price [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2]. "GAAP Net Income may continue to see swings from quarter-to-quarter due to equity stakes on our balance sheet" [Q2 2026 remarks, p.7].

Return on capital in plain words: $5.6 billion of operating profit in 2025 on $1.9 billion of property and equipment; what Uber does tie up is $9.5 billion of restricted investments pledged mainly as insurance collateral (security for future claims), and stakes in other companies [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. At June 30, 2026 it held $5.4 billion of unrestricted cash and short-term investments, stakes "marked at $12.5 billion", and $12.6 billion of debt [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Liquidity, city by city.** More riders mean more earnings per hour for drivers, more drivers mean shorter waits, and each side keeps the other. The company's phrase is "network scale and liquidity"; it admits "the low switching costs between competitor platforms", so what holds users is not lock-in but always having a car nearby [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Uber's edge is breadth: "Only 30% of our US gross bookings and 25% of our profits are coming from the top 20 cities" [Q2 2026 call, p.14].

**Cross-platform habit.** Users of both rides and delivery generate "over three times the Gross Bookings" of single-product users, and only "one in five eligible consumers" use both [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Uber One members "spend three times more" [Q1 2026 call, p.6].

**Signs it is weakening.** MAPC growth (16%) and trips per user (+2%) flattening together; driver incentives rising because a rival bids for the same supply, as in Brazil, where "the cost of securing that supply has gone up pretty significantly" and cost two points of trips growth; and management ceasing to say its category position rose in robotaxi cities [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 call, p.7] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. Robotaxi owners go direct.** In the company's words, AVs "could allow competitors to offer such services at a substantially lower price", and Waymo "has introduced a commercialized ridehailing fleet of autonomous vehicles on its own platform in addition to a fleet ... available through our platform" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. What would have to happen: a maker of self-driving cars reaches scale in big US cities and fills its cars without Uber's demand. Management's counter is to become "the world's leading commercialization platform for autonomous vehicles", because "AVs will change how trips are supplied, but not how demand is aggregated" [Q2 2026 call, p.3] [AV spotlight Feb 2026, p.7]. Its evidence: in San Francisco, Los Angeles and Phoenix "Uber's category share ... is higher today than it was a year ago", and robotaxis are still "less than 0.5% of our overall trip volume" [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6] [Q2 2026 call, p.7]. Partners named include Waymo, Nuro with Lucid, Zoox, Wayve, Rivian, NVIDIA and Waabi, with "approximately 120,000 vehicles" committed; AVs are live on Uber in 7 cities with 8 more launching by year-end [Q2 2026 call, p.4] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.5] [Q1 2026 remarks, p.5] [Q2 2026 supplemental, p.8]. Early warnings: Waymo not renewing Austin and Atlanta beyond 2026 ("We believe we'll continue to operate next year in those marketplaces"), and the first disclosed profit drag from the $10 billion program, which the CFO has not yet sized ("we'll size that for investors clearly as we've historically done") [Q2 2026 call, p.7] [Q2 2026 call, p.10]. Exposure: Mobility is 68% of segment profit and the US and Canada about half of revenue (both computed) [Q2 2026 release, p.3] [10-K FY2025, Note 13].

**2. Drivers reclassified as employees.** Uber's basis is that drivers "can choose whether, when, and where to provide services" and may work for rivals; otherwise "we would incur significant additional expenses for compensating Drivers", prices would rise, and competitors would be hit alike [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Status: California's Proposition 22 keeps drivers independent, though the Attorney General's pre-2020 case "remains ongoing"; "more than 150,000 Drivers" have filed or threatened arbitration claims (private hearings instead of court); New Zealand's Supreme Court ruled in November 2025 that four drivers are employees; Mexico's Congress passed a 2024 bill reclassifying higher-earning drivers as employees [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Note 14] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. Accruals for all legal, regulatory and non-income-tax matters were $1.8 billion at June 30, 2026, not itemized [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11]. Early warning: the non-GAAP add-back for legal and regulatory reserve changes, $1.1 billion in 2024 and $141 million in Q2 2026 [10-K FY2025, Note 13] [Q2 2026 release, p.12]. Exposure: every market, in proportion to driver pay.

**3. Insurance costs outrun fares.** Reserves rose 164% from end-2022 to end-2025 while Gross Bookings rose 68% (computed) [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. What would have to happen: per-mile rates keep rising, or old accidents cost more than reserved, for instance through the "mass tort" proceedings (many injury claims bundled into one case) plaintiffs are attempting [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11]. Early warning: reserve additions growing faster than Mobility Gross Bookings, and prior-year adjustments turning positive again (+$158 million in 2023, then −$78 million and −$21 million) [10-K FY2025, Schedule II]. Exposure: US and Canada Mobility, where driver insurance "is higher" than elsewhere [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**4. Fee caps and price competition.** Municipalities "have proposed delivery network fee caps ... and caps on take rate or surge pricing", and the model has been "blocked, capped, or suspended" in six countries including Germany, Italy and Spain [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Rivals named: Bolt, Didi, Lyft and Ola in rides; DoorDash, Instacart, Rappi, Delivery Hero, Just Eat Takeaway and Amazon in delivery [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Early warning: promotions and incentives growing faster than Gross Bookings; Revenue Margin falling for non-accounting reasons. Exposure: the whole profit pool, market by market.

**5. Delivery Hero: paying up and borrowing.** Uber has offered €41.50 a share in cash for the rest of Delivery Hero ($14.8 billion for 100%, $13.7 billion net of the roughly 37% economic stake built for about $4 billion), signed a €14.2 billion bridge loan (short-term borrowing to be replaced by longer-term debt), and says "Closing is expected in the second half of 2027" [Delivery Hero release, p.1] [Delivery Hero release, p.3] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.8] [8-K 2026-07-16, Item 1.01]. Uber pays €700 million if regulators block it; Delivery Hero pays €200 million if it takes a rival bid [8-K 2026-07-16, Item 1.01]. The pitch: 50 markets with $42 billion of bookings, "over $1.2 billion" of yearly synergies (savings and extra sales from combining), and leverage (debt relative to yearly earnings) kept "below two times" [Delivery Hero release, p.2] [Delivery Hero presentation, p.11] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.8]. Early warning: the bridge not replaced by term loans in Q3 2026 as planned, or buybacks stuck near Q2's $510 million [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 remarks, p.8]. Exposure: the balance sheet and share count, not the operating business.

**Other exposures.** UK tax authorities have assessed about $1.8 billion of VAT (sales tax) for 2022–2024, paid and booked as a receivable (money Uber expects back) pending appeal [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11]. **Concentration:** none; revenue comes from millions of drivers and merchants in "highly fragmented" markets and no customer is named [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

## Glossary

- **Gross Bookings:** the total riders, eaters and shippers pay, including taxes, tolls and fees, before driver and merchant earnings are taken out; Uber's measure of marketplace size.
- **MAPCs (Monthly Active Platform Consumers):** unique people who took a ride or received a delivery at least once in a month, averaged over the quarter.
- **Revenue Margin:** revenue divided by Gross Bookings, the company's own name for its cut.
- **Principal vs agent accounting:** whether a company books the whole sale as revenue and the provider's pay as a cost (principal) or only its fee (agent); Uber is an agent almost everywhere, which is why revenue can move without profit moving.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** the profit measure Uber used through 2025, which excluded depreciation, stock-based pay, and legal and tax reserve changes; still printed for comparison.
- **Segment Operating Income and Non-GAAP Operating Income:** the measures used from 2026, which deduct depreciation and stock-based pay but still exclude acquired-intangible amortization and legal, tax and restructuring items.
