## 3. Vision, strategy and growth engines (management's description, with numbers)

### 3.1 Overall framing

- CEO opening: "Q2 was another outstanding quarter for Uber with record audience engagement driving durable growth, expanding margins, and record cash generation. Gross bookings grew 22% year-on-year to more than $58 billion, above the high end of our guidance and marking our fourth consecutive quarter above 20% growth. Just as importantly, that growth translated into significant operating leverage, with non-GAAP EPS growing 35% year-over-year and trailing 12-month free cash flow exceeding $10 billion for the first time in our history." [Q2 2026 call, p.3]
- Remarks: "record highs across MAPCs, frequency, trips, and Gross Bookings"; growth "broad-based across business lines (Mobility at 20%, Delivery and Freight each accelerated to 25%) and geographies (the U.S. accelerated and international performance remained strong)". [Q2 2026 remarks, p.1]
- Audience: "We've added more first-time users in the past twelve months than in any period over the past five years, and a record 10.2 million drivers and couriers earned over $25 billion on the platform this quarter." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.1]; "16% audience growth and record engagement driving strong 18% YoY Trips growth" [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6]. (Release: Trips 3.9 billion, +18%; MAPCs +16%; monthly Trips per MAPC +2% [Q2 2026 release, p.1].)
- World Cup: "During the FIFA World Cup, more than 8 million tourists took rides across host cities, while Uber One International Benefits, Travel Pass, and Game Day Offers helped consumers around the world save approximately $300 million." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.1]; "The impact of the Middle East conflict was in-line with our expectations, and was offset by demand associated with the World Cup." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6]; CFO on call: "the World Cup definitely was a benefit, but it was as expected to a large extent." [Q2 2026 call, p.5]
- Three strategic pillars named by the CEO on the call: (1) core growth with operating leverage; (2) Delivery Hero ("extend the proven strategy that has underpinned our growth for years by roughly doubling the number of markets where we can offer the full power of our platform across Mobility and Delivery"); (3) autonomous vehicles ("one of the largest opportunities in Uber's history"). [Q2 2026 call, p.3]
- Growth drivers listed by the CEO (first-time users question): "whether it's low cost, cross-platform, newer products or sparse markets, that's really what's contributing to both the growth of the business as well as the growth of the audience." [Q2 2026 call, p.6]
- Pricing philosophy (CFO): "we do not manage our business to an average price output. We continue to focus on competitive pricing that maximizes durable engagement, marketplace health, and long-term profit dollars." Mix illustration: "an average Uber Black trip costs a rider over 15x more than an average Moto trip globally." Three mix effects raised average price in Q2 vs Q1: Delivery acceleration (higher transaction values than Mobility), US acceleration (higher prices than international), and growth of Reserve, Black, U4B and Grocery & Retail with slower Moto growth. [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6–7]

### 3.2 Cross-platform strategy and Uber One

- "only 20% of our consumers at this point use both Rides and Eats. And that's growing one and a half times faster than single product users as well. So that's a very sustainable advantage that we have as it relates to cross-platform." ... "not only is Rides moving consumers to Eats, but then our Eats business is moving consumers onto Rides as well. And that's true both globally and in the US as well." [Q2 2026 call, p.6]
- Delivery Hero rationale in cross-platform terms: "It will also nearly double the number of markets where we offer both Mobility and Delivery, significantly expanding our cross-platform opportunity, extending Uber One to millions more consumers, and letting us deploy our technology, AI, and marketplace capabilities at even greater scale." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.1]
- Uber One: "Uber One remains our strongest cross-platform consumer value proposition and is quickly becoming one of the world's leading membership programs. Membership reached another all-time high this quarter, with members now accounting for more than 70% of Delivery Gross Bookings, up roughly 20 percentage points in just two years." Member Days returned in May. Benefits being expanded: Travel Pass, Uber Cash back on hotels, merchant-funded offers, "in a disciplined, capital-efficient way." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.3–4]
- No absolute Uber One member count was given for Q2 (Q1 had said "surpassed 50 million" [Q1 2026 remarks, p.1]).

### 3.3 Mobility

- "Mobility delivered another quarter of 20% Gross Bookings growth while continuing to expand profitability, with segment operating income margin reaching 7.6%." US trips and Gross Bookings accelerated, "driven by continued consumer adoption across our portfolio of premium and lower-cost products, expansion into sparser areas, and reinvesting insurance savings to improve affordability. In the U.S. markets where prices declined the most, including Los Angeles and San Francisco, trip growth meaningfully outpaced the rest of the country." International standouts: Argentina, Germany, Japan, Spain. [Q2 2026 remarks, p.1–2]
- Premium: "U4B Gross Bookings grew 40%+ YoY, with business customers increasingly choosing premium experiences that generate nearly 2x the Gross Bookings and 4x the contribution profit per trip compared to UberX across our largest markets." Uber Health momentum within U4B. [Q2 2026 remarks, p.2]; on call: "U4B in particular, we saw 40% year-on-year growth. Uber Health is growing even faster." [Q2 2026 call, p.5]
- Low cost: "products such as Moto and Wait & Save continue expanding the category by making Uber more affordable and accessible. As these products mature, we're becoming increasingly disciplined around incentives while maintaining attractive engagement and unit economics." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.2]; CEO: "Globally, our low-cost products are kind of two wheelers and three wheelers. In the US it's Wait & Save, which allows consumers to kind of trade off time against price. And Wait & Save is growing very, very quickly." [Q2 2026 call, p.6]
- Sparse markets: "launching 100+ new cities, with suburban and sparser markets once again growing ~1.5x faster than denser markets while generating higher margins." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.2]; CFO: "in the US, less than 10% of eligible consumers in our sparse markets have used Uber in the past 12 months, whereas in dense markets, that number is over 50%." [Q2 2026 call, p.5]; CEO: "Margins are actually quite healthy as well." [Q2 2026 call, p.6]
- New products bringing new audiences: "women preferred is bringing a lot of women, both drivers and riders, into the marketplace. Uber Teens is another new product ... U4B gives us high margins, but is also introducing kind of a new enterprise type audience." [Q2 2026 call, p.6]
- Supply: "Active drivers reached another record and our marketplace once again demonstrated its resilience by absorbing significant demand spikes during the World Cup while maintaining strong reliability." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.2]

### 3.4 Delivery, grocery and retail

- "Delivery Gross Bookings accelerated to 25% YoY, while segment operating income margin reached another record of 3.8%. Growth was broad-based: consumer retention reached a multi-year high, Uber One continued to deepen engagement, restaurant delivery accelerated, and Grocery & Retail significantly outgrew restaurants. Internationally, Australia, the UK, and Mexico led growth, with our category position improving across all 10 of our largest international markets. Put simply, we are growing faster than the delivery category while becoming more profitable". [Q2 2026 remarks, p.2]
- Grocery & Retail: "reaching $15 billion in annualized Gross Bookings, growing roughly 40% YoY for multiple quarters in a row. Selection continues to be the primary growth engine, with new partnerships including Decathlon, GameStop, Kiehl's, Tesco, Toys"R"Us, and Ulta Beauty". G&R shoppers "retaining meaningfully better than restaurant-only customers." Product: live shelf verification, smarter substitutions, AI shopping. [Q2 2026 remarks, p.2]
- Occasions: "Large group orders and Pickup, which present a massive addressable opportunity, delivered strong growth this quarter". [Q2 2026 remarks, p.3]
- Europe: category position strengthening in UK, France, Germany; Finland launch; expansion beyond Copenhagen in Denmark; Getir food delivery (Türkiye) closed; Trendyol Go anniversary. [Q2 2026 remarks, p.3]

[Q2 2026 call, p.9]

### 3.8 Autonomous vehicles

- Vision (remarks): "We believe the long-term winner in AVs will be determined by who can commercialize the technology at global scale—what we have called the 'go-to-market puzzle.' Building a great autonomous driver is one part of building a successful autonomous business. Commercial success also requires marketplace demand aggregation and intelligent dispatch, vehicle integration and fleet operations, charging infrastructure, financing, insurance, and deep engagement with regulators and local communities. Our ambition is clear: to become the world's leading commercialization platform for autonomous mobility." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.5]; call wording: "our ambition is straightforward: to become the world's leading commercialization platform for autonomous vehicles." [Q2 2026 call, p.3]
- Industry-structure thesis: AVs "will follow a similar path" to AI foundation models (multiple frontier models plus open source, not one winner); "first-party and third-party distribution models will coexist, much like we see across sectors like travel, food delivery, and e-commerce"; "AVs are physical, regulated systems that must be deployed and operated market by market with the variable patterns of ridehailing, making utilization sensitive to demand peaks and troughs. That makes the commercialization layer even more important." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.4–5; Q2 2026 call, p.3]
- Recent partner events cited: "Just in the last week we've seen NVIDIA release its Alpamayo open weight model, and our partners at Wayve received a permit in the UK, and Zoox received approval to scale its robotaxi." [Q2 2026 call, p.3]
- Deployments: live in 7 cities, "on track for as many as 15 by year-end"; new European deployments with Autobrains and WeRide; advanced partnerships with Nuro, Wayve, Zoox; "multiple U.S. launches later this year (see supplemental slides for additional details)". [Q2 2026 remarks, p.5]
- AV Labs: "deploying hundreds of sensor-equipped vehicles capable of collecting millions of miles of high-fidelity driving data each month for our AV partners ... AV Labs is not an effort to rebuild the autonomous driver; it is part of the data and infrastructure layer that helps our partners develop and deploy faster." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.5]; call: "end-to-end models are replacing the heuristics approach"; "we're building out hundreds of cars that are riding in rideshare-specific scenarios with robotaxi grade kind of sensors ... we can provide that data to all of our partners so that we bring the benefit of scale to AV development." [Q2 2026 call, p.14–15]
- Utilization: "we can drive very significant utilization per vehicle, often mid to high 20s, low 30s in terms of trips per vehicle per day". [Q2 2026 call, p.4]
- Scale today: "AVs are doing kind of hundreds of thousands of trips per week. We're at 300 million kind of trips per week as well. So it's even less than 0.5% of our overall trip volume." [Q2 2026 call, p.7]
- Category effect: "in more mature AV markets including Los Angeles, San Francisco, and Phoenix, Uber's category share, both citywide and in AV operating zones, is higher today than it was a year ago." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6]; "our trip growth in San Francisco, LA, and Phoenix accelerated in Q2 versus Q1." [Q2 2026 call, p.13]
- Capital: "$10 billion of AV investments ... this is investment over a multi-year period"; two kinds: "equity investments with clear milestones that help us get roadmap visibility and position us front of the line for commercialization" (and "for every dollar that we have invested, our partners have been able to raise an additional $2.50 from other investors"), and "using our balance sheet selectively to bootstrap the AV infrastructure on the ground ... fleet ops, for real estate, or for the OEMs"; "we are working with third-party financial sponsors". [Q2 2026 call, p.10]
- Lesson: "Perhaps the biggest lesson we've learned over the past year is that commercialization compounds." [Q2 2026 remarks, p.6]
- Delivery robotics: sidewalk robots with Serve and Cartken; drones with Flytrex ("There are a number of partnerships coming up in the drone area"). [Q2 2026 call, p.4]
