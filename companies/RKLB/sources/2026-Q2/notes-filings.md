## 1. Business description (company's own words)

- Mission: "We Open Access to Space to Improve Life on Earth." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- "Rocket Lab is an end-to-end space company with an established track record of mission success. We deliver reliable launch services, spacecraft design services, spacecraft components, spacecraft manufacturing, optical systems and other spacecraft and on-orbit management solutions that make it faster, easier and more affordable to access space." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- "While our business has historically been centered on the manufacture of small-class launch vehicles and the related sale of launch services, we are currently innovating in the areas of medium-class launch vehicle and launch services, space systems design and manufacturing, on-orbit management solutions, and space data applications." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Two reportable segments, defined by the company: "Launch Services is the design, manufacture, and launch of orbital rockets to deploy payloads to various Earth orbits and interplanetary destinations." "Space Systems is the design and manufacture of spacecraft, spacecraft components and spacecraft program management services, space data applications, mission operations and optical systems." [10-K FY2025, Item 7] The 10-Q widens the Space Systems definition to "the design and manufacture of components and spacecraft program management services, space data applications, mission operations, optical systems, laser optical communications and space robotics" (reflecting the Mynaric and Motiv acquisitions). [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]
- Segment note wording: "Launch Services provides launch and launch related services to customers on a dedicated mission or ride share basis. Space systems is predominately comprised of spacecraft components and spacecraft manufacturing." The CEO is the chief operating decision maker and "uses gross profit as the measure of segment profit or loss." Contracts containing both elements are split "based upon the overall costs incurred for each of the reporting segments in comparison to total overall costs of the contract." "Management does not regularly review either reporting segment's total assets or operating expenses." [10-K FY2025, Note 20]

**Launch Services products**
- Electron: "our orbital small launch vehicle … capable of deploying spacecraft of up to 300 kg to low Earth orbit"; 18 m tall, 1.2 m diameter, lift-off mass approximately 14,000 kg; two stages plus a kick stage; ten Rutherford engines (5,600-lbf, liquid oxygen/kerosene, electric turbo-pumps, 3D-printed primary components) built in Long Beach. "Since its maiden launch in 2017, Electron has become the leading small spacecraft launch vehicle, delivering over 200 spacecraft to orbit for government and commercial customers across 75 successful missions through December 31, 2025. In 2025, Electron was the second most frequently launched orbital rocket." 77 successful missions as of February 26, 2026; 87 successful missions and over 250 spacecraft through June 30, 2026. [10-K FY2025, Item 1; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]
- HASTE: "a suborbital testbed launch vehicle derived from Rocket Lab's heritage Electron rocket. HASTE provides reliable, high-cadence flight test opportunities needed to advance hypersonic and suborbital system technology development." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Neutron: see §7.
- Launch-site capacity: LC-1 (Mahia, New Zealand, two pads) "capable of supporting up to 120 launches per year"; LC-2 (Wallops Island, Virginia) "licensed to support 8 missions per year"; LC-3 (Wallops, for Neutron) "licensed to support 2 missions per year, with the potential for an increased number of missions pending ongoing assessments." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- The New Zealand site operates under "a bi-lateral treaty between the United States and New Zealand governments that enables us to use U.S. launch and spacecraft technology for launches at LC-1 that otherwise would not be permitted for launches from foreign soil." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]

**Space Systems products**
- "Our space systems are the building blocks for spacecraft, which includes composite structures, reaction wheels, star trackers, solar power solutions, radios, separation systems, command and control spacecraft software and optical systems." Also "high voltage space-grade batteries" and "flight and ground software services." Spacecraft family (Photon and others) "configurable for a range of low Earth orbit, medium Earth orbit, geosynchronous orbit and interplanetary missions." Offers "spacecraft-as-a-service" and "constellation management services." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Solar: "a suite of vertically-integrated space solar cell, Coverglass Interconnected Cells ("CICs") and solar array products"; "Rocket Lab's space solar cells, CICs and panels are among the highest performing in the world." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Optical Systems (GEOST): "advanced electro-optical and infrared ("EO/IR") sensor payloads for national security space missions … space domain awareness, missile tracking, and tactical intelligence." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Flight heritage: "As of December 31, 2025, we have flight hardware on over 1,800 missions." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Mission examples named: NASA CAPSTONE (lunar, 2022); NASA ESCAPADE twin spacecraft "Blue and Gold" launched November 2025; SDA Tracking Layer Tranche 3 (see §8). [10-K FY2025, Item 1 and Item 7]

**Customers (company's words)**
- Launch: "government customers, such as the United States Department of War ("DoW"), NASA and other U.S. government agencies … Our launch services have been used by more than 20 organizations." Commercial names listed: "Blacksky Holdings, Canon, Kinéis, Capella Space, Planet, OHB Group and Synspective"; government names: DoW, NASA, DARPA, NRO. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Space Systems: "a diverse mix of commercial, aerospace prime contractors and government customers." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Sales model: "a unified global business development team that cross-sells launch and space systems … Given the well-defined and consolidated nature of our customer base, we are able to adequately address our market with a lean and focused team." "To date, conferences and industry events and direct outreach have been the primary drivers of our sales leads." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]

**Manufacturing footprint and facilities** — "We lease or have contractual rights to access, but do not own, these facilities." [10-K FY2025, Item 2]
- Long Beach, California (HQ; three buildings): Rutherford and Archimedes engine and avionics production, spacecraft design and production, Mission Operations Center; HQ lease expires June 30, 2027 (two five-year options); Engine Development Center lease to November 30, 2030. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Auckland, New Zealand: approximately 200,000 sq ft complex (four buildings), "our main production facility for Electron" (composites, batteries, tanks, fairings, harnesses, kick stages, final integration); lease to April 30, 2028. Propulsion test complex at Kopuku, ~45 km from Auckland, lease to November 15, 2029 with renewals to at least 2054. [10-K FY2025, Items 1 and 2]
- Mahia, New Zealand, LC-1: lease to November 30, 2027 with three three-year renewals (to at least 2036). [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Wallops Island, Virginia: LC-2 (Electron; agreement to September 28, 2028; first launch January 24, 2023) and LC-3 (Neutron; agreement to February 15, 2034). [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Albuquerque, New Mexico: approximately 160,000 sq ft solar cell-through-array facility; ground lease to September 18, 2050, second building to May 31, 2042. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Tucson, Arizona: optical systems (leases 2028–2032). Stennis Space Center, Mississippi: Archimedes engine test complex, lease to October 22, 2032. Middle River, Maryland: composites production and development complex, lease to March 31, 2034. [10-K FY2025, Item 2]
- Subsidiaries located in the United States, New Zealand, Canada and Australia (10-K); the 10-Q adds Germany (Mynaric). [10-K FY2025, Note 1; 10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]
- Long-lived assets by geography at 12/31/2025: United States $803,652 (94%), New Zealand $46,750 (5%), Canada $3,833 (1%). [10-K FY2025, Note 21]

**Vertical integration and supply chain**
- "We are highly vertically integrated, in that we design and manufacture many components and subsystems for our launch vehicles and family of spacecraft." "While we largely source raw materials and other inputs and services from multiple sources, in some cases we also purchase various inputs and services from a sole source … we manage this sole source risk through carrying increased buffer stock, particularly on long-lead items." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Category-1 certification by the NASA Launch Services Program. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]

## 7. Neutron

**What it is (verbatim)**
- "In March 2021, we announced plans to develop our reusable-ready medium-capacity Neutron launch vehicle that will increase the payload capacity of our space launch vehicles to approximately 13,000 kg for reusable configuration launches to low Earth orbit and support lighter payloads for higher orbits. Neutron will be tailored for commercial and U.S. government constellation launches and ultimately configurable for and capable of human space flight, enabling us to provide crew and cargo resupply to space stations. Neutron will also provide a dedicated service to orbit for larger civil, defense and commercial payloads that need a high level of schedule control and high-flight cadence. We expect to be able to leverage Electron's flight heritage across various vehicle subsystems designs, launch complexes and ground station infrastructure." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 and Item 7; repeated 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]
- "The medium-lift Neutron will be a two-stage launch vehicle that stands 43 meters tall with a 5.5-meter diameter fairings. Neutron will feature a reusable first stage designed to return to launch site as well as land on an ocean platform, enabling flexibility of use, higher launch cadence, and decreased launch costs for customers. Neutron launches are planned to take place from LC-3 at Virginia's Mid-Atlantic Regional Spaceport located at the NASA Wallops Flight Facility, which is currently under construction." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Payload spec change: the FY2023 10-K described Neutron as "approximately 15,000 kg for expendable launches to low Earth orbit and lighter payloads for reusable configurations"; the FY2025 10-K and 10-Q describe "approximately 13,000 kg for reusable configuration launches." [10-K FY2023, Item 7; 10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Engine: Archimedes, designed and produced in Long Beach; tested at Stennis Space Center, Mississippi (lease to October 22, 2032). LC-3 access agreement runs to February 15, 2034; LC-3 "is licensed to support 2 missions per year, with the potential for an increased number of missions pending ongoing assessments." Growth strategy: "Expand our addressable launch market with the development of the medium-lift Neutron launch vehicle, where the additional lift capacity will enable significantly higher revenue per launch." [10-K FY2025, Items 1 and 2]

**Status and timeline (verbatim)**
- 10-K (February 26, 2026): "We continue to make significant progress in the development of the Neutron launch vehicle. Neutron qualification testing of flight hardware from large structures through to component level systems is ongoing. Several major vehicle structures have completed successful qualification and are moving into final integration and test phase, including the fairing, second stage, and thrust structure. An unanticipated failure during qualification testing of the first stage tank occurred in January 2026 and this has impacted the expected timing of Neutron's first launch. Based on our evaluation of necessary time to produce a new tank and complete robust testing of the tank as well as the Archimedes engine, and qualification of remaining systems and hardware, Neutron's first launch is now targeted for Q4 2026. However, risk and uncertainty remains in the complex development cycle of a new launch vehicle which could impact our current best estimate of a targeted timeline for first launch." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Risk factor detail: "we experienced a qualification testing failure of the Neutron Stage 1 tank on January 21, 2026 which ruptured the tank during a hydrostatic pressure trial resulting in an impact to Neutron's launch schedule." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- LC-3 "is expected to be in service in 2026." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- 10-Q (August 10, 2026): "We continue to make significant progress in the development of the Neutron launch vehicle. Neutron qualification testing from large structures through to component level systems is ongoing. During Q2, we achieved significant milestones across the Neutron program with ongoing integration and readiness of first-flight hardware, continued progress on Archimedes engine testing, and advancement of the second stage and reusable fairing systems. Production of the Stage 1 tank is currently aligned with the target delivery of Neutron to the launch pad in Q4 2026. While the window for an end-of-year launch date is narrowing, we are balancing the schedule of the first launch with entering Neutron into service as a system ready for full-scale production and high-cadence launch beyond flight one. Exact launch timing will also depend on the outcome of first stage qualification and other critical tests occurring later in 2026. Risk and uncertainty remains in the complex development cycle of a new launch vehicle which could impact our current best estimate of a targeted timeline for first launch." [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]

## 10. Why not profitable (MD&A's own explanation)

- Gross margin trajectory (company-stated): 21.0% (FY2023) → 26.6% (FY2024) → 34.4% (FY2025) → 36.1% (Q2 2026). By segment **(computed)**: Launch 11.2% → 27.6% → 40.8% → 42.9%; Space Systems 25.1% → 26.2% → 31.3% → 34.6%. [10-K FY2025, Item 7; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2; Notes 20/18]

Meanwhile, cost per launch was $4.8 million, $5.7 million and $7.0 million … The decrease in cost per launch in the years ended December 31, 2025 and 2024 was driven by efficiencies of scale." Earlier: "revenue value per launch was $7.1 million, $6.7 million and $8.1 million" and "cost per launch was $7.0 million, $7.5 million and $9.2 million" for 2023, 2022, 2021.
