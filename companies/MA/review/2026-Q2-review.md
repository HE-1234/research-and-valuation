## 1. Rubric (§14)

Read as the owner would: a smart 16-year-old with no finance background.

1. **Can I explain what this company does, and who pays it, in two sentences?** **Yes.** b:6 does it: Mastercard runs the network that passes a card payment from the shop's bank to the cardholder's bank and moves the money between them; it is not a bank and does not lend; the banks on both sides pay it per transaction and per dollar of volume (b:26).
2. **Do I know exactly what would kill it, and what the early warning sign is?** **Yes.** §6 ranks five scenarios, each with what must happen, an early warning and the exposed profit pool: interchange or network-fee regulation (a U.S. credit-routing bill reaching a vote; rebates jumping), litigation (the provision line and accrued balance; a settlement rejected), domestic and government payment systems (switched transactions growing slower than GDV; Europe slowing), a large bank or merchant defecting (the five-largest share; U.S. debit growth), a breach or outage. The DOJ civil investigative demand that appears in the FY2025 10-K and vanishes from both 2026 10-Qs is flagged honestly (b:111).
3. **Do I know why the margins are what they are, and whether cost scales with usage?** **Yes.** §3 says there is no gross margin line and why (four operating-expense lines, no cost of revenue), shows revenue +74% against operating expenses +58% over four years, names what does rise (rebates above the revenue line, people, litigation, acquisitions) and labels the services-mix margin effect as an inference.
4. **Could I predict what the scorecard will check next quarter, from §5 of the outlook alone?** **Yes.** Ten claims, all single-direction and mechanical; claim 3 needs its basis named (see §5 below) so the grader knows which "adjusted operating expenses" growth to read.
5. **Did nothing in the report require knowledge I don't have?** **Yes, after the glosses below.** Before review about fifteen terms were unexplained (GDV at first use, Maestro, capex, depreciation and amortization, provision for litigation, capitalized software, restructuring, book equity, surcharging, opt-out merchants, civil investigative demand, antitrust, diluted shares, switching penetration, agentic commerce, stablecoin, endpoints, headwinds); all are now glossed inline (see "Fixed directly"). Still open for the writer: the "higher rate" claim for cross-border (REVISE item 5), which is not jargon but an unsourced assertion.
