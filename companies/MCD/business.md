# McDonald's Corporation — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

McDonald's is a chain of 46,028 restaurants in 114 countries selling burgers, chicken, fries, breakfast, desserts and drinks [Q2 2026 supplement, p.12] [10-K FY2025, Products]. About 95% are run by franchisees, independent business people who own the restaurant business, hire the crew, set prices and pay McDonald's for the right to use the brand, the operating system and, usually, the building [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A] [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business]. In the company's words, "The Company is primarily a franchisor" [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business]. So the customer who pays McDonald's is mostly the franchisee, not the diner, and what McDonald's sells the franchisee is a proven restaurant format, a brand the CEO calls "one of one", a supply chain, national marketing and a piece of real estate [Q2 2026 call, l.25] [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business].

Two numbers matter more than revenue. **Systemwide sales** are the sales of every McDonald's restaurant, franchised or company-run: $139.4 billion in 2025 [10-K FY2025, MD&A]. McDonald's revenue was only $26.9 billion, because franchisee sales are not its revenue; only the rent, royalties (a percentage of each restaurant's sales) and fees it collects on them are [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Statement of Income] [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Revenues)]. **Comparable sales** are the year-over-year sales change at restaurants open at least thirteen months, at last year's exchange rates, so they show whether existing restaurants are busier or pricier, not whether new ones opened [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Management's View of the Business)]. Systemwide sales growth is roughly comparable sales plus new restaurants [10-K FY2025, MD&A].

**How they got here.** McDonald's celebrated its 70th anniversary in 2025; October 5 is founder Ray Kroc's birthday, kept as "Founder's Day" [DEF 14A 2026, Chairman's Letter] [Q2 2026 call, l.52]. The recent story is a strategy called Accelerating the Arches, introduced in 2020 and refreshed in 2023 to add new-restaurant development as a growth pillar [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A]. Along the way the company sold its Russian business in 2022, closing 855 restaurants at a $1,281 million pre-tax charge [10-K FY2023, MD&A] [10-K FY2022, MD&A]. In June 2026 it announced a successor strategy, McDonald's > NEXT, with the ambition "to be more customers' first choice" [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A].

**The franchise deal.** Under a **conventional franchise**, "the Company generally owns or secures a long-term lease on the land and building for the restaurant location and the franchisee pays for equipment, signs, seating and décor" [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business (Conventional Franchise)]. The franchisee then pays "rent and royalties based upon a percent of sales, with specified minimum rent payments, along with initial fees paid upon the opening of a new restaurant or grant of a new franchise" [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business (Conventional Franchise)]. The rent rate, the royalty rate and the size of the initial fee are not disclosed in any filing [10-K FY2025, Note: Franchise Arrangements]. The agreement runs 20 years; at the end "the Company maintains control of the underlying real estate and building" and can re-franchise or close the site [10-K FY2025, Note: Franchise Arrangements]. Rents were 63% of franchised revenues in 2025 (computed), so McDonald's is landlord first and brand owner second [10-K FY2025, Note: Franchise Arrangements].

Under a **developmental license**, the licensee is responsible for "providing capital (including the real estate interest)" and McDonald's "generally does not invest any restaurant capital"; it receives only a royalty on sales plus initial fees [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business (Developmental License or Affiliate)]. An **affiliate** is the same deal in a market where McDonald's also owns part of the licensee, "primarily China and Japan" (48% and 35%) [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business (Developmental License or Affiliate)] [10-K FY2025, Note: Equity Method Investments]. Where each dominates: conventional franchises are the U.S. and the owned international markets (Australia, Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Poland, Spain, the U.K. and some smaller ones); licensees and affiliates cover "over 75 countries" in the International Developmental Licensed segment, which is 99% franchised [10-K FY2025, Note: Segment and Geographic Information] [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business]. At June 30, 2026 the U.S. had 13,128 conventional franchises and 641 company-run restaurants; the licensed segment had 9,642 developmental licensed, 11,461 affiliated (China alone 8,114, Japan 3,038) and 238 company-run [Q2 2026 supplement, p.12] [Q2 2026 supplement, p.13].

## 3. The economics

McDonald's reports no gross margin; the closest thing is "restaurant margins", one for each kind of restaurant [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Margins)]. On a franchised restaurant, McDonald's revenue is rent plus royalty and its only cost is the property: lease expense on ground it rents and depreciation on buildings it owns [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Margins)]. That cost barely moves when the franchisee sells more, so the franchised margin was 84% of franchised revenues in 2025 (computed) and rises with sales [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Margins)]. On a company-run restaurant, McDonald's pays for food and paper ($3.0 billion in 2025), crew payroll ($2.9 billion) and occupancy and other running costs ($2.4 billion) against $9.7 billion of sales; every extra burger costs more beef and labor, so the margin was 14.7% (computed) and has fallen under "ongoing inflationary cost pressures" [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Statement of Income] [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Margins)]. Result: franchised restaurants produced "approximately 90% of restaurant margin dollars" [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Margins)].

**Where the money goes.** Of $26.9 billion of 2025 revenue, $8.3 billion went to running the company restaurants, $2.6 billion to property costs on franchised sites, $0.6 billion to pass-through technology costs and $3.0 billion to overhead, leaving $12.4 billion of operating income, a 46.1% margin [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Statement of Income] [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Operating Income)]. Overhead is small against the system it supports: 2.2% of Systemwide sales in 2025 (computed) and in the first half of 2026 (as stated) [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Statement of Income] [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A].

**Capital intensity: modest to stand still, real to grow.** Franchisees, not McDonald's, buy the equipment and "are responsible for reinvesting capital in their businesses over time", including remodels the CEO says are due "every 10 years" under the franchise agreement [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business (Conventional Franchise)] [Q2 2026 call, l.174]. McDonald's own capex is mostly new restaurants: $3.4 billion in 2025, "mainly allocated to new restaurant openings and, to a lesser extent, to reinvestment in existing restaurants"; in 2022 and 2023 the split was "approximately 50% to each"; the dollar split is not disclosed (the chart is an image) [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Development and Capital Expenditures)] [10-K FY2023, MD&A (Restaurant Development and Capital Expenditures)]. The asset base behind the rent is large: the company owned about 56% of the land and 80% of the buildings in its consolidated markets (the markets it accounts for directly, mainly the U.S. and the owned international markets), held $28.2 billion of net property and equipment, and is itself the tenant on $14.8 billion of leases [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Restaurant Development and Capital Expenditures)] [10-K FY2025, Note: Property and Equipment] [10-K FY2025, Note: Leasing Arrangements].

The cash is real. Shareholders' equity is negative (a $1,791 million deficit) because $79.3 billion has been spent buying back stock, not because of losses [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Balance Sheet].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

There are two customers, diners and franchisees, and the moat is different for each.

**Diners** are held by habit, convenience and price, not a contract. McDonald's has "the most drive thru locations worldwide, with nearly 29,000", delivers from nearly 41,000 restaurants, and runs loyalty programs in 70 markets with nearly 220 million people active in the last 90 days, whose trailing-twelve-month spending was $40 billion [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Growth Pillars)] [Q2 2026 release, p.1]. The menu holds "seventeen unique billion-dollar brands" that competitors cannot copy by name [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Growth Pillars)]. Our inference: the source is scale, the same brand, price point and drive-thru on every road, reinforced by a loyalty app that turns an occasional visitor into a tracked, prompted one. Sign of weakening: comparable guest counts, which the company describes only by sign; they were negative in the U.S. in 2024 and again in Q2 2026 even as comparable sales rose, meaning fewer people paid more [10-K FY2024, MD&A] [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A].

**Franchisees** are held by the deal itself. McDonald's owns or leases the site, so a franchisee who leaves gives up the location; the agreement lasts 20 years and the company "generally does not work with passive investors" [10-K FY2025, Note: Franchise Arrangements] [10-K FY2025, Description of the Business (Conventional Franchise)]. In return the franchisee gets a restaurant that sells roughly $4 million a year in the U.S. (2025 U.S. franchised sales over the June 2026 count of conventional franchised restaurants, computed) and a supply chain and advertising pool funded by everyone's percent of sales [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Franchised Sales)] [Q2 2026 supplement, p.13] [10-K FY2025, Note: Summary of Significant Accounting Policies]. Signs of weakening: any return of "royalty relief and/or deferral of cash collection", which appeared in 2023 and 2024; franchisees not following pricing guidance, as the CEO said about a third did not with the new U.S. value menu in Q2 2026; and the U.S. company-operated margin, in our view the best public proxy for what a franchisee earns, which fell from 19.5% in 2021 to 11.6% in Q2 2026 (computed) [10-K FY2024, MD&A (Revenues)] [Q2 2026 call, l.104] [10-K FY2023, MD&A (Restaurant Margins)] [10-K FY2023, MD&A (Revenues)] [Q2 2026 supplement, p.7].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. The U.S. loses the value argument and traffic keeps falling.** The U.S. is 40% of revenue and 47% of segment operating income (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note: Segment and Geographic Information]. In Q2 2026 U.S. comparable sales rose 0.8% on "positive check growth... partly offset by negative comparable guest counts" (check is the average spend per order), the CFO said July comparable sales were "slightly negative", and the CEO blamed "a bad trade": a value menu that franchisees executed unevenly, paid for by cutting the digital offers loyal customers valued [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A] [Q2 2026 call, l.94] [Q2 2026 call, l.75]. Early warning: two consecutive quarters of negative U.S. comparable sales, or guest counts still negative while check growth shrinks. Exposure: $7.4 billion of U.S. franchised revenues move with franchisee sales [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Revenues)].

**2. Franchisee economics or alignment break.** "Our success as a heavily franchised business relies to a large degree on the financial success and cooperation of our franchisees", who must fund remodels and value programs and depend on "the availability to them of funding at reasonable interest rates" [10-K FY2025, Risk Factors]. A related legal risk is any ruling that erodes "the legal distinction between our franchisees and us for employment law", making McDonald's liable for franchisee employees [10-K FY2025, Risk Factors]. Early warning: royalty relief language in the 10-Q; franchised sales growth lagging comparable sales; a rising count of restaurants bought back from franchisees. Exposure: all $16.5 billion of franchised revenues [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Statement of Income].

**3. Consumers move away from fast food.** The 10-K names "use of weight-loss medications" among the changes that could "alter consumer behavior" [10-K FY2025, Risk Factors]. McDonald's measures itself against the whole "informal eating out" segment, including convenience stores and coffee shops, and management's yardstick is "are we taking share in each of the markets" [10-K FY2025, Competition] [Q2 2026 call, l.156]. Early warning: comparable guest counts negative across all three segments for a year.

**4. Geopolitics and anti-American sentiment abroad.** About 68% of operating income is earned outside the U.S., and the 10-K flags "geopolitical tensions, anti-American sentiment" and the risk of being unable "to operate in certain geographic areas" [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Liquidity and Uses of Cash)] [10-K FY2025, Risk Factors]. Precedents: the Russia exit cost $1,281 million and 855 restaurants; the war in the Middle East brought royalty relief in licensed markets [10-K FY2022, MD&A] [10-K FY2023, MD&A (Revenues)]. China has 8,114 restaurants and negative comparable sales in Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 supplement, p.12] [10-Q Q2 2026, MD&A]. Early warning: negative comparable sales in the licensed segment; an impairment (write-down) of an affiliate stake. Exposure: licensed-market royalties are only $1.9 billion of revenue, but the owned international markets are $13.6 billion [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Revenues)] [10-K FY2025, Note: Segment and Geographic Information].

**5. A food-safety event.** "Instances of food tampering, food contamination or food-borne illness, whether actual or perceived, could adversely affect our brand, reputation and financial results" [10-K FY2025, Risk Factors]. No early warning exists in the filings; the first sign is the comparable-sales line.

**6. Tax.** The IRS is auditing 2011–2012 and 2016–2021, foreign audits center on transfer pricing (how profit is split between the countries inside the company), and a 2022 French settlement cost $537 million [10-K FY2025, Note: Income Taxes] [10-K FY2022, MD&A]. Early warning: unrecognized tax benefits (tax the company might owe if audits go against it; $414 million at end-2025) jumping [10-K FY2025, Note: Income Taxes].

**Concentration.** No franchisee or licensee is named or large enough to disclose; the two affiliates in China and Japan together produced $579 million of 2025 revenue, 2.2% of the total (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note: Equity Method Investments] [10-K FY2025, Consolidated Statement of Income].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

Chris Kempczinski, 57, has been CEO since November 2019 and Chairman since May 2024; before that he ran the U.S. business [10-K FY2025, Information About Our Executive Officers]. Ian Borden, 57, has been CFO since September 2022 after more than 30 years at the company, including running the licensed markets [10-K FY2025, Information About Our Executive Officers]. On August 4, 2026 Skye Anderson, a 26-year McDonald's executive and since April its U.S. Chief Operating Officer, replaced Joe Erlinger as President, McDonald's USA; Erlinger stays as an adviser until early 2027, and the 8-K gives no reason for the change [8-K 2026-08-04] [Leadership release 2026-08-04] [Q2 2026 call, l.54]. The board has twelve directors, eleven independent, with Miles White as Lead Independent Director [DEF 14A 2026, Overview of Director Nominees] [DEF 14A 2026, Board Leadership].

**Pay.** The annual bonus is 40% operating income growth, 30% Systemwide sales growth, 15% new restaurant openings and 15% a strategic scorecard; 2025 paid out at 76.4% of target because operating income and Systemwide sales missed [DEF 14A 2026, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. Long-term awards are half performance shares, judged 75% on three-year EPS (earnings per share) growth and 25% on return on invested capital, with a modifier for the stock's return against the S&P 500, and half stock options; the 2023–2025 shares vested at 82.2% [DEF 14A 2026, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. Kempczinski's 2025 total was $20.6 million, 1,082 times the median employee, a restaurant crew member in Poland paid $19,020 [DEF 14A 2026, 2025 Summary Compensation Table] [DEF 14A 2026, 2025 Pay Ratio]. Shareholders approved the pay with 95.1% of votes cast for or against (computed) [8-K 2026-05-22]. Insiders own under 1%; Vanguard holds 10.03%, BlackRock 7.3%, State Street 5.1% [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership of Management] [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership of Certain Beneficial Owners].

**Cash.** The stated order is "(i) invest in opportunities to grow the business... (ii) prioritize our dividend and (iii) repurchase shares with remaining free cash flow over time" [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Foundation and Platforms)]. The dividend has been paid for 50 consecutive years and raised every year, to $1.86 a quarter from Q4 2025; dividends took $5.1 billion in 2025 [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Share Repurchases and Dividends)]. Buybacks absorbed the rest: $2.0 billion in 2025 and $1.3 billion in the first half of 2026, with $11.7 billion left under a $15.0 billion program [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Share Repurchases and Dividends)] [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 2]. Total returned in 2025 was $7.1 billion (as stated), almost exactly the $7.2 billion of free cash flow [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Share Repurchases and Dividends)] [10-K FY2025, MD&A (Cash Flows)]. A restructuring called Accelerating the Organization has cost $795 million since 2023, with about $250 million more expected in 2026 [10-Q Q2 2026, Note: Accelerating the Organization]. Track record on the stock: $100 at end-2020 became $160 at end-2025 against $196 for the S&P 500 [10-K FY2025, Stock Performance Graph].

## Glossary

- **Systemwide sales:** sales at all McDonald's restaurants, franchised and company-run; the base on which rent and royalties are calculated, not the company's revenue.
- **Comparable sales:** the change in sales at restaurants open at least thirteen months versus the same period a year earlier, excluding currency moves and hyperinflationary markets.
- **Conventional franchise:** the arrangement in which McDonald's owns or leases the land and building and the franchisee pays rent, royalties and an initial fee under a 20-year agreement.
- **Developmental licensee:** an operator that funds its own restaurants and real estate in a whole country or region and pays McDonald's a royalty on sales.
- **Affiliate:** a developmental licensee in which McDonald's also owns a stake, mainly the operators in China (48%) and Japan (35%).
