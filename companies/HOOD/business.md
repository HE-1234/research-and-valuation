# Robinhood Markets, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-09._

## 1. What they do

Robinhood is a phone app through which ordinary people buy and sell stocks, options (contracts giving the right to buy or sell a stock at a set price), cryptocurrencies, futures (contracts to buy or sell something at a set price on a future date) and "event contracts" (bets on whether something happens), keep cash, borrow against their holdings, hold a credit card and a bank account, and save for retirement [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]. It had 28.4 million Funded Customers holding $369 billion ("Total Platform Assets") at June 30, 2026 [Q2 2026 release, p.1]. Customers are retail investors, often new: "many of our customers have told us that Robinhood was their first brokerage account" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Founded "in 2013 on the belief that everyone should be welcome to participate in our financial system", it was "the first U.S. retail broker to offer commission-free stock trading with no account minimums" [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**Who pays, if trades are free.** Market makers pay Robinhood for its customers' stock, option and crypto orders; customers pay interest on borrowed money, a Gold subscription, commissions on event contracts and futures, and credit-card interest; banks pay a spread on customer cash swept to them [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Management's three "arcs": "being number one in active traders", "the leader in wallet share for the next generation" and "building the leading global financial ecosystem" [Q2 2026 call, p.2].

**A short history.** Vladimir Tenev and Baiju Bhatt founded the company in 2013; it began clearing its own trades in November 2018 and listed on Nasdaq on July 29, 2021 [10-K FY2025, Item 5] [10-K FY2023, Item 1A]. In January 2021 it "temporarily restricted or limited its customers' purchase of certain securities, including GameStop Corp. and AMC Entertainment Holdings, Inc." when its clearinghouse (the central body that settles trades) demanded more collateral (cash as security) [10-K FY2025, Note 15]. Acquisitions: Say Technologies (shareholder voting, 2021), X1 (credit card, 2023), TradePMR (custody for independent advisers, 2025), Bitstamp (crypto exchange, 2025), the MIAXdx exchange through the Rothera joint venture with Susquehanna (2026) and WonderFi (Canada, 2026); prices are in §7 [10-K FY2022, Note 3] [10-K FY2023, Note 3] [10-K FY2025, Note 3] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3]. In 2026 it became broker and trustee for the U.S. Treasury's Trump Accounts (launched July 4) and launched its own blockchain, Robinhood Chain [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A].

**(a) Transaction-based revenues: payment for order flow.** A customer's stock or option order does not go to a stock exchange; Robinhood "route[s] these orders to market makers and we receive consideration from those market makers", a payment "known as PFOF" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Options pay a fee per contract; stocks pay "a fixed percentage of the difference between the publicly quoted bid and ask"; crypto pays a rebate as a fixed percentage of the dollar value; event contracts and futures instead carry a commission paid by the customer [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The market maker gets retail orders to fill from its own inventory. All pay the same rate for a given trade type, and the router favours those that "have historically given customers the best prices", an "incentive for market makers to provide better prices for our customers, in order to receive more orders in the future" [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Of FY2025's $2,628 million of transaction revenue, $2,326 million came from routing to market makers [10-K FY2025, Item 8].

Why regulators care: a broker owes customers "best execution", and one paid by the executing firm has a conflict; the 10-K admits customers might "perceive our PFOF practices to create a conflict of interest between us and them" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Robinhood "settled an SEC investigation into our best execution and PFOF practices" in December 2020 (amount not in the cached filings) and has faced a New York Attorney General inquiry since July 2023 [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. The cached filings do not discuss U.K. or EU treatment of PFOF [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Because rivals depend less on PFOF, "any legal or regulatory change that impacts PFOF could have an outsized impact on our results", and the arrangements "are often not documented under binding contracts" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

## 3. The economics

**Cost is mostly fixed; revenue swings with trading.** Robinhood reports no gross profit [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. The nearest thing to a cost of sales is "Brokerage and transaction", of which "a large portion ... are variable and tied to trading and transaction volumes"; it was 5% of FY2025 revenue [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The rest is people and cloud: technology and development 20% of revenue, operations 3%, marketing 9%, general and administrative 14%, plus 3% for credit losses on the card book [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. So when retail trading faded in 2022, revenue fell 25% (computed) and the company lost money; in booming Q2 2026 the incremental Adjusted EBITDA margin (the share of each extra revenue dollar that became Adjusted EBITDA) was 60% [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [Q2 2026 slides, p.45].

**Read operating cash flow with care.** For a broker it swings with customer balances, not profit: in FY2024 Robinhood earned $1,411 million yet reported negative operating cash flow because margin loans grew by $4,592 million [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. Computed free cash flow is therefore unreliable; the steadier reading is Adjusted EBITDA less stock pay, about $2.2 billion in FY2025 (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Two items that flatter or punish net income.** FY2024 included a $347 million tax benefit, mostly from releasing the "valuation allowance": once "sustained profitability" made past losses usable against future tax, the future saving was booked as income at once [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-K FY2024, Note 9]. FY2023 included $485 million for cancelling the founders' 2021 award of 35.5 million shares; the unrecognised cost of a cancelled award is expensed immediately, although "No other payments, replacement equity awards or benefits were granted" [10-K FY2025, Note 12].

**Return on capital in plain words.** Shareholders' equity was $9.5 billion at June 30, 2026, of which $5.4 billion is corporate cash; FY2025 net income was 22% of average equity (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. The rest of the $56.6 billion balance sheet is customer money and customer loans passing through [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

Our inference: the moat is not a network effect but a bundle of habit, low prices, a widening product set and incentives that vest over years.

**Product breadth.** Thirteen business lines have each reached a $100 million annual revenue run-rate (a quarter's revenue times four) [Q2 2026 slides, p.16]. Management's flywheel: "We get a customer in, they become a Gold Subscriber ... And then a decent chunk of their earnings - if we get the direct deposit - go into Robinhood" [Q2 2026 call, p.21]. Weakening sign: new lines stop reaching $100 million.

**Gold, Banking and the card.** 4.84 million Gold Subscribers, 17% of customers; the average Gold Subscriber holds about 4.2 times the assets of the average customer [Q2 2026 slides, p.9]. Banking holds $3.1 billion from 245 thousand customers; the Gold Card has 1 million holders [Q2 2026 slides, p.34] [Q2 2026 release, p.2]. Weakening sign: new-customer Gold sign-up below the "approximately 40 percent" of Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

**Matches that vest.** IRA and transfer matches are paid up front but the customer must stay for years: about $761 million was unamortised (not yet charged against revenue) at June 30, 2026 over about four years, an explicit switching cost [Q2 2026 slides, p.37]. Weakening sign: assets transferred out ($2.4 billion in Q2 2026 against $5.2 billion in) closing the gap [Q2 2026 slides, p.10].

**Low prices, brand with younger investors, deposit inertia.** Trades are commission-free and Gold cuts other fees [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Customers "tend to be younger" and "use some of these drawdowns as ways to buy"; Net Deposits were $75.7 billion over twelve months, 27% of starting assets [Q2 2026 call, p.18] [Q2 2026 release, p.1]. Weakening sign: the annualized Net Deposit growth rate below management's 20% target for two quarters; churned customers above the 0.4–0.6 million a quarter of 2025–2026 [Q2 2026 slides, p.29].

**The adviser channel.** TradePMR holds $50 billion for independent advisers, "a good, durable, consistent source of Net Deposits" [Q2 2026 release, p.2] [Q2 2026 call, p.18]. Weakening sign: RIA assets flat while markets rise.

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by our judgement of plausibility.

**1. A U.S. ban or re-pricing of payment for order flow.** Options and equities were 36% of Q2 2026 revenue (computed), nearly all PFOF [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. What must happen: Congress or the SEC restricts PFOF, or the 2026 tick-size (minimum price step) and quote rules compress spreads so market makers pay less [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: "lower option rebate rates" in the 10-Q; Rule 605 execution-quality reports (the SEC's standard broker scorecards) from August 2026 showing Robinhood behind peers [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**2. Interest rates fall.** Net interest was 30% of Q2 2026 revenue; a 100 basis point (one percentage point) cut removes $350 million of pre-tax profit over twelve months, about 8% of FY2025 revenue (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 3] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Cash Sweep is excluded from that sensitivity because Robinhood can cut the rate it pays [10-K FY2025, Item 7A]. Early warning: the blended yield on interest-earning assets, down from 3.74% in 2023 to 2.34% in Q2 2026 [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [Q2 2026 slides, p.39].

**3. Crypto volumes collapse or rules turn.** Crypto was 23% of revenue in FY2021, 7% in FY2023, 20% in FY2025 and 8% in Q2 2026; app crypto volume fell 35% in a year [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 release, p.3]. Staking, onchain lending and stock tokens each risk being called unregistered securities activity; Robinhood Chain carries its own adoption, technical and illicit-use risks [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: monthly app crypto volume; a crypto market maker withdrawing [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**4. Prediction markets are restricted.** Event contracts grew from $10 million to $156 million of quarterly revenue in a year, 12% of the total [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Robinhood stopped offering new sports contracts in Nevada from December 1, 2025; Wisconsin and Kentucky sued in 2026; Illinois taxes contracts from July 1, 2026; such actions could "immediately or subsequently prevent us from offering" event contracts [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 15] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: new state actions in the legal note; Event Contracts Traded falling outside sports seasons.

**5. A retail-trading downturn like 2022.** In FY2022 revenue fell 25%, transaction revenue 42%, and the loss was $1,028 million [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 8]. Q2 2026 had help from "A really strong market backdrop" and "the SpaceX IPO" [Q2 2026 call, p.11]. Early warning: monthly volumes and Net Deposits [June 2026 metrics].

**6. Operational or regulatory failure.** Outages (March 2020; crypto, April–May 2021), a third-party overnight venue's disruption in August 2024 still under regulatory examination, the January 2021 collateral squeeze, a November 2021 data breach, and 2025 settlements of $45 million (SEC) and $26 million (FINRA, the brokers' self-regulator) show the pattern; the New York Attorney General, Massachusetts and the FDIC have open inquiries [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 15]. Early warning: the $89 million contingency accrual (money set aside for expected legal losses) jumping; net capital cushions shrinking [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 15] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

**7. Competition.** The 10-K names no rival, only "large legacy financial institutions, large technology companies, and smaller, new financial technology entrants" that copy its features and "might choose to forgo PFOF" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Coinbase and Webull appear only as co-defendants in the Wisconsin and Kentucky suits; Kalshi is both a venue Robinhood routes to and a rival [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 15] [Q2 2026 call, p.27]. Early warning: Funded Customer additions slowing while marketing rises.

**Customer concentration** is market-maker concentration: Citadel Securities was 16% of Q2 2026 revenue and all market makers 47%, without binding contracts (§2) [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Vladimir Tenev, 39, co-founder, has been sole CEO since November 2020 and chair since March 2021; co-founder Baiju Bhatt, 41, was Chief Creative Officer until March 2024 and remains a director [DEF 14A 2026, "Executive Officers"] [DEF 14A 2026, "Director Nominees"]. ## Glossary

- **Payment for order flow (PFOF):** money a market maker pays a broker for sending it customers' orders to execute, used here as Robinhood's main way of earning on stock and option trades.
- **Market maker:** a trading firm that stands ready to buy or sell a security at quoted prices and earns the gap between its buying and selling price, used to fill retail orders.
- **Margin loan:** a loan from the broker to a customer secured by the customer's holdings, used to buy more securities; Robinhood earns the interest.
- **Cash Sweep:** the automatic transfer of customers' idle cash into deposits at partner banks, used to earn a spread between what the banks pay and what customers receive.
- **Securities lending:** lending customers' shares to other traders (often short sellers) for a fee, used as a source of interest revenue.
- **Event contract:** a contract that pays $1 if a stated event happens and nothing if it does not, traded on a regulated exchange, used for what Robinhood calls prediction markets.
- **Net capital:** the liquid capital a broker-dealer must hold by regulation above its liabilities, used as the buffer that lets it keep operating in a crisis.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** net income with interest on debt, taxes, depreciation, stock-based compensation and one-off items added back, used by management as its main profit measure.
- **Adjusted Operating Expenses and SBC:** operating expenses excluding credit losses, legal settlements and one-off items but including ordinary stock pay, used as the measure management guides to for the year.
- **Convertible notes and capped call:** debt the holder can swap for shares above a set price, paired with an option the company buys to limit the shares it must issue up to a cap, used to raise $2.2 billion at 0% interest.
- **Registered investment adviser (RIA):** an independent financial adviser regulated by the SEC, the customer group served by TradePMR.
