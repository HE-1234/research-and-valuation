# Sandisk Corporation — The Business
_As of Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended July 3, 2026). Written 2026-09-09._

Sandisk's fiscal year ends on the Friday nearest June 30. FY2026 was a 53-week year ended July 3, 2026, with a 14-week first quarter; FY2025 (ended June 27, 2025) and FY2024 (ended June 28, 2024) had 52 weeks [10-K FY2026, Item 7; Note 1].

## 1. What they do

Sandisk makes NAND flash memory: the chips that keep data when the power is off, inside the storage drives (SSDs) in data centers, PCs, phones, cars, memory cards and USB sticks [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. It designs the memory and the controller chips that manage it, has the wafers made in Japan by Flash Ventures, its joint ventures with Kioxia, and assembles and tests the finished products mostly in its own plant in Penang, Malaysia [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 2]. "All of our flash-based memory is obtained from our joint ventures with Kioxia" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Customers fall into three end markets: Datacenter, Edge and Consumer [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

Two facts organise everything else. First, NAND is a commodity sold by the bit: Sandisk's products "are designed to be largely interchangeable with competitors' products", so the price is set by industry supply against demand, not by Sandisk [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Second, the factories are not Sandisk's: it owns 49.9% of three Japanese joint ventures, must pay half their fixed costs whether or not it takes the wafers, and may not make flash anywhere else [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 1A].

History, as far as the filings tell it. The joint ventures date from 2004, 2006 and 2010 and were originally with Toshiba; the partner today is Kioxia [10-K FY2026, Note 10; Item 15]. Two current officers worked at "the prior SanDisk Corporation" until 2016; the Form 10 calls the business "an operating segment of WDC" (Western Digital) for FY2022–FY2024, and that it had been one since 2016 is our inference from the officer bios [DEF 14A 2025, Executive Officers; Form 10, Note 1]. WDC announced the separation on October 30, 2023 and completed it on February 21, 2025, keeping 19.9% of the shares, which it has since been selling down [10-K FY2026, Item 7; 8-K 2025-02-03]. In FY2026 the company renamed its end markets ("Datacenter" was "Cloud", "Edge" was "Client"), signed its first multi-year customer contracts, repaid all its debt and began buying back stock [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 7].

**Reading the five-year tables.** FY2022–FY2024 are carve-out ("combined") statements of "The Flash Business of Western Digital Corporation", with WDC's shared costs allocated to it; FY2025 is carve-out until February 21, 2025 and standalone after; FY2026 is fully standalone [Form 10, Note 1; 10-K FY2026, Note 1; Item 7]. WDC's allocated costs were about 70% of R&D plus SG&A in FY2023 and 75% in FY2024 (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 10; 10-K FY2026, Note 10]. FY2022 exists only in the Form 10, which has no FY2022 balance sheet; in the tables "n/d" means not disclosed and "n/a" not in our sources [Form 10, Combined Balance Sheets].

## 2. How the money comes in

Sandisk sells chips and drives; there is no service or licensing revenue of any size, and revenue is split only by end market, not by product type [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 3].

**Datacenter (25% of FY2026 revenue, computed).** Enterprise SSDs for servers and "AI-related workloads", bought by "datacenters, cloud service providers, and private cloud customers" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Revenue rose 437% in FY2026 on "almost 120%" more bits at "almost 150%" higher price per gigabyte, and Datacenter went from 12% of bits shipped in Q4 FY2025 to 38% in Q4 FY2026 [10-K FY2026, Item 7; Q4 FY2026 slides, p.8].

**Edge (60%).** SSDs and embedded flash sold to the makers of PCs, phones, gaming consoles, cars and industrial gear (OEMs) and through distributors [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Bits grew "high single-digits percent" in FY2026 but price per gigabyte rose "almost 180%" [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**Consumer (14%).** Memory cards, USB drives and portable SSDs under the Sandisk brand, sold through "351K Points of Sale" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; 2026 Investor Day slides, p.10]. Bits fell "mid-teens percent" in FY2026 as supply went to the other two markets, while price rose "low-fifties percent" [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

## 3. The economics

NAND is priced per bit, and the price moves with industry supply and demand: down 39% in FY2023, down 8% in FY2024, up 4% in FY2025, then up roughly 150–180% in FY2026 [Form 10, MD&A; 10-K FY2025, Item 7; 10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Cost per bit, by contrast, falls slowly as each new chip generation (a "node"; Sandisk's are called BiCS) fits more bits on a wafer, an average "54% BiCS Gen-to-Gen" gain per the Investor Day deck [10-K FY2026, Item 1; 2026 Investor Day slides, p.14]. The factories run at about 100% of capacity, and Sandisk must "pay for half of Flash Ventures' fixed costs regardless of the output we choose to purchase" [10-K FY2026, Note 10; Item 1]. So when price moves, cost barely moves, and almost the whole difference lands in gross margin.

Where the money goes. Cost of revenue is mostly wafers bought from Flash Ventures at "cost plus a small markup": net payments to the ventures were $3.6B in FY2026 against $5.8B of total cost of revenue [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 10; Item 8]. R&D and SG&A together were 10% of FY2026 revenue and 28% of FY2023's (computed), so operating margin swings harder still [10-K FY2026, Item 7; 10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Non-GAAP and GAAP gross margins differed by 0.1 point in FY2026, so this report uses GAAP except where guidance is non-GAAP [Q4 FY2026 release].

Capital intensity is the unusual part. Sandisk's own capex was $177M in FY2026, under 1% of revenue, because the wafer fabs sit inside Flash Ventures [10-K FY2026, Item 8]. Kioxia owns the fab buildings and runs them "at cost"; Flash Ventures owns or leases the tools inside them; each partner takes about half the wafers; and the tools are paid for by the ventures' own cash flow (tool depreciation, the yearly charge that spreads a tool's cost over its life, is inside the wafer price), by equipment leases from Japanese banks that Sandisk half-guarantees, and by loans and equity from the two partners "in excess of Flash Ventures' operating cash flow" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 10; Q4 FY2026 slides, p.18]. Management's "gross capex", which adds Sandisk's share of JV tool spending however funded, was $562M in Q4 FY2026, 6.3% of revenue, against $153M of cash capex [Q4 FY2026 slides, p.14]. Capacity grows "primarily through nodal transitions rather than wafer additions" [Q4 FY2026 call, CEO prepared remarks].

"Adjusted free cash flow" is the company's preferred measure: free cash flow plus or minus the net loans to and from Flash Ventures and, since Q4 FY2026, less NBM "prepayments" and "deposits received and returned", which the company says "are not indicative of the core underlying cash flows" [Q4 FY2026 release]. Three things sit inside the $11.7B of FY2026 operating cash flow. Customers paid in advance: $2.7B of their money sat on the balance sheet at year-end ($1,242M of contract liabilities plus $1,500M of deposits that "must be refunded at the end of the contract term") [10-K FY2026, Note 4; Item 8]. Income taxes were mostly unpaid: $146M of cash taxes against $1,584M of expense, leaving $1,286M payable [10-K FY2026, Note 14; Item 8]. Against that, receivables rose $3.6B as sales ran ahead of collections [10-K FY2026, Item 8]. Return on capital: FY2026 net income of $11.4B exceeded the $9.2B of shareholders' equity (the owners' book stake in the business) the year started with, while FY2025 lost 15% of opening equity (both computed); the business earns extraordinary returns at the top of the cycle and negative ones at the bottom [10-K FY2026, Item 8; 10-K FY2025, Item 8].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

The honest answer is that in normal times they leave whenever someone else is cheaper. Products are "largely interchangeable with competitors' products"; for many OEM customers "we do not generally require firm order commitments and instead receive a periodic forecast"; and no customer was over 10% of revenue in FY2024–FY2026 [10-K FY2026, Item 1A; Item 1]. What holds customers today has five sources.

**Supply in a shortage.** "Demand from our customers is growing faster than our supply, and we expect bits to remain on allocation beyond calendar 2027" (that is, rationed among customers) [Q4 FY2026 slides, p.12]. A customer that cannot get NAND elsewhere stays: the weakest moat, because it is the cycle itself.

**Qualification on enterprise SSDs.** "We must also qualify our products with customers through potentially lengthy testing processes with uncertain results" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Once a drive is qualified into a data center's servers, switching means another qualification cycle; that is real, and it grows with Datacenter's share.

**Brand and shelf space in Consumer.** The 10-K claims "industry leading consumer brand awareness and global retail distribution presence" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The multi-year contracts "are not related to the consumer business" [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, BNP Paribas].

**The NBM contracts.** NBM stands for "New Business Model": a multi-year agreement in which the customer commits to buy stated volumes by quarter and by year, Sandisk commits to supply them, price has "fixed and variable components" with the variable part "subject to floors and ceilings", and the customer posts cash deposits or bank guarantees [10-K FY2026, Item 7; Q4 FY2026 call, CFO prepared remarks]. Eight customers had signed by August 2026; contracted revenue "at floor pricing" totals $93.9B; remaining performance obligations (RPO) were $59.8B at July 3, 2026 and $91.1B counting two deals signed after quarter-end; and management expects them to cover over half of FY2027 bits and about two-thirds of FY2028 bits [10-K FY2026, Note 4; Note 17; Q4 FY2026 slides, p.11; Q4 FY2026 call, CFO prepared remarks]. Management counts "$16.5 billion" of guarantees; the 10-K quantifies $5.0B of bank-held collateral plus the $2.7B of customer cash on the balance sheet and never mentions the $16.5 billion (our observation; part of the gap is probably that the slide counts the two deals signed after year-end while the 10-K balances are at July 3, 2026) [Q4 FY2026 slides, p.11; 10-K FY2026, Note 4; Note 17]. This is a genuine switching cost: a signed customer cannot walk away without forfeiting collateral. But every contract was signed in FY2026, in a shortage, and none has been through a downturn; the 10-K says the guarantees "may not fully offset such lost revenue" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A].

**Technology shared with Kioxia.** The BiCS process is co-owned with Kioxia, the venture is "the Largest Producer of NAND Wafers in the World (33%)", Sandisk holds about 8,000 patents, and both partners are barred from making flash with third parties [10-K FY2026, Item 1; 2026 Investor Day slides, p.7].

Signs of weakening, in order: NBM signings stop, or an existing customer renegotiates floors; RPO falls faster than revenue is recognised; the price-protection percentage in the 10-K rises again; Datacenter's bit share stops rising.

## 6. What could break it

Ranked from most to least plausible.

**1. The cycle turns while the contracts are young.** What has to happen: supply catches up, AI data-center demand pauses, or high prices make device makers cut storage per unit [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. FY2023 is the worked example: a 39% price fall took gross margin to 7% and produced a $2.1B loss [Form 10, MD&A]. The NBMs are meant to soften this with floors, deposits and "attractive margins even at floor pricing", but the floors are untested, about half of FY2027 bits and a third of FY2028 bits are uncontracted and "will fluctuate with the market", and half of Flash Ventures' fixed costs is owed regardless [Q4 FY2026 call, CFO prepared remarks; 10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Exposed: most of the profit pool. Early warning: the release's volume-versus-pricing sentence turning negative on price; days in inventory rising past the 178 of July 2026; idle-capacity charges reappearing in the related-party note [10-K FY2026, Item 7; Note 10].

**2. New supply from China.** Yangtze Memory Technologies (YMTC) is named as a competitor alongside Kioxia, Micron, Samsung and SK hynix, and the 10-K warns of competitors' "aggressive expansion of their output capacities" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 1A]. China and Hong Kong were 48% of FY2026 revenue by ship-to location (computed) [10-K FY2026, Note 3]. Early warning: Chinese capacity named in the MD&A's price commentary; China ship-to revenue falling while total revenue rises.

**3. Kioxia or the joint venture changes.** Sandisk "cannot unilaterally direct most of Flash Ventures' activities", and "Kioxia's management changes, ownership and capital structure could lead to delays in decision-making, disputes or changes in strategic direction"; the JV terms also limit Sandisk's own ability to merge or be acquired [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: a dispute in the related-party note; lease guarantees or notes receivable jumping.

**4. A node miss.** Growth comes from BiCS transitions, and "advances in NAND technology, including higher-capacity and higher-layer architectures ... may increase the complexity" of keeping up [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. BiCS 8 became the majority of bits in FY2026; BiCS 10 was "Sampling in August 2026" [Q4 FY2026 slides, p.5; 2026 Investor Day slides, p.27]. Early warning: BiCS 10 ramp dates slipping; gross capex rising faster than bit growth.

**5. Japan.** Every wafer comes from eight fabs in Yokkaichi and Kitakami; the company "maintain[s] limited insurance coverage and, in some cases, no coverage at all, for natural disasters", and a 2022 contamination event cost $207M [10-K FY2026, Item 2; Item 1A; Form 10, Flash Ventures note]. There is no early warning for an earthquake.

**6. Customer concentration.** No customer exceeded 10% of revenue in FY2024–FY2026, but the top ten were 44%, three customers held 41% of receivables at July 3, 2026 (computed), and the 10-K notes that "Fewer companies now hold greater market share ... giving these companies increased leverage in negotiating prices" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 3; Item 1A]. Early warning: a 10% customer appearing in the revenue note; a dispute in the contingencies note.

**7. Tariffs, export controls and the DRAM squeeze.** US tariff investigations under Sections 232 and 301 of trade law "may impact tariff rates for Sandisk products", though "the majority of our products sold in the U.S. are currently exempt" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Enterprise SSDs need DRAM (the fast working-memory chips that sit beside the NAND in a drive), "a commodity component that has experienced supply constraints"; the $1.0B Nanya stake and supply agreement are the response [10-K FY2026, Item 1A; 8-K 2026-03-25]. Early warning: changed tariff wording in the 10-Q; Nanya commitments shrinking.

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

David Goeckeler, 63, has been CEO and Chair since the spin; he was WDC's CEO from March 2020 and ran Cisco's networking and security business before that [DEF 14A 2025, Executive Officers; Proposal 1]. CFO Luis Visoso, 56, joined WDC in July 2024 after CFO roles at Unity Software, Palo Alto Networks and Amazon Web Services [DEF 14A 2025, Executive Officers]. CTO Alper Ilkbahar, 58, came from Intel and was at "the prior SanDisk Corporation" from 2006 to 2016 [DEF 14A 2025, Executive Officers]. The board has eight members: the two WDC transition directors left at the November 2025 meeting, Alexander Bradley joined in December 2025, and the other independents include former executives of TSMC Arizona, GlobalFoundries and AMD [8-K 2025-11-20; 8-K 2026-01-02; DEF 14A 2025, Proposal 1].

Ownership: insiders held under 1% (341,824 shares at February 6, 2026); the largest holders were FMR (13.9%), Vanguard (11.2%) and BlackRock (6.0%) [S-3ASR 2026-02-17]. WDC kept 19.9% at the spin, exchanged 14.6% for its own debt in June 2025 and 5.8M more shares in February 2026, and "expects to monetize all remaining shares of Sandisk common stock held by it by the end of 2026" [10-K FY2026, Item 7; S-3ASR 2026-02-17].

Pay. The FY2026 cash bonus is 50% profit (non-GAAP operating income and margin), 25% adjusted free cash flow and 25% "Corporate Strategy: Equally weighted across Net Debt, Consumer Net Revenue, and Data Center Market Share"; three-year performance shares pay on revenue and earnings per share (EPS) [DEF 14A 2025, CD&A]. One-time "launch grants" pay on the stock price alone: from a $47.07 base, 100% at $70.61 and 300% at $105.91, measured to February 2028; the stock closed at $626.56 on February 13, 2026 [DEF 14A 2025, CD&A; S-3ASR 2026-02-17]. The CEO's FY2025 total was $22.9M, $18.8M of it stock [DEF 14A 2025, Summary Compensation Table]. The 10-K warns that these awards have "appreciated materially" and that leaders may leave when they vest in 2028 [10-K FY2026, Item 1A].

## Glossary

- **NAND** — flash memory that keeps data without power; the storage inside SSDs, phones, cards and USB drives, and Sandisk's only product technology.
- **Bit** — the smallest unit of data; the industry measures output and price per bit (or per gigabyte) because chips of different sizes are otherwise not comparable.
- **Wafer** — the thin silicon disc on which hundreds of chips are made at once before being cut apart; Sandisk buys wafers from Flash Ventures and turns them into products.
- **Fab** — a chip factory; Flash Ventures' eight fabs are in Yokkaichi and Kitakami, Japan.
- **Node / BiCS** — a generation of manufacturing technology; each new node fits more bits on a wafer and lowers cost per bit; BiCS is the name of Sandisk and Kioxia's generations (BiCS 8 is current, BiCS 10 next).
- **SSD / enterprise SSD** — solid-state drive, a storage device built from NAND chips plus a controller chip; enterprise SSDs are the high-capacity, high-reliability versions sold into data centers.
- **NBM** — "New Business Model", Sandisk's name for a multi-year contract in which a customer commits to volumes, price has floors and ceilings, and the customer posts deposits or bank guarantees.
- **RPO** — remaining performance obligations; the revenue still owed to Sandisk under signed contracts, counted at contracted volumes and floor prices.
- **Gross margin / operating margin** — gross margin is the share of each sales dollar left after the cost of making the product; operating margin is what is left after research, selling and administrative costs as well.
- **Capex** — capital expenditure; cash spent on plants and equipment rather than day-to-day costs; the cash-flow line is "purchases of property, plant and equipment (PP&E)".
- **GAAP / non-GAAP** — GAAP is the official accounting standard; Sandisk's non-GAAP figures remove stock-based pay, impairments, separation costs and the Nanya gain.
- **Goodwill impairment** — an accounting write-down of "goodwill", the premium a buyer once paid for a business above the value of its identifiable assets; it cuts reported profit but costs no cash.
- **Carve-out** — financial statements for a business that was part of a larger company, with the parent's shared costs allocated to it; a reasonable but not exact picture of a standalone company.
- **10-K / 10-Q** — the annual (10-K) and quarterly (10-Q) reports every US-listed company files with the SEC; Q4 has no 10-Q, so the 10-K is this report's as-of filing; an 8-K reports a single event between them.
