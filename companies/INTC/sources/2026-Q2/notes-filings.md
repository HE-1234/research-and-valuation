## 1. Business description (company's own words)

- "We are a global designer and manufacturer of semiconductor products. The CPUs and other semiconductor solutions that we design, manufacture, market, sell and service are incorporated in computing and related end products and services, and utilized globally by consumers, enterprises, governments and educational organizations. Our customers primarily include OEMs, ODMs, CSPs, and other manufacturers and service providers, such as industrial and communication equipment manufacturers. We market and sell our products through a combination of direct sales through our global sales organization and indirect channels, including distributors, resellers, retailers and OEM partners." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Overview"]
- "As a U.S.-based integrated design manufacturer, or IDM, in addition to designing CPUs and other semiconductor products, we develop leading-edge semiconductor manufacturing process technologies, or nodes, and advanced packaging technologies and predominantly manufacture our semiconductor products at our manufacturing and assembly and test facilities, many of which are in the U.S. We are the only company undertaking research, design and development of leading-edge and next generation semiconductor manufacturing technologies and high-volume manufacturing of logic semiconductors utilizing leading-edge nodes in the U.S., making us a strategically important company from both a national economic and national security perspective. In addition to manufacturing our own products, we offer third-party foundry services to external customers and aim to establish this business as a leading external foundry." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Overview"]
- Four stated strategic priorities: "Transforming our culture to become an engineering-focused, customer-centric organization"; "Revitalizing the x86 ecosystem"; "Growing our external foundry business where we aim to leverage our U.S.-based capabilities … to become a trusted foundry partner to third-party semiconductor customers and to financially support our continued investment in capital-intensive, U.S.-based, leading-edge R&D and manufacturing"; and "Expanding our market opportunities by leveraging our engineering and design expertise to develop purpose-built ASICs and GPUs for customers." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Our Strategy"]
- Structure: "Our products are designed and marketed by our Intel Products business, which includes our CCG and DCAI operating segments, and are manufactured by our Intel Foundry operating segment and third-party suppliers. Our Intel Foundry operating segment also offers semiconductor design and manufacturing services to external customers." "We report our business results in three reportable segments: CCG, DCAI and Intel Foundry. In addition to these reportable segments, we present an 'all other' category, which is not a reportable segment." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Our Business"]
- What Intel Foundry is: "Intel Foundry, comprised of technology development, manufacturing and foundry services, develops new leading-edge semiconductor process technologies and advanced packaging technologies and provides manufacturing, assembly and test and advanced packaging capacity and design enablement solutions across multiple nodes and platforms … At present, nearly all of our Intel Foundry business supports internal manufacturing for Intel Products; however, we are offering our Intel Foundry services to external customers and aim to develop a more significant external foundry business in the future." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Intel Foundry"]. The Q2 2026 10-Q changes one word: "At present, substantially all of our Intel Foundry business supports internal manufacturing for Intel Products." [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]
- Why a foundry: "In recent years, the cost of developing leading-edge semiconductor process technologies and of the manufacturing facilities utilizing such process technologies has increased significantly and outpaced the growth in our products business. As a result, to improve the financial viability of our continued development of leading-edge semiconductor process technologies and the utilization of our manufacturing assets, a few years ago we started transitioning our semiconductor manufacturing business from one that has historically been designed to serve our internal product groups into a customer-centric foundry business designed to also serve third-party external semiconductor customers … While we have few external customers to date, developing an external foundry business is a key long-term strategy." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Growing Our External Foundry Business"]
- The "IDM 2.0" label appears in the FY2023 10-K: "IDM 2.0, the next evolution and expansion of our IDM model, is a differentiated strategy that combines three capabilities: Internal factory network … Strategic use of foundry capacity … Open System Foundry." [10-K FY2023, Item 1 "Manufacturing at Scale"]. The FY2025 10-K no longer uses the phrase "IDM 2.0". [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Customers by segment: CCG "direct customers include distributors and OEMs that design and sell PCs and related devices." DCAI "direct customers include global CSP/hyperscalers and OEMs. Our OEM customers, in turn, sell to multinational corporations, small- and medium-sized enterprises, independent hardware and software vendors, systems integrators, communications service providers and government entities." Foundry potential customers: "traditional fabless semiconductor companies in the computing, smartphone, cloud services, automotive, communications, aerospace and defense markets, as well as hyperscalers and the U.S. government." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Sales terms: "Because our customers generally order from us on a purchase order basis, they can typically cancel, change or delay product purchase commitments with little or no notice to us and without penalty." Payment "usually 30 days after shipment or delivery." "Our worldwide reseller sales channel consists of thousands of indirect customers—systems builders that purchase Intel processors and other products from our distributors." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Customers", "Sales Arrangements"]
- Seasonality: "Historically, our net revenue has typically been higher in the second half of the year than in the first half of the year, accelerating in the third quarter and peaking in the fourth quarter. In 2025, this trend was disrupted by our Q3 2025 divestiture of Altera." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Seasonal Trends"]
- Key products, CCG: Intel Core (Intel 7 node; "represented almost half of our CCG product sales by revenue in 2025, with their percentage contribution expected to decrease significantly in 2026"); Intel Core Ultra Series 1 (Intel 4, first "AI PCs" 2023), Series 2 (2024; Intel 3 or external foundry "depending on the version"), Series 3 ("the first products to be manufactured using our new Intel 18A process technology, in late 2025"); vPro commercial; Intel Arc discrete GPUs (B-Series 2024); edge versions of client CPUs; Wi-Fi 7, Bluetooth 6, Thunderbolt 5. "our next generation client CPU code named Panther Lake" is named in the strategy section. [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Key Products", "Our Strategy"]
- Key products, DCAI: Xeon Scalable 3rd/4th/5th Gen on Intel 7 ("represented a majority of DCAI product sales by revenue in 2025 and are expected to be a somewhat smaller majority … in 2026"); Xeon 6 (introduced 2024; compute die on Intel 3, I/O die on Intel 7, joined with EMIB; P-core and E-core variants; series 6300/6500/6700/6900); GPUs: "Crescent Island, our first GPU to be built on the advanced Xe3P architecture … engineered specifically for data center environments … for AI inference workloads" and next-generation "Jaguar Shores"; Ethernet controllers (E800, E600 series), IPUs ("manufactured at an external foundry"), FlexRAN software. Gaudi AI accelerators appear in the filings only as the source of inventory charges. [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Key Products"; Item 7]
- Process nodes and status (as of the FY2025 10-K): Intel 7 (HVM since 2017; "utilized for the majority of our internal processor production and products by revenue in 2025 and is expected to continue to be utilized for almost half … in 2026"); Intel 4 (first EUV node, HVM 2023, "modest portion"); Intel 3 (HVM 2024, Xeon 6, "modest portion … expected to increase in 2026"); Intel 18A ("first went into high-volume manufacturing in late 2025 … RibbonFET … PowerVia … expected to represent an increasing portion of our processor production and products by revenue in 2026 and subsequent years. We expect Intel 18A and its derivative nodes, notably Intel 18A-P, to be the key manufacturing process technology for multiple generations of our future client and server CPU products. We are seeking to establish Intel 18A as our first significant foundry node for government and commercial customers."); Intel 14A ("being designed to potentially incorporate an industry-first use of high-NA EUV lithography … in active development … our first node designed from inception for offering to external customers"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Process Technology"]
- Intel 14A condition, verbatim: "in 2025 we announced that if we are unable to secure a significant external foundry customer for Intel 14A, we may pause or discontinue our pursuit of next generation leading-edge process technologies. We continue to evaluate Intel 14A for use in future Intel products and our plan includes an initial product designed to utilize Intel 14A, though we are maintaining the option to utilize an external foundry for future Intel products requiring nodes with performance beyond Intel 18A and Intel 18A-P." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. "If we were to discontinue development of Intel 14A and successor nodes, we expect that a majority of our products would continue to be manufactured in our own facilities utilizing our nodes up to Intel 18A-P through at least 2030." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Q2 2026 update on 14A: "During Q2 2026, we committed to completing development of Intel 14A, with a number of future Intel products designed to utilize the node and manufacturing expansion projects underway for production of products on the node. We also made continued progress towards meeting performance and design milestones for potential significant customers to evaluate Intel 14A for their future products. We intend to accelerate various of our manufacturing expansion projects, though the scale and pace of our manufacturing expansion projects will ultimately be dictated by the amount of committed demand for Intel 14A that we are able to obtain from our Intel products roadmap and design wins with potential significant external customers." Also: Intel 18A-P "entered into risk production of products on such node in June 2026." [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2 "Future Node Development"]
- Packaging: EMIB (HVM 2017), EMIB-T (introduced 2025, "adoption expected to scale beginning in 2026"), Foveros (2019), Foveros-B and Foveros-R ("targeted for high-volume manufacturing in 2027"), Foveros-S, Foveros Direct ("hybrid-bonding support planned on our Intel 18A-PT process in 2028"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Packaging Technology"]
- Manufacturing footprint: "In 2025, our key production fabs were in Oregon (ramping Intel 18A), Arizona (Intel 7 and ramping Intel 18A), Ireland (Intel 4 and Intel 3) and Israel (Intel 7) … In 2025, our key assembly and test facilities were in China, New Mexico, Vietnam and Malaysia, with New Mexico being our key advanced packaging facility and a new advanced packaging facility being built in Malaysia." Technology development is in Oregon. [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Manufacturing", "Process Technology"]. The FY2024 10-K listed: "wafer manufacturing facilities in the United States (Oregon and Arizona), Ireland, and Israel, assembly and testing facilities in Costa Rica, China, Malaysia, and Vietnam, and packaging facilities in the United States (New Mexico), Costa Rica, Vietnam, and Malaysia." [10-K FY2024, Item 1]
- Footprint decisions in 2025: "we initiated the consolidation of our Costa Rican assembly and test operations into larger existing sites in Vietnam and Malaysia, which we expect to be completed by the end of 2026, slowed the pace of construction for our new Ohio fab and discontinued planned expansions in Germany (fab) and Poland (assembly and test)." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Manufacturing"]. Risk factors add Malaysia and Israel to the delayed list: "we have delayed or cancelled manufacturing facility construction or expansion projects in Ohio, Germany, Poland, Malaysia and Israel." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Use of TSMC: "In recent years, however, we have also strategically utilized third-party foundry manufacturing capacity, including at one of our key foundry competitors, TSMC, for various tiles or an entire product where advantageous for cost, performance, schedule or other considerations … We expect to continue to use TSMC and other third-party foundry suppliers for various key tiles in a number of current and future products." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Disaggregated Architecture and Supply Chain"]
- Capital-intensity framing: "From 2021 through 2024, we made substantial investments across three categories: technology development, manufacturing facility shells and advanced production tools … as we aimed to catch up with our leading manufacturing competitor on process technology, establish 'shell ahead' capacity for anticipated future market demand and adopt EUV lithography … In 2025, we transitioned to a more disciplined capital deployment strategy." [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Manufacturing"]
- Sole-source supplier: "ASML Holding N.V. (ASML) is currently the sole supplier of EUV lithography tools that we are deploying in our Intel 4, Intel 3, Intel 18A and planned future leading-edge manufacturing process nodes." Rare earths: "China is the primary source of global supply." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A; Item 1 "Supply Chain"]
- Competition named: CCG — AMD, Apple (M series), Qualcomm (Snapdragon), MediaTek (Kompanio). DCAI — AMD, NVIDIA ("whose GPU systems have experienced the highest demand in the market"), hyperscalers' custom silicon (Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft), ARM- and RISC-V-based entrants, Broadcom in custom ASICs. Foundry — TSMC ("primary competitor in leading-edge"), Samsung, GlobalFoundries, UMC, SMIC. [10-K FY2025, Item 1 "Competition" subsections]

## 2. Segments and the Products-vs-Foundry split

### 2a. Definition changes, with dates

- **Q1 2023:** "We previously announced the organizational change to integrate AXG into CCG and DCAI … we modified our segment reporting in the first quarter of 2023." Segments in the FY2023 10-K: CCG, DCAI, NEX (reportable); Mobileye, IFS (disclosed, non-reportable); "all other" (which then also held corporate charges such as restructuring, SBC and acquisition amortization). [10-K FY2023, Note 3]
- **Q1 2024:** "our internal foundry operating model, which took effect in the first quarter of 2024, and creates a foundry relationship between our Intel Products business (collectively CCG, DCAI, and NEX) and our Intel Foundry business." Altera moved from DCAI to "all other." Reportable segments: CCG, DCAI, Intel Foundry; NEX, Altera and Mobileye disclosed but not reportable. "All prior period segment data has been retrospectively adjusted." The FY2024 10-K also shows segment gross margin. [10-K FY2024, Note 3]
- **Q1 2025:** "we made an organizational change to integrate our NEX business into CCG and DCAI and modified our segment reporting to align to this and certain other business reorganizations. All prior period segment data have been retrospectively adjusted." Goodwill: "of the total $2.8 billion of goodwill previously allocated to NEX, we reallocated $1.8 billion to CCG and $1.0 billion to DCAI." IMS, described in the FY2024 10-K as "a business within our Intel Foundry operating segment," is listed under "All Other" in the FY2025 10-K. Segment cost detail changed: "Prior to the second quarter of 2025, our CODM regularly reviewed cost of sales and operating expenses, on a discrete basis, attributable to each segment. We have recast prior period segment operating results to reflect the significant segment-level expenses as currently reviewed by our CODM" (so the FY2025 10-K shows one combined "cost of sales and operating expenses" line per segment, no segment gross margin). [10-K FY2025, Note 3; Note 11; 10-K FY2024, Note 4]
- **September 12, 2025:** Altera deconsolidated; "Altera's financial results were included within our 'all other' category for all periods presented through September 11, 2025." After that date Altera is an external foundry customer (see §2e). [10-K FY2025, Note 3]
- **Q2 2026:** CCG renamed: "Client Computing and Physical AI Group (CCPG), formerly the Client Computing Group (CCG)." DCAI product description now says "purpose-built silicon, or ASICs" instead of "custom ASICs." The Q1 2026 10-Q still used "CCG." No numbers were restated for the rename. [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2; Item 2; 10-Q Q1 2026, Note 2]
- How intersegment revenue works: "Intel Foundry … Segment revenue: consists substantially of intersegment product and services revenue for wafer fabrication, substrates and other related products and services sold to Intel Products and certain other Intel internal businesses. We recognize intersegment revenue based on the completion of performance obligations. Product revenue is recognized upon transfer of ownership, which is generally at the completion of wafer sorting. Backend service revenue is recognized upon the completion of assembly and test milestones … Intersegment sales are recorded at prices that are intended to approximate market pricing." "The Intel Products operating segments and Intel Foundry are meant to reflect separate fabless semiconductor and foundry companies, respectively." Intersegment eliminations "primarily relates to inventory reserves, which are determined and recorded based on our accounting policies for Intel as a whole but are only recorded by the Intel Products operating segments upon transfer of inventory from Intel Foundry." [10-K FY2025, Note 3]
- Depreciation: "We estimate that the substantial majority of our consolidated depreciation expense was incurred by Intel Foundry in 2025, 2024 and 2023 … it is impracticable to determine the total depreciation expense included as a component of each Intel Products operating segment's operating income (loss)." Not allocated to segments: restructuring and other charges, share-based compensation, certain acquisition-related costs; and non-operating items. "our CODM [does not] receive total asset information by segment." [10-K FY2025, Note 3]
- Foundry as an independent subsidiary: the FY2024 10-K said "We plan to create greater independence for our foundry operations by establishing Intel Foundry as an independent subsidiary." The FY2025 10-K does not repeat this statement. [10-K FY2024, Item 7; 10-K FY2025]

## 8. Intel-specific risk factors (verbatim key sentences)

- Market share and AI: "we have lost market share in recent years, including in both client and data center markets, in the market for x86-based semiconductor products, and more generally in the markets for semiconductor compute products … Our data center business has been further negatively impacted in the past few years by the significant shift of customer spend toward GPUs optimized for AI workloads, a rapidly developing and very significant compute market where we have been unsuccessful to date in becoming a meaningful participant." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Foundry customers: "We have limited experience in the highly competitive and capital-intensive third-party foundry business." "We have been unsuccessful to date in securing any significant external foundry customers for any of our nodes and our prospects for securing a significant external foundry customer for Intel 14A are uncertain." "many of the largest potential foundry customers are fabless semiconductor companies whose products compete with our own." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Intel 14A and its consequences (risk factor heading): "If we are unable to secure a significant external foundry customer for Intel 14A, our next generation semiconductor manufacturing process technology, we may pause or discontinue our pursuit of next generation leading-edge process technologies." Listed repercussions: "Dependence on Third-Party Foundries. Our products business would, over time, become dependent on third-party foundries, particularly TSMC … We have no long-term contract with TSMC"; "Losses with Respect to our Investments … We had over $100 billion of property, plant and equipment, net on our balance sheet as of December 27, 2025, the substantial majority of which we estimate relate to our foundry business … we would likely discontinue the new leading-edge fabrication facilities we are building in Ohio"; "Loss of Eligibility for Government Incentives … we may be required to repay amounts already received"; "Potential Penalty Payments Under our SCIP Agreements"; "Limited External Foundry Potential"; "any decision to pause or discontinue our pursuit of Intel 14A and successor leading-edge process technologies may be effectively irreversible." The Q2 2026 10-Q's forward-looking list rewords the item as "our pursuit of Intel 14A … our investments in manufacturing expansion projects … and our efforts to secure product design wins with and demand commitments from potential significant external customers." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A; 10-Q Q2 2026, forward-looking statements]
- Node economics: "A new process node typically has higher costs compared to a mature node due to factors that include higher depreciation costs and lower yields, and costs and yields at times do not improve at the same rate as on prior nodes." "These delays have allowed competitors using third-party foundries, such as TSMC, to benefit from advancements in manufacturing processes introduced ahead of us." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- TSMC tiles: "some of our newer and more high-end products and a number of our future products involve the use of tiles manufactured by TSMC, a key competitor on the foundry side of our business … To the extent there is any interruption in the supply of tiles manufactured by TSMC … we would be unable to complete assembly of complete products requiring such tiles." "Some of our most advanced current and future products are or will be either exclusively manufactured by TSMC or reliant upon critical components, including various compute die, manufactured by TSMC." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Fixed costs: "our gross margin and operating income can be disproportionately affected due to our high fixed cost structure, which is difficult to reduce quickly in response to lower demand." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Israel: "As a significant portion of our revenues are generated from products using our Intel 7 node that are manufactured at our fabrication facility in Israel and we are not insured for business interruptions resulting from war or political violence, a disruption of that facility could have a significant adverse impact on our business." Q2 2026 MD&A: "In late March 2026, Iran published a list of U.S. companies with operations in the Middle East whose facilities they indicated they would target in retaliation for continued strikes on Iran, with Intel being near the top of that list." Also: "Iranian strikes on two energy fields in Qatar that supply a meaningful percentage of the global supply of helium have resulted in a global shortage of this gas that is essential to the semiconductor manufacturing process." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2 "Risks from the Conflict with Iran"]
- China and export controls: "Geopolitical and trade tensions between the U.S. and China, one of our largest markets, have led to increased tariffs and trade restrictions, including tariffs applicable to some of our products, and have affected customer ordering patterns … In 2025, the new U.S. administration further tightened export controls and trade measures, expanding the scope of restrictions, which has led to additional sales reductions and a greater need for specific government authorizations." "we could face increased competition as a result of China's programs to promote a domestic semiconductor industry." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Taiwan: "Many of our products depend on suppliers in Taiwan for critical components, including various compute die, that cannot be easily or quickly replaced. Other of our products are made entirely in Taiwan." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Supply: "we are currently being impacted by industry supply constraints for memory chips, substrates and foundry capacity." "To obtain future supply of certain materials and components, particularly substrates, and third-party foundry manufacturing capacity, we have entered into arrangements with some of our suppliers that involve long-term purchase commitments and/or large prepayments … our prepayments may not be fully utilized, and in some cases may not be fully recoverable." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Customers competing with Intel: "We also compete with internally developed semiconductors from OEMs, cloud service providers, and others, some of whom are customers … in 2020, Apple introduced PC products utilizing its own internally developed ARM-based semiconductor designs in place of our client CPUs … a number of hyperscaler customers have increasingly developed their in-house semiconductor design capabilities." - Product quality precedent: "during 2024, some of our customers experienced instability issues when using Intel Core 13th and 14th Gen desktop processors, which required us to undertake an investigation and deploy corrective actions. This adversely impacted sales volume during 2024 and may result in higher warranty costs in the future." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]

[10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
