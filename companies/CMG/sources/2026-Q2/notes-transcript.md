### 1b. Menu pricing and the price-versus-inflation gap

- "On pricing, the impact in Q2 was around 1.6%, and we anticipate it will increase to the mid-2% range in Q3. For the full year, we expect to land near the high end of the 1% to 2% range we communicated earlier this year." — Adam Rymer, CFO [Q2 2026 call, l.51]
- Margin path beyond Q3, answered as price versus inflation (no margin number given): "So that gap between price and inflation, as you know, is at its widest point really in the first half of the year. That is definitely easing in the second half as we continue this measured approach to taking price increases with this rolling strategy that we have. And so in Q3, I would expect inflation to be closer to about 3%, while pricing, like we said in the prepared comments, will be closer to that mid-2% range. So that dislocation has narrowed meaningfully from the first half where pricing was more in that 1% range, while inflation was kind of in that low to mid-3%." — Adam Rymer, CFO [Q2 2026 call, l.143]
- "And so on a go-forward basis, we're pretty much there in Q3, but from Q4 forward, we expect those 2 to match and that -- therefore, that dislocation will not be there on a go-forward basis." — Adam Rymer, CFO [Q2 2026 call, l.144]
- Why pricing trends to the high end of the range: "So what we've learned is the process is working very nicely. I mean we've talked about this in the past. [Q2 2026 call, l.152]; "And so I think you'll see us continue to follow this strategy going forward, and you won't have that dislocation. That was really just a timing impact.
