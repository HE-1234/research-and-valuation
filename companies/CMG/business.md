# Chipotle Mexican Grill — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Chipotle runs 4,186 fast-casual (counter-service, made-to-order) restaurants, 4,074 in the United States and 112 in Canada, the United Kingdom, France and Germany, serving burritos, bowls, quesadillas, tacos and salads from a line of freshly prepared ingredients [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1, Note 1] [IR release 2026-07-13 (Mexico)]. The pitch is "real food with wholesome ingredients and without artificial colors, flavors or preservatives", branded Food with Integrity [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (General)]. The diner pays at the counter, in the app or through a delivery company, and that is the whole of the revenue: $11.9 billion in 2025, about $3.1 million per restaurant [10-K FY2025, Item 8 (Consolidated Statements of Income and Comprehensive Income)] [Q2 2026 release, Supplemental Financial and Other Data].

**What makes it unusual: it owns every restaurant.** Most large chains are franchisors: independent operators run the restaurants and pay for food, crew and usually the building, sending the brand owner a percentage of sales as royalties and often rent, so the franchisor's revenue is a thin slice of what diners spend and its costs are small. Chipotle does the opposite. Its 10-K (annual report) says "Our revenue is derived from sales by our restaurants", its income statement has only two revenue lines, "Food and beverage revenue" and "Delivery service revenue", with no royalty or rent line, and the proxy (its annual shareholder-meeting filing) states "we own all of our restaurants (except the franchised restaurants in the Middle East)" [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (General)] [10-K FY2025, Item 8 (Consolidated Statements of Income and Comprehensive Income)] [DEF 14A 2026, CEO Pay Ratio]. Four things follow: revenue is large for a chain of this size, because every dollar the diner pays is Chipotle's; margins are lower than a franchisor's and move with food and wages, because Chipotle pays for every avocado and crew hour through its four restaurant cost lines, "Food, beverage and packaging", "Labor", "Occupancy" and "Other operating costs" [10-K FY2025, Item 8 (Consolidated Statements of Income and Comprehensive Income)]; growth needs Chipotle's own capital, about $1.5 million per new restaurant [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Use of Cash)]; and it controls execution directly, since the 125,408 people in its U.S. restaurants are its employees [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (Human Capital)].

The exception is 15 "partner-operated" restaurants in the Middle East run by Alshaya Group, whose royalties sit inside "all other revenue" and are not disclosed separately; the first in Mexico, run by Alsea (named in the proxy's shareholder letter and the Mexico release, not in the 10-K or 10-Q), opened on July 16, 2026, after the quarter closed [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (If we partner with third parties or acquire new businesses)] [10-K FY2025, Item 8, Note 14 (Segment Reporting)] [DEF 14A 2026, Letter to Shareholders] [IR release 2026-07-13 (Mexico)] [Q2 2026 call, l.44]. The FY2025 10-K renamed these from "licensed" to "partner-operated" and widened the definition to include minority-owned joint ventures [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (General)] [10-K FY2024, Item 7 (Licensing)].

**How they got here.** The first restaurant opened in Denver in 1993 [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (General)]. The deck's timeline marks 1998 as the year outside investment accelerated openings and 2006 as the year it went public on the NYSE; no filing names the investor, but the 10-K's exhibit list carries a January 2006 registration-rights agreement (a contract letting a shareholder sell its stock in a public offering) "among Chipotle Mexican Grill, Inc., McDonald's Corporation and certain shareholders", so McDonald's was a shareholder at the 2006 IPO (initial public offering) (our inference: it was the 1998 investor and the IPO was the separation) [Q2 2026 slides, p.4] [10-K FY2025, Item 15 (Exhibits)] [DEF 14A 2026, Certain Relationships and Related Person Transactions]. Food-safety outbreaks in 2015–2018 "had a significant negative impact on our sales and profitability", and in April 2020 the company signed a Deferred Prosecution Agreement (prosecutors drop charges if the company complies for a set period) and paid a fine whose amount the filing does not state; only the FY2022 10-K still recounts this [10-K FY2022, Item 1A]. CEO Brian Niccol left on August 31, 2024 to run Starbucks; Scott Boatwright, the operations chief, became interim CEO on August 12, the day the departure was announced, and CEO in November 2024, and launched a "Recipe for Growth" strategy with the February 2026 results after a year of falling customer counts [8-K 2024-08-13, Item 5.02] [8-K 2024-11-12, Item 5.02] [Q4 2025 release, Headline].

## 3. The economics

Chipotle reports no gross margin. Its own profit measure is **restaurant-level operating margin**: revenue less the four restaurant cost lines, as a percentage of revenue; it leaves out general and administrative expense (G&A, the head office), depreciation, pre-opening costs and impairment (write-downs of restaurants worth less than their cost) [Q2 2026 release, Non-GAAP definitions]. Of every $100 a guest spent in 2025, $29.60 went to food and packaging, $25.10 to crews, $5.20 to rent and $14.70 to other restaurant costs such as marketing, delivery, card fees and utilities, leaving $25.40 at the restaurant; the head office took $5.50, depreciation $3.00, pre-opening $0.40 and impairment $0.20, leaving a 16.2% operating margin [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Food, Beverage and Packaging Costs; Labor Costs; Occupancy Costs; Other Operating Costs)] [DEF 14A 2026, Appendix A] [10-K FY2025, Item 8, Note 1 (Other Operating Costs)].

**Cost rises with every burrito, but not evenly.** Food is linear: each extra meal costs its ingredients, so this line has sat between 29.5% and 30.6% of revenue and moves with beef, avocado and dairy prices, tariffs and portion sizes [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Food, Beverage and Packaging Costs)] [10-K FY2024, Item 7 (Food, Beverage and Packaging Costs)]. Labor and rent are largely fixed per restaurant, so when sales per restaurant fall they take a bigger share, which the 10-K's management discussion (MD&A) calls the impact of "lower sales volumes" (the reverse is "sales leverage"): in 2025 labor rose 0.4 points, 0.7 of them from lower volumes [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Labor Costs)] [10-K FY2023, Item 7 (Restaurant Operating Costs)]. G&A flattens with scale, from 8.0% of revenue in 2021 to 5.5% in 2025 [10-K FY2022, Item 7 (General and Administrative Expenses)] [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (General and Administrative Expenses)]. Menu price is the offsetting lever: 12.0% covered 2022's inflation, but 2.1% in 2025 did not cover the volume loss, and in H1 2026 pricing "more in that 1% range" ran against inflation "in that low to mid-3%", so restaurant-level margin has fallen since its 2024 peak [10-K FY2023, Item 7 (Revenue table)] [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Revenue table)] [Q2 2026 call, l.143] [DEF 14A 2026, Appendix A].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

A diner has no contract with Chipotle; the moat is habit built on four things. First, food and brand: "Responsibly Raised" meats, produce grown to the company's standards and no artificial preservatives, backed by "a limited list of approved suppliers" and 26 regional distribution centers (the 2024 count) [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (Food with Integrity)] [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (Quality Assurance and Food Safety)] [10-K FY2024, Item 1 (Purchasing)]. Second, value in the CEO's sense, "generous portions, speed and an accessible price point" [Q2 2026 call, l.36]. Third, convenience: 38% of sales are digital, and 1,326 Chipotlanes, the drive-through lanes for collecting digital orders, were open at year-end 2025 (one of them partner-operated; the June 2026 total is not disclosed) [Q2 2026 release, Results] [Q4 2025 slides, p.14]. Fourth, the Rewards program, relaunched in April 2026, with $80.6 million of unredeemed points at June 30, 2026 and, per the CEO, "23 million active members" (spoken; not in a filing) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1, Note 3] [Q2 2026 call, l.35]. Our inference: scale purchasing holds the food cost of a fresh menu near 30% of revenue, which lets Chipotle serve portions that rivals of its quality cannot match at the price, and the digital layer makes the habit frictionless.

**Signs it is weakening.** The clearest is transactions falling while average check rises, meaning fewer people are paying more: 2025 was exactly that, transactions (2.9%) and check +1.2% [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Sales Trends)]. Two more: digital share falling (as it did from 45.0% in 2021 to 35.1% in 2024) and restaurant-level operating margin below the prior year (2025 and both 2026 quarters) [10-K FY2022, Item 7 (Sales Trends)] [10-K FY2024, Item 7 (Sales Trends)] [DEF 14A 2026, Appendix A] [Q2 2026 release, Headline].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. A food-safety event.** The company says it "may have higher risk for food safety incidents than some of our competitors" because it uses fresh produce, handles raw chicken and shuns preservatives and frozen ingredients [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (Food safety and food-borne illness concerns)]. Even an outbreak elsewhere hurts: in late July 2026 the CFO saw "a softening, call it about 200 basis points or so right around the issue that's affecting the industry around cyclospora" (cyclospora is a food-borne parasite; 200 basis points is two percentage points), while the CEO said "we are impacted from a sales perspective, but we're not involved" [Q2 2026 call, l.79] [Q2 2026 call, l.84]. Early warning: the 10-Q's Sales Trends paragraph naming "food safety concerns", as the Q2 2026 one did, and transactions turning negative [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2 (Sales Trends)]. Exposure: the whole profit pool, since every restaurant carries the same brand.

**2. Wage and ingredient inflation the company cannot price through.** Food and labor took 54.7% of revenue in 2025 (computed), so one point of unrecovered cost is about $119 million of profit (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Food, Beverage and Packaging Costs; Labor Costs)]. California's $20 minimum wage for chains from April 2024 raised wages there "nearly 20%", and 2025 tariffs on imported ingredients cost "about 15 basis points on an ongoing basis" [10-K FY2023, Item 7 (Labor Costs)] [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Food, Beverage and Packaging Costs)]. The lever is a "rolling" menu-price strategy that the CFO says is held to "the 1% to 2% range" a year (spoken), so a cost shock lands first in margin [Q2 2026 call, l.51] [Q2 2026 call, l.143]. Early warning: restaurant-level operating margin below the prior year (as in every quarter since Q4 2025) while average check rises.

**3. Consumers trade down or stop believing in the value.** The 10-K lists "guest perceptions regarding smaller entrée portion sizes" among things that erode the brand, and a pending shareholder class action (Stradford, a lawsuit brought on behalf of all shareholders) alleges the company's portion-size statements were misleading [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (Failure to maintain the value and reputation of the Chipotle brand)] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1, Note 11 (Shareholder Actions)]. In 2025 transactions fell 2.9%, with younger and lower-income guests "under the most pressure", per the CFO [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Sales Trends)] [Q2 2026 call, l.136]. Early warning: transactions negative for two consecutive quarters while menu price runs above 2%. Exposure: each point of transactions is about $119 million of revenue (computed).

**4. New restaurants earn less than the old ones.** Average sales per restaurant fell from $3.213 million at end-2024 to $3.102 million at June 2026 while the count rose 12% (computed), and the CFO concedes new openings take "approximately 100 basis points" off comps (spoken) [Q2 2026 release, Supplemental Financial and Other Data] [Q2 2026 call, l.52]. Build costs rose from about $1.2 million in 2022 to $1.5 million in 2025 [10-K FY2022, Item 7 (Liquidity and Capital Resources)] [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Use of Cash)]. Early warning: trailing average restaurant sales still falling while openings continue; impairment charges rising ($13.8 million in Q2 2026 against $5.5 million a year earlier) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1]. Exposure: about $530 million a year of growth capex and the 7,000-restaurant goal [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Use of Cash)] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (Our failure to effectively manage and support our growth)].

**5. Competition and copycats.** The 10-K names as main competitors "restaurant formats that claim to serve higher quality ingredients without artificial flavors, colors and preservatives", and the 10-Qs add "competitor discounting" to the risk list [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (Competition)] [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1A]. Early warning: marketing rising as a share of revenue (it added 0.5 points in 2025) while transactions stay flat [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Other Operating Costs)]. Exposure: the whole comp base.

**6. Dependence on delivery partners and technology.** Over 16% of food and beverage revenue arrives through couriers Chipotle does not control, whose fees "may be less than the actual delivery cost", and "Many of these critical systems are provided and managed by third parties" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (Our use of third-party delivery services may not be profitable)] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (Risks Related to Cybersecurity, Data Privacy and IT Systems)]. Early warning: "delivery expense" named as a cost driver, as in Q1 2026, or digital share falling [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2]. Exposure: the delivery slice, roughly $1.9 billion of 2025 food and beverage revenue (computed).

**7. Execution risk from leadership turnover.** Since August 2024 the CEO, CFO, COO, chief brand officer, chief legal officer and chief accounting officer have all changed (§7), and the 10-K itself warns that "Changes in senior management could result in significant changes in strategic direction and initiatives" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (A failure to recruit, develop and retain effective leaders)]. Early warning: further officer-change 8-Ks, or a second year of missed comp guidance [10-K FY2024, Item 7 (Sales Trends)] [10-K FY2025, Item 7 (Sales Trends)]. Exposure: everything, since in a fully owned chain head office sets execution directly.

**Concentration.** No customer is 10% of revenue, so customer concentration does not apply [10-K FY2025, Item 8, Note 14 (Segment Reporting)]. Supply concentration does: "Certain key ingredients are purchased from a small number of suppliers", with "a limited number of suppliers for some of our ingredients, including lemon and lime juice, tomatoes and adobo", and distribution runs through 26 independently owned regional centers (2024 count) [10-K FY2025, Item 1 (Purchasing)] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A (Shortages or interruptions in the supply of ingredients)] [10-K FY2024, Item 1 (Purchasing)].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**The people.** Scott Boatwright, 53, joined in 2017 after 18 years at Arby's, was Chief Operating Officer when Niccol left, became interim CEO on August 12, 2024 when Brian Niccol left for Starbucks, and CEO and director on November 11, 2024 after an external search [DEF 14A 2026, Information Regarding the Director Nominees] [8-K 2024-08-13, Item 5.02] [8-K 2024-11-12, Item 5.02]. Jack Hartung, CFO since 2002, announced his retirement in July 2024, stayed through the transition and retired in March 2026; Adam Rymer, 44, with Chipotle since 2009, became CFO on October 1, 2024 [8-K 2024-07-09, Item 5.02] [8-K 2024-08-28, Item 5.02] [8-K 2025-05-07, Item 5.02] [DEF 14A 2026, Compensation Discussion and Analysis (Termination and Transition Agreements)]. **Pay and the 2025 revolt.** Right after Niccol's departure was announced, the board granted retention stock awards of $8 million each to Boatwright and Hartung, $7 million each to Curt Garner and Brandt, $3 million to Rymer and $1.5 million to the chief accounting officer [8-K 2024-08-28, Item 5.02]. ## Glossary

- **Comparable restaurant sales (comps):** the year-over-year change in revenue at company-owned restaurants open at least 13 full calendar months; the company splits it into transactions and average check.
- **Restaurant-level operating margin:** revenue less food, beverage and packaging, labor, occupancy and other operating costs, as a percentage of revenue; it excludes head-office costs, depreciation, pre-opening costs and impairment.
- **Chipotlane:** a drive-through lane for picking up orders placed in advance on the app or website; no ordering happens at the window.
- **Average restaurant sales (AUV):** the trailing twelve-month food and beverage revenue of the average company-owned restaurant open at least 12 full months; the company also calls it average unit volume.
- **Partner-operated restaurant:** a Chipotle run by a third party under a license, franchise or joint-venture agreement, not counted in the company-owned total; Chipotle earns a royalty.
