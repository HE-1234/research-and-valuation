# Marvell Technology, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended early May 2026). Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Marvell designs the chips that carry data around inside a data center and between data centers; it sells nothing a consumer would recognize. It is "fabless": it does the design work and pays outside factories, called foundries, to make the chips; most of those factories are in Taiwan, and packaging and testing are outsourced too, mainly in Asia [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 1A]. Its customers are mostly a handful of enormous cloud companies (the industry calls them hyperscalers) plus makers of networking and storage equipment [10-K FY2026, Item 1; DEF 14A 2026, letter to stockholders]. It reports two end markets: "data center", 76% of revenue in Q1 FY2027 (quarter ended early May 2026) and 74% in FY2026 (fiscal year ended January 2026), and "communications and other", which bundles telecom equipment, office networking gear, consumer devices and industrial products [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 3; 10-K FY2026, Note 3].

Within data center, management talks about three product groups [Q1 FY2027 call]. Interconnect, also called electro-optics, is "the largest portion of our data center business": chips at each end of a fiber-optic cable that turn electrical signals into light and back so data can travel between computers over fiber, plus DSPs that clean up and re-time the signal at ever higher speeds [Q1 FY2027 call; 10-K FY2026, Item 1]. A switch is the traffic director that decides which server each packet of data goes to; Marvell sells Ethernet switch chips for cloud and AI networks [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Custom means a chip designed for one customer: a giant cloud company wants an AI processor (an XPU) or a companion chip built to its own specification, Marvell does the engineering, and the customer buys the result, sometimes exclusively for a negotiated period [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 1A].

The founding date is not in the sources; our inference from its oldest stock plan, adopted in April 1995, is that the company dates from the mid-1990s [10-K FY2026, Note 11]. The modern company dates from 2016, when Matt Murphy became CEO of a business where data center was "only about 9% of our revenue base" [DEF 14A 2026, letter to stockholders]. Management then bet the company on data infrastructure through a decade of deals: "Cavium in compute, Avera in custom ASICs, Inphi in high-speed electro-optics, Innovium in cloud switching, and more recently Celestial AI and XConn in next-generation AI connectivity" [DEF 14A 2026, letter to stockholders]. Businesses that no longer fit were sold, most recently automotive Ethernet to Infineon in August 2025 for $2.5 billion in cash; the revenue that business contributed is not disclosed [10-K FY2026, Note 1; Item 7]. In February 2026 it closed Celestial AI (about $3.5 billion, mostly paid in stock), whose technology links AI processors with light, and XConn ($469 million), a switch-chip maker [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 4].

## 2. How the money comes in

Marvell sells chips and nothing else of any size [10-K FY2026, Note 14]. Customers pay per chip, mostly on purchase orders they can change "with relatively short notice", so revenue tracks how many systems they build each quarter [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Half now flows through distributors, who hold stock and resell it; the largest distributor's growth came from "four module makers" that were "supporting data center sales to hyperscale customers" [10-K FY2024, Item 1]. Distributors were 51% of revenue in Q1 FY2027, up from 26% in FY2022 [10-K FY2024, Note 3; 10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 3].

It sat near 40% of revenue for three years, then jumped to 72% in FY2025 and 74% in FY2026 as AI spending took off, "driven by AI-related demand for our custom products and electro-optics portfolio" [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. It halved in FY2025 as customers worked down excess inventory, has "largely recovered", and is expected to grow about 10% in FY2027 and low single digits in FY2028 [10-K FY2026, Note 3; Q1 FY2027 call].

## 3. The economics

Does cost rise with usage? Only partly. Designing chips means enormous fixed cost and modest cost per unit. Marvell spent $2.1 billion on research and development in FY2026, 25% of revenue, and that bill does not rise when a customer orders more chips [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. The cost that does rise is the factory invoice: product costs were $3.3 billion of the $4.0 billion cost of goods sold [10-K FY2026, Note 14]. So gross margin (revenue minus the cost of making the chips) is roughly stable per chip, while operating profit (what is left after R&D and overhead are also paid) swings hard with volume because the fixed R&D bill is spread over more or fewer chips. That is why operating margin swung from a 12.5% loss in FY2025 to a 16.1% profit in FY2026 on 42% more revenue (computed) [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**Two sets of numbers.** The table below shows two versions of each margin. GAAP is the official accounting standard, and Marvell's GAAP results carry two large charges: amortization, which spreads the price paid for technology and customer relationships in past acquisitions over several years as a yearly expense, and the cost of stock paid to employees; the company's preferred "non-GAAP" numbers remove both, plus acquisition costs and one-off items [Q1 FY2027 release]. In Q1 FY2027 those two items were almost the whole gap between 52.1% GAAP and 58.9% non-GAAP gross margin (amortization 6.3 points, stock pay 0.6), and with acquisition costs most of the gap between 14.0% and 35.0% operating margin [Q1 FY2027 release]. This report uses non-GAAP margins to see the pricing and cost structure, because the amortization is money already spent that falls sharply after FY2027 as older acquired assets finish amortizing [10-K FY2026, Note 5]. It uses GAAP and cash flow to judge what owners keep, because stock pay is real: $591 million in FY2026, and the shares it creates dilute every owner (each existing share owns a smaller slice) [10-K FY2026, Item 8].

Why is the adjusted gross margin about 59% rather than higher? Product mix (which products sell). Our inference is that custom chips for one customer carry lower gross margins than standard interconnect parts; no filing says so directly, but the board lowered the FY2026 bonus target for gross margin, explaining that it "reflected the expected near-term product mix shift" and that a lower target "was expected to increase overall financial performance by supporting competitive pricing, volume commitments, and start-up investments to capture share in the data center AI market" [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A].

Capital intensity is low in the usual sense because the factories belong to someone else: capital spending (capex: money spent on equipment and buildings) has run between 3.5% and 6.1% of revenue, and the real "capital" is R&D, counted as a cost in the year it is spent [10-K FY2024, Item 8; 10-K FY2026, Item 8]. There is a newer cash demand: to guarantee factory capacity Marvell committed $870 million of deposits payable quarterly from Q2 FY2027 through Q2 FY2028 and expects "approximately $1 billion in prepayments during this fiscal year" to be "applied against future material purchases" [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 9; Q1 FY2027 call]. These are chips paid for early, not factories bought.

Return on capital is hard to state neatly because so much of the capital is the price of past acquisitions: of $26.9 billion of total assets at May 2, 2026, goodwill (the accounting label for the part of an acquisition price above the value of the assets actually bought) was $13.9 billion and acquired intangibles (the assigned value of bought technology and customer relationships) $2.6 billion [Q1 FY2027 release]. Our view: free cash flow of $1.4 billion on that base is a modest return on everything ever paid in, and the return that matters now is on each new R&D dollar.

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Custom programs lock in for a chip generation.** New custom chips start contributing revenue only after "their typical development cycle of approximately 2 years", and once a cloud company builds systems around one, that chip ships for the whole product generation [Q1 FY2027 call; 10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. The flip side: "our failure to win a competitive bid can result in our foregoing revenues from a given customer's product line for the life of that product" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Sign of weakening: a program not renewed for the next generation, a customer announcing an in-house chip or moving to Broadcom; in the numbers, custom growth short of targets, or the largest direct customer's share of revenue falling (our inference: the filings never link Customer A to a product line, but a direct customer at 16% of revenue is most plausibly the flagship custom buyer, so its share is a proxy).

**Interconnect wins by being first to each speed generation.** Links move through speed generations (today 800G and 1.6T), and each transition is a fresh competition [Q1 FY2027 call]. Management claims a "first to market cadence" across successive generations of its optical DSPs and ships modules linking data centers "to all 5 major US hyperscalers" [Q1 FY2027 call]. Sign of weakening: a competitor first at the next speed step, 1.6T growth stalling, or interconnect growth below the stated target.

**Being a supplier that can deliver.** The chief operating officer says "Everything that touches AI has been constrained basically since the beginning of this", and Marvell has answered with 4-to-10-year capacity reservations and cash prepayments to foundries [Q1 FY2027 call; 10-K FY2026, Note 8]. Sign of weakening: commitments flattening while the market grows, or the shortage easing.

**NVIDIA is a signal, not a moat.** In Q1 FY2027 NVIDIA bought $2.0 billion of Marvell convertible preferred stock (a special class of shares that can later be swapped for common shares) alongside a partnership to make Marvell's custom XPUs and networking chips work with NVIDIA's systems [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Item 2; Note 10]. Sign of weakening: the partnership going quiet with no products announced.

Caveats: most sales are on cancellable purchase orders, and "average unit selling prices of our products will continue to be subject to significant pricing pressures" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A; Item 1].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked from most to least plausible.

**1. A few customers are almost the whole business.** One distributor was 45% of Q1 FY2027 revenue (36% a year earlier), the largest direct customer 16%, and the ten largest customers "represented 82% of our total net revenue for fiscal 2026" [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Item 2; 10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Three customers accounted for 75% of the money customers owed Marvell (gross receivables), and none is ever named [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Item 2; 10-K FY2026, Item 1]. If any changes course, most of the profit pool moves with it. Early warning: these percentages in every 10-Q.

**2. A custom program is lost or slips a generation.** Custom products are sometimes exclusive "for a negotiated period of time", and if the customer walks "there may be no other customers for these products" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Custom revenue is expected to grow more than 20% in FY2027 and "more than double" in FY2028, about a third of that from a new XPU program for a top-tier ("tier 1") customer with "firm requirements in place for all of next fiscal year" [Q1 FY2027 call]. Early warning: that language softening, the FY2028 custom target cut, or Customer A's share falling (our inference that Customer A is the custom buyer; see §5).

**3. AI data center spending slows.** Data center is 76% of revenue, and sales there "have fluctuated significantly from period to period" [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 3; 10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Management already plans "for the rate of cloud CapEx growth to moderate into the 30%-plus range" in FY2028 [Q1 FY2027 call]. Because R&D is fixed, a revenue drop cuts operating margin faster than revenue. Early warning: cloud companies' spending plans, and Marvell's days of inventory, meaning how many days of sales the chips on hand would cover (96, 91, 117 and 110 over the last four quarters, so no clear trend yet) [Q1 FY2027 supplemental, p.4].

**4. Competition from Broadcom and from customers' own designs.** Broadcom is on a long list of named competitors, and "some of our customers have chosen to develop certain semiconductor products internally and this trend may continue to proliferate" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. "Some large customers may begin developing and making their own semiconductor solutions" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: a customer announcing an in-house networking chip, or share loss at a speed transition.

**5. Taiwan and single-sourcing.** "Most of our products are manufactured by third-party foundries located in Taiwan", and "most of our products are not manufactured at more than one foundry at any given time, and our products typically are designed to be manufactured in a specific process at only one of these foundries" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. This exposes essentially all revenue, and the filings offer no early warning.

**6. The Celestial AI earn-out and integration.** Marvell owes Celestial's former owners more if the acquired business hits revenue milestones through FY2029, and the accounting value of that promise doubled in one quarter [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 6].

About 44% of Q1 FY2027 revenue shipped to China, mostly to factories serving non-China customers [10-Q Q1 FY2027, Note 3; 10-K FY2026, Note 3]. Early warning: new US Commerce Department rules and changes in the 10-Q risk-factor wording.

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

Matt Murphy, 53, has been CEO since joining in July 2016 and Chairman since June 2023 [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. A 2026 stockholder proposal asks that "The Chairman of the Board shall be an Independent Director"; the board opposes it, citing its lead independent director (a board member with no management role who leads the other independent directors) [DEF 14A 2026, Proposal 4]. Willem Meintjes has been CFO since January 2023 [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. Insiders own under 1%; the largest holders are FMR (14.95%), Vanguard (9.40%) and BlackRock (7.14%) [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership].

Pay is tied to the numbers this report tracks. The FY2026 cash bonus was weighted 50% on revenue, 15% on non-GAAP gross margin and 35% on non-GAAP operating margin, and paid at 144.84% of target [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Long-term equity is 70% performance-based for the CEO, measured on three-year total shareholder return against the S&P 500 (an index of 500 large US companies) with a multiplier of up to 150% for non-GAAP earnings-per-share growth versus peers [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A].

## Glossary

- **XPU** — industry shorthand for a custom AI processor built for one cloud customer; it does the heavy math of training and running AI models.
- **XPU-attach** — the companion chips that sit beside a custom AI processor, such as network cards and memory-expansion controllers, and are sold to the same customer.
- **ASIC** — application-specific integrated circuit; a chip designed for one job or one customer rather than sold off the shelf, the category all custom XPUs fall into.
- **DSP** — digital signal processor; here, the chip inside an optical module that cleans up, corrects and re-times a data signal so it can travel down fiber at very high speed.
- **TIA** — trans-impedance amplifier; the small analog chip that boosts the faint electrical signal produced when light hits the receiver at the end of a fiber.
- **Scale-out / scale-up** — scale-out is the network that links servers and racks across a data center; scale-up is the tighter, faster network that links AI processors inside one rack or system.
- **Foundry** — a factory that manufactures chips designed by other companies; Marvell owns none and relies mainly on foundries in Taiwan.
- **GAAP / non-GAAP** — GAAP is the official accounting standard; "non-GAAP" is the company's adjusted version that removes stock-based pay, acquisition amortization and one-off charges.
- **Amortization** — spreading the price paid for an acquired asset (here, bought technology and customer relationships) over several years as a yearly expense, with no cash leaving in those years.
- **Goodwill** — the part of an acquisition price above the value of the assets actually bought; it sits on the balance sheet until judged impaired (worth less than the amount booked).
- **Capex** — capital expenditure; cash spent on equipment, buildings and other long-lived assets, as opposed to day-to-day costs.
