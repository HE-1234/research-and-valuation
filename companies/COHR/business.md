# Coherent Corp. — The Business
_As of Q4 FY2026 (quarter and fiscal year ended June 30, 2026). Written 2026-09-09._

## 1. What they do

Coherent makes the parts that let computers talk to each other with light. Its biggest product is the optical transceiver: a small plug-in device at each end of a glass fiber that turns a chip's electrical signals into pulses of laser light, and back again at the other end [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. AI data centers link thousands of processors with these fibers; management's one-line version is "AI runs on compute, but it scales on optical connectivity" [Q4 FY2026 call]. Coherent also makes many of the pieces inside the transceiver, above all the laser chip itself, which is grown on wafers (thin discs) of indium phosphide, a semiconductor that emits light at the wavelengths fiber carries best, something ordinary silicon cannot do; the 10-K calls it "a foundational technology for next-generation AI optical interconnects" [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

Who pays: the 10-K lists "optical component and module manufacturers, networking equipment manufacturers, datacenter operators, enterprises, and telecom service providers" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In plain terms: the giant cloud companies building AI data centers, the equipment makers that supply them, and telecom carriers. Two customers each bought more than 10% of FY2026 revenue [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The one named relationship is NVIDIA, which in March 2026 signed "a multi-year strategic agreement" with "a multi-billion-dollar purchase commitment" and bought $2 billion of Coherent shares [8-K 2026-03-02, Ex.99.1; 8-K 2026-03-02]. A second, shrinking business, Industrial, sells lasers and engineered materials to factories: excimer and solid-state lasers for chip and display equipment, high-power lasers for cutting and welding, and specialty crystals and ceramics [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

History. The company was founded in 1971 as II-VI Incorporated, a Pennsylvania maker of optical materials [8-K 2026-03-02, Ex.99.1; DEF 14A 2025, CD&A; 10-K FY2023, Item 1]. It bought Finisar, a transceiver maker, in 2019 [10-K FY2023, Item 1]. On July 1, 2022 it paid $7.1 billion in cash and shares for Coherent, Inc., a laser company, financed with $4.0 billion of bank loans and $1.4 billion of preferred stock (shares that earn a fixed dividend ahead of common shareholders) sold to Bain Capital, and took the Coherent name [10-K FY2024, Note 3; 10-K FY2026, Note 14]. FY2023 and FY2024 went to digesting that deal while telecom customers worked down inventory; then AI demand arrived [8-K 2025-12-16, Ex.99.1, Item 7]. A new CEO came in June 2024, two new segments in July 2025, the aerospace and Munich tools businesses were sold, and FY2026 revenue reached $7.1 billion, up 22.5% [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 7; Q4 FY2026 release, Table 1].

## 2. How the money comes in

Coherent sells hardware, mostly recognized when it ships, and reports two segments since July 2025 [10-K FY2026, Note 1; Note 20]. **Datacenter & Communications** (74% of FY2026 revenue, computed) sells transceivers for 800G and 1.6T links, co-packaged optics, optical circuit switches, and the lasers, detectors and chips inside them, plus telecom transport gear and the transceivers that connect data centers to each other [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 20]. **Industrial** sells the lasers, laser systems and materials described above [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Revenue by customer headquarters was 65% North America and 11% China in FY2026 (computed); the filings give no ship-to split [10-K FY2026, Note 20].

## 3. The economics

This is a factory business. Cost of goods sold was 63% of revenue in FY2026 [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Materials rise with every unit; the plants do not, so fuller factories lift gross margin (revenue less the cost of making the product). FY2026 gross margin rose 233 basis points (2.33 percentage points), "primarily driven by cost reductions in product input costs, efficiency gains from improved cycle times in the manufacturing process as well as yield improvements in the Datacenter & Communications segment", plus "pricing optimization" [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. The lever cuts both ways: in FY2024 revenue fell 9% (computed) and gross margin sat at 30.9% [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Owning the scarce part.** The 10-K is candid that the transceiver itself is a standard product: "Aside from datacenter transceivers, there are very few industry-standard products" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The hold on customers comes from the laser inside it during a shortage. Coherent is "expanding our indium phosphide capacity in Sherman, Texas, to address our increased customer demand and industry-wide shortage", the CEO says "Indium Phosphide capacity continues to be our primary constraint", and no laser is sold outside: "Our data center transceiver demand is absorbing every bit of capacity" [10-K FY2026, Item 7; Q4 FY2026 call]. A customer cannot leave a supplier it cannot replace, and the 6-inch cost structure lets Coherent keep a margin if prices fall (our inference) [Q4 FY2026 call]. Sign of weakening: gross margin flat or down while revenue grows, the "pricing optimization" language disappearing, or management offering lasers to outside buyers [Q4 FY2026 call].

**Contracts and qualification.** The stated model is "strategic, long-term, sales agreements with market leaders, which enables our forward planning and production efficiencies" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. On the call these agreements have "increasing supply each year", "pricing related commitments" and "minimum demand guarantees from our customers or sometimes you refer to those as take or pay agreements"; "Some of those are 3 years. Many of those go out through the rest of the decade" [Q4 FY2026 call]. Also, "for some products a particular internal or external manufacturing site may be the sole qualified source" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Sign of weakening: the largest customer's share falling in the 10-K note, purchase commitments shrinking, or the "take or pay" description not repeated.

**Breadth and a US footprint.** Coherent sells components, subsystems and whole systems from about 60 sites in 14 countries, half in the United States, and claims to be "the largest US supplier of transceivers" [Investor Day 2025 deck, p.82; Q4 FY2026 call]. Sign of weakening: a large customer sourcing a new product generation elsewhere. Caveat: "Our large customers have in the past sought price concessions from us, and we expect that they will continue to do so", and the NVIDIA agreement is "non-exclusive" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A; 8-K 2026-03-02, Ex.99.1].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked from most to least plausible.

**1. Two customers are 32% of revenue.** One customer was 20% of FY2026 revenue, up from 10% in the two prior years, and a second was 12% [10-K FY2026, Note 20]. Management expects it to persist: "we expect that significant customer concentration will continue for the foreseeable future", and the NVIDIA capacity agreement "could affect revenue concentration, gross margin, and capital expenditures as volumes ramp" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A; Item 7]. Early warning: the annual "Major Customers" note (the 10-Q carries none), and a quarter-on-quarter drop in Datacenter & Communications revenue [10-K FY2026, Note 20; 10-Q Q3 FY2026, Note 18].

The FY2025 12% customer is "A third major customer", so not the FY2026 12% customer; the FY2022 figure is in an older 10-K not among the sources. No customer is named.

**2. AI spending pauses while Coherent is stocked for growth.** Inventory is $2.6 billion, purchase commitments $11.8 billion, and capex ran at 27% of quarterly revenue (computed) [10-K FY2026, Note 4; Note 13; Item 8]. The 10-K's words: "If forecasted orders do not materialize, we may incur excess or obsolete inventory, underutilized manufacturing capacity, liabilities under supplier arrangements, reimbursement obligations for supplier capital expenditures, noncancellable purchase commitments, or reduced margins" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. The FY2024 precedent, when telecom customers worked down inventory, cut revenue 9% and left a 2% operating margin (computed) [8-K 2025-12-16, Ex.99.1, Item 7]. Early warning: inventory days rising further, the purchase-commitment total falling, "record bookings" and "take or pay" vanishing from calls.

**3. Customers or rivals make the laser, or the shortage ends.** The risk factor: "we may fail to accurately estimate our competitors' or our customers' willingness and capability to backward integrate into our competencies and thereby displace us" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. An analyst asked about Chinese laser competition and the answer addressed contract pricing only [Q4 FY2026 call]. Early warning: a large customer naming a second laser source; gross margin falling while volume grows.

**4. Co-packaged optics shrinks the transceiver.** CPO puts the optics beside the switch chip instead of in a pluggable module; management's answer is that Coherent supplies the lasers, laser modules, fibers and connectors for it and sees "comparable levels of content for both CPO and NPO", with revenue beginning in the December 2026 quarter [Q4 FY2026 call]. The exposed pool is transceiver assembly, most of the segment (our inference; the mix is not disclosed). Early warning: CPO revenue not appearing when promised; content claims narrowing.

**5. The capacity ramp stumbles.** Output depends on "regulatory approvals, environmental and operational permits, clean-room and tool availability, hiring and training of qualified personnel ... and the pace of bringing equipment and processes online with the capability to manufacture high-quality products at acceptable yields" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: the indium phosphide doubling target slipping; gross margin dipping on ramp costs.

**6. China and trade.** China-headquartered customers were 11% of revenue; 28% of long-lived assets and 5.9 million square feet of plant sit in China (all computed) [10-K FY2026, Note 20; Item 2]. A Bureau of Industry and Security inquiry into past Huawei sales is open with no estimable loss, China restricts exports of rare earths the plants use, and the CEO called reports of US import restrictions on transceivers "speculative" [10-K FY2026, Note 13; Item 1A; Q4 FY2026 call]. Early warning: the BIS outcome; tariff and export-control wording in the 10-Q.

**7. A goodwill write-down on the old laser business.** The Lasers reporting unit carries about $3.1 billion of goodwill and its fair value exceeded book value by only "approximately 8%" at the April 2026 test, the auditor's critical audit matter (the judgement it singled out as hardest to audit) [10-K FY2026, Note 1; Item 8]. A write-down costs no cash but would confirm the $7.1 billion acquisition is not earning its keep (our inference). Early warning: Industrial segment profit falling; the headroom figure in the next 10-K.

## Glossary

- **Transceiver** — the plug-in device at each end of a fiber that converts electrical signals into laser light and back; Coherent's largest product.
- **Indium phosphide (InP)** — the semiconductor material on which Coherent grows its laser chips; the move from 3-inch to 6-inch wafers is its main cost lever.
- **800G / 1.6T** — transceiver speeds in bits per second (800 gigabits, 1.6 terabits); each generation carries more data per fiber.
- **CPO / NPO** — co-packaged and near-packaged optics: putting the optical parts on or beside the switch chip instead of in a pluggable transceiver, fed by external lasers.
- **OCS** — optical circuit switch; a box that redirects light between fibers without converting it to electricity; Coherent's uses liquid crystal rather than mirrors.
- **Scale-out / scale-up** — networks linking racks and buildings versus networks linking the chips inside one rack; management uses both to size the market.
- **LTA** — long-term agreement; a multi-year customer contract with volume and pricing commitments.
- **GAAP / non-GAAP** — GAAP is the official accounting standard; non-GAAP is the company's adjusted version that removes stock pay, acquisition amortization, restructuring and one-off items.
- **Capex** — capital expenditure; cash spent on factories and equipment rather than day-to-day costs.
