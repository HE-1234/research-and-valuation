## How the exhibits were converted (previous run's scripts, kept in /tmp/cohr-orch/ir/; tool versions confirmed on this host today)

- Fetching: `fetch.py` (urllib, `Accept-Encoding: identity`), UA `company-research-skill owner@example.com` for every sec.gov request, a 2.2 s minimum gap between requests enforced through a timestamp file, backoff 10/30/120 s on 403/429. All 40 SEC requests returned 200 on the first attempt (log below).
- Releases: `rel2txt.py` — Python 3 with BeautifulSoup 4.13.5 (`html.parser`); scripts, styles and head dropped; block elements walked in document order; each `<table>` emitted as `[TABLE]` … `[/TABLE]` with one `<tr>` per line and cells joined by " | "; stray "$", "(", ")", "%" and "bps" cells merged onto their neighbours; non-breaking and zero-width spaces normalised. Page-number paragraphs ("1" … "15") kept.
- Decks: `slides2txt.py` — the EX-99.2 HTML body split on the exhibit's own `page-break-before:always` + `<HR>` markers into 16 chunks (one per slide); each chunk's text (the hidden 1-pt text layer under the JPEG) extracted with BeautifulSoup as the [TEXT LAYER] block; the 16 slide JPEGs downloaded from the same accession folder and OCR'd with `tesseract <jpg> stdout` (tesseract 5.3.4, leptonica 1.82.0; OCR text files in `ocr-q4/` and `ocr-q3/`); `assemble_slides.py` merged text layer and OCR per slide with the "=== Slide N (p.N) ===" markers (each preceded by a form-feed character) and wrote the header notes. No pdftoppm step was involved because the exhibits are JPEGs.
- Only extracted text was cached in the repo; the HTML exhibits, JPEGs, the Q3 PDF and the IR HTML pages remain in `/tmp/cohr-orch/ir/` (6.6 MB, 94 files) and were re-inspected today without any new download.
