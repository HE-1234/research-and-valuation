## 1. Business description (company's own words)

**What the product is**
- "Bloom Energy is a global leader in onsite power generation, delivering a foundational platform purpose-built for the digital era and the global energy transition. We manufacture a versatile fuel cell energy platform, supporting the commercial availability of two main products: the Bloom Energy Server® fuel cell system for generating electricity and the Bloom Electrolyzer™ for producing hydrogen. Our primary product, the Bloom Energy Server is a proprietary high-temperature solid-oxide fuel cell technology that converts fuels—including natural gas, biogas, and hydrogen—into electricity at high-density without combustion or moving parts, achieving lower emissions and higher efficiency than legacy systems." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 159]
- "Our systems use high-temperature solid oxide fuel cells—a proprietary solid-state technology developed by Bloom—which leverage an electrochemical, non-combustion process and form the foundation of our platform, differentiation and competitive advantages. Our revenue is derived primarily from product sales of our Energy Server systems, with additional recurring revenue from long-term operations and maintenance agreements that support availability and performance over the contract life." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 167]
- Note 1 wording: "Our Energy Server systems convert natural gas, biogas, hydrogen, or blends of these fuels into electricity through an electrochemical process without combustion, significantly reducing greenhouse gas emissions and criteria pollutants compared to conventional fossil fuel generation. By generating power where it is consumed, our systems deliver 24x7 reliability, improved energy security, and resilience against grid disruptions—critical for sectors such as data centers, healthcare, manufacturing, and critical infrastructure." [10-K FY2025, Note 1, line 3027]
- "To date, nearly all of our product revenue has been attributable to sales of our power generating Energy Server system." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 208]
- Platform architecture: "composed of repeatable fuel cell building blocks—cell printing, stack assemblies, and column configurations"; "deployments ranging from hundreds of kilowatts to hundreds of megawatts"; "While most Energy Server deployments use natural gas as fuel, there is no combustion"; "the systems can also operate on biogas, hydrogen, or blends of these fuels, allowing customers to transition toward lower-carbon energy pathways without replacing installed assets." Cell printing is done in Fremont, California and assembly in Delaware. [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 260-268]
- Stated attributes: native DC output "compatible with emerging 800 VDC data center standards"; "near-zero smog forming criteria pollutant emissions such as oxides of nitrogen (NOx), oxides of sulfur (SOx), and particulate matter"; "consume no water during steady state operation"; "Looking at all systems installed after 2020, Bloom's fuel cells fleet availability has been approximately 99.9% for all non-redundant installations. When redundancy is added, the availability can be improved to achieve close to 99.999%." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 274-292]
- Time to power: "The Energy Server can be deployed on skid-mounted, modular units that support installation timelines measured in weeks and months rather than years, subject to permitting and site conditions… In certain project scopes, onsite generation can enable power availability within approximately 90 days." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 294]
- AI load following: "In AI load conditions the Energy Server can respond at least twice as fast as turbines and engines, and because it is used in combination with supercapacitors, its response time to a step load is instantaneous." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 296]
- "Islanded" definition (MD&A): "Bloom Energy Server systems can be configured as on-site fully-islanded, microgrid solutions that are not interconnected to the grid, which often can provide a customer power in months instead of years." "Our islanded microgrid solutions allow data center and other customers the ability to skip the interconnection queue and start construction." If the customer wants a "grid parallel" solution, "required interconnection studies and lengthy interconnection queues may remain, eroding the time to power value proposition." [10-K FY2025, Item 7, lines 1788-1796]
- Applications listed: primary power generation, microgrids, combined heat and power (CHP), carbon capture (CCUS: "the Energy Server emits a relatively pure stream of carbon dioxide, which can then be captured"), waste-to-energy (biogas). [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 304-318]
- Bloom Electrolyzer: "designed to produce scalable and cost-effective hydrogen using the same solid oxide platform"; operates at "700-900 degrees Celsius"; "consume up to 20–25% less electricity than low-temperature alternatives"; demonstrated "the world's largest solid oxide electrolyzer at NASA's Ames Research Center, producing over 2.4 metric tonnes of hydrogen per day" in 2023. "The market for the Bloom Electrolyzer is also expected to be largely driven by international demand." In FY2025 the company "ceased our efforts to market and sell the first-generation of the product" and reserved $19.7M of electrolyzer inventory plus $2.1M of asset impairment. [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 237 and 384-396; Note 1, line 3029; Note 6, lines 3664 and 3732]

**Markets and customers (company's framing)**
- Three power-generation market categories: Data Centers ("hyperscalers, colocation operators, neocloud providers, developers, and infrastructure investors—each anchored by lighthouse customers"), Commercial & Industrial, and Utilities. [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 210-233]
- "The U.S. is currently our largest market by revenue and installed base of our Energy Server systems. South Korea is our second-largest market, where we began commercial operations in 2018 and have grown our footprint to nearly 682 MW of deployed systems, supported by distribution partnerships with SK ecoplant Co., Ltd. and SK eternix Co., Ltd." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 235] Prior years: "more than 492 megawatts" in Korea at the FY2023 10-K; "nearly 600 megawatts" at the FY2024 10-K. [10-K FY2023, Item 1, line 180; 10-K FY2024, Item 1, line 211]
- Sales model: "We sell to financiers, strategic partners, distributors and directly to end user customers, all of whom we consider to be a customer… To date, the majority of our end user customers prefer to pay for the power they will consume from our Energy Server product, which means the sale of the Energy Server from Bloom is made to a financier or other strategic partner that owns the Energy Server product which is used to produce power for the end customer." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 423]
- Named partners in Item 1: SK ecoplant (Korea distributor and former shareholder, §8); American Electric Power (AEP), "landmark supply agreement announced in November 2024, under which AEP is expected to procure up to 1 GW of Bloom's solid oxide fuel cells … with an initial order of 100 MW"; Brookfield, "a prospective financing framework of up to $5.0 billion over five years for future Bloom Energy fuel cell projects" housed in an "AI Infrastructure Fund" (August 2025). [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 417-421] Oracle is named in Note 1 ("collaborations with Oracle and other hyperscale operators") and in the warrant disclosures (§7, §11). [10-K FY2025, Note 1, line 3031]
- Competition (company's list): gas reciprocating engines, small gas turbines, combined-cycle plants, solar-plus-storage ("a photovoltaic solar installation typically requires 125 times more space"), wind-plus-storage, small modular nuclear reactors ("some designs expected to be deployable before 2030 and others to follow later in the 2030s"), traditional co-generation, diesel backup, and other fuel cells (PEM, molten carbonate, phosphoric acid). "Additionally, major turbine OEMs are currently supply constrained." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 332-382]
- Sales cycle: "typically 8 to 12 months but can vary considerably"; "Generally, it takes between six to twelve months or more from the entry into a sales contract until the installation of our products." "Historically, the majority of our bookings have occurred in the second half of the year, with a significant portion in the fourth quarter; however, this historical dynamic could be changing due to the time-to-power needs of our customers." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A, line 815; Item 7, line 1858]

**Manufacturing sites and capacity**
- Principal properties: San Jose HQ 183,000 sq ft (leased to 2031); Fremont, CA manufacturing/R&D 326,000 sq ft (leased; terms to Dec 2027, Feb 2036, Nov 2037); Newark, DE manufacturing and warehousing 454,000 sq ft leased (terms Dec 2026 to July 2030) plus a 178,000 sq ft owned plant, "our first purpose-built Bloom Energy manufacturing center … designed specifically for copy-exact duplication," with "an additional 25 acres available for factory expansion." [10-K FY2025, Item 2, lines 1677-1685; Item 1, line 452]
- Fremont: "In July 2022, we announced the grand opening of our multi-gigawatt manufacturing facility in Fremont, California, representing a $200 million investment… In 2025, we announced plans to double the Fremont facility's annual production capacity from approximately 1 GW to 2 GW by the end of 2026." [10-K FY2025, Item 2, line 1691]
- "We are in the process of expanding our annual production capacity run rate at our Fremont facility from 1 gigawatt to 2 gigawatts and expect to complete the expansion by the end of 2026… the Fremont facility can accommodate additional capacity expansion of up to approximately 5 gigawatts of annual production capacity run rate. We expect each additional incremental 1 gigawatt increase in our capacity up to 5 gigawatts (if necessary) to require approximately six to nine months to install and capital expenditure of approximately $100 million to $150 million." [10-K FY2025, Item 7, line 1870]
- Q2 2026 10-Q adds Delaware: "We expect to continue to make capital investments to expand production capacity at our manufacturing facilities in Fremont, California and Delmarva, Delaware." [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2, line 1759]
- Korea: "We maintain a full-assembly facility in the Republic of Korea, in connection with our efforts to develop a local supplier ecosystem through a joint venture with SK ecoplant." Repair & Overhaul (R&O) facility in Delaware; two Remote Monitoring and Control Centers in the U.S. and India. Sunnyvale and Mountain View, CA facilities vacated in 2025. [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 456-458 and 483-487]
- Supply chain: "The production of fuel cells requires rare earth elements, specialty alloys and industrial commodities." "While our supply chain does not have significant exposure to China, significant tariffs on imports from other countries where we do source materials could materially impact our costs." "Our supply chain is not dependent on China. However, China as a country supplies multiple components including rare earth metals and compounds used in electronic and electromechanical components that are part of our tier 2 and tier 3 sub-assembly suppliers." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, lines 464-470; Item 7, lines 1862-1866] The 8-K of 2026-07-09 says the company has "clear visibility into our supply chain to support production of 25GW of fuel cells per year" (scandium oxide response; §13).
- Fleet availability and installed base: "approximately 1,100 sites in 9 countries" [10-K FY2025, Item 1, line 163]; proxy: "as of December 31, 2025, the Company has deployed more than 1.8GW" and "1.8 GW of low-carbon power deployed across 1,100 sites in 9 countries" [DEF 14A 2026, lines 159 and 482]; August 2025 press release (8-K exhibit): "deployed 1.5 GW of low-carbon power across more than 1,200 installations globally" [8-K 2025-08-06, Exhibit 99.1].

## 6. Backlog, remaining performance obligations, acceptances

- **Definition (10-K).** "We view product backlog as the revenue attributable to existing contractual commitments for the purchase or use of Energy Servers by a financier or an end customer in the future. ## 9. Company-specific risk factors (FY2025 10-K Item 1A unless noted; headings quoted, substance paraphrased)

- **Customer/financing concentration and project finance.** "Our products have significant upfront costs, and, for some customers, we need to attract financiers to help them finance purchases." "Additionally, our ability to deploy our backlog is directly tied to our ability to secure project financing, which is often an unpredictable process." Financing "depends in large part on the ability of financing parties to optimize the tax benefits." [lines 819-834]
- **AI/data-center concentration (new in FY2025).** "Our AI customer mix is increasing due to the continued adoption of AI tools… Slower expansion of AI data centers due to actual or perceived deceleration in AI adoption or other factors could have an adverse impact." Risks named: "financing constraints (including reduced availability of project finance or tax equity), longer permitting or construction lead times, local moratoria, protests or siting restrictions on data centers or distributed generation, or improved grid interconnection timelines." The Q2 2026 10-Q adds "Increasing Opposition to Data Center Development": "in July 2026, the governor of New York state signed an executive order to create a moratorium on new hyperscale data centers that included temporarily pausing State environmental permits for up to one year." [lines 836-840; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2, line 1665]
- **Natural-gas price and supply.** "The economic benefits of our Energy Server systems to our customers depend on both the price of gas available from the local gas utilities and the cost of electricity available from alternative sources… actual or perceived potential increases in the price of natural gas or other fuels or curtailment of availability … could make our Energy Server systems less economically attractive." "Deployment of our Energy Server systems relies on fuel supply and fuel specification requirements." Some municipalities (California, Northeast) limit new gas hookups, "although others are allowing new gas interconnections for non-combustion resources like our Energy Server systems." ## Quality caveats

- **Conversion.** HTML converted with BeautifulSoup/lxml; table rows are one per line with " | " separators; lone "$", "(", ")" and "%" cells were merged or dropped, so percentages print as "44%" and negatives as "( 88,434 )" with inner spaces in the financial statements (the MD&A tables print "(88,434)"). Inline-XBRL documents emit an XMLParsedAsHTMLWarning (harmless). Page numbers and "Table of Contents / Index to Financial Statements" running heads remain in the text between sections.
- **Curly apostrophes.** All Bloom filings use "’" (U+2019) in words like "Company’s" and "Management’s Discussion"; ASCII-apostrophe greps miss them. The Item headings use an em dash ("ITEM 7—MANAGEMENT" in the FY2025 10-K and 10-Qs; "ITEM 7 — MANAGEMENT" with spaces in the FY2023 and FY2024 10-Ks).
- **10-Q/A.** The amendment repeats the full Item 1 statements; both versions are cached. Use `10-QA-2026-Q2.txt` for the Customer Risk paragraph and `10-Q-2026-Q2.txt` for MD&A (which the amendment does not contain). Line numbers in the two files differ by about six lines.
- **Line references.** Given for the key tables and quotes; section-level references elsewhere. Line numbers refer to the re-converted files written 2026-09-09 (an earlier conversion with a spacing defect was discarded).
- **Image-only content.** None affecting the numbers; the 10-K stock-performance graph and the proxy's compensation-mix pie charts are images, but their underlying tables are in text (the proxy pie labels "ACI | Base Salary | RSUs | PSUs" appear without percentages).
- **Not extracted.** A few FY2021-FY2022 lines from the FY2023 10-K (other income, extinguishment loss, some balance-sheet items) were not pulled; marked "n/e" or "n/d" above. The FY2022 and FY2021 10-Ks were not fetched.
- **Computed items.** Gross margins by line, FY2021 gross profit by line, free cash flow, quarter-alone Q2 cash flows, share-of-revenue percentages, say-on-pay percentages and the customer-concentration dollar checks are arithmetic on disclosed figures and are labelled as such.
- **Oracle warrant fair value** appears as ~$261.3M (Q1 10-Q subsequent-events note) and $251.6M (Q2 10-Q); the Q2 figure is the one used in the accounting.
