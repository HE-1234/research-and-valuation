## 2. What management says about the core business

The Q2 release carries the only current-quarter words. CEO: "The demand for Bloom Energy's solutions keeps accelerating every quarter as customers who traditionally defaulted to combustion technologies are now proactively choosing Bloom as a superior power solution. Today, all the major US hyperscalers and over a dozen US neoclouds, AI labs, and colocation data center operators have validated and approved our power solutions for their AI factories. Bloom is now a standard for AI onsite power." CFO: "This quarter was the strongest in Bloom's history, with profitable growth and positive operating cash flow" [Q2 2026 release].

Supplements. The CEO in April: "Time to power has gone from a procurement consideration to an existential necessity"; "today, we are not order constrained and not capacity constrained"; "The pace of our revenue growth is decided by how fast our customers can build their greenfield sites, not how fast we can power them" (greenfield: new-build) [Q1 2026 call]. In February: "We are not an industrial era energy company" [Q4 2025 call].

## 3. Growth engines

**Data-center and AI customers.** No revenue by end market is disclosed; the 10-Q credits Q2's product growth to "a significant deployment for a large AI infrastructure customer", and one unnamed customer was 73% of the quarter [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2; 10-Q/A Q2 2026, Note 1]. Claims (supplement): Oracle's Project Jupiter, an "up to 2.45 gigawatt power block" that "will be 100% bloom", and "well more than half of our current data center backlog comes from other hyperscalers, neo clouds and colocation providers" [Q1 2026 call]. Tell: the largest-customer share, and the Oracle customer-consideration asset amortizing ($17.9 million so far), which happens only as Oracle systems are delivered [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3].

**Brookfield-financed projects.** The Fund JVs bought $862.1 million of product and installation in FY2025 (computed) and $373.3 million in Q1 2026, then $2.8 million in Q2 [10-K FY2025, Note 12; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1]. Claim: "up to $5.0 billion over five years" [10-K FY2025, Note 7]. Tell: related-party revenue and the "Equity in earnings (loss) of unconsolidated affiliates" line.

**Utilities (AEP).** AEP "is expected to procure up to 1 GW ... with an initial order of 100 MW" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The CEO: "our sale of that product is unconditional" [Q4 2025 call]. No delivery progress is disclosed. Tell: any megawatt or dollar update.

**Korea and the SK group.** "nearly 682 MW" installed; SK ecoplant's take-or-pay commitment is 500 MW for 2024–2027 [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Note 17]. No 2026 release, deck or call gives a Korea business update; the releases mention Korea only in footnotes on the Korean joint venture [Q2 2026 release]. Tell: the U.S. share of revenue (90% in Q2) falling below 80%.

**Hydrogen and electrolyzers.** First-generation sales ceased in 2025 with a $19.7 million inventory reserve [10-K FY2025, Note 6]. No 2026 release, deck or call mentions it; the Q2 10-Q names the Bloom Electrolyzer only in its overview as one of "two primary products" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Tell: any electrolyzer order.

**Service.** $69.0 million at an 18.7% gross margin, with about $14.0 billion of backlog in February 2026 [Q2 2026 release; Q4 2025 slides, p.6]. Claim: "durable growth in service revenue and profits in the years ahead" [Q4 2025 call]. Tell: double-digit gross margin with guarantee payments ($5.4 million in Q2) staying small [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

## 4. Guidance

**Full year 2026 (raised).** "Bloom Energy is increasing its financial guidance for full-year 2026 as follows: Revenue: $3.9B - $4.2B. Non-GAAP Gross Margin: ~34%. Non-GAAP Operating Income: $800M - $900M. Non-GAAP EPS: $2.55 - $2.85." (EPS: earnings per share) [Q2 2026 release]. The deck calls the revenue midpoint "~100%" growth and notes "EPS is fully dilutive" (counting all potential new shares); it charts non-GAAP operating expenses at 13% of revenue for 2026 [Q2 2026 slides, p.5; p.7]. The revenue range began at $3.1–3.3 billion in February [Q4 2025 release].

**Not guided.** No Q3 2026 guidance; no capex, cash-flow, capacity, tax-rate or share-count figure. The Q2 deck dropped the "Driving Profitability and Generating Cash" page that in April showed 2026 adjusted EBITDA of "$650 - $800" million and operating cash flow of "> $200" million [Q1 2026 slides, p.8; Q2 2026 slides, p.8]. The last capex statement is the February call's "We expect capital spending to be $150 million to $200 million" [Q4 2025 call]. The 10-K's Fremont target, "from 1 gigawatt to 2 gigawatts ... by the end of 2026", does not appear in the Q2 10-Q, which names only "Fremont, California and Delmarva, Delaware" as expansion sites [10-K FY2025, Item 7; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].
