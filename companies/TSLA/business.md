# Tesla, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-09._

## 1. What they do

Tesla builds electric cars and sells them straight to drivers through its website and its own stores, with no dealers [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It makes the Model 3, Model Y, Model S, Model X and Cybertruck in four car factories (Fremont and Austin in the United States, Shanghai, and Berlin), with the Semi to come from Nevada, and delivered about 1.64 million vehicles in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 2] [Q2 2026 Update, p.6] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Its second business is large batteries, Powerwall for homes and Megapack for utilities and companies, which store electricity and release it when the grid needs it; 46.7 GWh were deployed in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

Drivers pay for the car and, increasingly, for a subscription to the driver-assistance software FSD (Supervised), with 1.48 million active subscriptions at the end of Q2 2026; utilities and companies pay for Megapacks; other carmakers pay for regulatory credits (§2) [Q2 2026 Update, p.5] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Revenue in 2025 was $94.8 billion, about three quarters of it from vehicles and credits (§2) [10-K FY2025, Item 8].

The company now puts AI first: "We are focused on bringing artificial intelligence (“AI”) into the real world", and its mission changed in the same report from "to accelerate the world’s transition to sustainable energy" to "building a world of amazing abundance" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2024, Item 7]. Robotaxi (a driverless ride service, launched in Austin in June 2025), Cybercab (a car built only for it), Optimus (a humanoid robot) and in-house AI chips are what the company says it is building; none has disclosed revenue [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [Q2 2026 call, Elluswamy remarks].

History: incorporated in Delaware in 2003, listed in 2010; Elon Musk has been CEO since October 2008; SolarCity was bought in 2016; headquartered in Austin, Texas, with 134,785 employees at the end of 2025 [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 5] [10-K/A FY2025, Item 10] [10-K FY2025, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**Vehicles.** Revenue is cars delivered times average price, and both fell in 2024 and 2025: deliveries down 1% then 9%, prices cut through "overall price reductions" and then "higher customer incentives" [Q4 2025 Update, p.7] [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. FSD software sits inside this line, moving to "subscription-only"; its revenue is not disclosed, but "over 55% of new deliveries" in North America took a subscription in Q2 2026, and deferred revenue (cash taken for software and services not yet delivered) was $4.05 billion [Q1 2026 Update, p.9] [Q2 2026 Update, p.9] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1].

The line is vanishing: the July 2025 One Big Beautiful Bill Act eliminates "certain penalties for violations of certain regulatory credit programs", and Tesla expects "loss of certain regulatory credit sales tied to our products" [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. **Services and other** is the fleet's aftermarket (used cars, repairs, paid Supercharging, insurance, parts), up 19% in 2025 and 50% year on year in Q2 2026, with a record 14% gross margin that quarter [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q2 2026 Update, p.4] [Q2 2026 Update, p.9]. **Energy** is covered in §3. Tesla reports two segments, automotive (including services and other) and energy generation and storage [10-K FY2025, Note 16]. By geography, 2025 revenue was $47.6 billion in the United States, $21.0 billion in China and $26.2 billion elsewhere [10-K FY2025, Note 16].

## 3. The economics

**How a carmaker's margin works.** Most of a factory's cost (the building, the machines, the line workers, "depreciation costs of tooling and machinery") does not fall when fewer cars are made [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. So profit per car depends on how many cars share the fixed cost, and on the price. Tesla's own words: "better fixed cost absorption" when volume rose in 2023, "lower fixed cost absorption" when deliveries fell in 2025 [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Price cuts go straight to profit: in 2023 automotive gross margin fell from 28.5% to 19.4% "primarily due to a lower average selling price on our vehicles", a price the same report ties to "overall price reductions year over year" [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. New models lose money while they ramp: the 2024 margin fall cites the "Cybertruck ramp", and Q2 2026 carried "preproduction ramp costs" for the Semi, Optimus and Cybercab [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [Q2 2026 call, Taneja prepared remarks]. Mix (cheaper models earn less), tariffs and US manufacturing tax credits also move the number [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Note 2].

For four years the business paid for its own factories and still added cash (table above) [Q4 2025 Update, p.5]. Q2 2026 broke the pattern: capex more than doubled from Q1 and free cash flow was negative $1.1 billion [Q2 2026 Update, p.25]. 2026 capex is to be "in excess of $25 billion", "driven by our AI initiatives, including investments in compute infrastructure and data centers", and "will grow for the next two to three years" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 call, Taneja prepared remarks]. Debt of $9.1 billion is almost all "non-recourse", meaning lenders can claim only the borrowing subsidiary's assets, not Tesla's; the largest piece is a $5.9 billion unsecured Chinese working-capital facility (a revolving loan for day-to-day needs), and most of the rest is notes backed by leased or financed cars [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 8] [10-K FY2025, Note 9]. The CFO is "securing certain debt facilities that will give us the capacity to borrow up to $30 billion" (a spoken figure; the 10-Q gives no amount) [Q2 2026 call, Taneja prepared remarks] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Return on capital in plain words: each dollar of factories and equipment earned 58 cents of operating profit in 2022, 30 in 2023, 20 in 2024 and 11 in 2025 (computed: income from operations over year-end net property, plant and equipment), and the base keeps growing, to $47.3 billion at June 30, 2026, of which $10.8 billion (gross) is "AI infrastructure" [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 5].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Charging.** 8,704 Supercharger stations and 82,357 connectors at June 30, 2026, from 3,476 and 31,498 at the end of 2021 [Q2 2026 Update, p.5] [Q4 2025 Update, p.7]. Source: scale, though no longer exclusive, since "all major automakers" have adopted Tesla's plug standard [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Weakening sign: network growth (17% in Q2 2026) falling behind fleet growth [Q2 2026 Update, p.7].

**Software.** Cars update over the air and features are sold from the app; FSD subscriptions are up 56% in a year (§1) [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [Q2 2026 Update, p.5]. Source: switching cost, since the software does not move to another brand. Weakening sign: subscriptions flat for two quarters, or the North American attach rate (the share of new deliveries sold with an FSD subscription) below 55%.

**Cost and scale.** Installed capacity is over 2.3 million cars a year (computed from the deck), Model Y was "the best-selling vehicle, of any kind, globally" in 2025, and 2024 saw the "lowest average cost of goods per vehicle ever at less than $35,000" [Q2 2026 Update, p.6] [10-K/A FY2025, Item 11] [DEF 14A 2025, Executive Compensation for Fiscal Year 2024]. Weakening sign: automotive margin without credits below the 15.4% floor of 2024–2025 while deliveries grow.

**Brand and direct sales.** No dealers, "relatively low marketing costs", one price online [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The 10-K records the fragility: commentary on "Tesla and its management" has "incited protests, some escalating to violence" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Weakening sign: days of vehicle inventory (unsold cars measured in days of sales) above Q1 2026's 27 alongside price cuts [Q2 2026 Update, p.5].

**Vertical integration.** Own battery cells (a 4680 battery-cell line rated above 40 GWh), a lithium refinery, a cathode (battery-material) plant, in-house chip design and training computers [Q2 2026 Update, p.7] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Weakening sign: the deck still calling battery packs "the limiting factor on ramping our vehicle production globally" [Q2 2026 Update, p.7].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. A price war in electric cars, above all in China.** It has happened once, in 2023–2024, when price cuts took automotive margin without credits from 26.7% to 15.4% (§3) [Q4 2025 Update, p.32] [Q4 2025 Update, p.7]. China is 22% of revenue (computed; §2) and Shanghai the largest plant [10-K FY2025, Note 16] [Q2 2026 Update, p.6]. Early warning: automotive margin excluding credits below 15%; days of supply rising; "lower vehicle average selling price" recurring in the deck's bridge (its list of what moved revenue and profit year over year) [Q2 2026 Update, p.25]. Exposure: vehicles and credits, about three quarters of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 8].

**2. Regulatory credit revenue goes to zero.** Credits were 46% of 2025 operating income (§2), and Tesla itself expects "loss of certain regulatory credit sales tied to our products" [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. Early warning: the quarterly line, already $146 million, and the $287 million of contracted credits left [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]. Exposure: about $2 billion a year (2025) of nearly pure profit [10-K FY2025, Item 8].

**3. Capex outruns cash.** Over $25 billion of 2026 capex (§4) against $6.2 billion of 2025 free cash flow; Q2 2026 was already negative and capex will "increase further in the second half of 2026" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q4 2025 Update, p.5] [Q2 2026 call, Taneja prepared remarks]. The 10-Q reserves the right to "slow the pace of our capital expenditures" or "raise additional capital" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Early warning: free cash flow negative two quarters running; cash below $40 billion; new facilities drawn. Exposure: the $43.5 billion cushion, and dilution (each existing share owning a smaller slice) if new shares are sold to raise money [Q2 2026 Update, p.4].

**4. A safety, regulatory or legal setback to FSD and Robotaxi.** The 10-K says "our future business depends on development of our driver assistance systems and autonomous driving solutions" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Live cases: a Florida jury in August 2025 found Tesla 33% at fault in a fatal Autopilot crash and awarded $129 million compensatory (to cover losses) and $200 million punitive (to punish) damages, on appeal; consumer class actions, one of which once sought an order to "cease using the term “Full Self-Driving Capability”"; plus a shareholder suit and standing information requests from NHTSA, the SEC and the Department of Justice [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11] [10-K FY2025, Note 13]. Early warning: a court order changing how FSD is sold or named; the Robotaxi metro count stalling; the CEO's own gate, "if we injure even one person, it will be worldwide headline news" [Q2 2026 call, Musk prepared remarks]. Exposure: the strategy and the FSD subscription base; robotaxi revenue is undisclosed.

**5. Losing Elon Musk's attention or presence.** Verbatim: "We are highly dependent on the services of Elon Musk ... he does not devote his full time and attention to Tesla" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. About 207 million of his shares may be "pledged as collateral to secure certain personal indebtedness" [10-K/A FY2025, Item 12]. Early warning: new outside roles; share sales; the 2025 award's service condition (§7) being loosened. Exposure: the whole thesis, by the company's own account.

**6. Tariffs and geopolitics.** Tariffs "have impacted our supply chain costs" and "the pricing for our products" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: tariffs reappearing as a minus in the deck's cost bridge; China revenue falling. Exposure: energy first, by the 10-K's own ranking, then cars [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**7. Battery supply.** "Battery pack capacity" is "the limiting factor on ramping our vehicle production globally"; cells come from "a very limited number" of qualified suppliers, chiefly Panasonic and CATL [Q2 2026 Update, p.7] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: production trailing deliveries for more than a quarter, as in Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 deliveries release]. Exposure: growth, not today's profit.

**Customer concentration.** No customer was 10% or more of receivables at June 30, 2026, and no revenue concentration is disclosed [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]. The only named customers are related parties, xAI and SpaceX (§7 table); SpaceX's Megapack purchases were about 10% of Q2 2026 energy revenue (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 13].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Elon Musk, 54, "Technoking of Tesla and Chief Executive Officer", a founder and the largest shareholder; Vaibhav Taneja, 48, CFO since August 2023; Tom Zhu, 46, who built Gigafactory Shanghai and runs Asia-Pacific and global vehicle manufacturing; there were "no other executive officers" at the end of 2025 [10-K/A FY2025, Item 10]. Musk is also CEO, CTO and chairman of SpaceX, founder of The Boring Company and CEO of Neuralink, and led xAI Holdings, a SpaceX subsidiary since February 2026 (§7 table) [10-K/A FY2025, Item 10] [10-K/A FY2025, Item 13].

**Board.** Nine directors on three-year staggered terms, chaired by Robyn Denholm since 2018 and including Kimbal Musk (the CEO's brother) and co-founder JB Straubel (CEO of Redwood Materials); all but the two Musks are deemed independent, and the eight outside directors took no cash or equity pay in 2025 [10-K/A FY2025, Item 10] [10-K/A FY2025, Item 11] [10-K/A FY2025, Item 13]. A Special Committee of Denholm and Wilson-Thompson designed the CEO awards [10-K/A FY2025, Item 11]. Shareholders moved the company from Delaware to Texas in June 2024 [8-K 2024-06-14]. In May 2025 the board added to the bylaws a jury-trial waiver and a 3%-ownership threshold for bringing a derivative proceeding (a suit a shareholder brings on the company's behalf) [8-K 2025-05-16 bylaws]. In November 2025 shareholders left the 3% rule in place, passed an advisory call for annual director elections, and did not approve management's plan to drop supermajority voting (rules needing more than a simple majority) [8-K 2025-11-07].

**Ownership.** At December 31, 2025 Musk beneficially owned (owned or controlled) 717.1 million shares, 20.3%, counting the 304.0 million under his 2018 options; the figure excludes the 423.7 million restricted shares (shares forfeited if conditions are not met) of the 2025 award [10-K/A FY2025, Item 12] [10-K FY2025, Note 11]. Vanguard held 6.1% and BlackRock 5.0% [10-K/A FY2025, Item 12].

## Glossary

- **Regulatory credits:** certificates a carmaker earns by selling more zero-emission or fuel-efficient vehicles than the law requires, which it can sell to carmakers that fall short.
- **Gross margin:** revenue minus the direct cost of making and delivering the product, as a share of revenue; it leaves out research, sales and administration.
- **Operating margin:** profit after all running costs, including research and administration, as a share of revenue.
- **Free cash flow:** cash generated by operations minus cash spent on factories and equipment (capex); Tesla's own definition.
- **FSD (Supervised):** Tesla's "Full Self-Driving" driver-assistance software, sold as an option or a monthly subscription; the company's footnote says it "does not make the vehicle autonomous" and the driver must stay attentive.
- **GWh (gigawatt-hour):** a unit of stored electricity (one billion watt-hours), used to measure how much battery storage Tesla ships in a period.
- **Megapack:** Tesla's container-sized battery for utilities and large sites; many are grouped into one installation.
- **Non-GAAP:** a figure that departs from the standard accounting rules, here mainly by leaving out stock-based pay and one-time gains or losses; Tesla labels each such figure.
