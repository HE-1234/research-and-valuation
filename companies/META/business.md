# Meta Platforms, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Meta runs four of the world's most used apps: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Messenger, plus Threads and the Meta AI assistant [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. About 3.6 billion people use at least one of them daily, almost none paying [Q2 2026 release, p.1]. The customers are businesses: "we generate substantially all of our revenue from selling advertising placements on our family of apps to marketers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. A marketer pays when its ad is shown (an impression) or when a user acts on it [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Advertising was 98% of 2025 revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 2].

Two segments are reported [10-K FY2025, Note 15]. Family of Apps (FoA) is the apps and the ads, and it makes all the profit; Reality Labs (RL) sells Meta Quest virtual-reality headsets and their software, and AI glasses [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. RL loses about $19 billion a year; management expects it "to continue to operate at a loss for the foreseeable future" and says supporting it "is dependent on generating sufficient profits from other areas of our business" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Since 2025 the company has also set out to build "superintelligence, which we define as AI that surpasses human intelligence", delivered as "personal superintelligence for everyone" [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

A short history. The company was incorporated in Delaware in July 2004 and listed in May 2012 [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. In 2021 it announced a shift toward "the metaverse" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Founder Mark Zuckerberg, 41, is chairman and CEO and controls 60.8% of the votes (§7) [DEF 14A 2026, Director Nominees and Executive Officers] [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership].

**Reality Labs.** Revenue has sat near $2 billion for five years; Q2 2026 grew 16% on glasses, with Quest sales falling [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

## 3. The economics

**Does cost rise with usage?** Historically, barely. The direct running cost of the ad system (cost of revenue: mostly data centers, servers, and partner payments) was 18–19% of revenue in every year but 2022, so gross margin (revenue minus cost of revenue, divided by revenue; Meta does not report it, so it is computed here) sits near 80% [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. The big cost is people: research and development was 29% of revenue in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Operating margin (profit after all running costs, as a share of revenue) fell to 25% in 2022 when revenue dipped while spending rose, then recovered to 41–42% after layoffs of about 11,000 people announced in 2022 and about 10,000 in 2023 [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Note 3] [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

RL exists to build "the next computing platform", and "many products may only be fully realized in the next decade" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. About 70% of 2026 RL spending goes to wearables and 30% to VR and Horizon (Meta's virtual-reality software platform), and management expects the 2026 loss "to remain similar to 2025" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

**The capex program.** Capex, meaning capital spending, buys servers (including AI chips), data centers, and network; Meta counts finance-lease principal too, because leased equipment is the same spend "regardless of whether we procure such property or equipment with a finance lease" [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q2 2026 release, p.4]. Property and equipment rose from $121 billion at the end of 2024 to $226 billion at June 30, 2026, $80 billion of it unfinished construction, and the 2026 plan is $130–145 billion [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 6] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

**How capex becomes expense.** A server's cost is spread as depreciation over its useful life, which Meta lengthened to 5.5 years in January 2025, cutting that year's depreciation by $2.92 billion [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Depreciation of property and equipment rose from $11.0 billion in 2023 to $18.0 billion in 2025 and was $6.0 billion in Q2 2026 alone [10-K FY2025, Note 6] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 6]. That is why Q2 2026 cost of revenue rose 33% and research and development 67%, driven by infrastructure and a new item, "third-party AI token costs" (paying to use other companies' AI models) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Our inference: these costs are fixed, so if revenue growth slows the margin drops fast; Q2's 31% previews it [Q2 2026 release, p.1].

**Promised but not yet on the balance sheet (the list of what Meta owns and owes).** Signed data-center and network leases not yet started: $279 billion at June 30, 2026, plus about $68 billion signed in July, against $104 billion at the end of 2025 [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9] [10-K FY2025, Note 7]. Non-cancelable purchase commitments, mostly cloud capacity, servers, and data centers: $349 billion ($53.5 billion due in 2026, $81.7 billion in 2027), against $131 billion at year-end 2025 [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9] [10-K FY2025, Note 11]. In October 2025 Meta placed a Louisiana data-center campus into a venture it owns 20% of: the owners, Meta included, fund about $27 billion of construction in proportion to their stakes, Meta leases the buildings from 2029 for about $12.3 billion, and it has given residual value guarantees of about $28 billion (if Meta walks away it pays any shortfall against a guaranteed value); maximum exposure is $46.0 billion, kept off Meta's balance sheet because Meta does not control the venture [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 5]. A second venture in El Paso, Texas, with BlackRock, adds about $13 billion of guarantees [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 13] [Q2 2026 call, p.3].

**Management's justification.** The industry "has underbuilt historically for the wave of AI adoption", Meta can use the capacity in its core business, so plans are "geared towards maximizing 2026 and 2027 capacity", and "overall industry capacity is going to remain tight for the foreseeable future" [Q2 2026 call, p.8] [Q2 2026 call, p.9]. The CFO adds that Meta is "supply constrained" (short of computing capacity) even in the core business [Q2 2026 call, p.21].

At June 30, 2026 the company held $90.3 billion of cash and quickly sellable investments against $83.7 billion of long-term debt [Q2 2026 release, p.1].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Users stay because the people they know are there.** Facebook and Instagram each have at least 2 billion daily users, WhatsApp peaked at a record 30 million messages per second during the World Cup final, and Threads passed 500 million monthly users [Q2 2026 call, p.1]. The company admits the limit: "we continue to face competition from other products and services within certain demographics, in particular younger users" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Second, recommendation: Instagram time spent grew "double digits" in Q2 2026, "largely driven by improvements to our Feed and Reels recommendations" [Q2 2026 call, p.5].

**Advertisers stay because the ads work and are measured.** A marketer pays per impression or per action and sees the result, so the moat is performance, not contracts; Zuckerberg wants new products to "be like our ad systems where businesses only pay us when we achieve results for them" [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q2 2026 call, p.3]. The automated Advantage+ tools now bring in revenue at a pace above $75 billion a year, and 9 million small businesses use an AI creative tool [Q2 2026 call, p.7] [Q2 2026 call, p.2].

**Signs of weakening.** Users: daily active people growth below Q2 2026's 3% for two quarters, or time-spent commentary disappearing [Q2 2026 release, p.1]. Advertisers: price per ad falling while impressions rise, as in 2022–2023 (−16%, then −9%) [10-K FY2023, Item 7].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. The AI build-out costs more than it earns.** Meta plans $130–145 billion of 2026 capex against $43.6 billion of 2025 free cash flow, with $349 billion of purchase commitments and about $347 billion (computed) of not-yet-started leases behind it [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9]. Management has given no 2027 capex figure and no timeline for selling compute (computing capacity) [Q2 2026 call, p.10] [Q2 2026 follow-up call, p.5]. Early warning: free cash flow negative for two quarters; depreciation growing faster than revenue; operating margin near Q2's 31%. Exposure: the whole profit pool, plus $84 billion of debt [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 8].

**2. Youth-safety litigation forces product changes or huge payments.** As of July 30, 2026: a Los Angeles jury awarded $6 million in the first personal-injury trial; a New Mexico jury imposed a $375 million penalty and the state seeks $953 million more plus "extensive changes to the manner in which we provide our services"; and the first multi-state attorneys-general trial begins August 12 [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1]. Plaintiffs' demands run "in certain cases up to more than a trillion dollars", and across all legal matters the maximum sought "could amount to an aggregate of up to hundreds of billions of dollars" [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1]. The FTC is also trying to rewrite its 2020 settlement with the company to ban "our use of minors' data for any commercial purposes" [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1]. Early warning: verdicts or injunctions (court orders) that change product design; legal charges recurring after Q2's $2.4 billion [Q2 2026 release, p.1]. Exposure: US & Canada is 44% of revenue (computed) [Q2 2026 slides, p.3].

**3. Regulators break ad targeting in Europe and beyond.** The European Commission fined Meta €200 million in April 2025 over its "subscription for no ads" model; Meta appealed and now offers "less personalized ads", warning that further changes "could result in a materially worse user experience for European users and a significant impact to our European business and revenue" [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1]. Early warning: Europe's ad price growth trailing (Q2 2026: +10% versus +20% in US & Canada) and new Commission orders [Q2 2026 slides, p.13]. Exposure: Europe is 24% of revenue (computed) [Q2 2026 slides, p.3].

**4. Users drift away.** Daily active people growth slowed from 7% in December 2025 to 3% in June 2026, and the count dipped in Q1 2026 when Iran cut internet access and Russia restricted WhatsApp [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Early warning: DAP flat for two quarters; impressions growth in single digits with price carrying revenue. Exposure: every advertising dollar, 98% of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 2].

**5. Platform and supplier dependence.** The apps live on Apple's and Google's phones, and Apple's 2021 privacy change "has negatively impacted, and we expect will continue to negatively impact" ad budgets [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Infrastructure equipment comes from "a small number of third parties, often with significant operations in a single region such as Asia" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. The only chip supplier named in any cached filing or call is Broadcom, paid about $2.3 billion in 2025; Nvidia is not named as a supplier anywhere and appears only in the proxy's pay peer group [DEF 14A 2026, Related Party Transactions] [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Early warning: new operating-system privacy rules. Exposure: all distribution runs through those platforms [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**Other exposures.** A separate New Mexico Attorney General case over privacy and consumer protection, since widened to content-moderation claims, goes to trial on September 8, 2026, with the state seeking "up to $62.85 billion in penalties" [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1]. The IRS asserts $15.89 billion of extra tax for 2017–2019, contested in Tax Court [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11]. In June 2026 the Commission ordered WhatsApp to open its business messaging to rival AI chatbots for free; Meta intends to appeal [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1].

**Concentration.** No customer was 10% or more of revenue or receivables (money owed by customers) in 2023–2025, and 37% of 2025 revenue came from US-based marketers and developers [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. The number of advertisers is not disclosed. The one named pocket: "meaningful revenue from a small number of resellers serving advertisers based in China" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]; China-based customers were $13.7 billion, or 10% (computed), of 2023 revenue, undisclosed since [10-K FY2023, Note 2].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**Control.** Class B shares carry ten votes each, Class A one [10-K FY2025, Note 12]. Mark Zuckerberg held 99.8% of Class B and 60.8% of all votes at April 1, 2026, while owning about 13.5% of all shares (computed) [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership]. Meta is a "controlled company" under Nasdaq rules, and the 10-K states that "Our CEO has control over key decision making" [DEF 14A 2026, Corporate Governance] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. In practice capex, Reality Labs, buybacks, and the dividend are one person's decisions; the board opposed a 2026 shareholder proposal to end the dual-class structure [DEF 14A 2026, Proposal Five].

**People.** Zuckerberg has been CEO since 2004; Susan Li has been CFO since 2022 and at Meta since 2008; the three other named executive officers (COO, CTO, Chief Product Officer) joined between 2005 and 2007, while two of the seven listed executive officers, the President and Vice Chairman and the Chief Legal Officer, arrived in 2025–2026; no head of Meta Superintelligence Labs is an executive officer [DEF 14A 2026, Director Nominees and Executive Officers]. Headcount was 75,472 at June 30, 2026, including about 8,000 people cut in May [Q2 2026 release, p.1].

**Pay.** Zuckerberg takes a $1 salary and no bonus or stock; his $25.1 million of 2025 compensation is almost all personal security and private aircraft [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Other named officers get $1 million salaries, a cash bonus (target 200% of base pay since 2025, paid at 115% for 2025), and RSUs worth $20–22 million each vesting quarterly over four years for continued employment [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. New in 2026: 20 million stock options (rights to buy a share at a fixed price) to executives and employees at a weighted-average exercise price (that fixed price) of $2,788 [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 10].

**Cash.** Policy: keep paying the quarterly dividend, keep "investing in significant opportunities", and raise capital "through debt, equity, or other financing arrangements" if needed [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

## Glossary

- **DAP (daily active people):** Meta's estimate of the number of unique people who used Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, or WhatsApp on a given day, the headline measure of the user base.
- **Finance lease:** a lease that works like a purchase paid in installments, so Meta counts the principal payments as capital spending.
- **Residual value guarantee:** a promise to pay the owner of a leased building any shortfall between its value and an agreed threshold if Meta ends the lease, used in the data-center joint ventures.
- **RSU (restricted stock unit):** a promise of company shares delivered ("vested") to an employee over time, the main form of pay at Meta.
- **Depreciation:** spreading the cost of a long-lived asset such as a server over the years it is used, so today's capex becomes expense for several years afterward.
