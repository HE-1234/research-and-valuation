## 1. Business description

- Mission, verbatim: "Our mission is to develop the best education in the world and make it universally available." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Formed August 18, 2011; the Duolingo App launched to the public June 19, 2012; headquarters Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. Founders Luis von Ahn (then a CMU computer-science professor, MacArthur Fellow, grew up in Guatemala) and Severin Hacker (his Ph.D. student). [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Note 1]
- What it is, in the filing's words: "Duolingo is a US-based mobile learning platform, as well as a digital English language proficiency assessment exam. The Company has a freemium business model: the app and the website are accessible free of charge, although Duolingo also offers premium services for a subscription fee." Locations in the U.S., China, the United Kingdom and Germany. [10-K FY2025, Note 1]
- Single operating and reportable segment; the CEO is the chief operating decision maker and evaluates performance on consolidated net income "supplemented by disaggregated revenue details". [10-K FY2025, Note 2]
- Scale claims: "world's most popular way to learn languages and the top-grossing Education app in the app stores"; on Google, people search "Duolingo" more often than "learn Spanish"; more people learning Irish and Hawaiian on Duolingo than native speakers worldwide; in the U.S. more people learn a language on Duolingo than K-12 students taking a foreign-language class. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Courses and users: "As of December 31, 2025, Duolingo offers over 250 total language courses to more than 130 million monthly active users." Nearly 2 billion exercises completed every day ("what we believe to be the world's largest learning dataset"). About 43 million DAUs had a 7-day-or-longer streak and about 15 million a 365-day-or-longer streak at Dec 31, 2025. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Market size cited: HolonIQ (January 2024) expects consumer spend on online and offline language learning of about $123 billion by 2027; HolonIQ estimates roughly 2 billion potential language learners globally. [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 7]

### Products (filing wording)
- **The Duolingo App**: free; >250 language courses; also on the web at duolingo.com; Math, Music and Chess courses are integrated into the same app. Math launched 2022, Music 2023, Chess 2025 ("growing rapidly"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- **Super Duolingo**: the subscription; "offers learners additional features to enhance their learning experience." Family plan launched 2021, "includes up to six subscribers under one annual plan." Learners on a paid plan get "an ad-free experience and access to additional features." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- **Duolingo Max**: "a premium subscription tier launched in 2023 and offered to a portion of our user base and priced higher than Super Duolingo. It gives learners access to the existing features of Super Duolingo in addition to incremental features and exercises powered by AI technology" (Video Call, Roleplay, Explain My Answer are named as AI-powered features). [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 7]
- **Duolingo English Test (DET)**: launched 2016; "an online, on-demand, high-stakes (e.g., used for university admissions) English proficiency assessment"; needs a computer with webcam plus a smartphone; computer-adaptive; under an hour; one-time fee per test. Acceptance at Dec 31, 2025: "over 6,100 education programs around the world ... with over 5,300 of those being higher education programs", including 24 of the top 25 U.S. undergraduate programs by international enrollment, all of the Ivy League, MIT and Stanford. "University program acceptance is a driver of Duolingo English Test revenue." [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 7]
- **Other initiatives**: Duolingo for Schools (web tool for teachers) and Duolingo ABC (early-literacy app for young children; "does not collect personal information directly from the user beyond that required to use the app"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- **Duo's** (Dos Lenguas LLC): a restaurant next to the Pittsburgh HQ opened 2022 (called "Duo's Taquería" in the FY2023 10-K); revenue recognised at point of sale, sits in "Other" revenue. [10-K FY2025, Note 2; 10-K FY2023, Note 4]

### Why a free app makes money (the filing's own words)
- "We use a freemium business model that relies on premium subscription offerings, advertising, and in-app-purchases (IAPs) to produce revenue." Three attributes: large market; free users ("we intentionally do not put our learning content behind a paywall"); paid subscriber conversion ("We convert free users to paid subscribers over time. We see stronger conversion from users that recently joined or reengage with the platform, but also see learners who use our product for months or even years before they decide to subscribe."). [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Free users "see an ad at the end of each lesson." Advertising is sold through "the major programmatic advertising networks"; ad revenue "is primarily a function of the number of our free users, lessons completed by our free users, and our ability to provide innovative advertising placements." [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 7]
- IAPs are "one-time benefits within the app, such as 'Streak Freezes' and 'Timer Boosts.'" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Paid penetration: "As of December 31, 2025, approximately 9% of our monthly active users were paid subscribers"; more precisely "subscribers made up 9.2% of our average MAUs over the last twelve months as compared to 8.8% ... during the year ended December 31, 2024." [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 7]
- Two "mutually-reinforcing flywheels": learning flywheel (scale → data → engagement/efficacy → word of mouth) and investment flywheel (word-of-mouth growth lets capital go to product rather than "brand or performance marketing"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Growth is "organic, primarily driven by word-of-mouth and brand buzz, and supplemented by selective and targeted paid user acquisition." Marketing pillars: brand building (viral social content, memes), owned media (push notifications, emails), paid acquisition, geographic expansion with local marketing managers and influencer campaigns. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- AI role: "AI plays an increasingly important role in how we provide effective learning experiences and generate content at scale ... it also requires increased investment in computing infrastructure and related costs." The company says it can "generate high quality course content at materially faster speeds due to leveraging AI." Hundreds of A/B tests per quarter. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]

### Revenue recognition (one paragraph)
Duolingo has "four predominant sources of revenues; time-based subscriptions, in-app advertising placement by third parties, the Duolingo English Test, and In-App Purchases." Subscriptions are sold monthly or annually (the family plan is annual only); the payment is recorded to deferred revenue and recognised over the subscription period as a single stand-ready obligation, so a year's cash arrives up front and revenue trickles out over 12 months. This is why the company reports **bookings** ("the amounts we receive from a purchase") alongside revenue: "we recognize subscription revenues ratably over the lifetime of a subscription, the majority of which are twelve months in duration." All deferred revenue is classified current "as the longest subscription plan is for twelve months, and Duolingo English Test purchases must be taken within 21 days." Advertising is recognised "at a point in time when the advertisement is placed"; DET revenue when the test has been proctored and certified (usually within 48 hours; unused tests expire after 21 days, bulk coupons after a year); IAPs over the period the virtual good is consumed, "generally within a month." **Gross versus net**: users pay through the Apple and Google app stores, but Duolingo "is the principal in the transaction with the end user" and therefore "records revenue gross as a principal and records fees paid to third-party payment processors as Cost of revenues." The matching app-store fee on annual plans is capitalised as **deferred cost of revenues** and amortised over the subscription term. Revenue is net of sales-type taxes. [10-K FY2025, Note 2; Item 7]

- The FY2021 10-K also mentioned a "six-month" subscription option; later 10-Ks mention only monthly and annual. [10-K FY2021, Note 3]

## 3. Key operating metrics

### Definitions (FY2025 10-K wording)
- **MAUs**: "unique users who engage with our Duolingo App or the learning section of our website each month. MAUs are reported for a measurement period by taking the average of the MAUs for each calendar month in that measurement period." Measurement period is the fourth quarter. [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- **DAUs**: "unique users who engage with our Duolingo App or the learning section of our website each calendar day", averaged over each day in the quarter. "DAUs are a measure of the consistent engagement of our global user community." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- **Paid subscribers**: "users who pay for access to any Duolingo subscription offering and had an active subscription as of the end of the measurement period. Each unique user account is treated as a single paid subscriber regardless of whether such user purchases multiple subscriptions, and the count of paid subscribers does not include users who are currently on a free trial or who are non-paying members of a family plan." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- **Subscription bookings**: "the amounts we receive from a purchase of any Duolingo subscription offering." **Total bookings** add "income from advertising networks for advertisements served to our users, purchases of the Duolingo English Test, and in-app purchases of virtual goods." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Metrics come from an internal analytics platform "not validated by an independent third party"; "Because we update the methodologies we employ to create metrics, our operating metrics may not be comparable to those in prior periods." [10-K FY2025, Special Note Regarding Key Operating Metrics]
- Definition drift: the FY2021 10-K defined MAUs/DAUs as users of "our mobile language learning application or the language learning section of our website" and paid subscribers as users "who pay for access to Duolingo Plus" (the subscription's earlier name). From FY2022 the wording is "any Duolingo subscription offering." [10-K FY2021, Item 7; 10-K FY2022, Item 7]
- **Q2 2026 change of emphasis**: "We primarily evaluate user engagement using DAUs, with MAUs as a supplementary metric." MAUs moved from the metrics table to a footnote, and the MD&A overview now leads with "over 58 million daily active users" instead of MAUs. [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]

### Cost of revenues: what is in it
- "Cost of revenues predominantly consists of third-party payment processing fees charged by various distribution channels in addition to hosting fees and Artificial Intelligence ('AI') costs. To a much lesser extent, cost of revenues includes customer support costs ... It also includes the amortization of revenue generating capitalized software, and depreciation of certain property and equipment." The 10-Q says "third-party AI costs." [10-K FY2025, Item 7; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]
- App-store take: "we paid Apple and Google, as applicable, a meaningful share (generally 15-30%) of the payments we receive from transactions processed through in-app payment systems." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- DET proctoring is named as a cost driver only in FY2021 ("decrease in Duolingo English Test margins driven by increased proctoring costs"). [10-K FY2021, Item 7]
- Amortisation of revenue-generating capitalised software in cost of revenues: 2,020 (2023); 5,310 (2024); 8,658 (2025); 5,598 (6M 2026). [10-K FY2025, Note 7; 10-Q Q2 2026, Note 6]

### Management's stated gross-margin drivers, by year
- FY2021 (72.4% from 71.6%): "increased subscription margins driven by reduced payment processing fees for subscription revenue due to improved retention, and increased advertising margins ... partially offset by a decrease in Duolingo English Test margins driven by increased proctoring costs." [10-K FY2021, Item 7]
- FY2022 (73.1% from 72.4%): "increased subscription margins from both improved retention and reduction in fees charged by the Google Play store. Offsetting this increase was a decline in Advertising margins ... due to decreases in average advertising revenue per DAU." [10-K FY2022, Item 7]
- FY2023 (73.2% from 73.1%): "higher subscription margins as a result of a reduction in third-party payment processing fees per subscriber in addition to higher margins on the Duolingo English Test as a result of an increase in the average test price ... partially offset by a decline in Advertising margins." [10-K FY2023, Item 7]
- FY2024 (72.8% from 73.2%): "lower advertising gross margin, partially offset by an increase in subscription revenue as a percentage of total revenue." [10-K FY2024, Item 7]
- FY2025 (72.2% from 72.8%): "a decline in subscription gross margin, reflecting increased AI costs used in features like Video Call, and a shift in revenue mix toward advertising, which carries lower margins than subscriptions." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Q2 2026 (72.6% from 72.4%) and 6M 2026 (72.8% from 71.8%): "an increase in subscription gross margin, reflecting continued reductions in per-unit third-party AI costs." [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]

## 8. Risk factors specific to Duolingo (not boilerplate)

- **Apple and Google dependence**: "Our mobile applications are almost exclusively accessed through and depend on the Apple App Store and the Google Play Store." "In the twelve months ended December 31, 2025, we derived 62% of our revenue and 61% of our total bookings from the Apple App Store, and 20% of our revenue and 21% of our total bookings from the Google Play Store" (2024: 61%/62% Apple; 20%/20% Google). Fees "generally 15-30%". Distribution agreements "are generally terminable by Apple or Google without cause with 30 days prior written notice." Platforms may "change its fee structure ... limit the use of personal information for advertising or attribution purposes." Duolingo relies on the platforms' reporting "to accurately report our financials." Named court rulings (e.g., Epic), alternative billing or the EU Digital Markets Act are **not mentioned** in the FY2025 risk factor. [10-K FY2025, Item 1A; 10-K FY2024, Item 1A]
- **Cloud hosting**: "a significant portion, if not almost all, of our product traffic, data storage, data processing and other computing services and systems is hosted by AWS and Google Cloud." Specific incident: "on October 20, 2025, an outage of the us-east-1 region of the AWS platform caused Duolingo to go offline for over 12 hours." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- **Generative AI as cost, dependence and competitor**: AI features (Video Call in the top tier; content generation for the app and the DET) rely on "integrations with a third party's AI technologies"; "our ability to continue to develop, maintain or use such technologies may be dependent on access to specific third-party software and infrastructure ... We cannot control the availability or pricing." The model providers are **not named** in the 10-K. Competitive threat: "a new product could gain rapid scale at the expense of existing brands through harnessing a new technology (such as generative artificial intelligence)"; also "As translation services and products, such as wearable devices that provide translation, improve ... people may generally become less interested in language learning." GenAI risks listed: hallucination, IP/copyright uncertainty, training-data litigation exposure. Regulation: EU AI Act (most requirements from August 2, 2026, with a proposed but unfinalised extension to December 2, 2027; fines up to 7% of turnover), Colorado AI Act, Texas TRAIGA, California laws, China's generative-AI provisions, and the December 11, 2025 U.S. executive order on a national AI framework. [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- **DET acceptance and immigration**: DET was ~4.0% of 2025 revenue (6.1% in 2024). "a country's decision to cap or reduce immigration, particularly international student immigration, could reduce the number of Duolingo English Tests"; "if trends in international education shift away from English-speaking destination countries, such as the U.S. ... we may see a reduction"; "Certain schools, governments, and other institutions have in the past rescinded their acceptance of the Duolingo English Test." Loss of confidence in the test's "validity, security" is a named risk. [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- **Advertising**: ~7.7% of 2025 revenue; depends on programmatic networks; Apple's identifier changes and state opt-out laws reduce targeting value. [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- **Growth from social media and influencers**: marketing depends on social platforms, "for example, the potential U.S. governmental restrictions or limitations on the TikTok platform"; influencer FTC-disclosure risk ("we do not regularly monitor what our influencers post"). S&M spend $125.7M (2025) vs $90.5M (2024). [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- **Key persons**: "We currently depend on the continued services and performance of our key personnel, including Luis von Ahn and Severin Hacker"; talent "subject to poaching efforts"; succession planning named. [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- **Children's privacy**: COPPA (age-gate: U.S. under-13 registrants get an ad-free version with restricted data practices), Texas SCOPE Act, GDPR Article 8, UK Age Appropriate Design Code, China's minors regulations (effective January 1, 2024), state age-verification laws; DET requires parental consent under 13. ROSCA governs recurring subscriptions. [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 1A]
- **Seasonality**: users and subscribers "increase in the beginning of the year and then moderate throughout the first quarter and second quarter"; Q3 lift from back-to-school in some geographies; late-December New-Year surge; a New Year promotion lifts subscribers in late December and January. [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- **Foreign exchange**: reporting and functional currency USD; opex in the U.S., China, the UK and Germany; "Certain of our payment providers translate our payments from local currency into USD at time of settlement, which means that during periods of a strengthening U.S. dollar, our international receipts could be reduced." No hedging. A 10% move "would not have a material effect on our operating results." [10-K FY2025, Item 7A; Item 1A]
- **Tax**: valuation-allowance reversal risk is listed among forward-looking cautions; OECD Pillar Two and digital-services taxes named; effective rate sensitive to stock price via equity deductions. [10-K FY2025, Special Note; Item 1A; Note 9]
- **International operations**: tariffs on "our products", fund-repatriation restrictions, China office exposure (the FY2023 10-K said "Our office of over 30 employees in Beijing makes it easier for the Chinese authorities to bring enforcement actions against us"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1A; 10-K FY2023, Item 1A]
- **Metric measurement**: internal analytics platform, not independently validated; methodology updates can break comparability. [10-K FY2025, Special Note Regarding Key Operating Metrics]
- **Dual class / index exclusion**: 76.3% of voting power with insiders and 5% holders; index providers may exclude multi-class companies. [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Concentration of DAUs or revenue by country other than the U.S. (38% of 2025 revenue): **not disclosed**. [10-K FY2025, Note 5]
- 10-Q Q2 2026: "There have been no material changes from the risk factors previously disclosed." [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1A]
