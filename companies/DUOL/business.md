# Duolingo, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Duolingo is a mobile app, also usable on the web, that teaches languages through short game-like lessons, and now also math, music and chess [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It runs on "a freemium business model: the app and the website are accessible free of charge, although Duolingo also offers premium services for a subscription fee" [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. In the quarter ended June 30, 2026 an average of 58.7 million people used it daily and 140.6 million monthly; 12.7 million paid at quarter end [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

**Why a free app makes money.** The free product is the marketing. "We intentionally do not put our learning content behind a paywall"; a free learner sees "an ad at the end of each lesson", a paying one gets "an ad-free experience and access to additional features" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Free users are an ad audience and a pool to convert: "We convert free users to paid subscribers over time ... but also see learners who use our product for months or even years before they decide to subscribe" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. About 9% of monthly users pay [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. So management optimises daily active users (DAUs): more DAUs mean more ads now and more subscribers later, though "our users don't monetize immediately" [Q2 2026 call].

**A short history.** Luis von Ahn and Severin Hacker, professor and Ph.D. student at Carnegie Mellon, formed the company on August 18, 2011 and launched the app on June 19, 2012 [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. The Duolingo English Test (DET) came in 2016, the paid subscription in 2017, the Nasdaq listing in July 2021, Math in 2022, Music and the AI-powered Max tier in 2023, Chess in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Note 10]. In February 2026 management said DAU growth had "decelerated throughout 2025", partly from "our increased focus on monetization in recent years", and that it would now "prioritize teaching better and user growth" at the cost of slower 2026 bookings growth [Q4 2025 letter, p.4].

## 2. How the money comes in

One segment, four revenue streams plus "Other" [10-K FY2025, Note 2] [10-K FY2025, Note 5].

**Subscriptions (84% of 2025 revenue, computed).** Monthly or annual plans: Super Duolingo (no ads, extra features); Duolingo Max (Super plus AI features such as Video Call, "priced higher than Super Duolingo" and "offered to a portion of our user base"); a Family Plan for up to six people [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Prices are not disclosed.

**Advertising (7.7%).** Shown to free users and sold through "the major programmatic advertising networks" (automated ad exchanges) [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Duolingo English Test (4.0%, from 6.1% in 2024).** A one-time fee per test, "high-stakes (e.g., used for university admissions)", accepted by over 6,100 programmes [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2024, Item 1A].

**In-app purchases (3.9%).** One-time items "such as 'Streak Freezes' and 'Timer Boosts'" [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

## 3. The economics

**Does serving one more learner cost much?** A little, and it depends on the learner. Cost of revenues "predominantly consists of third-party payment processing fees charged by various distribution channels in addition to hosting fees and Artificial Intelligence ('AI') costs" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The largest piece, the app-store cut, scales with paid revenue, not free users: Duolingo "paid Apple and Google, as applicable, a meaningful share (generally 15-30%) of the payments we receive from transactions processed through in-app payment systems" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. It books the full price as revenue and the fee as cost of revenues because it "is the principal in the transaction with the end user", so the fee lands in gross margin [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. Hosting (servers) and AI scale with usage, so a free learner costs both and earns only ad revenue. The CFO: "Our expenses on AI and cost of goods sold are tens of millions of dollars ... Hosting is another big cost for us as well"; the dollar figure is not disclosed [Q2 2026 call].

**Gross margin and its drivers.** Gross margin has sat between 72% and 73% for five years while the parts moved [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Subscription margins rose in 2021–2023 as "payment processing fees" per subscriber fell, including a "reduction in fees charged by the Google Play store", offset by falling advertising margins [10-K FY2021, Item 7] [10-K FY2022, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. 2024 dipped on "lower advertising gross margin"; 2025 on "increased AI costs used in features like Video Call, and a shift in revenue mix toward advertising" [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. In 2026 the AI line reversed: first-half gross margin rose to 72.8% on "continued reductions in per-unit third-party AI costs", and Video Call went from "like $0.30 per call" to "under $0.01", "mainly a move towards open source models" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 call]. DET proctoring (test-supervision) costs were last cited as a driver in 2021 [10-K FY2021, Item 7].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Habit, built on the streak.** At December 31, 2025 about 43 million DAUs had a streak of seven days or more and about 15 million of a year or more [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. When a June 2026 event let lapsed learners restore their longest streak by finishing three lessons, 15.4 million did, "including nearly 8 million users who had no active streak" [Q2 2026 letter, p.5]. Management's retention gauge, Current User Retention Rate (CURR), "the proportion of 'current user DAUs' who come back the next day", is at "an all-time high of 84%, up by about 1% from last year" [Q2 2026 letter, p.4].

**Brand and organic reach.** Growth is "organic, primarily driven by word-of-mouth and brand buzz" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Its social accounts "have surpassed one billion organic impressions each quarter", and "the vast majority of our user growth continues to be organic" [Q2 2026 letter, p.5].

**Scale of content and experimentation.** Over 250 courses; nearly 2 billion exercises a day; a new app version every week with "approximately 350 changes" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [Q2 2026 call]. AI multiplied content output more than tenfold in two years (§3), and the core product is free for as long as the learner likes [Q1 2026 letter, p.5] [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

Our inference: a habit-and-brand moat, not switching costs or network effects; a learner who leaves loses only a streak. Weakening signs: DAU growth (23% now) falling below the 20% guided for 2026 [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q4 2025 letter, p.4]; CURR (84%) turning down [Q2 2026 letter, p.4]; paid subscribers lagging DAUs beyond 2026 (17% versus 23% now) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]; sales and marketing climbing as a share of revenue, 12% in 2025 and 13% in the first half of 2026 (computed), meaning word of mouth is being bought [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility; subscriptions are 84% of revenue, so most scenarios hit the same pool (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 5].

**1. General-purpose AI makes a language app redundant, or cheap to build.** "a new product could gain rapid scale at the expense of existing brands through harnessing a new technology (such as generative artificial intelligence)" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Duolingo's own AI comes from "a third party's AI technologies", available to any rival [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Nobody asked about ChatGPT or AI tutors on the Q2 call; the AI questions were about cost [Q2 2026 call]. Early warning: DAU growth below 20% with CURR falling. Exposure: all revenue.

**2. Users-first does not convert.** The bet is that "more than $50M of foregone bookings from friction (or about 5 points of year-over-year bookings growth)" buys DAUs who pay later [Q4 2025 letter, p.5]. Early warning: subscription bookings growth still near 10% in 2027 as revenue growth converges down to it. Exposure: the subscription line.

**3. Apple and Google change the terms, either way.** "we derived 62% of our revenue and 61% of our total bookings from the Apple App Store, and 20% of our revenue and 21% of our total bookings from the Google Play Store"; the revenue note puts the processed shares at 61.6% and 23.4%, unreconciled [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Note 5]. Agreements "are generally terminable by Apple or Google without cause with 30 days prior written notice" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. A fee cut would lift gross margin as surely as a policy change could cut revenue [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Early warning: the processor shares in the quarterly revenue note. Exposure: 82–85% of revenue (computed).

**4. AI suppliers raise prices or close doors.** "We cannot control the availability or pricing" of third-party AI; the 10-K names no provider, the CEO named OpenAI and Anthropic [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [Q2 2026 call]. Early warning: the "per-unit AI cost" sentence turning from falling to rising. Exposure: gross margin; Max revenue (not disclosed).

**5. The DET loses acceptance or students stop coming.** "a country's decision to cap or reduce immigration, particularly international student immigration, could reduce the number of Duolingo English Tests"; institutions "have in the past rescinded their acceptance" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. DET revenue fell in 2025 and was flat in Q2 2026, unexplained in the filings [10-K FY2025, Note 5] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4]. Exposure: 4% of revenue (computed).

**6. Founder dependence.** "We currently depend on the continued services and performance of our key personnel, including Luis von Ahn and Severin Hacker" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: an officer-change filing (8-K Item 5.02); the CEO's selling plan enlarged (§7).

**7. One cloud region.** "on October 20, 2025, an outage of the us-east-1 region of the AWS platform caused Duolingo to go offline for over 12 hours" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**8. China.** "our second largest market in terms of daily active users", expected to be the largest "in a year or 2", running only Chinese AI models by law; "I don't know what the government will decide at any point" [Q2 2026 call]. Exposure: not disclosed; only the United States (38% of revenue) is broken out [10-K FY2025, Note 5].

**Concentration.** No advertiser or customer is material; Apple, Google and Stripe held 65.5%, 15.7% and 11.7% of receivables (money owed to Duolingo) at June 30, 2026 [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Luis von Ahn, 47, is Chairman, CEO and co-founder; Severin Hacker, 41, is CTO (chief technology officer), co-founder and a director [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. On January 8, 2026 CFO Matthew Skaruppa resigned after "nearly six years"; director and former Vimeo CFO Gillian Munson became CFO on February 23, 2026 with a $14 million starting RSU grant, and "Neither Mr. Skaruppa nor Ms. Munson resigned because of any disagreements" [8-K 2026-01-12] [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Transition]. Nine directors, seven independent, in three staggered classes; von Ahn chairs, and the proxy names no lead independent director [DEF 14A 2026, Proposal No. 1] [DEF 14A 2026, Director Independence] [DEF 14A 2026, Board Leadership Structure].

**Who controls the votes.** Class B shares carry 20 votes, Class A one, with identical economic rights [10-K FY2025, Note 13]. At April 7, 2026 von Ahn held 55.2% of Class B and 40.0% of all votes, Hacker 47.7% and 35.8%; directors and officers together 76.0% of votes with 1.3% of Class A [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership of Certain Beneficial Owners and Management]. Class B converts on most transfers; the sunset (when the extra votes end) is not disclosed in any fetched filing and sits in the uncached charter [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**Pay.** "Our compensation program includes just two components, salary and equity"; no bonus plan, and annual RSUs vest over four years with no performance condition [DEF 14A 2026, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. Von Ahn's salary is $750,000, flat across the three years the proxy shows, with no annual grants; his equity is the 2021 Founder Award of 1,200,000 performance shares (600,000 for Hacker) vesting in ten portions at share-price hurdles from $127.50 to $816, of which eight are earned [DEF 14A 2026, Base Salary] [DEF 14A 2026, Special Multi-Year Performance-Based Founder Awards]. In 2026 the company "modified the service condition applicable to the final two tranches of the Founder Awards held by one of its founders", reversing $8.2 million of past expense for a $7.0 million stock-compensation *benefit* in Q2 2026; the founder is not named [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 8]. Also outside guidance: "a bonus plan that will trigger if Q4 DAU growth is 25% or higher ... roughly $10 million in cash" [Q2 2026 call].

**What they do with the cash.** No dividend, ever, and no buyback programme before 2026 [10-K FY2025, Item 5].

## Glossary

- **DAU (daily active user):** a unique account that used the app or the website's learning section on a given day, averaged over the quarter; Duolingo's main measure of audience size.
- **Bookings:** cash received from subscriptions, ads, tests and in-app purchases in the period, before the accounting rule that spreads subscription revenue over the subscription term.
- **Deferred revenue:** subscription cash already received but not yet counted as revenue, held as a liability and released month by month.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** net income with interest, taxes, depreciation, stock-based compensation and acquisition items added back; the profit measure management guides to.
- **Free cash flow:** operating cash flow minus purchases of property and equipment and capitalised software; the company's definition since Q1 2025.
- **CURR (Current User Retention Rate):** the share of learners active in the past week who come back the next day; management's chosen leading indicator of stickiness.
- **Valuation allowance:** an accounting reserve that hides tax assets a company doubts it can use; releasing it books a one-time tax benefit, as in Q3 2025.
- **Capitalised software:** development wages recorded as an asset and expensed over three years rather than immediately, which lowers reported R&D.
- **RSU (restricted stock unit):** a share grant that vests with time; the founders' awards instead vest when share-price targets are met.
- **GAAP / non-GAAP:** GAAP is the standard U.S. accounting rulebook; non-GAAP figures strip out items such as stock-based compensation.
- **Constant currency:** growth measured at the prior year's exchange rates, so currency swings are removed.
