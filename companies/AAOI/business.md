# Applied Optoelectronics, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026 (three months ended June 30, 2026). Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Applied Optoelectronics (AOI) makes optical transceivers: small plug-in modules that sit in the switches and servers of a data center and turn electrical signals into pulses of light and back, so data can travel between machines over fiber-optic cable [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Each generation carries more data per second (100G, 400G, 800G and now 1.6T), and AI data centers are pulling demand toward the fastest [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Unlike most module makers, AOI grows its own laser chips, the part that produces the light, at its own factory in Sugar Land, Texas [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Its data center customers are "large internet-based ("hyperscale") data center operators" and the equipment makers that supply them; data center was 56% of revenue in Q2 2026 [10-K FY2025, Item 1; 10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3].

The second business is equipment for cable-TV networks (CATV), AOI's "most established business" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Cable operators such as Comcast and Charter are upgrading to a standard called DOCSIS 4.0 to sell faster internet over the same coaxial cable, which means replacing the amplifiers, nodes and transmitters along the network; AOI designs and builds that gear, and its amplifiers contain no optics but rely on radio-frequency electronics and software developed in house [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Since 2023 it has also sold directly to cable operators under its Quantum Bandwidth brand; CATV was 42% of Q2 2026 revenue [10-K FY2025, Item 1; 10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3].

**History.** Thompson Lin, a former University of Houston research scientist, founded the company in Texas in February 1997 and has run it since; it bought its Ningbo factory in 2006 and listed on Nasdaq on September 26, 2013 [DEF 14A 2026, Director biographies; 10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 5]. For a decade the story was one or two giant customers at a time: Microsoft was 46.6% of revenue in 2023, and in CATV the equipment maker ATX Networks was 47.3% in 2022 before Digicomm displaced it, rising from zero in 2022 to 53.1% in 2025 [10-K FY2023, Item 1; 10-K FY2025, Item 1]. In March 2025 AOI issued Amazon a warrant tied to $4 billion of future purchases; DOCSIS 4.0 deployments and data center demand lifted 2025 revenue 83%; and in 2026 it began volume 800G shipments and a US manufacturing expansion funded by selling new shares [10-K FY2025, Note C; Item 7; Q1 2026 release; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

## 2. How the money comes in

AOI sells hardware, unit by unit, on short-term purchase orders "subject to rescheduling, revision or cancellation on short notice", with no customer commitments longer than a year; it reports one segment, so profit is not split by business line [10-K FY2025, Item 1; Item 7; Note R].

**Data center transceivers.** Hyperscale operators and their equipment suppliers pay per module; every new AI server rack needs many optical links and each speed upgrade means new modules [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Sales in 2025 were still mostly 100G and 400G (our inference: the 10-K expected 800G to overtake 400G only "later in 2026", and on the May call 800G was put at "5.6% of our total data center revenue" for Q1 2026) [10-K FY2025, Item 7; Q1 2026 call]. AOI says its products "are only minimally differentiated" from competitors' and face "strong pricing pressure", so volume and cost, not pricing power, drive this stream [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Q2 2026 data center revenue was $107.7 million, up 140% from a year earlier [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3; Item 2].

**CATV equipment.** Nearly all of it flows through one customer, Digicomm International, which holds inventory "to quickly provide products to customers when needed for their network builds" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. On the May call the CFO said "the vast majority" of 2026 CATV revenue would be amplifiers [Q1 2026 call]. Q2 2026 CATV revenue was $80.6 million; Digicomm was about $80.3 million, or 41.8%, of total revenue (computed from the disclosed six-month and Q1 figures) [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3; Item 2; 10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2].

**Telecom and other.** Lasers and modules for 5G networks plus a residual "other" line, about 2% of revenue [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3]. A separate fiber-to-the-home (FTTH) line was folded into "FTTH and other" from FY2024 [10-K FY2023, Note C; 10-K FY2025, Note C].

## 3. The economics

**Cost rises with every unit.** This is a factory business: cost of goods sold was 70% of FY2025 revenue (computed), and Q2 2026 costs rose with "increased direct material, direct labor and other manufacturing costs associated with increased production volumes" [10-K FY2025, Item 8; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. But because AOI owns the laser fab (the factory that makes the laser chips) and the assembly lines, "We have a high fixed cost base due to our vertically integrated business model", and gross margin is "greatly affected by our sales volume" and how well fixed overhead is absorbed [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Each extra module costs mostly materials and labor, but the margin on it depends on how full the factory is.

**Gross margin** (revenue minus the cost of making the products, as a share of revenue) ranged from 15.1% to 30.0% over five years; the 2025 rise came from "larger scale production of our CATV products, which resulted in improved manufacturing efficiencies" [10-K FY2023, Item 7; 10-K FY2025, Item 7]. In Q2 2026 GAAP gross margin slipped to 27.7%: "increased costs associated with certain data center products" cost about $5.0 million (the 10-Q gives no cause; our reading is start-up cost on new lines), and a $3.6 million discontinued-products charge took 1.9 points (computed); non-GAAP gross margin, which strips out that charge and stock pay, was 29.8% [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2; Q2 2026 release]. New products start with lower margins by design, since "our pricing is based upon anticipated volume-driven cost reductions over the life of the design win" [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Operating margin** (what is left after R&D and overhead, as a share of revenue) has been negative in each of the last five years and both 2026 quarters, because gross profit has never covered research and development (18.8% of FY2025 revenue, up 55.6% that year to support "customer testing and qualification" of 800G and 1.6T), sales and marketing (6.6%) and general and administrative (16.6%) [10-K FY2023, Item 7; 10-K FY2025, Item 7; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Stock pay is small, $11.7 million in FY2025, so the GAAP versus non-GAAP gap is narrow: a $10.3 million non-GAAP operating loss in Q2 2026 against a $24.7 million GAAP loss [10-K FY2025, Note P; Q2 2026 supplemental, p.4; Q2 2026 release]. The path to profit is volume over a fixed base, which is also the risk.

**Capital intensity.** For years it was modest: capex ran 1.4% to 4.2% of revenue in FY2021–FY2023, below depreciation and amortization (the yearly charge spreading equipment and similar costs over their useful lives) of $20–25 million a year, our rough proxy for the cost of standing still (the filings do not split maintenance from growth spending) [10-K FY2023, Item 8; 10-K FY2025, Item 8]. Then it exploded: capex, equipment prepayments and land together cost $633.4 million in the first half of 2026, 185% of six-month revenue (both computed), and management "expects to continue to make investments in capacity expansion through at least the end of 2027", mainly for lasers and 800G and 1.6T transceivers [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1; Item 2].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Qualification and design wins.** Before buying, customers "qualify" a product with sampling, reliability testing and factory audits; once designed in, "our solution is likely to continue to be purchased for that design throughout that product's life cycle because of the time and expense associated with redesigning the product" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A; Item 7]. AOI counted 6, 8 and 9 design wins in 2023, 2024 and 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Sign of weakening: a rival qualified first at a speed transition; in the filings, a named customer's share dropping as Microsoft's did from 46.6% to 28.8% [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**Its own lasers.** AOI makes "the majority of the laser chips and optical components that are used in our products", with a combination of growth processes it believes is "unique in our industry" and "difficult and time-consuming for other vendors to replicate" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. On the May call the CFO said in-house lasers had "allowed us to avoid some of the shortages that affected others in the industry" and that laser capacity was short industry-wide; the plan is to expand the fab "by around 350% by 2027" [Q1 2026 call]. Sign: laser supply loosening, or AOI buying lasers outside.

**Automation and a US address.** Management calls its automated production "Largely Location-Agnostic", letting AOI build in Texas for customers who want US-made parts, which the 10-K calls "a competitive advantage" [2026-03 investor deck, p.18; 10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Sign: the US line in the revenue-by-manufacturing-site table, 0.7% of Q2 2026 revenue (computed), failing to grow as the Texas plants open [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 17].

**Cable relationships.** Equipment vendors outsource design as well as manufacturing to AOI, which says it faces "less downward price pressure than many of our competitors in this market"; it was an early DOCSIS 4.0 developer and stretches payment terms to keep Digicomm stocked [10-K FY2025, Item 7; Item 1; 10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Sign: CATV revenue or Digicomm's share falling before the upgrade cycle ends.

**The Amazon warrant.** Amazon holds a warrant for 7,945,399 shares at $23.6956; 1,324,233 vested at grant and the rest vest as Amazon's purchases climb toward $4 billion over ten years, a financial reason to keep buying [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3]. Revenue "associated with customer arrangements that included the issuance of warrants" was $84.8 million in the first half of 2026, 24.7% of revenue (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3]. Sign: no further tranches (portions of the warrant) vesting, that is, becoming exercisable.

**The honest caveat.** Products "only minimally differentiated", cancellable purchase orders, and two former near-half customers (ATX in 2022, Microsoft in 2023) that fell below 10% and below a third within two years: what holds customers today is capacity and lasers during a shortage, not a franchise [10-K FY2025, Item 7; Item 1; 10-K FY2023, Item 1].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked from most to least plausible.

**1. One of three customers turns away.** Three customers were 42%, 26% and 24% of Q2 2026 revenue and the top ten were 99%; the 42% is Digicomm (its six-month share is 42.8% against a 43% six-month top customer, and Q2 computes to 41.8%), and the other two are not named [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2; Item 2]. Our inference, with unequal confidence: the 24% is very likely Amazon, because the 10-Q books the warrant "as a reduction of revenue from Amazon" and warrant-linked revenue of $46.8 million (computed) is 24.4% of the quarter; the 26% is only a guess at Microsoft, the one other data center customer the FY2025 10-K names (28.8%); Oracle (12.4% in FY2024) is also a candidate, since the May call's second hyperscaler would "return this customer as a 10%-plus customer" [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3; 10-K FY2025, Item 1; Note B; Q1 2026 call]. Customers "may increase, decrease, cancel or delay purchase orders already in place without significant penalty" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: the three percentages in each 10-Q's concentration note and Digicomm's share of receivables. Exposure: nearly all of it.

**2. Capacity built ahead of demand, paid for with shareholders' money.** The company's own first-listed risk: "If AI adoption slows, stalls, or follows a trajectory that differs from our planning assumptions, we could incur overcapacity, lower utilization rates, and negative operating leverage" (operating leverage: fixed costs magnifying profit swings), possibly forcing it "to impair long-lived assets, including construction-in-progress" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warnings: inventory ($278.8 million at June 30, 2026, from $183.1 million in six months) rising faster than revenue; capex continuing while data center growth slows; impairment language (a charge admitting that plant is worth less than its book value) in the property note [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 7; Note 8]. Exposure: $697 million of plant plus $324 million of prepayments.

**3. A price collapse, or a rival wins the next speed step.** Named competitors include Coherent, Eoptolink, InnoLight, Lumentum and Source Photonics, and customers "may decide to manufacture the optical subsystems incorporated into their network systems in-house" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Early warning: the data center cost drag in the 10-Q's list of what moved gross profit persisting beyond the next two quarters (our threshold; the 10-Q gives no cause); 1.6T orders not materializing. Exposure: 56% of revenue and the growth case.

**4. The cable upgrade cycle ends.** CATV revenue fell from $118.2 million to $59.9 million between 2022 and 2023 and can do so again once operators finish DOCSIS 4.0; Comcast and Charter "have announced plans to spend billions of dollars over the next few years", a finite program [10-K FY2023, Note C; 10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Early warning: CATV revenue falling quarter on quarter, Digicomm receivables shrinking. Exposure: 42% of revenue, and the products with the least price pressure.

**5. The equity window closes.** The expansion is funded by selling shares at rising prices, 7.8 million of them for $1,028.8 million net in the first half of 2026 at monthly average prices from $101.72 to $197.26, and "Should additional liquidity be needed, our Board may authorize issuance of additional common stock under an at-the-market offering in the future" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. If the share price fell sharply, capex would have to slow or debt rise; unused bank lines total $106.5 million [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Early warning: cash ($499.7 million at June 30, 2026) against $633 million of six-month capacity spending; a new share-sale agreement; the cover-page share count [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1; cover].

**6. China and tariffs.** 57.5% of FY2025 revenue and 44.2% of first-half 2026 revenue (computed) was manufactured in Ningbo, China (measured by manufacturing site, not customer location), so new tariffs are a listed risk [10-K FY2025, Item 7; Item 1A; 10-Q Q2 2026, Note 17]. In February 2026 the Supreme Court invalidated "certain tariffs previously imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act", AOI has since recovered about $5.9 million, and it now says assembly in China does "not always establish the country of origin for these products as China for U.S. tariff purposes" [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 18; Note 19; Item 2]. Early warning: the China row in the 10-Q's geographic note and the tariff line in sales and marketing.

**7. The single laser fab.** "If our MBE or MOCVD fabrication facility in Sugar Land, Texas were to be damaged or destroyed for any reason, our manufacturing process would be severely disrupted" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. No early warning exists.

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

Thompson Lin, 63, founded the company, has been President and CEO since 1997 and Chairman since January 2014; Stefan Murry, 53, joined in February 1997 as an engineer and has been CFO since August 2014 [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. The board has seven members in three classes (a third stand for election each year); lead independent director (the non-management director who leads the independent directors) William Yeh has served since 2000 and Richard Black, 92, since 2001 [DEF 14A 2026, Proposal 1; Board Leadership]. Officers and directors together own 3.8%, Lin 1.8%; the only 5% holders are Jane Street and Vanguard at 5.1% each [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership]. In late May 2026 Lin adopted plans to sell up to 80,000 shares personally and 144,000 through a family LLC [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 5].

Pay rewards growth and design wins more than profit: the 2025 bonus was weighted 25% revenue, 25% non-GAAP operating income and 50% design wins, and paid 104.8% of target with revenue and design wins at maximum while the profit measure (a non-GAAP operating loss of $11.17 million) fell short [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Long-term stock is half time-vested and half performance units judged on three-year shareholder return against peers and share-price hurdles; the 2022 units paid at the 200% maximum [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Lin's 2025 pay was $4.77 million, 469 times the median employee's, since 84.5% of employees are in China and Taiwan [DEF 14A 2026, Summary Compensation Table; CEO Pay-Ratio].

Capital allocation is one-directional: everything into capacity, funded by new shares; there have never been dividends or buybacks, and the loan agreements restrict dividends [10-K FY2025, Item 5].

## Glossary

- **Optical transceiver** — a plug-in module that converts electrical data into light for travel over fiber-optic cable and converts incoming light back into electrical data; AOI's main data center product.
- **100G / 400G / 800G / 1.6T** — transceiver speed classes, in gigabits (G) or terabits (T) per second; each step up means a new product generation and new customer qualification.
- **Laser chip** — the tiny semiconductor that produces the light inside a transceiver; AOI grows its own in Sugar Land using two crystal-growth processes (MBE and MOCVD).
- **Hyperscaler** — a very large cloud or internet company that builds its own data centers at enormous scale, such as Microsoft or Amazon.
- **CATV / MSO** — cable television networks and the operators that run them (multiple system operators, such as Comcast and Charter), who now sell broadband internet over those networks.
- **DOCSIS 4.0 / 1.8 GHz** — the newest cable-network standard and the higher frequency range it uses; upgrading to it requires new amplifiers and nodes, which is what AOI sells to cable operators.
- **Qualification / design win** — the customer's testing of a product before it will buy; a design win means the customer has said it intends to purchase.
- **GAAP / non-GAAP** — GAAP is the official accounting standard; AOI's non-GAAP figures remove stock-based pay, discontinued-product charges, and one-off items.
- **ATM program** — an at-the-market offering, in which a company sells new shares gradually into the stock market through a broker at prevailing prices.
- **Convertible notes** — debt that the holder can swap for shares at a set price; AOI has $125 million due in 2030 convertible at about $43.31.
- **Warrant** — the right to buy shares at a fixed price for a set period; Amazon's warrant vests as it buys AOI products.
- **Capex** — capital expenditure, cash spent on buildings and equipment rather than day-to-day costs.
- **Free cash flow** — operating cash flow minus capex; what is left for owners after keeping and growing the factory.
