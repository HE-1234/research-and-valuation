# American Electric Power Company, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-09._

## 1. What they do

AEP is a holding company that owns regulated electric utilities serving "more than five million retail customers" in eleven states from Michigan to Texas [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The utilities generate electricity, move it over high-voltage transmission lines and deliver it over local wires; at the end of 2025 they owned about 25,000 MW of generation, 38,000 circuit miles of transmission (including about 2,000 miles of 765-kV lines, the "backbone" of the eastern grid) and 252,000 circuit miles of distribution [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 7].

Almost all of this is a regulated monopoly: the utilities hold franchises that "effectively grant the exclusive ability to provide electric service", and state commissions (or FERC, the federal energy regulator, for transmission) set their prices [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Four segments are reported: **Vertically Integrated Utilities** (plants, wires and customers together, in ten states); **Transmission and Distribution Utilities** (AEP Ohio and AEP Texas, wires only, because Ohio and the ERCOT grid region of Texas let customers buy the electricity itself from competing suppliers); **AEP Transmission Holdco** (transmission-only companies under FERC formula rates); and **Generation & Marketing** (the small unregulated retail supplier AEP Energy and power trading) [10-K FY2025, Note 9 (Business Segments)] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The strategy is "to be a pure play, regulated, electric utility" [DEF 14A 2026, Corporate Governance].

History. AEP "was incorporated under the laws of the State of New York in 1906" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It agreed in 2021 to sell Kentucky Power, recorded a $363 million pre-tax loss on the expected sale in 2022, saw FERC deny it that December, and cancelled it in 2023; it sold its unregulated renewables in 2023 at a $93 million pre-tax loss [10-K FY2023, Note 7] [10-K FY2025, Note 7]. Its Ohio utility lobbied for Ohio House Bill 6, the 2019 law behind a federal racketeering case against an Ohio legislator; AEP says "Management does not believe that AEP was involved in any wrongful conduct", shareholder suits were dismissed (one appeal was pending when last disclosed), and an SEC investigation led AEP to book a $19 million loss contingency (an expected settlement cost) in 2024, with nothing further disclosed [10-K FY2023, Note 6] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Bill Fehrman, formerly CEO of Berkshire Hathaway Energy, became CEO in August 2024 [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. In December 2025 AEP gave Carl Icahn's group a non-voting board observer in place of two Icahn-nominated directors, then ended the arrangement on April 28, 2026 [8-K 2025-12-29, Item 1.01] [DEF 14A 2026, Related Person Transactions].

**How a regulated utility earns money.** Rates are set "on a cost-of-service basis, which is designed to allow a utility an opportunity to recover its cost of providing service and to earn a reasonable return on its investment used in providing that service" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Once an asset is in service and "found to be a prudent investment, it was then added to rate base and entitled to a return through rate recovery" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The commission sets the allowed return on the equity share of that investment (the authorized ROE) and prices bills to cover fuel, operating costs, depreciation (the yearly charge that spreads a plant's cost over its life), taxes, interest and that return [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. So profit grows mainly by investing more and getting it approved into rates; selling more kilowatt-hours matters less, because fuel is passed through at cost (§3).

The catch is **regulatory lag**: rate cases (the formal proceedings in which a commission resets prices) "have long timelines, are primarily based on historical costs", and can leave the utility "earning less than the allowed returns" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. **Riders, trackers and formula rates** shorten the lag by updating prices for specific costs or new investment without a full case [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Transmission is the cleanest version: **FERC formula rates**, refiled yearly, "establish the annual transmission revenue requirement" from current and planned investment with "a true-up calculation for the previous year's billings", and "PJM and SPP pay the transmission owners" (the regional grid operators) that amount; the filed rate base was $13.3 billion for 2025 at FERC-approved ROEs of 9.85%–10.50% [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

## 3. The economics

**Cost rises with each unit sold, but it is mostly someone else's cost.** Purchased electricity and fuel, the largest expense ($7.0 billion, 32% of 2025 revenue, computed), is largely passed through: rate riders recover them "such that the revenues and expenses associated with these items generally offset and do not affect Earnings" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. So revenue swings with fuel prices while profit does not; AEP reports no gross margin (revenue less direct costs). The rest is mostly fixed: other operation and maintenance $4.4 billion in 2025, depreciation $3.4 billion, property and other taxes $1.6 billion, interest $2.0 billion [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. Operating margin (operating profit as a share of revenue) therefore tracks approved investment, not efficiency: 19% in 2023, 24% in 2025 (computed) as rate cases and transmission additions came through [10-K FY2025, Item 8].

**Why capex is enormous.** Profit grows with rate base, so the utility spends far more than it depreciates: $8.5 billion of construction plus $3.5 billion of plant purchases in 2025 against $3.4 billion of depreciation; net plant rose from $66.0 billion at the end of 2021 to $96.7 billion at June 30, 2026 [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-K FY2022, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1]. The FY2025 10-K forecast $12.2 billion for 2026 and $59.7 billion for 2027–2030, the "$72 billion capital plan" of October 2025; the 2026 10-Qs raised that to $12.8 billion and $65.1 billion, a "$78 billion, five-year capital plan" [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. By function it is transmission $33 billion, generation $24 billion and distribution $17 billion, and management projects rate base rising from $80 billion in 2025 to $134 billion in 2030 [Q2 2026 slides, p.21].

**What drives it.** Demand. Combined retail kilowatt-hours grew 5% in 2024 and 7% in 2025 (computed), and commercial deliveries in the Ohio and Texas wires business grew 28% in 2025 (computed) on "new data processing loads coming online in 2025 in the commercial customer class" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Serving it means new generation (2.2 GW of plants bought in 2025) and new 765-kV lines awarded by the grid operators PJM, SPP and MISO [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q1 2026 release, p.2].

## 4. How profitable, really

Free cash flow is negative by design. Operating cash flow of about $4–7 billion a year has not covered construction in any of the last five years, so the gap plus the dividend is filled with new debt and new shares [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. For a growing regulated utility that is normal: each dollar invested should earn its allowed return for decades, so borrowing against it is the model. The risk is equally plain: the plan pays only if regulators approve the spending into rates at a decent return, and every share sold dilutes existing owners.

**Where the money came from.** Mostly borrowing, plus a share count that has risen every year (§7 table) [10-K FY2022, Item 8] [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1]. In 2025–2026 AEP widened the toolkit: junior subordinated debentures, long-dated bonds that rating agencies count half as equity [8-K 2025-09-25, Item 8.01] [Q2 2026 slides, p.32]; a 19.9% stake in its Ohio and Indiana Michigan transmission companies sold to KKR and PSP funds [10-K FY2025, Note 7]; an at-the-market program selling new shares over time [8-K 2025-11-25, Item 8.01]; and a May 2026 forward sale, price fixed at $124.968 now, shares delivered for cash by May 2028; with the 2025 forwards, 45 million shares (about 8% of the count, computed) were still to be issued at June 30, 2026 [8-K 2026-05-14, Item 1.01] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 12]. In management's five-year plan, operating cash covers about half of capital spending plus dividends; new debt supplies most of the rest, about three and a half dollars per dollar of new shares (computed from the plan's rows) [Q2 2026 slides, p.24].

**Return on capital in plain words.** Each dollar of net plant earned about 5.8 cents of operating profit in 2025 and 5.2 cents in 2024 (computed), before interest and tax [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. AEP's own measure, the regulated earned ROE, was 9.2% for the twelve months to June 2026, against authorized ROEs of 9.25%–10.5% [Q2 2026 slides, p.28] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The 2025 bonus scorecard's "Plan ROE" target was 9.375%; actual was 9.060%, scoring zero [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Because the model runs on borrowing, AEP targets FFO/debt of 14%–15% and reported 14.6% (S&P basis) and 14.0% (Moody's basis) at June 30, 2026, against a 13% downgrade threshold; the filings state neither ratings nor a target [Q2 2026 slides, p.31] [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**One-off item to read past.** In June 2025 FERC accepted AEP's treatment of tax loss carryforwards in transmission rates, adding $499 million to Q2 2025 GAAP earnings (GAAP: the official accounting rules); management excludes the $480 million (about $0.90 a share) that relates to 2021–2024 from "operating earnings", its non-GAAP measure [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q2 2026 release, p.7]. So GAAP earnings fell from $1,226 million in Q2 2025 to $713 million in Q2 2026 while operating earnings slipped only from $766 million to $742 million, and FY2025 GAAP EPS (earnings per share) of $6.70 sits above operating EPS of $5.97 [Q2 2026 release, p.3] [Q4 2025 release, p.1].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Retail customers cannot leave; the customer to keep is the regulator.** Franchises give the integrated utilities "the exclusive ability to provide electric service"; the only competition named is "self-generation" and other fuels [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The moat is legal, and its price is that eleven state commissions and FERC decide what AEP earns. The 2026 orders show the range: West Virginia raised Appalachian Power's ROE on reconsideration from 9.25% to 9.75%, while Kentucky cut a settlement by $22 million and Oklahoma staff argued for as little as 8.3% [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4].

**Large customers are locked in by contract.** New data-center tariffs (approved price schedules) carry "contract lengths of up to 20 years and take-or-pay contractual minimums which can require a customer to pay for as much as 80-90% of their contracted demand", plus deposits; five of eight jurisdictions had approved them by June 2026 [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Management's pitch: "growth helps pay for growth", with "up to $16 billion in expected cost offsets for residential customers" over the life of signed agreements [Q2 2026 release, p.2] [Q2 2026 release, p.1].

**Signs of weakening.** Earned ROE drifting down from 9.2% instead of toward management's 9.5% target; rate orders cutting ROEs; disallowances (costs regulators refuse to put into rates) growing beyond 2026's small ones (Pirkey plant $31 million; Texas tracker $22 million in the release, $23 million pretax in the 10-Q); FERC reopening transmission ROEs [Q2 2026 slides, p.28] [Q2 2026 release, p.9] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. Data-center load arrives late, smaller, or not at all.** The 10-K says AEP's plans "depend, in part, on the continued growth and viability of data centers and large load customers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Management counts 69 GW of contracted load additions by 2030, 90% data centers, 45 GW of it in Texas, where the only instrument is a letter of agreement rather than a full service contract and ERCOT's one-time "Batch Zero" review decides in 2026–2027 which projects get capacity and when; AEP Texas's peak today is about 8 GW [Q2 2026 slides, p.14] [Q2 2026 slides, p.15] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Early warning: contracted GW falling; ERCOT rejecting AEP Texas projects; commercial kWh growth in the wires business fading from 28% toward single digits [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Exposure: the $72 billion plan was set when AEP Texas's signed load was 13 GW, so most Texas load is upside not yet in the plan, but 13 GW of turbines already secured could sit unrecovered [Q4 2025 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

**2. Regulators refuse or delay recovery.** "The increase in spending could trigger increased regulatory scrutiny to authorizing cost recovery", and "Customer affordability considerations may impact regulatory recovery outcomes and future rate design" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-Q Q2 2026, Forward-Looking Information]. Live examples: Kentucky reclassified $47 million of capitalized costs, and Texas staff proposed a 9.6% ROE against SWEPCo's (Southwestern Electric Power's) 10.75% request [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4]. Early warning: approved ROEs below 9.5%; earned ROE below 9%; disallowance items growing in the release's reconciliation. Exposure: essentially all of the profit.

**3. The balance sheet cannot carry the plan.** Debt was 61.4% of capital at June 30, 2026, and FFO/debt sits about one point above the 13% downgrade line [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 slides, p.31]. The 10-K admits its metrics "have approached, and may in the future approach, thresholds designated by the credit rating agencies for potential ratings downgrades" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. A downgrade raises the cost of $45.5 billion of planned borrowing; the alternative is more equity [Q2 2026 slides, p.24]. Early warning: FFO/debt below 14%; a negative agency outlook; equity beyond the plan's $1 billion a year of at-the-market sales plus the forwards.

**4. FERC changes the transmission deal.** More than half of 2026 operating earnings are expected from transmission [Q2 2026 slides, p.5]. "If the FERC were to adopt a different policy ... AEP's strategy of investing in transmission could be impacted", and its formula rates "have been challenged, which could result in lowered rates and/or refunds" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. The $480 million tax ruling itself is under appeal [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4]. Early warning: a FERC inquiry into transmission ROEs or an adverse ruling.

**5. PJM's rules shift costs onto AEP.** The 10-K warns that capacity-market reforms launched by a January 2026 Statement of Principles "have the potential to materially impact AEP's competitive retail operations and could materially alter OPCo's cost allocations to retail customers" (OPCo is AEP Ohio) [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. In July 2026 the PJM Board approved a "Reliability Backstop Procurement mechanism", an "Interim Resource Adequacy Service" that AEP's distribution utilities "would be required to administer" from June 1, 2027, and provisions on a transmission owner's withdrawal from PJM [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. The CEO's tone swung from "does not give me great confidence" in Q1 2026 to "very optimistic" in Q2 [Q1 2026 call, prepared remarks, Fehrman] [Q2 2026 call, Q&A, Truist Securities]. Early warning: AEP proposing to leave PJM.

**6. Physical and fuel risks.** About 10,200 MW of a 26,500 MW fleet is coal, exposed to rules that "could force AEP to close additional coal-fired generation facilities earlier than their estimated useful life", and storm costs "may not be recoverable" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: storm costs "not earning a return" rising above June 2026's $300 million [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4].

**Concentration.** Two Texas retail electric providers, NRG Energy and Vistra, were 38% of AEP Texas's 2025 revenue (about 4% of consolidated, computed); none of the other five registrant utilities has a customer above 10% [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. The real concentration is of future growth: 90% of the 69 GW pipeline is data centers, mostly "hyperscale customers" (the largest cloud companies), yet the filings name no customer, give no MW per customer and no contracted total outside Texas, and the deck says "AEP load data are not publicly disclosed for most projects" [Q2 2026 slides, p.14] [Q1 2026 slides, p.8] [Q2 2026 slides, p.18].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Bill Fehrman, 65, President and CEO since August 2024 and Chair since August 2025, ran Berkshire Hathaway Energy from 2018 to 2023 [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. He is the third CEO since 2022, after Julia Sloat and interim chief Ben Fowke [DEF 14A 2026, Pay Versus Performance]. CFO Trevor Mihalik came from Sempra in January 2025, and most of the executive team arrived in 2025, several from Berkshire Hathaway Energy [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The board shrank from twelve to ten in April 2026 and added two directors in July, including Equinix's executive chairman Charles Meyers [DEF 14A 2026, Item 1] [8-K 2026-07-21, Item 5.02].

**Pay.** The annual bonus scores six equally weighted principles; in 2025 every measure except "Plan ROE" scored above target, the total was 162.5% of target, and operating EPS of $5.97 beat a $5.90 target [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Fehrman's 2025 pay was $36.6 million, including a $15 million retention award that vests only if he stays to December 31, 2030 [DEF 14A 2026, Summary Compensation Table] [8-K 2025-12-19, Item 5.02]. Say-on-pay (the advisory shareholder vote on executive pay) support fell from about 96% in 2025 to 82% in 2026 (computed); insiders own under 1% [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A] [8-K 2026-04-29, Item 5.07] [DEF 14A 2026, Share Ownership].

**Cash.** All operating cash and more goes into the grid, so the dividend is in effect funded by new financing (§4). It has risen every year, from $3.00 a share in 2021 to $3.74 in 2025 (the "462nd consecutive quarterly dividend" came in October 2025), with a payout target of 50%–60% of operating earnings [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Summary] [Q2 2026 slides, p.24]. No buybacks. Beyond regulated plants, two unregulated bets: $150 million for 15% of Gigawatt AI, and a $2.65 billion fuel-cell purchase for a Wyoming project with an unnamed "high investment grade third party customer" (one with a strong credit rating) under a 20-year contract [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 13] [8-K 2026-01-08, Item 8.01].

## Glossary

- **Rate base:** the value of the utility's investment in plant and equipment that regulators have accepted as prudent, on which it is allowed to earn a return.
- **Authorized (allowed) ROE:** the percentage return on the equity share of rate base that a commission sets in a rate order; earned ROE is what the utility actually achieves.
- **Regulatory lag:** the time between spending money and getting it into customer rates, during which the utility earns less than its allowed return.
- **Rider / tracker / formula rate:** mechanisms that adjust prices for specific costs or new investment between full rate cases; FERC's transmission formula rates reset every year with a true-up.
- **FFO/debt:** funds from operations (cash earnings before working-capital swings) divided by adjusted debt, the ratio the rating agencies use to judge a utility's credit.
- **Take-or-pay minimum:** a contract term under which a large customer pays for a set share of its contracted capacity whether or not it uses it.
