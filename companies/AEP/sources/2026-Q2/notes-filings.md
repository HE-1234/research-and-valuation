**Quirks of AEP's filings a writer must know**
- The 10-K and 10-Q are COMBINED filings for eight registrants: AEP plus AEP Texas, AEP Transmission Company (AEPTCo), Appalachian Power (APCo), Indiana Michigan Power (I&M), Ohio Power (OPCo), Public Service Company of Oklahoma (PSO) and Southwestern Electric Power (SWEPCo). Every note table has an AEP column and subsidiary columns; only the AEP consolidated column is cited here unless a subsidiary figure is itself the point (e.g., a rate case). The AEP-level statements are titled "AMERICAN ELECTRIC POWER COMPANY, INC. AND SUBSIDIARY COMPANIES". [10-K FY2025, Item 8]
- Segment profit measure: the FY2025 10-K and the 2026 10-Qs report segment "Earnings (Loss) Attributable to AEP Common Shareholders" (after noncontrolling interests). The FY2023 10-K reported segment "Net Income (Loss)" (before noncontrolling interests). The two agree to within the small noncontrolling-interest amounts for FY2023 (e.g., VIU 1,093.9 vs 1,090). Note numbering also differs by filing: Rate Matters is Note 4 in every filing, but Commitments is Note 6 (10-K FY2025), Note 5 (10-Qs) and Note 6 (10-K FY2023); Business Segments is Note 9 (both 10-Ks) and Note 8 (10-Qs); Financing Activities is Note 15 (10-K FY2025), Note 14 (10-K FY2023) and Note 12 (10-Q Q2 2026); Revenue from Contracts is Note 20 (10-K FY2025), Note 19 (10-K FY2023) and Note 15 (10-Q Q2 2026). Tags below use each filing's own numbering.
- The FY2023 10-K reports to one decimal place (e.g., 18,982.3); the FY2025 10-K rounds to whole millions (18,982). Both are quoted as printed.
- Segment revenues appear in two forms: the income statement's segment revenue lines are revenues from EXTERNAL customers (e.g., VIU 12,556 in 2025), while the Business Segments note's "Total Revenues" include intersegment sales (VIU 12,819 in 2025). Both are given below and labelled.
- The "Customer Demand" table of percentage changes in sales volume by customer class is an IMAGE in both the 10-K FY2025 and the 10-Q Q2 2026 (only its footnotes survive in the text). The volume figures below therefore come from the "Summary of KWh Energy Sales" tables, and percentage changes are computed.
- The FY2025 10-K's "Budgeted Capital Expenditures" table gives 2026 by segment and category and 2027–2030 only as a total per segment; the 10-Qs give 2026–2030 totals by segment only.
- Fuel and purchased-power costs are largely pass-through: "the results include revenues from rate rider mechanisms designed to recover fuel, purchased power and other recoverable expenses such that the revenues and expenses associated with these items generally offset and do not affect Earnings Attributable to AEP Common Shareholders." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]

## 1. Business description (company's own words)

### 1a. What AEP is
- "AEP was incorporated under the laws of the State of New York in 1906 and reorganized in 1925. It is a public utility holding company that directly owns all of the outstanding common stock of the public utility subsidiaries identified below." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- "AEP is one of the largest investor-owned electric public utility holding companies in the United States. AEP's electric utility operating companies provide generation, transmission and distribution services to more than five million retail customers in Arkansas, Indiana, Kentucky, Louisiana, Michigan, Ohio, Oklahoma, Tennessee, Texas, Virginia and West Virginia." [10-K FY2025, Item 7, Executive Overview]
- Asset portfolio: "Approximately 252,000 circuit miles of distribution lines. Approximately 38,000 circuit miles of transmission lines, including approximately 2,000 circuit miles of 765 kV lines. Approximately 25,000 MWs of regulated owned generating capacity as of December 31, 2025." [10-K FY2025, Item 7, Executive Overview]
- The FY2023 10-K gave: "Approximately 225,000 circuit miles of distribution lines that deliver electricity to 5.6 million customers. Approximately 40,000 circuit miles of transmission lines, including approximately 2,200 circuit miles of 765 kV lines, the backbone of the electric interconnection grid in the eastern United States. Approximately 23,000 MWs of regulated owned generating capacity as of December 31, 2023, one of the largest complements of generation in the United States." [10-K FY2023, Item 7, Executive Overview] (Note the transmission mileage is stated as 40,000 in FY2023 and 38,000 in FY2025; the proxy still says 40,000, see below.)
- Proxy statement of strategy: "AEP's long-term strategy is to be a pure play, regulated, electric utility focused on investment in infrastructure and energy solutions that customers want and need. We are committed to improving our customers' lives with reliable, affordable power. ... We operate and maintain the nation's largest electricity transmission system with 40,000 line circuit miles and more than 252,000 line circuit miles of distribution to efficiently deliver safe, reliable power to nearly 5.6 million regulated customers in 11 states. [DEF 14A 2026, Corporate Governance – Linking Business Strategy with Key Skills]

### 1d. How rate regulation works (10-K's own explanation)
- Cost-of-service: "Historically, state utility commissions have established electric service rates on a cost-of-service basis, which is designed to allow a utility an opportunity to recover its cost of providing service and to earn a reasonable return on its investment used in providing that service. A utility's cost of service generally reflects its operating expenses, including operation and maintenance expense, depreciation expense and taxes. State utility commissions periodically adjust rates pursuant to a review of: (a) a utility's adjusted revenues and expenses during a defined test period and (b) such utility's level of investment." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, Vertically Integrated Utilities – Regulation – Rates]
- Rate base and regulatory lag: "Public utilities have traditionally financed capital investments until the new asset is placed in-service. Provided the asset was found to be a prudent investment, it was then added to rate base and entitled to a return through rate recovery. Given long lead times in construction, the high costs of plant and equipment and volatile capital markets, management actively pursues strategies to accelerate rate recognition of investments and cash flow. AEP representatives continue to engage state commissioners and legislators on alternative ratemaking options to reduce regulatory lag and enhance certainty in the process. These options include pre-approvals, a return on construction work in progress, rider/trackers, formula rates and the inclusion of future test-year projections into rates." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Fuel clauses: "Fuel adjustment clauses permit periodic adjustments to fuel cost recovery from customers and therefore provide protection against exposure to fuel cost changes." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- State-by-state basis (verbatim key phrases): Arkansas – "historical cost-of-service basis and formula rates"; Indiana – "fully bundled rates ... set on a forecasted cost-of-service basis ... I&M is subject to a semi-annual Indiana jurisdictional earnings test"; Kentucky – "historical cost-of-service basis"; Louisiana – "historical cost-of-service basis and formula rates"; Michigan – "unbundled standard service and open access distribution service rates ... forecasted cost-of-service basis. Open access distribution service is limited to 10% of I&M's retail load"; Oklahoma – "historical cost-of-service basis. Fuel and purchased energy costs are recovered through a fuel adjustment clause"; Tennessee – "historical cost-of-service basis"; Texas (SWEPCo) – "bundled rates approved by the PUCT with rates set on a historical cost-of-service basis"; Virginia – "unbundled generation and distribution rates ... historical cost-of-service basis ... APCo is subject to a biennial Virginia retail generation and distribution earnings test"; West Virginia – "combined APCo and WPCo historical cost-of-service basis ... single surcharge mechanism" for fuel, purchased power and transmission. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- FERC transmission formula rates (State Transcos): "The State Transcos and the Transmission Joint Ventures located outside of ERCOT establish transmission rates annually through forward-looking formula rate filings with the FERC pursuant to FERC-approved implementation protocols." "The FERC-approved formula rates establish the annual transmission revenue requirement (ATRR) and transmission service rates for transmission owners in annual rate base filings with the FERC. The formula rates establish rates for a one-year period based on the current projects in-service and proposed projects for a defined timeframe. The formula rates also include a true-up calculation for the previous year's billings, allowing for over/under-recovery of the transmission owner's ATRR. PJM and SPP pay the transmission owners their ATRR for use of their facilities and bill transmission customers ..." "In the annual formula rate filings described above, the State Transcos in aggregate filed formula rate base totals of $13.3 billion, $11.4 billion and $10.7 billion for 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively. The total filed transmission revenue requirements, including prior year over/under-recovery of revenue and associated carrying charges were $2.1 billion, $1.9 billion and $1.8 billion for 2025, 2024 and 2023, respectively." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, AEP Transmission Holdco – Regulation]
- "Management monitors pending matters before the FERC, including inquiries and challenges related to ROEs and transmission formula rates, that have the potential to reduce AEP's future transmission formula rates and/or the transmission ROE methodology." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- ERCOT (AEP Texas): "Prior to the passage of Texas House Bill 5247 (HB 5247) in June 2025, AEP Texas set rates through a combination of base rate cases and interim Transmission Cost of Services (TCOS) and Distribution Cost Recovery Factor (DCRF) semi-annual filings which update rates to reflect changes in net invested capital. In October 2025, AEP Texas submitted its first annual interim rate adjustment filing with the PUCT seeking recovery of eligible costs through the UTM established by HB 5247." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, Transmission and Distribution Utilities – Regulation]
- Securitization is listed among long-term funding sources: "Sources of long-term funding include issuance of long-term debt, long-term asset securitizations, leasing agreements, hybrid securities or common stock." [10-K FY2025, Item 1, Financing]
- Franchises: "Other than AEGCo, the Vertically Integrated Utilities hold franchises or other rights that effectively grant the exclusive ability to provide electric service in various municipalities and regions in their service areas." Competition: "The Vertically Integrated Utilities compete with self-generation and with distributors of other energy sources, such as natural gas, fuel oil, renewables and coal, within their service areas." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- PJM capacity: "Utilities in PJM can meet their capacity obligations by either: (a) participating in capacity auctions administered by PJM, or (b) via the Fixed Resource Requirement alternative (FRR) in which load-serving entities self-supply their generation through owned or contracted resources. All AEP East Companies other than AEP Ohio utilize the FRR alternative." [10-K FY2025, Item 7, PJM Capacity Market Reform]

### 1e. Segment descriptions (Business Segments note)
- "AEP's primary business is the generation, transmission and distribution of electricity. Within its Vertically Integrated Utilities segment, AEP centrally dispatches generation assets and manages its overall utility operations on an integrated basis because of the substantial impact of cost-based rates and regulatory oversight applicable to each public utility subsidiary. Intersegment sales and transfers are generally based on underlying contractual arrangements and agreements." [10-K FY2025, Note 9 (Business Segments)]
- The four reportable segments, exactly as named in the filing: **Vertically Integrated Utilities**; **Transmission and Distribution Utilities**; **AEP Transmission Holdco**; **Generation & Marketing**. "The remainder of AEP's activities are presented as Corporate and Other, which is not considered a reportable segment." [10-K FY2025, Note 9]
- Vertically Integrated Utilities: "Generation, transmission and distribution of electricity for sale to retail and wholesale customers through assets owned and operated by AEGCo, APCo, I&M, KGPCo, KPCo, PSO, SWEPCo and WPCo." [10-K FY2025, Note 9]
- Transmission and Distribution Utilities: "Transmission and distribution of electricity for sale to retail and wholesale customers through assets owned and operated by AEP Texas and OPCo. OPCo purchases energy and capacity to serve standard service offer customers and provides transmission and distribution services for all connected load." [10-K FY2025, Note 9]
- AEP Transmission Holdco: "Development, construction and operation of transmission facilities through investments in AEPTCo. These investments have FERC-approved ROEs. Development, construction and operation of transmission facilities through investments in AEP's transmission-only joint ventures. These investments have PUCT-approved or FERC-approved ROEs." [10-K FY2025, Note 9]
- Generation & Marketing: "Marketing, risk management and retail activities in ERCOT, MISO, PJM and SPP. Competitive generation in PJM." [10-K FY2025, Note 9] Item 1 adds: "Generation & Marketing focuses primarily on a retail supply business and a wholesale energy trading and marketing business ... The segment also includes rights to Cardinal Plant Unit 1's power and capacity through 2028 pursuant to a PPA with a nonaffiliated electric cooperative. Generation & Marketing previously included AEP OnSite Partners prior to its sale in September 2024 and AEP Renewables prior to its sale in August 2023." "The retail energy supply business, AEP Energy, provides electricity and/or natural gas to residential, commercial and industrial customers in Illinois, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland, New Jersey, Ohio and Washington, D.C. AEP Energy had approximately 922,342 customer accounts as of December 31, 2025." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Corporate and Other "primarily includes the purchasing of receivables from certain AEP utility subsidiaries, Parent's guarantee revenue received from affiliates, investment income, interest income and interest expense, income tax expense and other nonallocated costs." [10-K FY2025, Note 9]
- CODM: "The CODM of AEP is the President and CEO of AEP ... The CODM uses earnings (loss) attributable to AEP common shareholders (presented on a GAAP basis) as a measure of segment profit or loss in making these decisions." [10-K FY2025, Note 9]
- AEP Transmission Holdco detail: "As of December 31, 2025, the State Transcos had $17.1 billion of transmission and other assets in-service, excluding CWIP, with plans to construct approximately $11.6 billion of additional transmission assets through 2030." State Transcos "are independent of, but respectively overlay" APCo, I&M, KPCo, OPCo, PSO, SWEPCo and WPCo; "SWTCo does not currently own or operate transmission assets." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Transmission joint ventures (AEPTHCo ownership %, in-service net PP&E 12/31/2025, approved ROE): ETT (Texas/ERCOT) 50%, $3,744, 9.6% ("ETT's investment in completed and active projects in ERCOT is expected to be $5.6 billion by 2030"); Midcontinent Grid Solutions, LLC (Wisconsin, 2034) 50%, projects ~$1.2 billion, 10.5%; Prairie Wind Transmission (Kansas, 2014) 25%, $121, 12.8%; Pioneer Transmission (Indiana, 2018) 50%, $177, 10.5%; Transource Energy (MO, WV, MD, OK, PA) 86.5%, $496, 10.3%–11.3% (consolidated); Valley Link Transmission (MD, VA, WV, 2029) 31.1%, projects ~$3.0 billion, 11.4% (base ROE "subject to ongoing settlement and hearing procedures"). [10-K FY2025, Item 1, AEPTHCo Joint Venture Initiatives]
- Seasonality: "In many parts of the country, demand for power peaks during the hot summer months ... In some areas, power demand peaks during the cold winter months ... AEP has historically sold and delivered less power, and consequently earned less income, when weather conditions are milder." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- OVEC: "AEP and several nonaffiliated utility companies jointly own OVEC. The aggregate equity participation of AEP in OVEC is 43.47%. Parent owns 39.17% and OPCo owns 4.3%. Under the Inter-Company Power Agreement (ICPA) ... the sponsoring companies are entitled to receive and are obligated to pay for all OVEC capacity (approximately 2,400 MWs) in proportion to their respective power participation ratios. The aggregate power participation ratio of APCo, I&M and OPCo is 43.47%. The ICPA terminates in June 2040." [10-K FY2025, Item 1] "In June 2026, OPCo completed the transfer of its 4.3% ownership in OVEC to Parent and its 19.93% OVEC power participation entitlement to AGR. ## 9. Specific (non-boilerplate) risk factors — 10-K FY2025 Item 1A, quoted
- Capital recovery: "AEP may not be able to recover the costs of substantial planned investment in capital improvements and additions ... If these regulatory commissions do not approve adjustments to the rates charged, affected AEP subsidiaries would not be able to recover the costs associated with their investments."
- Data-center dependence (new, headline risk): "The business and capital investment plans of AEP depend, in part, on the continued growth and viability of data centers and large load customers interconnecting with the AEP System ... AEP is experiencing current and projected load demands that exceed historical experience, creating a business need for new power generating resources and transmission facilities. Much of this demand is driven by interconnecting with and providing power to data centers and other large load customers to serve an increasingly digital economy and to support AI ... If these increased demands for electricity do not occur as projected or are not sustained as projected, for any reason, it could affect AEP's financial condition."
- Execution and financing scale: "Meeting the significant increase in electricity demand from new data centers and other large-load customers will require substantial investment in new generation and transmission facilities. These projects may require levels of capital that exceed historical utility financing needs, and the ability of the capital markets to supply sufficient funding for large-scale infrastructure expansion is uncertain ... if any of these projects are canceled for any reason, including shifts in large customer needs, preferences or financial stability ... it could result in significant unrecoverable costs."
- Rate regulation: "If regulated utility earnings exceed the return established by a relevant commission, that commission could reduce future rates"; "The increase in spending could trigger increased regulatory scrutiny to authorizing cost recovery, especially in a rising cost environment, whether due to inflation, tariffs, high fuel prices or otherwise." Prudency: "Regulators may disallow costs found not to have been prudently incurred." Regulatory lag: "The length of these base rate proceedings can cause the regulated utility businesses to experience regulatory lag in recovering costs and result in earning less than the allowed returns."
- Transmission policy and formula rates: "A significant portion of AEP's earnings is derived from transmission investments and activities. FERC policy currently supports the expansion and updating of the transmission infrastructure ... If the FERC were to adopt a different policy ... AEP's strategy of investing in transmission could be impacted." "Certain elements of AEP's transmission formula rates have been challenged, which could result in lowered rates and/or refunds of amounts previously collected ... Inquiries related to rates of return, as well as challenges to the formula rates of other utilities, are ongoing in other proceedings at the FERC."
- RTO/PJM: "The rules governing the various RTOs, including SPP and PJM, may also change from time to time which could affect costs or revenues ... the allocation among existing members, of losses caused by unreimbursed defaults of other participants in these markets." (MD&A adds that PJM reforms "have the potential to materially impact AEP's competitive retail operations and could materially alter OPCo's cost allocations to retail customers.")
- Nuclear: "I&M owns the Cook Plant ... about a tenth of the regulated generating capacity in the AEP System ... Costs associated with the operation (including fuel), maintenance and retirement of nuclear plants continue to be more significant and less predictable than costs associated with other sources of generation."
- REP concentration (AEP Texas): "As of December 31, 2025, AEP Texas did business with approximately 146 REPs ... In 2025, AEP Texas' two largest REPs accounted for 38% of its operating revenue."
- Cyber and physical security: "The increased implementation of, and reliance on, information technologies and networks ... create additional points of vulnerability that could be, and in certain instances have been, exploited by malicious threat actors." "AEP has not identified any cybersecurity incidents that have materially affected or are reasonably likely to materially affect its business strategy, results of operation or financial condition."
- Tax law: "At the federal level, management is monitoring the potential for changes in current tax policy, including tax rates, tax credits and incentives."
- Trade policy/tariffs: "Executive actions have been taken and additional measures proposed that are intended to alter the U.S. approach to international trade policy ... could result in additional increases in the cost of certain goods, services and cost of capital and exacerbate supply chain issues."
- Capital access and ratings: "AEP relies on access to capital markets as a significant source of liquidity for capital requirements not satisfied by operating cash flows ... As of December 31, 2025, approximately 8%, 23% and 15% of the Registrants' available credit facilities were with European, Canadian, and Asian banks, respectively." "In the past, certain sources of insurance and debt and equity capital have expressed unwillingness to provide insurance for or to invest in companies, such as AEP, that rely on fossil fuels." "Downgrades in AEP's credit ratings could negatively affect its ability to access capital ... From time to time, AEP's financial metrics have approached, and may in the future approach, thresholds designated by the credit rating agencies for potential ratings downgrades."
- Shareholder activism (new): "Shareholder activism, which can take many forms and arise in a variety of situations, could result in substantial costs and divert management's and the AEP Board's attention and resources from AEP's business."
- Holding-company structure: "AEP and AEPTCo are holding companies and have no operations of their own ... AEP and AEPTCo indebtedness and dividends are structurally subordinated to all subsidiary indebtedness."
- Pension and decommissioning trust market risk; supply chain, tariffs and inflation ("The current administration has implemented tariffs on certain imported goods and may impose additional tariffs"); load growth/recession; workforce ("Advancements in artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies may require significant changes to the size, skills, and composition of AEP's workforce"); leadership continuity ("gaps in preparing or transitioning individuals into critical roles may impact performance").
- Fuel and purchased power: "While AEP typically recovers such fuel-related expenses pursuant to rate recovery mechanisms in regulated jurisdictions, the failure to recover these costs could reduce future net income."
