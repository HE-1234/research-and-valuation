# AVGO valuation evidence — mature-business benchmarks

_Prepared 2026-09-18 for the 2026-06-09 cutoff. Dollar amounts are USD millions. These are historical operating comparisons, not valuation results. All calculations below are this gatherer's arithmetic from the cited primary filings._

## Selection and boundaries

- **Qualcomm:** a mature fabless chip designer, with recurring engineering expense, outsourced production, customer concentration and customer self-design risk; its handset exposure and separate patent-licensing profits make it an imperfect analogue for Broadcom's eventual custom-compute mix. In particular, Qualcomm says Apple began using its own modem and expects further substitution to hurt its chip business: selection therefore includes a mature franchise confronting erosion, rather than only expanding winners. [QCOM FY2025 10-K, Items 1 and 7; Note 8]
- **Cisco:** mature networking hardware, software and services, with acquisition accounting and mixed growth; its finished systems include components purchased from chip suppliers, so its revenue is a different level of the supply chain. FY2025 software revenue was $22.3 billion, including a full year of Splunk versus about four months in FY2024; its acquisition-heavy capital base and lower margins are useful counterweights to unusually successful software companies. [CSCO FY2025 10-K, Items 1 and 7; Note 4]
- **Microsoft Productivity and Business Processes (PBP):** a mature software/distribution economics comparison, not a direct VMware competitor or a clean pure-license business; it includes Microsoft 365, LinkedIn and Dynamics. Segment margins are useful, but no segment invested-capital denominator is disclosed; Microsoft consolidated capital is shown only as a separate context for the same company, with its much more capital-intensive cloud infrastructure. [MSFT FY2025 10-K, Note 18]
- **Broadcom's own FY2023–FY2025 record:** the closest product/business evidence, but VMware makes growth and capital discontinuous. It is a fourth benchmark observation, not an independent peer. The benchmark selection intentionally excludes Marvell as a mature destination because its current growth and acquisition transition resemble the uncertainty being modeled; no post-cutoff Marvell materials were used. [AVGO FY2025 10-K, Notes 4 and 13; benchmark-selection judgment]

## Capital and returns: definitions before comparison

For a reproducible **book-capital screen**, opening/closing capital is common book equity + reported current and noncurrent interest-bearing debt + finance leases not already in debt − cash/equivalents − current marketable investments. Goodwill and acquired intangibles remain in capital. Operating leases stay in operating expenses and are not added to debt. No internally generated R&D asset is invented. Noncurrent investments remain in this deliberately simple screen: it is not an assertion that every dollar is operating capital. Qualcomm's FY2025 restricted cash also remains in capital; its cash was restricted for the pending Alphawave acquisition. [QCOM FY2025 10-K, balance sheets and Notes 2 and 9; CSCO FY2025 10-K, balance sheets; MSFT FY2025 10-K, balance sheets and Note 13; AVGO FY2025 10-K, balance sheets and Note 10; calculation convention]

Return uses **FY2025 EBIT × (1 − tax rate) / opening FY2025 capital**. The reported-tax column applies each filing's consolidated effective tax rate; it is computed, not company-reported ROIC, and can be distorted by nonoperating tax items. The 21% column instead applies a uniform **analytical assumption**, not an estimate of anyone's actual or future tax rate. Ending sales/capital is an accumulated-capital ratio; it is not incremental revenue divided by new investment. [Same filings; calculation convention]

Qualcomm's FY2025 tax charge includes a $5.7 billion deferred-tax valuation allowance, while its FY2024 tax provision was only $226 million on $10,336 million of pretax income; either year's tax rate would be a poor permanent operating-tax assumption. Broadcom's FY2025 tax benefit likewise makes the reported-tax return an unsuitable terminal target. [QCOM FY2025 10-K, Note 3; AVGO FY2025 10-K, statements of operations and Note 12]

The current book value of internally generated research is zero under the chosen accounting convention even though the engineering work can have future benefits; this tends to make reported capital returns look higher than returns on all economic investment. Conversely, purchased goodwill stays in the denominator. Acquisition-heavy companies can therefore show much lower book returns than otherwise similar companies that developed their products internally. None of these four returns measures the return on Broadcom's next dollar of investment. [QCOM FY2025 10-K, Note 1; CSCO FY2025 10-K, Note 2; MSFT FY2025 10-K, Note 1; AVGO FY2025 10-K, Note 2; accounting interpretation]

## What the evidence can and cannot support

- **Analyst interpretation:** a future Broadcom consolidated margin above Qualcomm/Cisco needs retained differentiated IP, a substantial software profit contribution, tight cost control and no matching collapse in customer bargaining power. Its own amortization sensitivity provides a better bridge toward a margin above current GAAP than adding back SBC. Microsoft's mature software margin offers evidence that high recurring software profitability can exist; its segment mix, distribution advantages and allocated-cost definitions do not establish Broadcom's exact sustainable percentage. [Benchmark tables; interpretation]
- **Analyst interpretation:** Broadcom's uniform-tax historical return is approximately 16%, or approximately 21% before acquired amortization; Cisco's corresponding range is approximately 16–19%. Qualcomm and Microsoft show returns in the mid-to-high 30s on this book basis, but both omit the stock of internally created research, and Qualcomm includes patent royalties. A central mature return in the teens to low 20s has closer direct accounting support than a permanent mid-30s return. This is a qualitative placement range, not a prescribed terminal input, and it must be tested against the future mix and chosen tax rate. [Capital-return and sensitivity tables; interpretation]
- **Analyst interpretation:** terminal returns on future investment must be argued separately. A finite AI design win is insufficient by itself; a lasting premium needs repeated design wins, recurring software retention, networking IP, continued research and limits to buyer substitution. Qualcomm's customer self-design warning and Cisco's declining pre-acquisition-period revenue provide concrete adverse analogues. [QCOM FY2025 10-K, Item 7; CSCO FY2025 10-K, statements; interpretation]
- **Demand-bound gap:** the April 6, 2026 Google agreement covers future TPU generations/network components through up to 2031, and Anthropic's approximately 3.5 GW deployment begins in 2027, dependent on its commercial success. The filing supplies neither Broadcom revenue per GW nor dollar purchase obligations. GW must not be converted to revenue using an invented chip price, and the arrangement is not evidence of perpetual demand. [AVGO Google/Anthropic 8-K, Item 8.01]
- **Unresolved:** none of these sources supports an exact 2036 AI market size or Broadcom share; use the separately gathered pre-cutoff industry demand evidence and explicitly labeled growth assumptions. PBP standalone capital/ROIC and Qualcomm QCT standalone capital/ROIC remain unusable because segment capital is not disclosed. These missing peer segment metrics do not require guessing: consolidated screens and Broadcom's own consistent record provide the documented substitute. [MSFT FY2025 10-K, Note 18; QCOM FY2025 10-K, Note 8; evidence limitation]
