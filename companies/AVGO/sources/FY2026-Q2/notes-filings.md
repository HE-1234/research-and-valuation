## 1. Business description (company's own words)

- Overview: "We are a global technology leader that designs, develops and supplies a broad range of semiconductor and semiconductor-based solutions and infrastructure software solutions. Our more than 60-year history of innovation dates back to our diverse origins from AT&T/Bell Labs, Lucent and Hewlett-Packard Company, and has evolved through acquisitions, including LSI Corporation, Broadcom Corporation, Brocade Communications Systems, Inc., CA, Inc., Symantec Enterprise Security, and VMware, Inc. ("VMware")." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Strategy: "Our strategy is focused on sustained technology leadership and developing category-leading solutions to deliver a comprehensive suite of innovative infrastructure technology products to the world's leading business and government customers. We seek to achieve this through extensive internal research and development, as well as strategic acquisitions of businesses and technologies, to ensure our products retain their technology market leadership." [10-K FY2025, Item 1] The FY2023 wording was sharper about acquisitions: "combine best-of-breed technology leadership in semiconductor and infrastructure software solutions, with unmatched scale, on a common sales and administrative platform ... through responsibly financed acquisitions of category-leading businesses and technologies." [10-K FY2023, Item 7]
- Two reportable segments: "Our semiconductor solutions segment includes all of our semiconductor-based product lines and intellectual property ("IP") licensing. Our infrastructure software segment includes our private cloud, mainframe software, cybersecurity and enterprise software portfolios, and our FC SAN business." [10-K FY2025, Item 7] (FY2023 wording: "Our infrastructure software segment includes our mainframe, distributed and cyber security solutions, and our FC SAN business." [10-K FY2023, Item 7])

### 1a. Semiconductor solutions — what it sells and to whom
- Product scope: "a broad portfolio of complex digital and mixed signal devices based on silicon wafers with complementary metal oxide semiconductor ("CMOS") transistors, III-V based devices, network interface cards ("NICs") and other modules, switches, subsystems and, in some cases, racks." Used in "enterprise and artificial intelligence ("AI") data centers, servers and networking and connectivity equipment, as well as storage systems, home connectivity devices, set-top boxes ("STB"), broadband access, telecommunication equipment, wireless devices and base stations, factory automation, power generation and alternative energy systems, and electronic displays." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- AI paragraph, verbatim: "Our semiconductor and semiconductor-based solutions also enable our customers to build and deploy AI data center infrastructure for their training and inference workloads and manage the movement of data across their AI network infrastructure based on open, flexible, standards-based Ethernet. ... Customers of these solutions are hyperscalers and companies with AI frontier models, as well as original equipment manufacturers ("OEMs") and system integrators that develop servers, switches and racks deployed in large-scale AI data centers to run training and inference workloads. Our AI semiconductor solutions include custom accelerators or XPUs, Ethernet switching and routing silicon, Ethernet NICs, physical layer devices ("PHYs") and optical components, as well as racks and systems based on our XPUs." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- "We offer our semiconductor and semiconductor-based solutions in five major end markets: Networking Connectivity, Wireless Device Connectivity, Servers and Storage Systems, Broadband and Industrial." [10-K FY2025, Item 1] Key products by end market [10-K FY2025, Item 1]:
  - Networking Connectivity: custom silicon (ASICs) — "ASICs are custom products built to our individual customers' specifications, such as our custom accelerators or XPUs, for hyperscalers, companies with AI frontier models and system integrators, and in some cases used in racks or systems"; Ethernet switching and routing ("switching fabric technologies provide the ability to build highly scalable flat networks supporting tens of thousands of servers"); Ethernet NIC controllers; physical layer devices (including automotive Ethernet); fiber optic components.
  - Wireless Device Connectivity: RF front-end modules and filters using "our proprietary commercial film bulk acoustic resonator ("FBAR") filter technology"; Wi-Fi/Bluetooth combination chips; custom touch controllers; inductive charging ASICs. Products are "designed for use in wireless devices including smartphones, tablets and wearable products."
  - Servers and Storage Systems: PCIe switches; SAS and RAID controllers and adapters; fibre channel host bus adapters; HDD and SSD chips (read-channel SoCs, preamplifiers, custom flash controllers).
  - Broadband: set-top box SoCs; broadband access SoCs (DSL, cable, PON, Wi-Fi) for consumer premise equipment and central offices.
  - Industrial: optocouplers, industrial fiber optics, sensors, motion encoders, LEDs, Ethernet ICs for factory automation, power, medical, defense/aerospace and vehicle subsystems.
- FY2023 end-market table used the labels Broadband, Networking, Wireless, Storage, Industrial with the same product families. [10-K FY2023, Item 1]
- How semiconductors are sold: "Distributors and OEMs, or their contract manufacturers ("CMs"), typically account for the substantial majority of our semiconductor sales." "Many of our semiconductor customers design products in North America or Europe that are then manufactured in Asia." "Our direct sales force focuses on supporting our large OEM customers." "Many of our major customer relationships have been in place for many years and are often the result of years of collaborative product development." [10-K FY2025, Item 1] "We recognize revenue upon the delivery of our products to the distributors, which can cause our quarterly net revenue to fluctuate significantly." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
- Manufacturing model: "we outsource a majority of our manufacturing operations, utilizing third-party foundry and assembly and test capabilities ... The majority of our front-end wafer manufacturing operations is outsourced to external foundries, including Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Limited ("TSMC"). We use third-party CMs for a significant majority of our assembly and test operations, including TSMC, Advanced Semiconductor Engineering, Inc., Foxconn Technology Group, Amkor Technology, Inc. and Siliconware Precision Industries Co., Ltd. We use our internal fabrication facilities for products utilizing our innovative and proprietary processes, such as our FBAR filters for wireless communications and our vertical-cavity surface emitting laser and side emitting lasers based on gallium arsenide ("GaAs") and indium phosphide ("InP") lasers for fiber optic communications, while outsourcing commodity processes such as standard CMOS." "The majority of our internal III-V semiconductor wafer fabrication is done in the U.S. and Singapore." "We store the majority of our product inventory in our warehouse in Malaysia." [10-K FY2025, Item 1] Title and control for most products transfer in Penang, Malaysia. [10-K FY2025, Note 13]
- Own fabs named as sole sources: "Our Fort Collins [Colorado] and Breinigsville [Pennsylvania] facilities are the sole sources for the FBAR filters used in many of our wireless devices and for the InP-based wafers used in our fibre optics products, respectively." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]
- Seasonality (pre-VMware wording): "Historically, our net revenue has typically been higher in the second half of the fiscal year than in the first half, primarily due to seasonality in our wireless communications products ... due to launches of new mobile devices manufactured by our OEM customers." [10-K FY2023, Item 1]
- IP: "As of November 2, 2025, we had approximately 19,000 U.S. and other patents and 2,170 U.S. and other pending patent applications." "A portion of our revenue comes from IP licensing royalty payments and from litigation settlements relating to such IP." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]

### 1b. Infrastructure software — what it sells and to whom
- "Our infrastructure software solutions help enterprises simplify their information technology ("IT") environments. Many of the largest companies in the world, including most of the Fortune 500, and many government agencies rely on our infrastructure and security software solutions to modernize, optimize, and secure the most complex private cloud, hybrid cloud and edge environments." "We offer five major infrastructure software portfolios: Private Cloud, Mainframe Software, Cybersecurity, Enterprise Software and FC SAN Management." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Private Cloud (VMware): "VMware Cloud Foundation ("VCF"): VCF delivers integrated, enterprise-class compute, networking, storage, management and security across any supported environment. VCF includes native Kubernetes to support both virtual machines and containerized workloads on a single platform ..." Advanced services sold on top: "VMware vDefend, VMware Avi Load Balancer, VMware Tanzu Platform, VMware Private AI and VMware Live Recovery." Also VCF Edge, vSphere Foundation, Telco Cloud Platform. [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Mainframe Software: AIOps & Automation, Databases & Data Management, DevX & DevOps, Cybersecurity & Compliance, Foundational & Open Mainframe (the former CA, Inc. business; the filing does not say "CA" here). Cybersecurity: "Our Symantec and Carbon Black endpoint security solutions"; network, information, application security; identity & access management. Enterprise Software: AIOps, automation, network observability, DevOps, value stream management. FC SAN Management: fibre channel storage-networking hardware (the former Brocade business; not named as such here). [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Software customers and channel: "Our software customers generally consist of large enterprises that have computing environments from multiple vendors and are in most major industries worldwide, including banks, insurance companies, other financial services providers, government agencies, global IT service providers, telecommunication providers ..." "Our private cloud infrastructure suite of solutions are available directly from Broadcom, resellers and distributors, hyperscale cloud providers, value-added OEMs and VMware cloud service provider partners. VCF provides license portability, which enables customers to purchase subscriptions of VCF software and move their VCF environments between on-premises data centers and supported cloud endpoints. We remain focused on strengthening relationships and increasing penetration within our existing core, mainframe, VMware, and Symantec endpoint customers and expanding the adoption of our enterprise software offerings with these customers. We believe our enterprise-wide license model will continue to offer our customers reduced complexity, more flexibility and an easier renewal process that will help drive revenue growth." [10-K FY2025, Item 1]
- Channel programs named in risk factors: "our Accelerate Program and Catalyst Initiative for certain infrastructure software products, with certain sole distribution relationships by region." [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]

### 1c. Subscription licensing and how software revenue is booked
- "Our software arrangements primarily consist of fees, which may be paid either at contract inception or in installments over the contract term, that provide customers with a right to use the software, access general support and maintenance, and utilize our professional services. ... Certain of our software arrangements permit our customers to unilaterally terminate or cancel these arrangements at any time at the customer's convenience, referred to as termination for convenience provisions, without substantive termination penalty and receive a pro-rata refund of any prepaid fees. For software arrangements without termination for convenience provisions, we recognize revenue for the license portion of the agreements upfront upon transfer of control to the customer, referred to as upfront license revenue, within products revenue. For software arrangements with termination for convenience provisions, we account for these arrangements as a series of daily contracts, resulting in ratable revenue recognition of software revenue over the contractual period, and include them within subscriptions and services revenue." [10-K FY2025, Note 2]
- Pre-VMware: "The majority of our subscriptions and services arrangements permit our customers to unilaterally terminate or cancel these arrangements at any time at the customer's convenience." [10-K FY2023, Note 2] "Subscriptions and services predominantly includes software licenses with termination for convenience clauses." [10-K FY2023, Note 3]
- Reclassification in FY2025: "In fiscal year 2025, we included upfront license revenue of $7,800 million within products revenue in our consolidated statements of operations. To conform to the current year presentation, we reclassified $4,601 million and $1,058 million of upfront license revenue from subscriptions and services revenue to products revenue for fiscal years 2024 and 2023, respectively." [10-K FY2025, Note 3] Upfront license revenue in products revenue: Q2 FY2026 $1,964; H1 FY2026 $3,719; Q2 FY2025 $1,803; H1 FY2025 $3,775. [10-Q Q2 FY2026, Note 2]
- Why software revenue grew: FY2025 "increased primarily due to strong demand for our VCF product, including license revenue recognized on contracts where customers do not have the right to terminate and the transition to a subscription license model." [10-K FY2025, Item 7] Q2/H1 FY2026: "primarily due to strong demand for our VMware Cloud Foundation ("VCF") product." [10-Q Q2 FY2026, Item 2]
- Lumpiness warning: "For infrastructure software, the transition to subscription licenses, as well as whether or not a customer has the right to terminate, causes variations in revenue recognized in each period." [10-K FY2025, Item 7] "the relative volume of customer contracts signed with the right to terminate causes variations in revenue recognized in each period." [10-Q Q2 FY2026, Item 2]
- Contract liabilities subject to termination for convenience: approximately 67% at November 2, 2025; approximately 64% at May 3, 2026. [10-K FY2025, Note 3; 10-Q Q2 FY2026, Note 2]
- Auditor's critical audit matter for FY2025 was revenue recognition for "certain software and support revenue in the infrastructure software segment" (upfront vs ratable). [10-K FY2025, Item 8, auditor's report]

### 1d. The VMware acquisition
- Date and price: "On November 22, 2023, we completed the VMware Merger. ... each share of VMware common stock ... was indirectly converted into the right to receive, at the election of the holder ... either $142.50 in cash or 2.52 shares of Broadcom common stock (on a split adjusted basis). The stockholder election was prorated, such that the total number of shares ... entitled to receive cash and ... Broadcom common stock, in each case, was equal to 50% ... the VMware stockholders received approximately $30,788 million in cash and 544 million shares of Broadcom common stock with a fair value of $53,398 million." Total purchase consideration $86,290; less cash acquired $6,642 = $79,648 net. Also "Cash paid by Broadcom to retire VMware's term loan" $1,257; "We assumed $8,250 million of VMware's outstanding senior unsecured notes." [10-K FY2025, Note 4]
- Financing: "We funded the cash portion of the VMware Merger with the net proceeds from the issuance of the 2023 Term Loans ... as well as cash on hand." 2023 Term Loans: "an aggregate principal amount of $30,390 million", floating rate tied to SOFR; "During fiscal year 2025, we repaid the remaining $13,595 million of 2023 Term Loans and terminated the 2023 Credit Agreement." [10-K FY2025, Note 4; Note 10]
- Purchase price allocation: goodwill $54,206; intangible assets $45,572 (developed technology $24,156, 8-year life; customer contracts and relationships $15,239, 8 years; trade name $1,205, 14 years; off-market customer contracts $242, 2 years; IPR&D $4,730); other current liabilities assumed $(11,041); long-term debt assumed $(6,254) plus current $(1,264). "Goodwill is primarily attributable to the assembled workforce and anticipated synergies and economies of scale ... Goodwill is not deductible for tax purposes." [10-K FY2025, Note 4]
- What it added: "VMware was a leading provider of multi-cloud services for all applications, enabling digital innovation with enterprise control. We acquired VMware to enhance our infrastructure software capabilities." [10-K FY2023, Item 7] "VMware cloud foundation is a private cloud platform that integrates compute, storage, networking, and management into a single solution and provides license portability." [10-K FY2025, Note 4]
- Divestiture: "On July 1, 2024, we sold VMware's end-user computing ("EUC") business to KKR & Co. Inc. for cash consideration of $3.5 billion, after working capital adjustments." EUC is in discontinued operations (FY2024 net revenue $858; loss $(273) net of tax). [10-K FY2025, Item 7; Note 5]
- Contribution and cost: "Our results of continuing operations included $12,384 million of net revenue attributable to VMware for fiscal year 2024. It is impracticable to determine the effect on net income attributable to VMware as we immediately integrated VMware into our ongoing operations. Transaction costs related to the VMware Merger of $255 million were included in selling, general and administrative expense for fiscal year 2024." Pro forma (as if acquired at start of FY2023): net revenue FY2024 $52,188, FY2023 $48,227; net income FY2024 $6,473, FY2023 $8,215. [10-K FY2025, Note 4]
- Restructuring: "In connection with the VMware Merger, we initiated restructuring activities to integrate the acquired business, align our workforce and improve efficiencies in our operations." Restructuring charges FY2024 $1,787 (employee termination $1,510; lease and impairment $277); FY2025 $597 (employee $428; lease/impairment $169). "We have substantially completed these restructuring activities." [10-K FY2025, Note 15]
- Assumed equity: VMware RSU/PSU awards held by continuing employees "were converted into approximately 46 million Broadcom RSUs" (post-split basis; FY2023 10-K said "approximately 5 million"). Stock-based compensation from assumed VMware awards: $1,613 FY2024, $479 FY2025. [10-K FY2025, Note 11; 10-K FY2023, Item 7]
- Software margin after integration: FY2025 gross margin percentage rose partly on "higher infrastructure software gross margin percentage, driven by an increase in license revenue and lower infrastructure software labor costs following our integration of the VMware business." [10-K FY2025, Item 7]
