# Alphabet Inc. — The Business
_As of Q1 2026. Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Alphabet is the parent company of Google, and Google is by far the largest thing inside it [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Google builds products that billions of people use for free (Search, YouTube, Maps, Gmail, Android, Chrome, the Gemini app); the company says all 15 of its products with more than half a billion users now run on its Gemini AI models [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The users are not the customers; advertisers are. Someone types a question into Google; advertisers bid, through Google's ad tools, to have their ad appear next to the results; and Google's yardstick is "paid clicks", meaning the advertiser is charged when a user clicks the ad [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. On YouTube, "brand advertising" is ads (mostly video) meant to be seen rather than clicked [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Advertising was 73% of all revenue in 2025, and Search alone was 56% (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

The second business is Google Cloud, which rents out computers, storage, software, and AI models to companies and charges mostly by how much they use, plus subscriptions [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It also sells consumer subscriptions, takes a cut of Play store app sales, and sells Pixel phones [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. "Other Bets" is a group of long-shot companies, the best known being Waymo, the self-driving taxi service [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. A shared research group (Google DeepMind) builds the Gemini models; its cost is not charged to any segment [10-K FY2023, Note 15] [10-K FY2025, Note 15].

A short history. Google was incorporated in California in September 1998, moved to Delaware in 2003, listed on Nasdaq in August 2004, and in 2015 reorganized so that a new parent, Alphabet, sits above Google and the other bets [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 5]. The company has called itself "AI-first" since 2016 [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. One structural fact: there are three kinds of shares, and because Class B shares carry ten votes each and are almost all held by the two founders, Larry Page and Sergey Brin controlled 52.7% of the votes at the end of 2025 while owning far less than half of the company [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**Search & other.** Advertisers pay to appear next to searches on Google.com and other Google properties such as Gmail, Maps, and Play; the line also includes searches arriving through partners who make Google the default in their browser or phone [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Growth comes from more searches (mostly on phones), advertisers spending more, and better ad formats; in Q1 2026 paid clicks rose 13% and the average price per click rose 5% [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2].

**YouTube ads.** Advertisers pay to run ads before and during videos; direct-response ads (buy this now) are growing faster than brand ads [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2].

**Google Network.** Google places ads on other people's websites and apps and keeps a slice; most of this revenue is paid back out to the site owners, and it is the one line that has shrunk every year since 2022 [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Subscriptions, platforms, devices.** Consumers pay monthly for YouTube Premium and Music, YouTube TV, NFL Sunday Ticket, and Google One; developers pay Play store fees; buyers pay for Pixel devices [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Paid subscriptions reached about 350 million in Q1 2026, with YouTube and Google One the main drivers, and this line grew 19% in the quarter [Q1 2026 call, p.2] [Q1 2026 release].

**Google Cloud.** Companies pay for computing, storage, databases, security, and AI models through Google Cloud Platform, and for Workspace (Gmail, Docs, Meet for business) [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Revenue grew 63% in Q1 2026, and signed-but-not-yet-delivered contracts (backlog) stood at $462.3 billion for Cloud at March 31, 2026, up from $242.8 billion in total at year end; the jump is not exactly like-for-like because from Q1 2026 the company also counts contracts of one year or less, about $7.3 billion of the total [10-Q Q1 2026, Note 2] [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. The United States supplied 48% of 2025 revenue [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

## 3. The economics

**Does cost rise with usage?** For the ad business, historically, barely. Google builds one search index and one set of data centers and then answers billions of extra queries on the same machines, and the price of an ad is set by advertisers bidding against each other, not by what the query cost to serve. That is why revenue grew 56% from 2021 to 2025 (computed) while gross margin (the share of revenue left after the direct cost of serving users) rose, and operating margin (the share left after all running costs) went from 31% to 32%, with a dip to 26–27% in 2022–2023; 2023 alone carried $2.1 billion of severance and $1.8 billion of office-exit charges [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. In the company's words, "certain of our costs and expenses are relatively fixed and may not correlate to changes in revenue" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

**Where the money goes.** Cost of revenues (the direct cost of delivering the service) has two halves: TAC, traffic acquisition costs, meaning money paid to browser makers, phone makers, and carriers to make Google the default search plus the share of ad money passed to Network partner sites [10-K FY2025, Item 7]; and everything else needed to serve users: payments to YouTube creators, depreciation of servers and data centers, the staff who run them, and the cost of building Pixel devices [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Below that, research and development took 15% of revenue in 2025, sales and marketing 7%, and general and administrative 5% [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Why TAC matters.** TAC is the price of being the default. The rate has drifted down for years mostly because the Network line, where most revenue goes straight back to partners, is shrinking relative to Search [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2]. The company expects TAC dollars to keep rising with ad revenue and says the rate depends on "partner agreement terms" and "revenue share terms" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. If a partner demanded more, or a court changed those deals, this is the line where it would show.

**What is changing: the cost structure is getting heavier.** Capital spending was $24.6 billion in 2021 and $91.4 billion in 2025, and the company has told investors to expect $180–190 billion for 2026 with 2027 expected to "significantly increase" again [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [Q1 2026 call, p.13]. That spending becomes depreciation over the following years, and the company says "depreciation, energy, equipment, and network capacity" costs "are expected to significantly increase as developing and serving AI offerings require more compute power than our historical consumer and enterprise offerings" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Our inference, put simply: a classic search rode almost free on machines already paid for; an AI answer needs far more computing per question; and the company is buying that computing years ahead of the revenue it hopes will follow. Management says it has cut the cost of core AI answers by more than 30% since moving to Gemini 3, which helps, but the direction is toward heavy fixed assets that depreciate whether or not the queries come [Q1 2026 call, p.4].

The striking row is free cash flow: stuck between $60 billion and $73 billion for five years while revenue rose by more than half [10-K FY2023, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. This suggests almost every extra dollar of operating cash went into servers and data centers. Q1 2026 free cash flow was down 47% from a year earlier, and the figure for the last four quarters combined, $64.4 billion, was down 14% [Q1 2026 slides, p.10].

Two things flatter headline profit. Net income in 2025 included $24.1 billion of gains on stakes in private companies, and Q1 2026 alone $36.9 billion; these are paper revaluations, not cash [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2]. And 2025 operating cash flow was helped by a July 2025 US tax-law change that let the company deduct research and equipment costs faster, pulling tax savings forward [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

Return on capital, in plain words: for every dollar of property and equipment on the books at year end, the company earned 66 cents of operating profit in 2024 and 52 cents in 2025 (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. Our inference: each new dollar of machinery is earning less than the old ones did, as you would expect while assets are bought ahead of revenue. The company ended Q1 2026 with $126.8 billion of cash and quickly sellable investments (marketable securities) against $77.5 billion of long-term debt [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 1].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Search.** The company admits that for users "other products and services are literally one click away" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. So the moat is not lock-in but four softer things stacked together. Habit: queries were "at an all time high" in Q1 2026, with AI features described as driving more usage, not less [Q1 2026 call, p.4]. Distribution: Google pays browser providers, phone makers, and carriers to be the default, and that spending (TAC) was $59.9 billion in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Data: the US court's search remedy (the changes the court ordered after ruling that Google had broken competition law) requires Google to share certain search data with competitors [10-Q Q1 2026, Note 10]; our inference is that regulators regard that data as the hard-to-copy asset. Brand: the company calls its brand "one of the most recognized in the world" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Sign it is weakening: Search & other growth falling toward single digits while the TAC rate rises (paying more for the same defaults), or management no longer calling queries a record.

**YouTube.** Viewers come for creators; creators come for viewers and for the money YouTube pays them as a share of ad and subscription revenue [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Over ten million channels post Shorts every day, and YouTube has led US streaming watch time for three straight years [Q1 2026 call, p.6] [Q1 2026 call, p.9]. This suggests a rival would need both sides at once. Sign it is weakening: YouTube ad growth trailing Search for several quarters, or content payments growing faster than YouTube revenue.

**Cloud.** Companies sign multi-year contracts and move their data and software onto Google's systems; American Express, for example, is moving a data platform with "hundreds of production applications" [Q1 2026 call, p.5]. Our inference: once moved, moving back is slow and expensive. Customers also get something available nowhere else: the company says it is "the only provider to offer first party solutions across the entire Enterprise AI stack", meaning its own chips (TPUs), its own Gemini models, and the cloud around them [Q1 2026 call, p.4]. Existing customers used 45% more than they initially committed to [Q1 2026 call, p.4]. Sign it is weakening: backlog flat or falling, or Cloud margin falling from price competition.

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. AI answer products replace search queries.** People ask a chat assistant (including Google's own Gemini app) instead of Search, and the answers carry fewer or cheaper ads. The company names both halves: "consumers may change how they obtain information online", and AI features in Search may earn money differently, "which could affect revenue growth rates and margin trends" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Early warning: Search & other growth slowing for two or more quarters while management talks more about subscriptions as a way to pay for Search [Q1 2026 call, p.23]. Exposure: Search & other is 56% of revenue, and Google Services produces more than all of the company's operating income because the other segments are small or loss-making (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 15].

**2. The capital spending does not pay off.** The company plans to spend $180–190 billion in 2026 on a business whose free cash flow was $73 billion in 2025, funding the gap with debt ($77.5 billion, from $10.9 billion at the end of 2024) and by stopping buybacks [Q1 2026 call, p.13] [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-Q Q1 2026, Note 11]. If AI demand slows, the servers still depreciate and $75.6 billion of signed data-center leases still start [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2]. The company also warns that "expected technology advancements" could change how long it expects equipment to stay useful; our inference: a shortening, which raises depreciation [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: backlog flattening while capex keeps rising; quarterly free cash flow stuck near Q1 2026's $10.1 billion [Q1 2026 slides, p.10]. Exposure: the whole company's cash flow.

**3. Antitrust remedies in the United States.** (Antitrust law bars a dominant company from using its position to shut out competitors; a remedy is what a court orders changed after finding a violation.) In the search case, a final judgment entered in December 2025 "imposes restrictions on how Google distributes its services and requires Google to share certain search data with and offer syndication services to certain competitors" (syndication: letting rivals show Google's results or ads on their own sites); Google appealed in January 2026 and asked to pause some remedies, and the government also appealed [10-Q Q1 2026, Note 10]. In the advertising-technology case, a court found in April 2025 that Google's publisher tools (the software website owners use to sell ad space) "unfairly excluded rivals"; the government seeks "structural remedies that could have a material adverse effect on our business", and after closing arguments in November 2025 the company says "we are awaiting a final judgment", which it plans to appeal [10-Q Q1 2026, Note 10]. Early warning: the search appeal ruling; the ad-tech remedy judgment and whether it orders a sale of any business. Exposure: the ad-tech case bears most on Google Network, $29.8 billion of 2025 revenue and shrinking; the search case bears on how the $224.5 billion Search line is distributed [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**4. A distribution partner changes the deal.** Part of Search traffic arrives through partners who make Google the default, and no partner is named in the 10-K or 10-Q [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Early warning: a jump in the TAC rate on Search revenue (which the company says was "substantially consistent" in Q1 2026) or a sudden drop in paid-click growth [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2]. Exposure: the share of Search revenue arriving through partners is not disclosed.

**5. Cloud demand normalizes.** Cloud grew 63% in Q1 2026 and management said revenue "would have been higher if we were able to meet the demand" [Q1 2026 release] [Q1 2026 call, p.19]. Early warning: backlog growth stalling, the "customers outpaced their initial commitments" statistic disappearing, TPU hardware revenue arriving later than the 2027 timing given [Q1 2026 call, p.12]. Exposure: Cloud was 14.6% of revenue and 10.8% of operating income in 2025 (computed), so the direct profit at risk is modest; the real exposure is the capital spending justified by this demand [10-K FY2025, Note 15].

**Concentration.** No customer was more than 10% of revenue in 2023, 2024, or 2025 [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. AI chips and servers come from "a small number of qualified suppliers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. New in Q1 2026, the company disclosed guarantees backing other companies' data centers and power plants, warning that if the other parties to those deals fail it "may incur additional liabilities, have excess capacity that we cannot easily redeploy" [10-Q Q1 2026, Part II Item 1A].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Sundar Pichai, 53, joined Google in 2004 and became CEO of Google in October 2015 and of Alphabet in December 2019 [DEF 14A 2026]. Anat Ashkenazi, 53, has been CFO since July 2024, after the same role at Eli Lilly; her predecessor Ruth Porat is President and Chief Investment Officer [DEF 14A 2026]. The board's chair, John Hennessy, is independent; founders Larry Page and Sergey Brin are directors without executive roles and as of April 2026 held 27.4% and 25.3% of the voting power, with all officers and directors together at 54.3% [DEF 14A 2026].

**Pay.** Pichai's salary is $2.0 million and the other named executives' $1.0 million; the cash bonus program was discontinued in February 2025 and its value moved into stock [DEF 14A 2026]. Executives receive time-based stock units vesting over three years, and performance stock units (PSUs) that pay out from 0% to 200% of target on one measure only, Alphabet's total shareholder return (share price change plus dividends) ranked against the S&P 100 (an index of 100 large US companies) over three years; no revenue, margin, or cash-flow metric is used [DEF 14A 2026]. The CEO gets one large award every three years; the March 2026 award had target values of $84 million in time-based units and two $63 million PSU pieces (tranches), plus roughly $130 million and $45 million tied to the valuations of Waymo and Wing [DEF 14A 2026].

**Cash.** The stated priority: "the primary use of capital continues to be to invest for the long-term growth of the business" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The past year matches that: buybacks stopped, debt rose, and cash went into servers, acquisitions, and stakes in other companies.

## Glossary

- **TPU (Tensor Processing Unit):** a computer chip Google designs itself to run and train AI models, used in its data centers and now also sold to a few customers for their own data centers.
- **Backlog:** the total value of signed customer contracts whose revenue has not yet been recorded, used to see how much future Cloud revenue is already committed.
- **TAC (traffic acquisition costs):** payments to browser, phone, and carrier partners for making Google the default search, plus payments to partner websites for ads shown on them, used to measure the cost of distribution.
- **Depreciation:** spreading the cost of a long-lived asset such as a server over the years it is used, so a purchase made today shows up as expense for several years afterward.
- **Free cash flow:** cash generated by operations minus cash spent on property and equipment, used to see how much cash is left for buybacks, dividends, acquisitions, or debt repayment.
