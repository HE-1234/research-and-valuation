# Reddit, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Reddit is a website and app made of more than 100,000 communities, each about one interest and named with an "r/", where people post questions, links and opinions and everyone else comments and votes them up or down [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Voting "is completely anonymous", and volunteer moderators run each community under its own rules plus site-wide rules the company adds on top [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. In Q2 2026, 130.3 million people visited daily and 514.6 million weekly, and twenty years of this have built an archive of "26+ billion posts and comments" [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 release, p.3].

Almost all users pay nothing; a paid tier exists but its revenue "was not material" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Advertisers pay to appear in the feed and inside conversations: 94% of first-half 2026 revenue (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3]. AI companies pay to use Reddit's conversations to build and run their models: most of the other 6%, which the company calls "content licensing" [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. The pitch: "Reddit conversations are where interests meet intent", so a tent ad inside an r/camping thread is worth more than one shown to someone scrolling photos [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

Reddit counts logged-in users, who have accounts, and logged-out visitors; "Visitors that come to Reddit from search engines are generally not logged in" and arrive on the web rather than the app [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Logged-out visitors were 77.7 million of the 130.3 million daily users in Q2 2026, they earn less ("logged-in users generally contribute significantly more to ARPU"), and their number depends on how Google ranks Reddit pages [Q2 2026 release, p.6] [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A].

**A short history.** Reddit launched in 2005, was bought by Condé Nast in 2006, was spun out of Condé Nast's parent Advance Magazine Publishers in 2011, and "did not begin meaningful monetization efforts at Reddit until 2018" [424B4 2024, Risk Factors]. In January 2024 it signed its first large data-licensing contracts, $203.0 million over two to three years, and on March 21, 2024 it listed on the New York Stock Exchange at $34.00 a share, raising $600.0 million net [424B4 2024, MD&A] [8-K 2024-03-26, Item 8.01] [10-K FY2025, Note 2].

## 2. How the money comes in

**Advertising.** Advertisers pick an objective (a view, click, install or purchase), pay when it happens, and an auction decides which ad shows [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Fast growers in Q2 2026 were purchase-driving products: Dynamic Product Ads (ads built from a retailer's product catalog) and app-install ads each "more than doubling year-over-year", and Reddit Max (AI-automated campaigns) with revenue "over 150%" above Q1 [Q2 2026 call, p.5–6] [Q2 2026 letter, p.8]. Call-only figures: mid-market and small-business revenue "doubled year-over-year", active advertisers "grew over 70%", and "more than half of our revenue comes from credit line relationships" (advertisers on account) [Q2 2026 call, p.5–7]. Growth came more from price than volume: "the price of ads increased by approximately 40%" while impressions (ad views) rose "approximately 17%" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

## 3. The economics

**Does cost rise with usage?** Barely. Cost of revenue is "primarily ... payments to third parties for the cost of hosting" on Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. Hosting grows with users and with the AI models Reddit runs, yet it was 9% of revenue in both Q2 2025 and Q2 2026, and gross margin has held above 90% since 2024 [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The content is written and policed for free: "any content that gains traction on Reddit has been reviewed in context by humans", the volunteer moderators [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**Where the money goes.** Of each 2025 revenue dollar, 9 cents went to cost of revenue, 35 to research and development, 23 to sales and marketing and 13 to general and administrative, leaving 20 cents of operating profit; in Q2 2026 the split was 9 / 29 / 24 / 9, leaving 29 [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Stock-based compensation (paying employees in shares) explains the gap between the two profit measures: 15.6% of revenue in 2025 and 12.5% in Q2 2026 (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 13] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11]. Adjusted EBITDA adds it back; management guides to it and is paid on it [Q2 2026 release, p.5] [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A].

**Capital intensity is close to zero.** "We use third-party infrastructure partners to host our services and therefore we do not incur significant capital expenditures to support revenue generating activities" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. The real commitment is contractual: in June 2026 Reddit signed an AWS extension committing "an aggregate of $880.0 million between July 2026 and June 2029" [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9]. So capex/revenue will always look tiny.

**A tax item.** Past losses left $1.7 billion of federal loss carryforwards (past losses that can be set against future taxable profit), so Reddit pays almost no income tax, but a "full valuation allowance" of $779.0 million means it has not yet booked those savings as an asset [10-K FY2025, Note 15]. The 10-Q calls a release "a reasonable possibility ... within the next 12 months"; when it comes, GAAP net income jumps by a large non-cash tax benefit unrelated to that quarter's business [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 12].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Users.** The moat is the communities and the archive, not the software: twenty years of conversations, and people "seek it out intentionally by name" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [Q2 2026 call, p.13]. Huffman: "Once users get Reddit, they really get it, but it can be a hard product to get" [Q2 2026 call, p.19]. The habit is thin: one weekly visitor in four comes daily (computed), and the CFO said retention is "lower than others in the space" [Q2 2026 release, p.1] [Q2 2026 call, p.22]. Our inference: the moat is strong for the roughly 53 million logged-in daily users and weak for the logged-out rest, who have no switching cost; hence management's "top priority remains growing our daily active user base" [Q2 2026 letter, p.5]. Signs it is weakening: U.S. daily users (53.2 million, down from 53.5 million in the quarter) falling further; the DAUq-to-WAUq ratio (25%, from 27% through 2024) not turning up [Q2 2026 release, p.6] [Q1 2026 release, p.6] [10-K FY2024, Item 7].

**Advertisers.** Most have no long-term commitment [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. What holds them is measured return; the company says topic-based targeting is "more resilient to the loss of signal" as tracking is restricted [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The COO's loop: easy onboarding, then "more efficiency, which retains them and allows them to scale spend", then credit lines that keep them "always-on" [Q2 2026 call, p.11]. Signs it is weakening: ad-price growth in the 10-Q falling toward zero while impressions still grow; U.S. ARPU growth (51%) slipping toward user growth [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 release, p.6].

**Data licensees.** Contracts, not loyalty: two to three years, then renewal; management's argument is that Reddit's content is "not commodity content" [Q2 2026 call, p.13]. The sign is the §2 backlog table: obligations running off with no new 2027-and-later amounts would mean the partners are not re-signing [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. Google changes how it ranks or answers, and the logged-out audience shrinks.** The 10-Q lists reliance on Google for traffic and, since Q2 2026, "AI-generated search or answer features" as a risk [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. It cuts both ways: search changes helped lift daily users 39% in late 2024 and in Q2 2026 "partially offset" the quarterly gain; referrals were "choppy", and management expects search "to probably continue to be volatile" [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 letter, p.5] [Q2 2026 call, p.15]. On the size of the drop: "I won't comment on the magnitude" [Q2 2026 call, p.15]. Google's AI Overviews "has yet to make a similar level of positive impact", and a June 2025 securities class action (an investor lawsuit) alleges the company misled investors about that impact [Q2 2026 call, p.15] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9]. **Exposure:** logged-out visitors are 60% of daily users (computed); no revenue split is disclosed, but app users "are worth multiples more than the search referral traffic", so their share of ad revenue is well below 60% (our inference) [Q2 2026 release, p.6] [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [Q2 2026 call, p.23]. **Early warning:** U.S. DAUq (flat to down in Q2), the DAUq-to-WAUq ratio, and the "search referral" wording in the letter and 10-Q, since the logged-in split ends after Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 release, p.6] [Q1 2026 release, p.6].

**2. The two licensing partners do not renew, or renew for less.** Other revenue was $140.0 million in 2025, 6.4% of revenue and, with almost no direct cost, more of profit (our inference); the backlog ends in 2027, and the deals "may not be renewed, or they may be renewed based on less favorable terms" [10-K FY2025, Note 3] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. **Early warning:** remaining performance obligations falling with no new 2027-plus amounts. **Exposure:** about 6% of revenue (computed), more of profit.

**3. People ask an AI instead of visiting Reddit.** "Redditors may choose to find information using AI tools, which in some cases may have been trained using Reddit content, instead of visiting Reddit directly" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. **Early warning:** WAUq growth (24%) falling into single digits; U.S. WAUq (197.2 million) declining [Q2 2026 release, p.6]. **Exposure:** all revenue, slowly.

**4. Volunteer moderators revolt, or a brand-safety incident (ads shown next to offensive content).** The company "depends on Redditors who volunteer to be moderators", and in June 2023 some "inhibited normal operations of the communities they operate" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. **Early warning:** another coordinated shutdown. **Exposure:** the free labor behind a 9% cost of revenue.

**5. Regulation of user data, minors and content.** The UK data regulator fined Reddit £14.5 million in February 2026 (under appeal); online-safety and age-verification rules in the EU, UK and Australia "have prevented and could continue to prevent us from making our services available to certain users" [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. **Early warning:** international DAUq growth (28%) breaking in a named country [Q2 2026 release, p.6]. **Exposure:** the 21% of revenue billed abroad (computed), and licensing.

**6. Advertisers cut budgets, or the cloud fails.** Macroeconomic conditions "may continue to impact revenue growth in the near term" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]; an AWS outage on October 20, 2025 "rendered our platform temporarily inaccessible", and Reddit has committed $880.0 million to AWS through mid-2029 [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9]. **Early warning:** ad-price growth in the 10-Q dropping toward zero.

**Concentration.** No advertiser above 10% of revenue or receivables; no country other than the U.S. above 10% [10-K FY2025, Note 2] [10-K FY2025, Note 3]. What is concentrated, two licensing partners and Google as a traffic source, is not quantified.

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Steve Huffman, 42, co-founded Reddit in 2005 and has been CEO since July 2015; Jen Wong, 51, COO since 2018; Drew Vollero, 60, CFO since 2021, was Snap's first CFO [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. Founding engineer Chris Slowe left the CTO role in April 2026; no successor is named [DEF 14A 2026, Executive Officers]. Eight directors, seven independent, two of them "Advance Designees" [DEF 14A 2026, Director Independence].

**Who controls the votes.** Class A shares carry one vote, Class B ten [10-K FY2025, Note 12]. Advance Magazine Publishers, Condé Nast's owner, holds 42.2 million Class B shares: about 22% of all shares but 65.2% of the votes; Tencent (6.1%) is the only other 5% holder [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 13]. Both gave Huffman an "irrevocable proxy" to vote their shares "in Mr. Huffman's sole discretion", so with his own 6.1% he controls 76.1% of the votes [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. For a Class A holder, votes are decided before they are cast. Behind the proxy, Advance keeps the big decisions: Reddit needs its written consent to "terminate, reduce, or enlarge the responsibilities of, or elect, appoint, or remove, our Chief Executive Officer" or to agree a change of control, and Advance names two directors [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. **Sunsets (dates when the extra votes expire):** none is dated. Class B converts on transfer (Advance's internal transfers excepted) or when Class B falls below 7.5% of all shares; it was 24.2% at June 30, 2026 (computed), and Advance's Class B count is unchanged since the IPO, so the sunset arrives only if Advance sells (our inference) [10-K FY2025, Note 12] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1] [424B4 2024, Principal and Selling Stockholders].

**Pay and incentives.** Bonuses are set 40% on DAUq, 40% on revenue and 20% on Adjusted EBITDA; 2025 paid 165% of target because revenue and Adjusted EBITDA beat their maximums while DAUq fell short of its 124.0 million target [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. Huffman's wealth sits in December 2023 pre-IPO stock and option awards, which made his 2023 compensation $193.2 million; his 2025 pay was $3.1 million with no new grant [DEF 14A 2026, Summary Compensation Table] [DEF 14A 2026, Outstanding Equity Awards]. In May 2026 Wong and Huffman filed plans to sell up to 780,000 and 600,000 Class A shares under trading plans that run to August and September 2027 [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 5].

**What they do with the cash.** No dividend, ever [10-K FY2025, Item 5]. Vollero's order: "first investing in the business; second, opportunistic M&A; and third, share repurchases" (M&A: buying other companies) [Q2 2026 call, p.10]. Buybacks began in 2026:

## Glossary

- **DAUq (daily active unique):** a visitor, logged in or not, identified by a unique identifier who opened Reddit at least once in a day, averaged over the quarter; the company's main measure of audience size.
- **WAUq (weekly active unique):** the same measure over a trailing seven-day window, used to size the wider audience that visits less than daily.
- **ARPU (average revenue per unique):** quarterly revenue in a region divided by that region's average DAUq, used to measure how much each user's attention earns.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** net income with interest, taxes, depreciation, stock-based compensation and its payroll taxes, and one-off items added back, used by management for guidance and bonuses.
- **Remaining performance obligations / deferred revenue:** contracted revenue not yet booked, and cash received for services not yet delivered, used here to see how much of the content-licensing contracts is left.
- **Valuation allowance:** an accounting reserve that cancels out the future tax savings from past losses until the company is confident it will earn enough to use them; releasing it produces a one-time non-cash tax benefit.
- **Dual-class shares:** two share classes with different votes per share (here one and ten), used to keep control with insiders after a listing.
