# Nike — The Business
_As of Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended May 31, 2026). Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Nike designs and markets sports shoes, clothing and equipment under the NIKE, Jordan and Converse brands and calls itself "the largest seller of athletic footwear and apparel in the world" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. It owns almost no factories: "Nearly all of our products are manufactured by independent contractors. Nearly all footwear, apparel and equipment products are manufactured outside the United States." [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In FY2026 Vietnam, Indonesia and China made 52%, 27% and 16% of NIKE Brand footwear through 15 contract manufacturers [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

It sells two ways. In **wholesale** the customer is a retailer that may order "five to six months ahead of delivery", pays "within 90 days or less" and then carries the risk of selling to shoppers [10-K FY2026, Item 1A] [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. In **NIKE Direct** the shopper is the customer: about 980 leased stores, "which primarily consist of factory stores" (outlets), plus the NIKE Brand Digital apps and sites in over 40 countries [10-K FY2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Direct keeps the retailer's slice of the price but carries leases, staff and unsold stock, so it earns more at full price and less on markdowns (our inference from the year-by-year gross-margin explanations in §3) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

History. Nike was "incorporated in 1967 under the laws of the State of Oregon", co-founded by Philip Knight, and listed in 1980 with two share classes [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [DEF 14A 2026, Capital Structure]. Jordan Brand is reported inside the NIKE geographies; Converse, bought at a date the filings do not state, reports separately [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In fiscal 2021 the "Consumer Direct Acceleration" strategy pushed sales into Nike's own stores and apps, and Direct reached about 44% of NIKE Brand revenue in FY2024 [10-K FY2022, Item 7] [10-K FY2024, Item 7]. Elliott Hill, who joined in 1988 and retired in 2020, returned as President and CEO in 2024 in place of John Donahoe (offer letter dated September 19, 2024), launched the "Win Now" turnaround in December 2024 and is reorganising around individual sports, the "Sport Offense", while rebuilding wholesale [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2026, Item 15] [DEF 14A 2026, Pay Versus Performance] [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, Sherman/Bernstein] [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill].

## 3. The economics

Cost rises with every pair sold: cost of sales is the factory price of the goods plus inbound freight, import duties, warehousing and royalties, so gross margin is about how much of the list price survives discounting [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. Below gross profit sit two large, mostly fixed lines. Demand creation ($4.8 billion, 10.2% of revenue) is brand advertising plus "sports marketing expense" (athlete and team contracts, events), a chosen spend that does not fall with sales [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Operating overhead ($11.4 billion, 24.5%) is wages, rent, depreciation and technology [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Friend has said pandemic-era investment left "a higher fixed cost base that weighed significantly on our EBIT margins as revenue came down"; a 10% revenue fall in FY2025 cut the EBIT margin from 12.7% to 8.2% [Q3 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend] [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Capital intensity is low: capex (spending on buildings, equipment and technology) is 1–2% of revenue, and net property and equipment of $4.8 billion is dwarfed by $7.5 billion of inventory and $5.9 billion of receivables; the capital sits in stock and unpaid invoices, not plant [10-K FY2026, Item 8].

**Gross margin, one clause per year.** FY2022, 46.0% (+120 basis points): more full-price and Direct sales, offset by "elevated freight and logistics costs" [10-K FY2022, Item 7]. FY2023, 43.5% (−250): higher input and freight costs and "higher promotional activity to liquidate inventory" [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. FY2024, 44.6% (+110): price rises worth about 200 basis points and cheaper freight, offset by "Lower margin in our NIKE Direct business" [10-K FY2024, Item 7]. FY2025, 42.7% (−190): lower average prices "primarily due to higher discounts" (about 180 basis points) and other costs including reserves for stock expected to sell below cost (about 90) as Nike cut Classics supply [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. FY2026, 42.9% (+20) reported, but the refund added about 210, so the underlying 40.8% was the lowest of the five years [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend] [10-Q Q3 FY2026, Item 2].

**Inventory and markdowns.** Product is ordered "at least four to five months in advance of sale", so a fashion miss becomes surplus [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Surplus is discounted, which cuts gross margin, and selling "excess inventory at discounted prices could significantly impair our brand image" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. The Classics (Air Force 1, Dunk, Air Jordan 1) are the case in point: by the end of FY2026 Nike will have "intentionally reduced over $4 billion of revenue from the peak levels of classic footwear franchises", "$2 billion" of it in FY2026 [Q3 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill] [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, Yih/Barclays]. Inventory has sat at $7.5 billion for three year-ends while revenue fell 10%, so inventory turns (cost of sales divided by year-end inventory, roughly how many times a year the stock is sold through) slowed from 3.8 in FY2024 to 3.5 in FY2026 (computed) [10-K FY2026, Item 8] [10-K FY2024, Item 8].

**Tariffs.** Nike had paid "approximately $1.0 billion" of tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) by April 2026, and in Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended February 28, 2026) tariffs cost about 270 basis points of gross margin [10-Q Q3 FY2026, Item 2]. After the Supreme Court ruled on February 20, 2026 that those tariffs "were unauthorized", Nike booked a $986 million refund in Q4 cost of sales, about 900 basis points of Q4 gross margin and $0.52 of the quarter's $0.72 diluted earnings per share, treated here as one-time [10-K FY2026, Note 1] [Q4 FY2026 release, headline bullets]. Other tariffs continue: the outlook assumes "incremental tariff rates of 10% continuing through the end of July and then increasing to 15% thereafter" [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**The brand.** The NIKE name and Swoosh are registered "in over 190 jurisdictions worldwide", and Hill's argument is that sport, not fashion, is the source of their pull: "what creates authenticity for NIKE is our sport business. And that creates the halo over both of those brands." [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, Yih/Barclays]. Weakening sign: discounting. FY2025 gross margin lost about 180 basis points to "higher discounts", in March Friend said "digital is still too promotional. Markdowns across the marketplace remain elevated", and Sportswear "declined double digits" in Q4 FY2026 [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q3 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend] [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend].

**Athletes and Jordan.** About $15.5 billion of endorsement contracts buy the link between winning and the Swoosh, and Jordan Brand is $7.0 billion of revenue [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. The 10-K now names "the growing influence of athletes' personal brands, the proliferation of athlete-led commercial ventures" as a threat [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Weakening sign: Jordan revenue, down 16% in FY2025 and 5% currency-neutral in FY2026, and any star leaving [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**Scale in marketing and sourcing.** Nike spends $4.8 billion a year on demand creation, four times Converse's entire revenue (computed), buys from 15 footwear and 64 apparel manufacturers, and makes its own Air cushioning [10-K FY2026, Item 7] [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In FY2026 it says it gained, in Western Europe and North America, "5 points of running market share in statement footwear, more than any other top 5 brand" [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill]. Weakening sign: running growth stalling.

**Distribution.** Nike reaches shoppers through about 980 own stores, apps in over 40 countries and wholesale partners, where it refreshed "more than 15,000 spaces in wholesale doors" in FY2026 [10-K FY2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill]. Weakening sign: the order book; in March "Order books are growing, and we are taking back shelf space", by June Nike was "reducing the order book in holiday" [Q3 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend] [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, Sherman/Bernstein].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility of serious damage.

**1. Sportswear and Jordan Streetwear keep shrinking and the brand cheapens.** They "represent approximately half of our revenue" and will "continue to be negative this fiscal year" [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill]. What must happen: the styles promised for the second half of FY2027 fail at full price. Early warning: NIKE Brand Digital and Direct declines, gross margin excluding one-offs, and the $1.6 billion returns and discount reserve [10-K FY2026, Note 14]. Exposure: about half of revenue [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill].

**2. Performance brands take running, then the rest.** The 10-K names "adidas, Anta, ASICS, Deckers, Li Ning, lululemon athletica, New Balance, On, Puma, Under Armour and V.F. Corporation" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. What must happen: the running share gains reverse. Early warning: running growth below double digits after "5 consecutive quarters", or the performance business turning negative [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill]. Exposure: footwear, 65% of NIKE Brand revenue (computed) [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**3. Tariffs and a three-country supply chain.** Vietnam, Indonesia and China make 95% of NIKE Brand footwear (computed), four manufacturers about 60% of it, and "protectionist measures have resulted in increased costs of our products" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. What must happen: tariffs rise above the 10% then 15% assumed, the refund is clawed back, or a supplier country faces retaliation. Early warning: the "product costs" line in each quarter's gross-margin explanation [10-Q Q3 FY2026, Item 2]. Exposure: the U.S. is 44% of revenue; North America earned $4.4 billion of EBIT excluding the refund (computed) [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [Q4 FY2026 release, EBIT table].

**4. Greater China does not come back.** Revenue fell from $7.5 billion in FY2024 to $5.8 billion and EBIT from $2.3 billion to $1.3 billion; Nike expects "negative impacts from Greater China and Converse to continue throughout fiscal 2027" [10-K FY2026, Note 15] [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Early warning: Greater China revenue and EBIT, and whether "profitability will bottom before sales" [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, Hutchinson/Bank of America]. Exposure: 13% of NIKE Brand revenue (computed) and $1.3 billion of EBIT [10-K FY2026, Note 15].

**5. A big retailer fails or squeezes Nike.** Nike sells on credit "generally without requiring collateral", and "some customers have experienced financial difficulties up to and including bankruptcies" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. The three largest U.S. customers are about 29% of U.S. sales, up from 21% two years earlier [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2024, Item 1]. Early warning: receivables ($5.9 billion at May 2026, up 26%) rising faster than wholesale revenue [10-K FY2026, Item 8].

**6. Fixed costs outrun a shrinking top line.** Overhead at 24.5% of revenue was built for a $51 billion company [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Nike cut about 10,700 jobs (computed) from May 2023 to May 2026 and booked $443 million of restructuring in FY2024 and $385 million of severance in FY2026 [10-K FY2023, Item 1] [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2026, Note 18]. Early warning: overhead dollars against revenue, and whether the promised FY2027 margin expansion appears [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Friend].

**Concentration and legal.** No customer is 10% of revenue; supplier concentration is scenario 3 [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Belgian customs authorities claim underpaid duties on imports since fiscal 2018; Nike "is unable to estimate the range of loss" and an adverse outcome "could have a material adverse effect" [10-K FY2026, Note 16].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

Elliott Hill (62) has been President and CEO since 2024; Mark Parker (70), CEO from 2006 to 2020, is Executive Chairman [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. CFO Matthew Friend is due to leave on September 4, 2026; David Denton, CFO of Pfizer, was announced on June 23, 2026 to start August 17 (the 8-K body says August 16), with a $7.25 million cash sign-on and a $4 million award tied to FY2027 EBIT-margin growth [8-K 2026-06-16] [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In December 2025 Hill named Venkatesh Alagirisamy Chief Operating Officer, eliminated the Chief Commercial and Chief Technology Officer roles, and put the four geography heads on his leadership team; about 8,000 employees moved "into vertical sport teams" [8-K 2025-12-01] [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hill].

**Track record.** Since FY2024 revenue has fallen from $51.4 billion to $46.4 billion and EBIT from $6.5 billion to $3.85 billion; the proxy statement (the document sent to shareholders before the annual meeting) shows $100 invested in May 2021 worth $36.47 in May 2026 against $48.36 for the peer group [10-K FY2026, Item 7] [DEF 14A 2026, Pay Versus Performance].

## Glossary

- **Wholesale** — selling to retailers and distributors who resell to shoppers; Nike's customer is the store, which pays within about 90 days.
- **NIKE Direct** — Nike's own selling: NIKE-owned stores plus NIKE Brand Digital, the Nike apps and websites.
- **Demand creation** — Nike's name for marketing expense: advertising, events and payments to athletes and teams under endorsement contracts.
- **Operating overhead** — all other running costs: wages, rent, depreciation, technology and administration.
- **Gross margin** — revenue left after the cost of the product, freight, duties and warehousing, as a share of revenue.
- **EBIT** — earnings before interest and taxes; Nike's measure of operating profit, used for segments and as a margin on revenue.
- **Currency-neutral** — growth recalculated at the prior year's exchange rates, so that movements in the dollar do not hide the underlying trend.
- **Basis point** — one hundredth of a percentage point; 100 basis points is one point of margin.
- **IEEPA tariffs** — U.S. import tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act, ruled unauthorized by the Supreme Court on February 20, 2026, leading to the refund Nike booked in Q4 FY2026.
- **Sell-through** — the share of goods shipped to a store that shoppers actually buy in the season; low sell-through means markdowns and smaller reorders.
- **Classics** — Nike's long-running lifestyle shoe lines (Air Force 1, Dunk, Air Jordan 1) whose supply Nike is deliberately cutting.
