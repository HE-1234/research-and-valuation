## Conversion method

Python 3 with BeautifulSoup (lxml parser): dropped `script`, `style`, `noscript`, `head`, `ix:header` and any element styled `display:none`; each table row replaced by its non-empty cells joined with " | " between `[TABLE]` / `[/TABLE]` markers (cells that are only ")", "%" or "$" merged into their neighbour); `p`, `div`, `br`, headings and list items converted to newlines; whitespace collapsed; curly apostrophes and other UTF-8 kept. BeautifulSoup emitted `XMLParsedAsHTMLWarning` for the inline-XBRL 10-Ks (they start with an XML declaration); output checked and unaffected. Same method as the earlier run used for the reused files. The 8-K cache files concatenate the body and the chosen exhibits with `===== <label> =====` separators naming the source document, accession and filing date.

## Notes for the writer

- **Fiscal calendar and labels.** FY ends May 31; FY2026 = June 2025–May 2026. Q4 has no 10-Q; Q4 figures are FY minus nine months (computed) unless taken from the press release. Quarter label: `Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended May 31, 2026)`.
- **Segments are stable.** North America, EMEA, Greater China, APLA, Global Brand Divisions, Converse, Corporate in every 10-K FY2022–FY2026; the FY2024 column is identical in the FY2024 and FY2026 10-Ks. Segment profit measure is EBIT (non-GAAP at the total level). Corporate carries the gap between standard and actual FX rates on product purchases, so segment gross margins are on standard rates.
- **Presentation changes to watch.** (1) "Wholesale equivalent revenues" (a non-GAAP measure) stopped in FY2025; the Men's/Women's/Kids'/Jordan split moved to a reported basis in the FY2025 10-K and the FY2026 10-K dropped Men's/Women's/Kids' entirely (Jordan Brand dollars survive only in a footnote: 7,034 / 7,270 / 8,701 for FY2026 / FY2025 / FY2024). (2) NIKE Brand Digital FY2023 is $12.6 billion as first reported and $12.4 billion after the FY2024 10-K's store/digital reclassification. (3) Endorsement-contract obligations from FY2025 onward "include associated marketing commitments" previously inside other purchase obligations, so the $10.6 billion (May 2024) to $16.2 billion (May 2025) jump is partly reclassification and the 10-Ks do not quantify it. (4) The FY2025 10-K's $7.9 billion product purchase obligation was corrected to approximately $5 billion by the 2025-07-28 8-K. (5) The FY2026 10-K labels the cash-flow line "Depreciation and amortization" (older 10-Ks: "Depreciation"); amounts for overlapping years agree. (6) The FY2026 10-K balance sheet shows no "Notes payable" line for May 31, 2025 (the $5 million sits inside accrued liabilities, 5,916 vs 5,911 in the FY2025 presentation).
- **Numbering quirks.** Segment note is Note 15 in FY2023–FY2026 (titled "Segment Information" from FY2025) and Note 17 in FY2022; restructuring/severance is Note 19 (FY2023–FY2024) and Note 18 (FY2025–FY2026); divestitures Note 18 (FY2023–FY2024). 10-Q segment note is Note 11, severance Note 13. The MD&A heading uses an ASCII apostrophe.
- **Tariffs.** The FY2026 10-K gives no forward tariff cost estimate; the quantified facts are the $986 million IEEPA refund recognised in Q4 FY2026 cost of sales ($965 million North America, $21 million Converse; $302 million received by May 31 and $684 million receivable, "substantially all" collected after year-end), the 10-Q's "approximately $1.0 billion" of IEEPA tariffs paid, and Q3's 270 bps gross-margin hit from "higher tariffs in North America". FY2026 gross margin excluding the refund is 40.8% (computed). The 8-K of 2026-06-16 says the refund "was not contemplated in the company's previously provided guidance".
