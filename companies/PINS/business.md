# Pinterest, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Pinterest is an app and website where people save and search for visual ideas. Saved images are called Pins, filed on boards; the company calls itself "an AI-powered visual search and discovery platform, positioned at the intersection of search, social, and commerce" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It had 640 million monthly active users (MAUs) at June 30, 2026, about two-thirds women, more than half Gen Z, and 377 million outside the United States, Canada and Europe [Q2 2026 release, p.2] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Users pay nothing. The customers are advertisers, "who come to Pinterest to reach our users with high commercial intent" [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**How an ad business turns users into money.** Pinterest sells advertisers the chance to appear in front of people while they browse and search. Advertisers pick an objective and are billed for it: "brand" campaigns per thousand views or video view, "performance" campaigns per click, action or conversion such as an add-to-cart [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. An auction picks the ad "based on the likelihood of a desired action occurring and how much that action is worth to advertisers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Revenue is therefore users × what each user's attention earns, reported as average revenue per user (ARPU); that depends on how many ads a user sees, how well they are targeted and measured, and what advertisers in that country will pay; the company monetizes "different geographies at different average rates" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Shopping ads "promote specific products in their catalogs", and management calls the push toward buying "actionability" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [Q2 2026 call, p.2]. A portion of revenue comes from "partnerships with third-party advertising platforms", partners the filings do not name (our reading: other ad platforms bringing their advertisers onto Pinterest) [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. The pitch: over 96% of text searches are unbranded, so users have intent but no decision yet, and their saves and searches form a "Taste Graph" that trains the AI [Q2 2026 call, p.2] [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**A short history.** Incorporated in Delaware in 2008; listed on the New York Stock Exchange on April 18, 2019 [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 5]. Co-founder Ben Silbermann was CEO until June 2022; Bill Ready, previously a Google commerce executive, became CEO in 2022 and Silbermann became chair [DEF 14A 2026, Our board of directors]. Under Ready the company moved toward "full funnel advertising solutions" (ads that drive purchases, not only awareness), launched Pinterest Performance+ in the second half of 2024, and in February 2026 bought tvScientific to extend performance ads "from mobile to connected TV" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [Q2 2026 slides, p.7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 3].

The pattern: in Q2 2026 the U.S. and Canada had 17% of users and 75% of revenue; a U.S. or Canadian user earned about six times a European user and 36 times a Rest of World user (computed) [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

**Why international ARPU is a fraction of U.S. ARPU.** The company's explanation: "Our revenue in U.S. and Canada and, to a lesser extent, Europe is higher primarily due to the relative size and maturity of the digital advertising markets in these geographies", and "we currently only monetize certain countries and currencies" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Sales coverage: in some countries it relies on contractors and "local third-party sales organizations, which we refer to as resellers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Ad volume: much recent growth in ads shown came from "previously under-monetized international markets" [Q2 2026 call, p.5]. Pricing: the average ad price fell every year from 2022 to 2025 while ads served rose faster (FY2025: +49% and −22%) [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2022, Item 7]. Our inference: international users see fewer ads, at lower prices, sold by a thinner sales force, in less mature ad markets; management calls it "significant opportunity to close the gap between engagement and revenue" [Q2 2026 call, p.4]. Shopping-catalog coverage by region is not disclosed.

## 3. The economics

**Does cost rise with usage?** Partly. Pinterest runs on "data center hosting facilities operated by AWS" (Amazon Web Services) rather than its own, and cost of revenue is mainly "the cost of hosting our website and mobile application" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Hosting rises with usage: the 2024, 2025 and first-half 2026 increases were attributed "primarily" to "increased users and engagement", the 2022 and 2023 increases to "higher compute utilization" (using more computing power) [10-K FY2024, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2022, Item 7] [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. But it is a small slice, so gross margin has stayed between 76% and 80% and the lines below it do not move with each user [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The change is AI: the company is buying "additional GPU capacity" (chips that run AI models), non-GAAP cost of revenue (before stock pay and one-offs) rose 25% in Q2 2026 against 18% revenue growth, and the CFO expects "modest headwinds from cost of revenue as a percentage of revenue in the second half" [Q2 2026 call, p.5–6].

**Where the money goes.** In 2025, of each revenue dollar, 20 cents went to cost of revenue, 34 to research and development, 28 to sales and marketing and 11 to general and administrative, leaving 8 cents of operating profit [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Stock-based compensation (paying employees in shares) is why GAAP profit (profit under the official accounting rules) is thin: $880 million in 2025, 21% of revenue, mostly in R&D [10-K FY2025, Note 8]. Strip it out, with its payroll tax, amortization (acquisition costs spread over years) and one-offs, and R&D falls from 38% of revenue to 20% in Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 slides, p.17]. That is Adjusted EBITDA, the figure management guides to and is paid on; its trailing-twelve-month margin climbed from 15% in early 2023 to 30% in mid-2026 [Q2 2026 release, p.5] [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A] [Q2 2026 slides, p.5]. Hold both: 30% before stock pay, 8% after.

**Capital intensity is close to zero.** Capex is office fit-outs; property and equipment was $66 million net at the end of 2025 against $4.2 billion of revenue [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Note 4]. The real commitment is contractual: in May 2026 Pinterest agreed to buy "at least $4,000.0 million of cloud services from AWS through May 2031", with $3,927.9 million remaining at June 30, 2026 [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 6].

**Two things distort net income.** FY2024's $1,862 million included a $1,597.0 million tax benefit from releasing the valuation allowance on deferred tax assets, an accounting judgment that past losses will offset future tax, not operating profit [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The first half of 2026 shows a $120.3 million GAAP loss because of $61.4 million of restructuring charges (a January 2026 plan cutting under 15% of staff), stock compensation up $141 million to $556 million and lower interest income [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 11] [8-K 2026-01-27] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Users.** "One hundred percent of our reported users are logged in, and 85% come to Pinterest directly through our mobile app, meaning we are not heavily reliant on third parties for traffic" [Q2 2026 call, p.4]. The habit is personal: saved boards and a Taste Graph of "many billions of associations" make the feed more relevant "every time a user comes back" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [Q2 2026 call, p.2]. Management's answer to AI chatbots: "AI by itself does not have style or taste. People have style and taste" [Q2 2026 call, p.16]. Our inference: the user moat is a switching cost of accumulated personal curation plus habit, not a network effect. Signs it is weakening: U.S. and Canada MAUs (flat at 106 million from Q1 to Q2 2026, up 4% in a year) turning down; the "85% direct" figure dropping [Q2 2026 slides, p.9] [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

**Advertisers.** Most "do not have long-term advertising commitments with us" and "spend a relatively small portion of their overall advertising budget with us", so they stay only while the ads pay [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. What holds them is measured return: unbranded intent, "Conversions API and clean rooms" for measurement (tools that link an advertiser's own sales data to the ads it ran), product catalogs for shopping ads, and Performance+ automation [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Performance+ users "see meaningfully better ROAS and grow spend faster", and a pilot linking large advertisers' own measurement to Pinterest's bidding "contributed to our outperformance in Q2" [Q2 2026 call, p.3] [Q2 2026 call, p.9]. Signs it is weakening: the Performance+ share of lower-funnel revenue (about 30% at Q1 2026, not updated in Q2) stalling; U.S. and Canada ARPU growth slipping back toward FY2025's 6% from Q2's 14% [Q2 2026 call, p.11] [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. Search moves to AI answer engines and outside traffic dries up.** The company depends "in part on internet search engines, such as Google, Bing and Yahoo!" for traffic and warns that "consumers may increasingly search for products using chatbots" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: global MAU growth below 10% after eleven double-digit quarters; the direct-traffic share no longer cited [Q2 2026 slides, p.4]. Exposure: all revenue.

**2. Advertisers shift budgets to bigger platforms, or the ad market turns.** Budgets are short-term and small, larger platforms have "more capabilities to help advertisers measure their conversions", and the company has "seen reduced spending from certain advertisers due to the impact of tariffs" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1A]. Early warning: U.S. and Canada revenue growth falling while MAUs still grow; ad pricing negative again [Q2 2026 call, p.5]. Exposure: 75% of revenue (computed) [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

**3. Regulation, especially in Europe and around teenagers.** The company operates under the EU Digital Services Act and GDPR (the EU's data-privacy law) and lists teen laws ("prohibiting showing teens advertising, requiring age verification or assurance") that could "decrease our advertising and subscription revenue" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Already live: "pressure mid-quarter from Asia-based cross-border retailers impacted by regulatory actions, particularly in Europe", continuing into Q3 [Q2 2026 call, p.7]. Early warning: Europe revenue growth (27% in Q1 2026, 12% in Q2) in single digits; Europe MAUs falling [Q1 2026 release, p.2] [Q2 2026 release, p.2]. Exposure: Europe is 18% of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**4. AI compute costs outrun revenue.** Cost of revenue already outgrows revenue (§3), and the 10-Q adds a new risk of "constraints on access to, cloud infrastructure capacity, including GPUs" [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1A]. Mitigations: open-source models at "less than 8% of the cost" of closed ones and an undisclosed "multiyear infrastructure agreement" [Q2 2026 call, p.2] [Q2 2026 call, p.5]. Early warning: non-GAAP cost of revenue above 23% of revenue (our threshold) for several quarters; Adjusted EBITDA margin below the 30–34% long-term range [Q2 2026 slides, p.11] [Q2 2026 call, p.14]. Exposure: the 78% gross margin (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1].

**5. Reliance on AWS and on unnamed third-party ad demand.** Pinterest must keep "a substantial majority of our monthly usage" on AWS, hosted "in one geographic region" [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Separately, "a portion of our revenue is derived from partnerships with third-party advertising platforms"; partners and revenue share are not disclosed [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: third-party demand no longer described as expanding [Q2 2026 call, p.4]. Exposure: not disclosed.

**6. U.S. and Canada user growth stalls for good.** The company expects "our active user growth rate will decline over time" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: U.S. and Canada MAUs (106 million, +4% in a year, flat in the quarter) turning down while ARPU growth slows [Q2 2026 release, p.2] [Q2 2026 slides, p.9]. Exposure: 75% of revenue (computed) [Q2 2026 release, p.2].

**Concentration.** "No customer accounted for more than 10% of our revenue for the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023", but "a substantial portion of our revenue is derived from a small number of advertisers and is currently concentrated in certain verticals, particularly retail and CPG" (consumer packaged goods) [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. No country other than the United States exceeded 10% of revenue [10-K FY2025, Note 11].

**People.** Bill Ready, 46, has been CEO since 2022, after senior roles at Google and PayPal [DEF 14A 2026, Our board of directors]. **Who controls the votes.** Class B shares carry twenty votes each and Class A one, so Class B holders had 75.3% of the votes at June 30, 2026 with 13% of the shares (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Part II Item 1A] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1]. **Pay.** Salary is $625,000 for every named executive; the cash bonus is split equally between revenue and Adjusted EBITDA targets (121% of target for 2025); the CEO's equity is 60% performance shares paying 0–200% on relative total shareholder return (stock return compared with a peer index) over 2025–2027 [DEF 14A 2026, CD&A]. ## Glossary

- **MAU (monthly active user):** a logged-in user who opened Pinterest at least once in the last 30 days, used to measure the audience advertisers can reach.
- **ARPU (average revenue per user):** revenue in a region divided by its average MAUs for the period, used to measure how much advertising each user's attention earns.
- **Adjusted EBITDA:** net income with depreciation, stock-based compensation and its payroll tax, interest, taxes and one-off items added back, used by management as its main profit measure and for guidance.
- **Performance+:** Pinterest's AI-automated ad campaign product that sets targeting, bidding, budgets and creative, used for lower-funnel (purchase-driving) campaigns.
- **Connected TV (CTV):** television sets and streaming devices connected to the internet, used to show targeted ads the way a phone app does.
- **Convertible notes:** debt the holder can swap for shares at a set price, used here to raise $1 billion from Elliott at 1.75% interest.
- **Accelerated share repurchase (ASR):** a buyback where the company pays a bank up front and receives most of the shares immediately, used to retire shares faster than open-market buying.
- **Capped call:** an option the company buys to limit the extra shares it must issue if convertible notes convert at a high share price, used here with a cap of $30.59.
- **ROAS (return on ad spend):** revenue an advertiser gets back per dollar of ads, used by advertisers to decide where budgets go.
