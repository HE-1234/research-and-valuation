# Microsoft — The Business
_As of Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026). Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Microsoft sells the software that offices run on and rents out the computers that run it [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Companies and governments pay a yearly fee per employee for Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams, security tools and now the Copilot AI assistant), and pay by the unit of computing used on Azure, Microsoft's cloud of servers, storage, databases and AI models [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. PC makers pay for a Windows licence on every machine they ship; consumers pay for Microsoft 365 subscriptions, Xbox consoles, games and Game Pass; advertisers pay to appear on Bing, Edge, Copilot and LinkedIn [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

Of the three segments, Productivity and Business Processes is Microsoft 365 for businesses and consumers, LinkedIn and the Dynamics business software; Intelligent Cloud is Azure plus the on-premises server products (software run on the customer's own machines) and support services; More Personal Computing is Windows, Surface devices, Xbox and search advertising [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The first two produced 91% of operating income in FY2026, and 51% of revenue came from the United States (both computed) [10-K FY2026, Note 18].

Microsoft was "Founded in 1975" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Satya Nadella joined in 1992, became CEO in February 2014 and Chairman in June 2021 [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Under him the company bought ZeniMax (games, March 2021, $8.1 billion), Nuance (healthcare speech software, March 2022, $18.8 billion) and Activision Blizzard (games, October 2023, $75.4 billion) [10-K FY2023, Item 1A] [10-K FY2022, Note 8] [10-K FY2026, Note 7]. LinkedIn was acquired in December 2016; the cached filings do not date the GitHub purchase [DEF 14A 2025, Compensation Discussion and Analysis] [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The OpenAI partnership "was originally established in 2019" and was extended in October 2025 and April 2026 [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. In August 2024 Microsoft redrew its segments, "most notably bringing the commercial components of Microsoft 365 together in the Productivity and Business Processes segment", restating only FY2024 and FY2023 [10-K FY2025, Note 1].

**Microsoft 365 Commercial, 31% of revenue (computed).** Businesses pay per user on multi-year agreements invoiced annually; revenue "is mainly affected by a combination of continued installed base growth and average revenue per user expansion" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In FY2026 the cloud part grew 17% on seats (paid users) up 6%; the gap is price per user, "driven by Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft 365 E5" [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**Server products and cloud services, 39% (computed).** Azure customers pay for what they use: "Azure revenue is mainly affected by infrastructure-as-a-service and platform-as-a-service consumption-based services" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The 10-K never gives Azure in dollars; Nadella said "Azure surpassed $100 billion, up 41%" for the year, while the on-premises server software in the same line grew 1% [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Nadella] [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**LinkedIn, Dynamics, Microsoft 365 Consumer, 11% together (computed).** Recruiters, advertisers and premium members pay LinkedIn; businesses pay per user for Dynamics 365 business software; households pay subscriptions [10-K FY2026, Item 1] [10-K FY2026, Note 18].

**Windows and Devices, XBOX, Search advertising.** PC makers pay for the Windows licences they pre-install, so revenue moves with PC shipments [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Gamers pay for consoles, games and Game Pass; advertisers pay for placement on Bing, Edge and Copilot [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Two lines shrank in FY2026: XBOX by 7%, with hardware down 29%, and Windows and Devices by 1% [10-K FY2026, Item 7] [10-K FY2026, Note 18].

Management's own aggregate, "Microsoft Cloud" (Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud, Azure, commercial LinkedIn, Dynamics 365), was $214.4 billion in FY2026, up 27%, or 65% of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Revenue peaks in the fiscal fourth quarter, when more multi-year contracts are signed [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

## 3. The economics

For most of its life Microsoft sold licences, booked in full on delivery: once Windows or Office was written, the next copy cost almost nothing [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. Cloud changed that, because every Azure or Microsoft 365 user consumes servers, power and network in Microsoft's datacenters, and AI changes it again, because a Copilot answer needs far more computing than an email (our inference) [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The defence is scale: the 10-K says the cloud business "benefits from three economies of scale", lower cost per unit in big datacenters, pooling of "diverse customer, geographic, and application demand patterns" so machines stay busy, and shared systems that cut maintenance labour [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

**Where the money goes.** Cost of revenue took 32% of FY2026 revenue (computed), research and development 11%, sales and marketing 8%, general and administrative 2% [10-K FY2026, Item 8] [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Operating expenses grew 7% against revenue growth of 18%, so operating margin (profit after all running costs, before interest and tax, as a share of revenue) rose even as gross margin (revenue left after the direct cost of delivering the product, as a share of revenue) slipped [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell from 72% in FY2023 to 66% in FY2026, "driven by continued investments in AI infrastructure and growing AI product usage", and Intelligent Cloud's cost of revenue rose 44% in one year [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

**Two bookkeeping changes.** Lengthen an asset's assumed life and each year's depreciation charge falls. In July 2022 Microsoft raised server and network equipment lives "from four years to six years", adding "$3.7 billion" to FY2023 operating income [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. From FY2027 it extends datacenter and office building lives "from 15 to 25 years", with "a minimal benefit to FY27 operating income"; the bigger effect is that more datacenter leases become operating leases, which are not counted as capex, so the calendar-2026 capex expectation fell from "roughly $190 billion" in April to "approximately $175 billion" in July while "Investment expectations remain unchanged" [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood] [Q3 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood] [Q1 FY2027 outlook slide].

**How locked in is the spending.** About two-thirds of Q4 FY2026 capex went to short-lived assets, "primarily CPUs and GPUs", the rest to buildings and land [Q4 FY2026 slides, s.6]. At June 30, 2026 signed datacenter leases not yet started totalled $329.1 billion (FY2027 to FY2033) and purchase commitments $194.1 billion, $169.0 billion of it due in FY2027 [10-K FY2026, Note 13] [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Hood's answer to overbuild worries: "if the demand environment changes, you just slow down what is, in fact, the largest component and the driver of COGS", COGS being cost of revenue [Q4 FY2026 call, Q&A, Moerdler/Bernstein].

Profit converts into cash unusually well: operating cash flow was 137% of net income in FY2026 (computed), because depreciation and stock pay are charged but not paid in cash, and because customers on annual invoices pay ahead [10-K FY2026, Item 8] [10-K FY2026, Note 12]. The striking row is free cash flow (operating cash flow minus capex): between $59 billion and $74 billion in all five years while revenue rose 67% (computed), because the extra operating cash went into datacenters [10-K FY2026, Item 8] [10-K FY2024, Item 8].

Net income now carries OpenAI's swings. Microsoft holds "an approximate 25% interest on an as-converted basis" (counting all convertible holdings as shares) and books its share of OpenAI's results in other income under the equity method [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. That meant net losses of $1.5 billion in FY2024 and $4.8 billion in FY2025, then a $6.5 billion net gain in FY2026, mostly a paper gain from OpenAI's October 2025 recapitalization (a reorganisation of who owns what) diluting the stake [10-K FY2026, Note 3]. The company therefore also reports "adjusted net income" and "adjusted diluted EPS" that "exclude net gains and losses from investments in OpenAI": $17.28 a share in FY2026 against the GAAP $17.95 [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

Return on capital: each dollar of net property and equipment (buildings and machines at book value, after depreciation) earned $1.12 of operating income in FY2022 and $0.50 in FY2026 (computed) [10-K FY2026, Item 8] [10-K FY2022, Item 8]. Our inference: new datacenters earn less per dollar because they are bought ahead of the revenue meant to fill them. Cash exceeds debt and the credit rating is AAA, the highest grade, but finance-lease liabilities of $66.6 billion now exceed the debt [10-K FY2026, Note 10] [10-K FY2026, Note 5] [10-K FY2026, Note 13].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Switching costs on a whole estate.** A Microsoft 365 customer has email, files, chat, identity and security in one suite under one contract; the cloud bundle includes "Enterprise Mobility + Security, the cloud portion of Windows Commercial", Exchange, SharePoint and Teams [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Contracts run for years and are invoiced annually, which is why unearned revenue (cash collected for service not yet delivered) was $75.7 billion and commercial remaining performance obligation (signed, not yet delivered) was $678 billion at June 30, 2026, about 30% of it due within twelve months, average length 2.3 years [10-K FY2026, Note 12]. On Azure the estate is the customer's own applications and data, costly to move again (our inference).

**Bundling raises the price without new customers.** Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud revenue grew 17% in FY2026 on seat growth of 6%; the rest was higher-priced suites, "Microsoft 365 Copilot and Microsoft 365 E5" [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. The new E7 suite bundles Copilot and E5 with Agent 365, a "control plane" for governing AI agents (programs that carry out tasks on their own) [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Nadella].

**Distribution and scale.** Microsoft sells direct to large organisations, through partners to smaller ones, through PC makers that pre-install its software, and through volume licensing programs [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. It added 88 datacenters in FY2026 and plans to "roughly double our overall capacity in just two years", and its rights to OpenAI's models "are extended through 2032" [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Nadella] [8-K 2025-10-28].

**The sign of weakening.** Paid Microsoft 365 Commercial seats grew 6% in each of the five quarters from Q4 FY2025 to Q4 FY2026, and 6% in both FY2025 and FY2026 as whole years; a fall toward zero means the installed base has stopped growing, and cloud revenue growth falling toward seat growth means price per user has stopped rising [IR workbook FY26Q4, Metrics] [Q4 FY2026 slides, s.9]. Also watch commercial bookings (the value of new contracts signed in the period) excluding OpenAI (up 18% in Q4 FY2026) and the on-premises server line, where growth came from "licenses running in multi-cloud environments", that is, Microsoft software on rival clouds [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood] [10-K FY2026, Item 7].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility of serious damage.

**1. The AI build-out outruns demand and depreciation eats the margin.** What must happen: Azure growth slows while capacity keeps arriving under the commitments in §3 [10-K FY2026, Note 13]. Depreciation rose from $22.0 billion to $34.3 billion in one year and net property and equipment stands at $313 billion [10-K FY2026, Note 6] [10-K FY2026, Item 8]. The 10-K says the investments "are in advance of fully developed revenue streams" and that overestimating demand "may lead to impairment of assets on our balance sheet", that is, to writing down their value [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: Azure constant-currency growth slowing for two quarters while capex still rises; Microsoft Cloud gross margin below the 64% guided in April 2026; Intelligent Cloud operating margin below 40% [Q4 FY2026 outlook slide] [10-K FY2026, Note 18]. Exposure: Intelligent Cloud's $57.0 billion of operating income, plus the infrastructure cost allocated to every segment [10-K FY2026, Note 18].

**2. OpenAI, in both directions.** FY2026 revenue "from commercial arrangements with OpenAI, inclusive of revenue-sharing payments" was $24.1 billion, 7.3% of total revenue and 17.5% of Intelligent Cloud revenue (computed; the 10-K does not say which line), with $6.0 billion receivable (invoiced but not yet paid) [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. OpenAI "has contracted to purchase an incremental $250B of Azure services", but Microsoft "will no longer have a right of first refusal" to supply it [8-K 2025-10-28]. OpenAI's share of the $678 billion RPO is not disclosed; RPO grew 25% excluding OpenAI against 84% in total [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood]. Microsoft has also funded $11.9 billion of a $13.0 billion commitment and books OpenAI's results in its own net income [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. Early warning: the related-party revenue and receivable lines; RPO growth excluding OpenAI diverging from the total. Exposure: 7% of revenue, an unknown share of RPO, and the OpenAI line in other income.

**3. Models become commodities and Copilot pricing collapses.** Microsoft's own pitch is that "every model is substitutable" [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Nadella]. It also means no model, OpenAI's included, commands a premium for long (our inference). The 10-K warns that if AI pricing "declines as a result of competition, commoditization, or other market forces" margins would suffer [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud growth falling toward the 6% seat growth; Copilot seat additions slowing. Exposure: Productivity and Business Processes, the highest-margin segment, $83.9 billion of operating income [10-K FY2026, Note 18].

**4. Governments.** The FY2026 10-K names no antitrust case; it says "Government agencies closely scrutinize us under U.S. and foreign competition laws" and cites the EU AI Act and export controls [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. The only named legal matter is the Irish Data Protection Commission's 2024 decision that LinkedIn's advertising broke EU privacy law, under appeal, fine not stated [10-K FY2026, Note 14]. Larger is tax: for 2004 to 2013 "the IRS is seeking an additional tax payment of $28.9 billion plus penalties and interest" over transfer pricing (how profit is split between its units in different countries), which Microsoft "will vigorously contest" [10-K FY2026, Note 11]. Early warning: a case named in the contingencies note. Exposure: the IRS figure is about a fifth of FY2026 net income (computed), and "We derive substantial revenue from government contracts" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A].

**5. A security breach.** The moat rests on trust in the systems holding customers' identities and email (our view). In late November 2023 "a nation-state associated threat actor used a password spray attack to compromise a legacy test account and, in turn, gain access to Microsoft email accounts" and then source code, which the 10-K says still harms "our reputation and customer relationships" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: another 8-K on a cyber incident. Exposure: the whole commercial franchise.

**6. PCs and consoles keep shrinking.** Windows and Devices is 5% of revenue (computed) and guided down "in the high-teens" for FY2027, as users turn to phones and tablets [10-K FY2026, Note 18] [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood] [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Exposure: More Personal Computing's $14.4 billion of operating income, 9% of the total (computed) [10-K FY2026, Note 18].

**Concentration.** No customer reached 10% of revenue in FY2024–FY2026, and OpenAI is the only customer named [10-K FY2026, Note 18] [10-K FY2026, Note 1]. "There are few qualified suppliers for certain components of our servers and devices"; none is named [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

Satya Nadella has been CEO since February 2014 and Chairman since June 2021, and Amy Hood CFO since May 2013 [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Judson Althoff became "Chief Executive Officer, Microsoft Commercial Business" in October 2025 and Amy Coleman Chief Human Resources Officer in March 2025 [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The board has 13 directors, Nadella the only non-independent nominee in the 2025 proxy, with Sandra Peterson as Lead Independent Director; Carlos Rodriguez left in December 2025, Carmine Di Sibio joined in May 2026 and Reid Hoffman is not standing for re-election at the 2026 annual meeting [10-K FY2026, Signatures] [DEF 14A 2025, Board Leadership] [8-K 2025-09-30] [8-K 2025-12-05] [8-K 2026-05-13] [8-K 2026-06-02].

**Pay.** Nadella's FY2025 pay was $96.5 million, of which $84.2 million was stock, all performance stock awards with a target value of "$50 million, a level unchanged since fiscal year 2022" [DEF 14A 2025, Summary Compensation Table] [DEF 14A 2025, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. The FY2025 award metrics are "Azure and Other Cloud Services Revenue Growth 35%; Microsoft Cloud Revenue Growth, excluding Azure and Other Cloud Services 35%; Consumer Services Revenue Growth 15%; Xbox Content and Services Revenue Growth 15%" over three years, with a share-price modifier that helps only above the 60th percentile of the S&P 500 [DEF 14A 2025, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. The cash bonus is 70% formula on revenue and operating income against budget and 30% judgment on security, product and culture [DEF 14A 2025, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. Our observation: every stock metric is a revenue growth rate; none measures margin, cash or return on capital. The pay ratio (CEO pay to the median employee's) was 480 to 1 and 91.94% of votes backed the pay [DEF 14A 2025, CEO Pay Ratio] [8-K 2025-12-05].

**Ownership.** Nadella held 900,572 shares, under 1%; all 18 directors and officers held 2,279,620 shares, 0.03% (computed); Vanguard held 8.95% and BlackRock 7.30% [DEF 14A 2025, Stock Ownership Information] [DEF 14A 2025, Principal Shareholders]. Nadella set up a plan in March 2026 to "sell 80% of the net vested shares" from an award vesting August 31, 2026 [10-Q Q3 FY2026, Part II Item 5].

## Glossary

- **Azure** — Microsoft's cloud: computing, storage, databases and AI models rented from Microsoft's datacenters and billed by use.
- **Constant currency** — a growth rate recalculated as if exchange rates had not moved, so the underlying business can be compared across years.
- **Remaining performance obligation (RPO)** — revenue under signed customer contracts that Microsoft has not yet delivered or recorded.
- **Finance lease** — a long lease that accounting treats like a purchase on credit: the datacenter appears as an asset and a liability, and Microsoft counts it in capital expenditures.
- **Equity method** — accounting for a large minority stake by recording the investor's share of the investee's profit or loss in its own income each period.
- **GPU** — graphics processing unit; the chip that does the arithmetic behind training and running AI models.
- **Token** — the unit of text an AI model reads or writes; AI usage and pricing are measured in tokens.
- **OEM** — original equipment manufacturer; a PC maker that pre-installs Windows and pays Microsoft a licence per machine.
- **Capex** — capital expenditure; cash spent on servers, datacenters and buildings rather than day-to-day costs.
- **Depreciation** — spreading the cost of a long-lived asset over the years it is used, so today's purchase becomes expense over several years.
- **Copilot** — Microsoft's brand for its AI assistants: Microsoft 365 Copilot inside Office, GitHub Copilot for programmers, and others.
