### 1c. Useful-life change, lease accounting, and calendar-2026 capex (Hood, prepared remarks)

- Useful lives extended: "Now, before I move to outlook, effective at the start of FY27, we are extending the estimated useful lives of our datacenters and office buildings, from 15 to 25 years, reflecting our operating history and expected use of these assets. The impact of this update is reflected in today's guidance." — Amy Hood, CFO [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood]
- Effect on depreciation, leases and CY2026 capex: "This change affects only the timing of future depreciation and is expected to have a minimal benefit to FY27 operating income. The greater impact is on capital expenditures as more of our future datacenter leases will shift from finance leases to operating leases as a result of this update. Finance leases are included in capital expenditures while operating leases are not. Outside of this useful life impact, our calendar year 2026 CapEx investment expectations remain unchanged. However, the shift from finance to operating leases adjusts our expectation to approximately $175 billion." — Amy Hood, CFO [Q4 FY2026 call, prepared remarks, Hood]. (For the prior figure this replaces, "roughly $190 billion" for calendar 2026, see §7; the Q4 call does not restate the old number.)

- **Gabriela Borges (Goldman Sachs):** Asked Hood to put capex and monetization together: how does ROI on today's capex decisions compare with a year ago, and what levers remain (for example internal silicon) for incremental monetization. — Hood: "I don’t know that, quite frankly, my math has changed in terms of how I do it over the past year"; what has changed is "the confidence in the TAM expansion, the margin levers that we have in terms of both product improvements than the infrastructure improvements"; levers: "best price performance on silicon, including our investments in first party", "model diversification also is a margin improvement opportunity", token and cost efficiency, a broad portfolio "across knowledge work, coding, security, then basically the agent layer, I’ll call that Agent 365 as kind of a cheat", and on Azure "model efficiency, silicon, and component efficiency, including our investments in 1P solutions"; "this is the grind work".
