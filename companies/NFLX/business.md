# Netflix, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Netflix is a subscription streaming service "offering TV series, films, games and live programming across a wide variety of genres and languages", one segment whose "revenues are primarily derived from monthly membership fees" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Two groups pay. Members pay monthly in advance, on plans that at June 30, 2026 ranged from the dollar equivalent of $1 to $38 a month by country and features, plus "extra member" sub-accounts at $2 to $10 for someone outside the household, the product side of enforcing "our terms of use to limit multi-household usage" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Advertisers pay to appear on the cheaper ad-supported plan, called "recently introduced" in the FY2022 annual report and priced at $8.99 a month in the US in mid-2026 [10-K FY2022, Item 1A] [Q2 2026 interview, p.10].

**Scale, and what you can no longer see.** The last count is "over 325M paid memberships" at the end of 2025, an audience "approaching one billion people" [Q4 2025 letter, p.2]. There will be no newer one: in 2025 "we discontinued the reporting of membership numbers, including average paying memberships and average monthly revenue per paying membership, focusing instead on revenue and operating margin as the primary financial metrics" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The business is therefore read through regional revenue, operating margin, content cash versus expense and free cash flow.

**History.** Incorporated in 1997, Netflix rented DVDs by mail until September 29, 2023 [10-K FY2025, Note 1] [10-K FY2023, Item 7]. It "launched streaming in 2007", began original animation in 2018 and live events in 2023 [Q2 2026 letter, p.2] [Q2 2026 letter, p.3]. Growth stalled in 2022: paid net additions fell to 8.9 million from 18.2 million and the US and Canada lost 0.9 million memberships; after the ad plan and the sharing crackdown they rebounded to 29.5 million in 2023 and 41.4 million in 2024 [10-K FY2023, Item 7] [10-K FY2024, Item 7]. A ten-for-one split took effect on November 14, 2025; per-share figures here are split-adjusted [8-K 2025-11-14, Item 5.03] [10-K FY2025, Note 1].

Membership revenue is members times what each pays a month; since 2025 neither factor is disclosed. The 10-Q's drivers: "the growth in memberships, price increases, and increased advertising revenue" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Plan mix matters too: "there's still a gap between ad tier ARM and then ARM for our standard without ads tier. But that gap is narrowing" [Q2 2026 interview, p.10]. Ad revenue is not broken out in any 10-K or 10-Q; the letters say it "rose more than 2.5x to over $1.5B" in 2025 and should roughly double to "approximately $3 billion" in 2026, "about a 25% contributor to growth" [Q4 2025 letter, p.1] [Q2 2026 letter, p.2] [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.9].

## 3. The economics

**Two numbers for the same show.** When Netflix makes or licenses a title it pays cash, shown on the cash-flow statement as "Additions to content assets"; the title sits on the balance sheet as "Content assets, net" [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. The expense comes later, as amortization inside cost of revenues, spread "over the shorter of each title's contractual window of availability, estimated period of use or ten years", "on an accelerated basis", with "over 90% of a licensed or produced content asset ... amortized within four years" [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Cash runs ahead because "production costs are paid as the content is created, well in advance of when the content is available on the service and amortized" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. So a growing budget means cash above expense; when the 2023 writers' strike "temporarily shut down new production", cash fell below expense, hence the sub-1.0 ratio that year (our inference) [Q4 2025 letter, p.3].

Management guides both sides: the expense through operating margin (amortization "to increase ~10% for 2026") and the cash through free cash flow and "an annual cash content spend to amortization ratio of ~1.1x", about "$20 billion of cash content spend" [Q2 2026 letter, p.2] [Q2 2026 letter, p.6] [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.5]. A rising ratio lets margin widen while free cash flow lags; a falling one flatters cash.

**Why margins have widened.** Content is a fixed cost: payment terms "are not tied to member usage or the size of our membership base" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. A show costs the same whether 100 million or 300 million households watch it, so revenue from price rises, extra members and ads arrives with little added cost; in 2025 revenue grew 16% and amortization 7% (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Sarandos: "We grow the content spend slower than revenue" [Q2 2026 interview, p.6]. The CFO: "a little over 2 percentage points of margin growth per year" for five years, and "we don't see any ceiling in the near future or even the medium future" [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.5] [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.6]. What does rise with use (delivery over Netflix's own Open Connect server network, payment processing, customer service) is "other cost of revenues", about 15% of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Note 13].

**Where the money goes.** Of each 2025 revenue dollar, 52 cents went to cost of revenues (36 of them amortization, computed), 7 to sales and marketing, 8 to technology and development and 4 to general and administrative, leaving 29.5 cents of operating profit [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Capex is tiny: property and equipment was $2.0 billion at end-2025 against $32.8 billion of content assets [10-K FY2025, Item 8]. The real capital intensity is the content table: standing still means spending roughly the $16 billion a year it amortizes (our inference).

Netflix defines free cash flow as "cash provided by (used in) operating activities less purchases of property and equipment" [Q2 2026 letter, p.6]. It was far below net income in 2021–2022, when cash content spend ran at 1.2–1.45 times amortization, and above it in the 2023 strike year (§3); in 2024, 2025 and the first half of 2026 it ran at roughly 75–90% of net income (computed from the table).

**Read 2026 with care.** First-half net income is $8.7 billion against $6.0 billion a year earlier because the $2.8 billion Warner Bros. termination fee "was recognized in 'interest and other income'" in Q1 [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1] [Q1 2026 letter, p.2]. It is not operating income, which grew 14% in the half (computed) [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1]. The fee is taxed, and the free-cash-flow guide rose from "roughly $11B" to "approximately $12.5B" "due primarily to the after-tax impact" [Q4 2025 letter, p.6] [Q1 2026 letter, p.5]. The half carried "approximately $85 million" of loan-arrangement fees written off in Q1 interest expense when the Warner Bros. financing ended and "$729 million of non-routine payments" for Brazilian tax assessments in operating cash flow, and Q2 free cash flow "included higher cash tax payments due in part to the Warner Bros. termination fee" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 letter, p.6].

**Balance sheet.** Debt is "fixed rate unsecured debt in twelve tranches due between 2026 and 2054" (twelve separate bond issues), with $1 billion due in November 2026 "which we plan to refinance"; net debt (debt less cash) was $5.2 billion at June 30, 2026 [10-K FY2025, Item 7A] [Q2 2026 letter, p.6] [Q2 2026 letter, p.14]. There is no numeric leverage target, only "ample liquidity" and "a solid investment grade rating" [Q2 2026 letter, p.6] [Q4 2025 letter, p.7].

**Return on capital, in plain words.** 2025 operating income of $13.3 billion less tax at the 13.7% effective rate (tax actually charged as a share of pre-tax profit) is about $11.5 billion, on capital employed of about $32.0 billion (equity $26.6 billion plus debt $14.5 billion less cash and short-term investments $9.1 billion): roughly 36% (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Note 11]. Fast write-offs mean book value understates what was spent, so the true figure is lower (our inference).

## 5. Why customers don't leave

Members "can change their plans at any time", so the moat is not a switching cost [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. It is scale economics plus habit. The biggest content budget is spread over the biggest paying base: "We have better programming ROI because we amortize across this global footprint and ... that programming is very travelable" [Q2 2026 interview, p.11]. Non-English titles "drove more than a third of all viewing" in the first half of 2026 [Q2 2026 letter, p.3]. Recommendation technology is a named risk-factor dependency, backed by $3.4 billion of technology spend in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. US subscribers "pay the least per hour of viewing compared to comparable SVOD offerings" (other subscription video services) [Q2 2026 interview, p.10]. The aim is habit, "the first place people go for entertainment and the last they cancel", and management claims "industry-leading retention" [Q1 2026 letter, p.3] [Q2 2026 interview, p.6].

Signs it is weakening: FX-neutral growth in UCAN or EMEA falling below the pace of price increases (UCAN slowed from 18% in Q4 2025 to 10% in Q2 2026 with a price rise only partly in) [Q2 2026 letter, p.7] [Q2 2026 letter, p.2]; view hours growing 1.5–2% a year while membership grows faster, so hours per member fall, partly a mix shift toward lower-viewing markets such as Japan [Q2 2026 letter, p.3] [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.7]; the re-testing of free trials [Q2 2026 letter, p.5]; and the US TV-time share (9.0% in December 2025, per Nielsen) going uncited [Q4 2025 letter, p.6].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility; UCAN and EMEA are 76% of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**1. Saturation and price fatigue in UCAN and EMEA.** "In countries where we have been operating for many years or where we are highly penetrated, our membership growth is slower" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Management's counter: "less than 45%" of broadband households penetrated [Q1 2026 letter, p.3]. Early warning: UCAN growth at or below Q2's 10% once the 2026 price rise is fully in; EMEA FX-neutral growth (16% in 2025, 11% in the first half of 2026) slowing further [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2].

**2. Content cost inflation.** "As competition increases, we see the cost of certain programming increase"; the US guild (writers', actors' and directors' union) contracts "each expire in 2026"; sports rights are growing (an "expanded agreement with the NFL") [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [Q2 2026 letter, p.4]. Early warning: cash content spend above 1.1 times amortization for a full year; amortization or content obligations growing faster than revenue; a margin-guide cut [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 9]. Exposure: the whole 30-point margin.

**3. Advertising falls short.** "We have limited experience and operating history offering advertising on our service, and our advertising revenue may not grow as we expect" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Ads are about a quarter of 2026's planned growth (§2). Early warning: the letter dropping or lowering "approximately $3 billion"; the ad-tier ARM gap described as widening [Q2 2026 letter, p.5].

**4. A large acquisition.** Netflix agreed to pay about $72 billion for Warner Bros.' studios and HBO Max before losing to a higher bid (§7) [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Now: "we are primarily builders, not buyers ... we have a very high bar to do any big M&A" [Q2 2026 interview, p.12]. Early warning: any 8-K Item 1.01 material agreement; buybacks paused again. Exposure: the balance sheet.

**5. Regulation and taxes abroad.** The EU requires "that European works comprise at least thirty percent (30%)" of catalogs, and other countries add levies and quotas [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Brazil's non-income tax assessments produced "approximately $619 million" of cumulative loss in 2025 and $729 million of cash payments in the first half of 2026 [10-K FY2025, Note 9] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Early warning: new tax or levy items in the 10-Q. Exposure: EMEA is 32% of revenue (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**6. YouTube, short-form and free TV take the screen.** The 10-K lists "open content platform providers" among competitors; the CFO: "YouTube is a key competitor" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.7]. Netflix's 9.0% of US TV time (§5) compares with linear (traditional scheduled) TV's "over 40%" [Q4 2025 letter, p.6]. Early warning: flat or falling hours in the annual report. Exposure: all revenue, through retention.

**Concentration.** Not applicable: revenue is "primarily derived from monthly membership fees" from individuals [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. The risk factors name dependence on device and billing partners, payment processors and Amazon Web Services [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]; on supply, "no single supplier that's more than a small minority share of our viewing" [MS TMT conference 2026-03-04, p.8].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Ted Sarandos, 61, co-CEO since 2020 after two decades as Chief Content Officer; Greg Peters, 55, co-CEO since January 2023, at Netflix since 2008; Spencer Neumann, 56, CFO since January 2019 [DEF 14A 2026, Our Company: Executive Officers]. Reed Hastings, "the co-founder and former co-CEO and President of the Company", "transitioned from Executive Chairman to Chairman of the Board and a non-executive director" on April 17, 2025, did not stand for re-election in June 2026, and Jay Hoag became Chairman [DEF 14A 2026, How We Govern and are Governed] [DEF 14A 2026, Netflix 2025 Year in Review] [8-K 2026-04-16, Item 5.02] [8-K 2026-06-05, Item 8.01].

**Pay.** The 2025 bonus paid on FX-neutral revenue (35%) and FX-neutral operating margin (65%) at 117.57% of target; long-term equity is half performance shares, half time-vesting shares [DEF 14A 2026, Compensation Discussion and Analysis]. 2025 totals: Sarandos $53.9 million, Peters $53.2 million, Neumann $20.8 million; 83.7% of votes cast approved (computed) [DEF 14A 2026, Summary Compensation Table] [8-K 2026-06-05, Item 5.07]. In October 2025 the board doubled cash severance outside a change in control to "two times the sum of" salary and target bonus and added retirement vesting, without giving a reason [8-K 2025-11-04, Item 5.02] [DEF 14A 2026, Compensation Discussion and Analysis].

**Ownership.** Directors and officers together held 1.24% at April 6, 2026, Hastings 37.8 million shares [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership of Certain Beneficial Owners and Management]. The proxy lists Vanguard 8.65%, BlackRock 7.34% and FMR 5.29% on filings for 2023 holdings, and notes Vanguard's March 2026 filing shows "0.0%" after a reorganization, so current large holders are not reliably disclosed [DEF 14A 2026, Security Ownership of Certain Beneficial Owners and Management].

**Capital allocation.** "We prioritize reinvestment in the business, both organically and through selective M&A, while maintaining a healthy balance sheet and ample liquidity, and then returning excess cash to shareholders via share repurchases" [Q2 2026 letter, p.6]. No dividend has ever been paid [10-K FY2025, Item 5].

## Glossary

- **Content amortization:** the yearly charge that spreads a title's cost across the years people are expected to watch it, used so the expense follows viewing rather than the cash payment.
- **FX-neutral (constant currency):** growth recomputed as if exchange rates had stayed at the prior-year period's rates and excluding hedging gains or losses, used to see underlying growth in a business that earns 56% of revenue outside the dollar.
- **Free cash flow:** operating cash flow less purchases of property and equipment (Netflix's definition), used as the cash left after content and running costs.
- **ARM (average monthly revenue per paying membership):** monthly revenue divided by average paid memberships, the per-member price measure Netflix reported through 2024.
- **UCAN / EMEA / LATAM / APAC:** United States and Canada; Europe, Middle East and Africa; Latin America; Asia-Pacific, the four regions in which revenue is reported.
