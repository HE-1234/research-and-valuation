## 2. Guidance and forward-looking statements — VERBATIM

### 2a. Guidance items stated in the interview (all by the CFO, in the answer to the first question)

- Q3 2026 revenue growth: "We're guiding, as you say, to 12% revenue growth in Q3 reported, 11% FX neutral." — Spencer Neumann, CFO [Q2 2026 interview, p.5]
- Q3 drivers: "The Q3 revenue drivers are very similar to Q2. It's primarily growth in our subscription revenue from increases in memberships and pricing and higher ads revenue. We continue to see healthy acquisition and retention trends on the membership side and our recent price adjustments are going well on the pricing side." — Spencer Neumann, CFO [Q2 2026 interview, p.5]
- Why Q3 growth is a point lower than Q2 (FX-neutral): "Now recall, there is a little bit of quarter-to-quarter choppiness in growth because last year was more back half weighted. So that may be a little bit of what you see in the deceleration. But honestly, it's not what we manage to. We manage to the full year." — Spencer Neumann, CFO [Q2 2026 interview, p.5]
- Full-year 2026 revenue: "And halfway through the year, we're making strong progress against our goals, and we're tracking to our financial plan for 2026. We expect to deliver another strong year with, as we saw -- as you see in the guide, 13% to 14% top line growth for the full year. That's roughly 12% FX neutral or about $6 billion of incremental revenue year-over-year." — Spencer Neumann, CFO [Q2 2026 interview, p.5]
- Content expense for 2026: "So we're forecasting content expense up about 10% this year. It's a little higher than the 8% we averaged over the last 5 years and below the 14% that we averaged over the past decade." — Ted Sarandos, Co-CEO [Q2 2026 interview, p.6]. - Live programming share of 2026 content budget: "So live, we expect will be 5% of our content budget this year, but we think that will only be 1% of view hours." — Greg Peters, Co-CEO [Q2 2026 interview, p.5]
- Not stated in the interview by any speaker: an operating-margin figure for Q3 or 2026, a free-cash-flow figure, a cash-content-spend figure, an advertising-revenue dollar figure or the "roughly double" phrase, a membership count, a capital-structure or leverage target, any Q4 2026 or 2027 number. ## 3. Vision, strategy and growth engines as management described them

Vision and how the business is steered
- "So look, we don't manage the business on a quarter-to-quarter basis. Our goal is to sustain healthy revenue and profit growth. We talked about that in our letter every quarter." — Spencer Neumann [Q2 2026 interview, p.5]
- The three dimensions of engagement: "We look at these as 3 dimensions: quality, variety, quantity, because they, taken collectively, drive acquisition, they drive retention, they drive the value that our consumers and our advertising partners ascribe to our service." — Greg Peters [Q2 2026 interview, p.6]
- What ultimately matters: "We get more and more sophisticated because we know, ultimately, it's combined quality, variety and quantity of engagement that translates into satisfaction and value for members. And that drives the strong business outcomes we see right now, industry-leading retention. We see increased willingness to pay, strong advertiser demand. And those ultimately drive the top-level metrics of our business, revenue and operating profit, which are really the ultimate signs of our health." — Greg Peters [Q2 2026 interview, p.6]
- Scale as the flywheel: "These in combination all deliver this kind of flywheel of advantages. We have the biggest, most engaged audience in the world. Creators and advertisers love that. We lead the industry in monetization. We have better programming ROI because we amortize across this global footprint and that -- very often, that programming is very travelable. This is good for our members. It's good for our business. It creates a really healthy model for organic growth." — Ted Sarandos [Q2 2026 interview, p.11]
- Investment approach: "But the second one is that we're really disciplined investors. So there isn't some hyper acceleration of content investment. We grow the content spend slower than revenue while we're continuing to invest in a huge addressable market." / "The third thing we want you to remember here is that when we expand into new entertainment offerings, new initiatives, we do it gradually. We do it where we believe we can add more value for our members, and we do it where we believe we have the right to win. And then we look for the positive signals before we invest at material scale. This is our MO. It's been our MO for some time." — Ted Sarandos [Q2 2026 interview, p.6]
- Value proposition: "I believe that we are delivering one of the best entertainment values that has ever existed. As a comparison point, if you go to the U.S. and you take what Netflix subscribers are paying, they pay the least per hour of viewing compared to comparable SVOD offerings. In some cases, they would have to pay twice as much per hour for a competitive service. And our ads plan at $8.99 in the United States, we think, is an amazing entry point." — Greg Peters [Q2 2026 interview, p.10]
- The letter's three areas of focus ("delivering more entertainment value, leveraging technology to improve every aspect of our service, and improving monetization") are not recited as a list in the interview; Wang's topic order follows them. [Q2 2026 letter, p.1; Q2 2026 interview, p.5–13]

## 6. Content spend vs amortization; operating leverage and margins — VERBATIM

- The spend-slower-than-revenue rule: "So there isn't some hyper acceleration of content investment. We grow the content spend slower than revenue while we're continuing to invest in a huge addressable market. So we're forecasting content expense up about 10% this year. It's a little higher than the 8% we averaged over the last 5 years and below the 14% that we averaged over the past decade." — Ted Sarandos [Q2 2026 interview, p.6]
- Return on content: "The result there is a highly attractive, scalable return on content investment, and it ladders up to healthy business metrics that Greg just detailed and our strong growth in revenue, dollar profit and profit margin." — Ted Sarandos [Q2 2026 interview, p.7]
- Global amortization as the source of better ROI: "We have better programming ROI because we amortize across this global footprint and that -- very often, that programming is very travelable." — Ted Sarandos [Q2 2026 interview, p.11]
- AI lowering cost per unit of content: "we are going to have better and more impact for every dollar we spend on our programming. So content creation time lines can be shortened and quality can be enhanced. So the cost savings will likely be reinvested into more content on the service" — Ted Sarandos [Q2 2026 interview, p.12]
- Games investment relative to content: "still very small relative to our overall content spend" — Greg Peters [Q2 2026 interview, p.11]
- The interview contains no statement on the gap between cash content spend and content amortization, no ratio, and no statement about which cost lines grow slower than revenue. Those appear in the letter: "we continue to expect content amortization to grow slower in the second half of the year and to increase ~10% for 2026" [Q2 2026 letter, p.2]; "an annual cash content spend to amortization ratio of ~1.1x" [Q2 2026 letter, p.6]; "Our forecast implies annual operating income growth of 20%+ for 2026" [Q2 2026 letter, p.2].
