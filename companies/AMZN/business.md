# Amazon.com, Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-08._

## 1. What they do

Amazon is two businesses under one name. The first is the store: a website and app where Amazon sells goods itself and, more often, lets other merchants sell theirs while Amazon runs the warehouse, the van and the customer service for a fee [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The second is Amazon Web Services (AWS), which rents computing power, storage, databases and artificial-intelligence services to companies and governments so they need not buy their own machines [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Amazon reports three segments: North America and International, which are the store split by geography, and AWS [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

Who pays. Shoppers pay for goods and for Prime, "a membership program that includes fast, free shipping on tens of millions of items, access to award-winning movies and series, live sports, and other benefits" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Merchants (sellers) pay to list, store and ship their products, and Amazon is not the legal seller: "We are not the seller of record in these transactions. We earn fixed fees, a percentage of sales, per-unit activity fees, interest, or some combination thereof" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Advertisers, mostly those same sellers and brands, pay to have their products shown first [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. The store segments brought in 82% of 2025 sales, but AWS earned 57% of the operating profit (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 10].

A short history. Jeff Bezos founded the company in 1994 and was chief executive from May 1996 until July 2021, when Andy Jassy, who had led AWS since 2006, took over and Bezos became Executive Chair [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Amazon sold shares to the public in May 1997 [2025 shareholder letter, p.10]. AWS began with storage and compute; Netflix moved onto it in 2008 [2025 shareholder letter, p.1]. Amazon bought Whole Foods Market in 2017, designed its first chip, Graviton, in 2018, and has spent seven years building the Amazon Leo satellite network [2025 shareholder letter, p.3] [2025 shareholder letter, p.4] [2025 shareholder letter, p.2].

That is about 3.3 years of AWS's trailing revenue (computed) [Q2 2026 release].

## 3. The economics

**Does cost rise with usage?** Two answers. The store is largely linear: every extra package needs buying, picking, packing and a van, and shipping costs alone were $102.7 billion in 2025 [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. AWS is fixed-asset-heavy: data centers, servers and networking gear are bought up front and depreciated, meaning their cost is spread over their working lives and hits the income statement whether or not customers rent them; the "majority of infrastructure costs" in the technology and infrastructure line "are allocated to the AWS segment based on usage" [10-K FY2025, Note 10]. Our inference: that is why AWS margins swing and store margins creep.

Amazon does not report gross margin (sales minus the direct cost of what was sold): "We believe that operating income is a more meaningful measure than gross profit and gross margin", operating income being profit before interest, investment gains and tax [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. **Where the money goes.** Technology and infrastructure carries most of AWS's cost, the depreciation of new data centers and the Leo satellite spending, which is expensed (counted as a cost at once, not spread over years) until the service "achieves commercial viability"; it grew 23% in 2025, while fulfillment fell as a share of sales for the third straight year [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Profit quality.** Q2 2026 net income of $62.6 billion is not a measure of the business. It includes $53.4 billion of non-operating other income, of which $50.5 billion is a paper markup of Amazon's Anthropic preferred stock (a class of shares ranking ahead of ordinary shares) "to reflect observable changes in price related to Anthropic's fundings", with $15.9 billion of related tax expense in the six-month provision (the tax charge booked for the half-year), mostly deferred (owed later) rather than paid [Q2 2026 release] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 7]. Even operating income had two one-offs in Q2 2026: about $640 million of tariff refunds in North America's cost of sales, and $551 million of unrealized (paper) gains on energy contracts, mostly in AWS; the CFO put the pair at "approximately $1.2 billion" [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1] [Q2 2026 call]. Prime Day (the annual members-only sale) fell in Q3 in 2025 and Q2 in 2026, lifting Q2 2026 growth and depressing Q3 [10-Q Q1 2026, Item 2] [Q2 2026 release] [Q2 2026 call].

**Return on capital, in plain words.** For each dollar of property and equipment on the books at year end, Amazon earned 18 cents of operating profit in 2023, 27 cents in 2024 and 22 cents in 2025, and 21 cents in the twelve months to June 2026 on the $446 billion then held (computed) [10-K FY2025, Item 8] [10-K FY2025, Note 10] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1] [Q2 2026 release]. Our inference: the return is slipping because assets are bought ahead of the revenue they are meant to earn, as management itself says. At June 30, 2026 cash and marketable securities (investments that can be sold quickly) were $123.0 billion against $133.0 billion face value of debt (the amount owed at maturity), before July's $25.0 billion of new notes [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 2] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 5] [8-K 2026-07-09, Item 8.01].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Shoppers: Prime and speed.** Amazon treats free fast shipping as marketing and says it will "continue offering them indefinitely" [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. It delivered "over 40% more items same day or overnight" in the first half of 2026, and paid units grew 17% in Q2 [Q2 2026 call] [Q2 2026 release]. Jassy's argument: with selection, price and speed, "people consider you for a lot more of their total purchases and shopping visits" [Q2 2026 call]. Sign of weakening: paid-unit growth falling toward single digits.

**Sellers: traffic and fulfillment.** Sellers supply 61% of units and pay for two things hard to get together anywhere else, Prime shoppers and the delivery network, so leaving costs both (our inference) [Q2 2026 release]. The dependence runs both ways: "China-based sellers account for significant portions of our third-party seller services and advertising revenues" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Sign of weakening: the seller share of units falling, or seller services growing slower than online stores.

**AWS: switching costs, backlog and chips.** Our inference: moving a company's software and data out of a cloud is slow and expensive, which holds customers more than price does. The $496 billion backlog is money already signed [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]. Management's stated edge: customers "want their AI inference to reside near their other applications and data, and more of it resides in AWS than anywhere else" (inference is running a trained AI model to answer requests) [Q2 2026 call]. Amazon's own chips add a second lock: Anthropic's and OpenAI's commitments each include "contractual obligations related to the performance of AWS chips", so both have agreed to run on Trainium [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1] [Q2 2026 release]. Sign of weakening: backlog flat or falling for two quarters.

**Advertisers: shopping intent.** Our inference: an ad shown to someone already searching for a product on Amazon is one click from a sale, which is why sellers pay for it. Sign of weakening: advertising growth falling below third-party seller services growth.

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility.

**1. The AI build-out does not pay.** What has to happen: AWS demand or AI prices fall after the roughly $220 billion of 2026 spending is committed, leaving depreciation on machines without renters. Free cash flow is already negative, bonds (notes) outstanding rose from $68.0 billion to $132.1 billion in six months plus $25.0 billion sold in July, and Amazon expects "additional financing activities in 2026" [10-K FY2025, Note 6] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 5] [8-K 2026-07-09, Item 8.01] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 2]. Jassy's answer, his "ROIC equation" (ROIC is return on invested capital): data-center money is spent "two years before" servers go in but the buildings earn "for 30-plus years"; servers break even in "a little less than three years", last "at least five to six years", and most AI capacity is "contracted for at least five-year terms" [Q2 2026 call]. That is a claim, not yet a result. Early warning: backlog growth stalling while capex rises; the $17.5 billion term loan (a bank loan taken in one lump) being drawn. Exposure: AWS supplied 57% of 2025 operating income (computed, §4); the debt sits on the whole company.

**2. AWS loses share to Microsoft, Google or customers' own chips.** Amazon names as competitors "companies that provide information technology services or products" and relies on "a limited group of suppliers for semiconductor products" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: AWS growth slowing for two quarters; Trainium commitments not renewed. Exposure: the same 57%.

**3. Antitrust and gatekeeper regulation of the marketplace and Prime.** Antitrust law bars a dominant company from using its position to shut out rivals. Since March 2020 private plaintiffs, state attorneys general and the FTC have alleged "abuse of dominance, monopolization, and attempted monopolization" over "pricing policies, selection of the Featured Offers, use of seller data, advertising practices, the structure of Prime, and promotion of our own products", seeking damages and "structural relief" (court-ordered changes to how the business is built, up to a break-up) [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4]. Status: "most of Amazon's motions to dismiss were granted in part, but in each case, at least some of the claims survived"; one U.S. class action (a suit on behalf of a whole group) has been certified, meaning allowed to proceed as a group, and three await that ruling [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4]. A $2.5 billion Q3 2025 charge settled "a lawsuit with the FTC" that the filings do not identify by case [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Regulators who treat large technology companies as online "gatekeepers" also have open investigations into "its fulfillment network and Prime" and parts of AWS, and Amazon faces "tax and other challenges in Italy" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A] [10-Q Q2 2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: any order or fee cap changing how Amazon ranks offers, charges sellers or bundles Prime. Exposure: third-party seller services and advertising, $241 billion of 2025 sales (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 10].

**4. Retail competition and tariffs.** Rivals are not named; the competitor list covers "physical, e-commerce, and omnichannel retailers" (omnichannel means selling both online and in stores) and the risks name "tariffs proposed or implemented by the U.S. and other countries" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: online-store growth turning negative; North America margin below its 2024 level. Exposure: the stores' $34.4 billion of 2025 operating income (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 10].

**5. Labor and cost to serve.** With about 1.6 million employees, Amazon warns that union efforts "if successful, decrease our operational flexibility" and faces litigation over "the characterization of delivery drivers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Its answer is automation: "over one million robots" in fulfillment centers, with the robotic-arm fleet to more than double in 2026 [2025 shareholder letter, p.2] [Q2 2026 call]. Early warning: fulfillment plus shipping cost growing faster than paid units.

**6. Amazon Leo.** Q1 2026 guidance included "approximately $1 billion of higher year-over-year Amazon Leo costs", all expensed, and Amazon has agreed to buy Globalstar for about $10.9 billion including debt, closing expected in 2027 [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 4]. Early warning: service launch slipping past 2026. Exposure: spending only, so far.

**Concentration.** No customer concentration (a single customer large enough to have to be named) is disclosed, nor Anthropic's or OpenAI's revenue contribution; "no vendor accounted for 10% or more of our purchases" [10-K FY2025, Note 7]. The chip-supplier and China-seller dependencies are quoted above.

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Andy Jassy, 58, has been President and CEO since July 2021 after leading AWS from 2006; Brian Olsavsky has been CFO since June 2015; Matt Garman has run AWS since June 2024 and Doug Herrington the stores since July 2022 [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Jeff Bezos, 62, is Executive Chair and owned 950 million shares, 8.8%, in February 2026; the board says this "is appropriate given Mr. Bezos's role in founding Amazon and his significant ownership stake" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [DEF 14A 2026, p.45] [DEF 14A 2026, p.16]. The board has eleven members after Keith Alexander did not stand in May 2026; a shareholder proposal to require a board chair from outside management drew 14% of votes cast (computed) [DEF 14A 2026, p.2] [8-K 2026-05-22, Item 5.07]. Bezos's Blue Origin is a launch supplier to Leo, paid about $1.8 billion since the start of 2025 [DEF 14A 2026, p.69].

**Pay.** Each named executive other than Bezos earns a $365,000 salary plus periodic grants of restricted stock units (shares handed over in instalments for staying), with "No annual cash bonuses or incentive awards" and no performance metric: "we avoid tying compensation to discrete, short-term performance goals, financial or otherwise" [DEF 14A 2026, p.51] [DEF 14A 2026, p.46]. Jassy's only award since becoming CEO is the 2021 grant, vesting 50,000 shares a quarter through February 2031; no named executive received a grant in 2025, and Bezos "has never received any stock-based compensation" [DEF 14A 2026, p.56] [DEF 14A 2026, p.57]. Support for the pay vote rose from 78% in 2025 to 94% in 2026 (computed) [DEF 14A 2026, p.28] [8-K 2026-05-22, Item 5.07]. Our inference: pay tied only to the share price over five to ten years suits a management willing to run negative free cash flow for years.

**Cash.** The stated goal is "long-term, sustainable growth in free cash flow" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The past year: no buybacks, no dividend, notes outstanding nearly doubled in six months, and tens of billions into two AI labs that have each also signed AWS commitments of $100 billion or more [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1].

## Glossary

- **AWS (Amazon Web Services):** Amazon's business that rents computing, storage, databases and AI services from its data centers to other organizations.
- **Trainium and Graviton:** chips Amazon designs itself, Trainium for AI work and Graviton for general computing, used inside AWS instead of buying every chip from Nvidia or Intel.
- **Fulfillment:** everything between an order and the doorstep: storing, picking, packing and handing to a carrier, including for sellers' goods.
- **Depreciation:** spreading the cost of a long-lived asset such as a server over the years it is used, so a purchase made today shows up as expense for several years afterward.
- **Free cash flow:** Amazon's term for operating cash flow minus cash spent on property and equipment (net of proceeds and incentives), used to see whether the business funds its own investment.
- **Backlog:** the value of signed customer contracts longer than a year whose revenue has not yet been recorded, used to see how much future AWS revenue is already committed.
