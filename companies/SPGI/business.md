# S&P Global Inc. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026. Written 2026-09-09._

## 1. What they do

S&P Global sells the reference points that financial and energy markets run on: credit ratings on borrowers, the S&P 500 and other indexes that funds track, the Platts benchmark prices for commodities, and the data and software analysts work with [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. Its own words: "a global, diversified, and highly differentiated provider of benchmarks, data, analytics and workflow solutions in the global capital and energy and commodity markets" [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]. Since July 1, 2026 it has four divisions: Ratings, Indices (S&P Dow Jones Indices), Energy (Commodity Insights until November 13, 2025) and Market Intelligence [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1] [8-K 2025-11-13]. Customers are banks, asset managers, insurers, borrowing companies and governments, exchanges, and energy producers and traders [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. "Benchmark products" (Ratings, the indexes, Platts and the resale of ratings content) are "~65% of S&P Global revenue" and "~80% of S&P Global adjusted operating profit" [Q2 2026 slides, p.7].

History. Incorporated in New York in December 1925; the rating business has "played a leading role for over 150 years" [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. On February 28, 2022 it merged with IHS Markit for 113.8 million shares worth about $43.5 billion, adding most of Market Intelligence and Energy plus Mobility, a car-data business; FY2021 figures below are for a much smaller company [10-K FY2023, Note 2]. It then sold Engineering Solutions (May 2023, $975 million) and its half of the OSTTRA trade-processing venture (October 2025, $1.5 billion of proceeds) [10-K FY2025, Note 2]. On July 1, 2026 it spun off Mobility to shareholders, one Mobility Global (NYSE: MBGL) share per S&P Global share [10-Q Q2 2026, Note 1]. Martina Cheung became CEO on November 1, 2024 [10-K FY2025, Part I, Executive Officers] [DEF 14A 2026, p.32].

**Ratings: the borrower pays for its own grade.** The issuer (the company, bank or government borrowing) is the customer: transaction revenue is recognized "by issuing a rating on our customer's instruments" [10-K FY2025, Item 1] [10-K FY2025, Note 1]. Transaction revenue is fees for rating new corporate and government bonds, structured-finance deals and bank loans; non-transaction revenue is "fees for surveillance of a credit rating, annual fees for customer relationship-based pricing programs, fees for entity credit ratings" and research at Crisil, its Indian subsidiary [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. So a new bond deal pays a one-off fee and the issuer then pays yearly to keep the rating monitored: half the division moves with how much debt the world issues, half is an annuity (a steady yearly payment). Ratings are published for anyone to read, so investors cannot easily be charged (our inference; the 10-K never says "issuer-pays"), and regulators keep the model under review (§6).

**Indices: a toll on other people's assets.** Fund managers pay "asset-linked fees" on the assets in ETFs and mutual funds that track an index; exchanges pay "sales usage-based royalties based on trading volumes of derivatives contracts" such as S&P 500 futures; data users pay subscriptions [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. An index is a rulebook and a calculation: one more fund or contract costs almost nothing to license, while the fee rises with the customer's assets or volume (our inference; no marginal cost is disclosed). CME Group owns 27% of the venture and can at any time make S&P Global buy at least 20% of that stake, carried at $4.9 billion [10-K FY2025, Note 9].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Ratings: embedded in the plumbing.** S&P Global Ratings is registered with the SEC as a Nationally Recognized Statistical Rating Organization, a status created so regulators could use agency ratings in capital rules; the 10-K calls it "an important part of the world's financial infrastructure" [10-K FY2025, Item 7] [10-K FY2025, Item 1]. A borrower wanting the widest set of buyers needs a rating their rules recognise, then pays surveillance for the life of the debt [10-K FY2025, Item 1].

**Indices: the benchmark is the product.** "S&P Dow Jones Indices remains the #1 global provider of indices based on ETF-linked AUM, and ranked #1 in flow capture during the second quarter" (AUM: assets under management; flow capture: share of new money going into ETFs); in June one S&P 500 ETF passed "$1 trillion in AUM" [Q2 2026 slides, p.10]. An ETF that changed its index would change its name, track record and what its holders bought (our inference; the filings do not discuss switching costs).

**Data and workflow: hard to copy, hard to rip out.** Management says "less than 5% of total revenue comes from undifferentiated data", and that Compustat and SNL are hard to replicate because "Often, employees would have to physically scan and paper documents in local offices" [Q1 2026 call, prepared remarks, Cheung]. Some workflow products "function as networks for industry groups, not just for an individual client" [Q1 2026 call, Q&A, Faiza Alwy / Deutsche Bank]. Platts assessments settle "Platts based contracts" on commodity exchanges, hence the royalties [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. Recurring revenue was 97.0% of Market Intelligence and 91.2% of Energy in Q2 2026 [Q2 2026 supplemental, p.14] [Q2 2026 supplemental, p.13]. Pricing is by enterprise value: "we don't do seat-based licensing. We don't do usage only" [Q1 2026 call, Q&A, Toni Kaplan / Morgan Stanley].

**Signs of weakening.** Market Intelligence subscription growth below its recent 6%, or its recurring share slipping; Ratings transaction revenue growing slower than billed issuance; ETF assets linked to S&P DJI growing slower than the market, or the "#1 in flow capture" line leaving the deck [Q2 2026 slides, p.10]; asset-linked fees lagging average assets (Q1 2026 saw "a modest decline in average realized price") [Q1 2026 call, prepared remarks, Aboaf]; and competition that can "drive us to lower the fees we charge" showing in the numbers [10-K FY2025, Item 1A].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked by plausibility. Of $6,596 million of FY2025 segment operating profit, Ratings was 46%, Indices 19%, Market Intelligence 15%, Energy 14% and Mobility 6% (computed) [10-K FY2025, Note 12].

**1. An issuance drought.** Transaction revenue "is essentially dependent on the number and dollar volume of rated debt securities issued in the capital markets" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. In 2022 it fell from $2,253 million to $1,241 million and Ratings profit fell $957 million (computed) [10-K FY2023, Note 12]. Q2 2026's 25% jump in billed issuance was "driven primarily by large issuances to finance AI infrastructure expansion, as well as strong M&A issuance" (M&A: mergers and acquisitions) [Q2 2026 slides, p.8]. Early warning: the monthly billed-issuance workbook; credit spreads (the extra interest riskier borrowers pay over government bonds). Exposure: transaction revenue is 16% of company revenue (computed) and, with fixed costs, more of profit [10-K FY2025, Note 12].

**2. Regulators change how rating agencies are paid or used.** The SEC "can censure NRSROs, revoke their registration or limit or suspend their registration" [10-K FY2025, Item 7]. The 10-K lists possible rules "seeking to reduce regulatory and investor reliance on credit ratings or to increase competition among credit rating agencies, provisions regarding remuneration and rotation of credit rating agencies" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A], and gives "no assurance that the SEC or another regulator will not seek remedies" [10-K FY2025, Note 13]. Early warning: a bill or European consultation on agency pay; an SEC order. Exposure: all of Ratings, 46% of segment profit (computed).

**3. Index fees get squeezed.** Market changes "drive us to lower the fees we charge", and competitors include "non-traditional providers, many of whom are our clients, such as asset managers" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A], and "equity flows from active to passive" is on the company's list of swing factors [10-Q Q2 2026, Forward-Looking Statements]. Early warning: asset-linked fees lagging average ETF assets for several quarters; a big ETF sponsor (fund issuer) switching index provider. Exposure: asset-linked fees were $1,206 million in 2025, 65% of a division earning 19% of segment profit [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**4. AI makes proprietary data cheap.** The 10-K warns that competitors may deploy AI "in ways that make collection and processing of information relatively inexpensive or free" and that "Third parties could also use our data with AI tools to create their own insights and potentially supplant our products and services" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. The Q2 deck notes "Some customers looking to build in-house solutions with S&P Global data in lieu of reliance on third party platforms" [Q2 2026 slides, p.13]. Early warning: Market Intelligence subscription growth or recurring share falling. Exposure: Market Intelligence and Energy, 29% of segment profit (computed).

**5. A key partner or supplier walks.** CME Group can force S&P Global to buy at least 20% of its 27% Indices stake at any time ($4.9 billion carried), and all of it within 15 days of a change of control (a takeover of S&P Global) [10-K FY2025, Note 9]. "Many of our suppliers are also our competitors", and "any disruption of or interference with our use of AWS would impact our operations" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: changed CME terms; a disclosed supplier dispute. Exposure: a cash call (CME) or product gaps (data).

**6. Litigation and reputation.** An Australian class action filed in 2020 over collateralized debt obligations (bundled-debt securities) rated in 2005–2007 is open with no amount disclosed; the company "can provide no assurance that we will not be obligated to pay significant amounts" [10-K FY2025, Note 13]. "We have faced significant regulatory and media scrutiny following prior periods of volatility" [10-K FY2025, Item 1A]. Early warning: Ratings' legal costs ($42 million in 2025) [10-K FY2025, Item 7].

**Other exposures.** The Mobility spin must "qualify for tax-free treatment"; failure would be a tax bill [10-Q Q2 2026, Forward-Looking Statements]. Customer concentration is not a risk (§2).

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

**People.** Martina Cheung, 50, CEO since November 1, 2024, joined in 2010 and ran Ratings and Market Intelligence; Eric Aboaf, 61, CFO since February 19, 2025, came from State Street [10-K FY2025, Part I, Executive Officers] [DEF 14A 2026, p.32]. The bench has churned: a new Indices CEO (November 2025), Energy's co-president retired, the Market Intelligence president's exit announced May 26, 2026 (effective July 30), and a Market Intelligence reorganisation on July 6, 2026 [10-K FY2025, Part I, Executive Officers] [8-K 2025-10-30] [8-K 2026-05-26] [8-K 2026-07-06]. **Board and ownership.** Eleven directors under an independent Chair, Ian Livingston [DEF 14A 2026, p.3]. Vanguard held 9.49% and BlackRock 7.99% at March 2, 2026; insiders together held under 1%; there is no controlling owner [DEF 14A 2026, p.101].

**Pay.** The CEO's 2025 total was $12.8 million: $1.0 million salary, $2.4 million bonus, $9.0 million of stock and $0.4 million of other pay; pay ratio 320 to 1 [DEF 14A 2026, p.73] [DEF 14A 2026, p.90]. Growth, margin and per-share earnings, which buybacks help; nothing on return on capital.

**Cash.** Buy back shares with most of the cash, raise the dividend every year, reshape the portfolio by deal.

## Glossary

- **Investment grade / high yield:** the two broad bands of credit ratings; investment grade (BBB- and above at S&P) is safer and cheaper to borrow at, high yield is riskier.
- **Structured finance:** bonds backed by pools of loans (mortgages, car loans, corporate loans) rather than by one company; a large rated market with its own fee stream.
- **Surveillance:** the ongoing monitoring of an existing rating, billed as an annual fee.
- **ETF (exchange-traded fund):** a fund that trades on a stock exchange and usually tracks an index; the index owner is paid a fee based on the fund's assets.
- **Exchange-traded derivatives (ETDs):** futures and options listed on an exchange, here contracts on the S&P 500 and other indexes; the index owner earns a royalty per contract traded.
- **Organic constant currency (OCC) growth:** revenue growth with acquisitions, divestitures and exchange-rate moves stripped out.
- **GAAP / adjusted / pro forma:** GAAP is the standard U.S. accounting rulebook; "adjusted" (non-GAAP) strips out acquired-intangible amortization and one-off items; "pro forma" here means restated as if Mobility had already been spun off.
- **EBITDA:** operating profit before depreciation and amortization, the profit measure used in the company's leverage ratio.
