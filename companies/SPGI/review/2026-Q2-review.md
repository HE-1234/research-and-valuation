## 1. Rubric (§14)

Read as the owner would: a smart 16-year-old with no finance background.

1. **Can I explain what this company does, and who pays it, in two sentences?** **Yes.** b:6 and the three bold paragraphs of §2 do it: S&P Global sells the reference points markets run on; borrowers pay for their own credit rating (a fee per new bond, then a yearly fee to keep it monitored), fund managers and exchanges pay a toll on the assets and contracts that track its indexes, and banks, fund managers and energy companies pay yearly subscriptions for its data and tools.
2. **Do I know exactly what would kill it, and what the early warning sign is?** **Yes.** §6 ranks six scenarios with the profit pool exposed: an issuance drought (the monthly billed-issuance workbook), a change in how rating agencies are paid or used (a bill, a European consultation, an SEC order), an index-fee squeeze (asset-linked fees lagging average ETF assets), AI making proprietary data cheap (Market Intelligence subscription growth or recurring share falling), CME or AWS changing terms, litigation. The exposures are quantified from the FY2025 segment table (Ratings 46% of segment profit).
3. **Do I know why the margins are what they are, and whether cost scales with usage?** **Yes.** §3 says there is no gross-margin line and why, shows cost is people and technology (headcount +10% against revenue +23%, capex about 1% of revenue), shows Ratings' operating leverage in both directions (2022 revenue −26%, profit −36%; 2023–2025 revenue +42%, profit +62%), shows Indices' flat 66–69% margin, and explains that Market Intelligence's low margin is mostly the $588 million a year of merger amortization.
4. **Could I predict what the scorecard will check next quarter, from §5 of the outlook alone?** **Yes.** Ten claims, each single-direction and tied to a named document and figure (release p.3 guidance table, supplemental p.18 division ranges, slides p.8 billed issuance, supplemental p.22 recast margin, Note 8 buybacks, Note 1 discontinued operations, slides p.13 AI indicators, Note 2 closing window). After the p.28 → p.29 tag fix every quote points at the right page.
5. **Did nothing in the report require knowledge I don't have?** **Yes, after the glosses below.** Before review a dozen terms were bare (annuity, frequent-issuer programmes, flow capture, credit spreads, M&A, sponsor, change of control, vest, ICP, restructuring, leverage measure; in the outlook S&P DJI, ETD, API, EDO, CERA); all are now glossed inline (see "Fixed directly"). The three explanations the owner asked for are present and plain: how a rating agency gets paid (b:28, "the borrower pays for its own grade ... a new bond deal pays a one-off fee and the issuer then pays yearly to keep the rating monitored"), why index licensing is so profitable (b:40, "a toll on other people's assets ... one more fund or contract costs almost nothing to license, while the fee rises with the customer's assets or volume", with the 66–70% margin row), and what a subscription data business looks like (b:54, annual subscriptions recognized over the contract, 83–88% of revenue, recurring share 97% and 91%, renewal rates not disclosed).
