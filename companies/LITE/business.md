# Lumentum Holdings Inc. — The Business
_As of Q4 FY2026 (quarter ended June 27, 2026). Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Lumentum makes the parts that turn data into light and back: laser chips for transceivers, finished transceivers, and optical circuit switches [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In plain words (ours, not the filing's): data-center computers talk over glass fiber, a transceiver is the small device at each end that converts electrical signals into laser light and back, and an optical circuit switch redirects light between fibers so a data center can be rewired without turning the light back into electricity. Its parts also carry telecom traffic between cities, under oceans and, increasingly, between AI data centers [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Two smaller lines: industrial lasers for cutting, welding and drilling inside factory tools, and laser light sources for 3D-sensing cameras in smartphones [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

Who pays: "cloud and network service providers, AI infrastructure providers, and network equipment manufacturers" [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. In plain terms: transceiver makers, the giant cloud companies (hyperscalers) building AI data centers, telecom equipment makers, and makers of factory tools and phones. Unlike a chip designer that outsources everything, Lumentum owns wafer fabs (the plants that make the chips) and assembly sites in the United States, Thailand, China, the United Kingdom, Slovenia and Japan, plus contract manufacturers mostly in Thailand, Taiwan, Malaysia and the Philippines [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. The laser chips are made on indium phosphide, a semiconductor material, in two fabs in Japan; management's label is "a premier laser chip manufacturer" [Q4 FY2026 call].

History. Lumentum was spun off from JDS Uniphase in August 2015 [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. It bought breadth: Oclaro (2018), NeoPhotonics and IPG Photonics' telecom transmission lines (2022), and Cloud Light (2023), a maker of "advanced optical modules for data center applications" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; 10-K FY2024, Note 4]. US export rules ended all shipments to Huawei, "historically our largest networking customer in China", in early 2024 [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. February 2025 brought the first CEO change since the spin-off, from Alan Lowe to Michael Hurlston [DEF 14A 2025, CD&A]. Then FY2026: revenue rose 83% to $3.0 billion and, in the 10-K's words, "This demand is outpacing our current supply which has required us to make decisions on supply allocation" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 7].

## 2. How the money comes in

Lumentum sells hardware, mostly on purchase orders with no volume promise: customers buy "under purchase orders or under contracts that do not contain volume or long-term purchase commitments" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Since FY2026 it reports as one business and splits revenue only by product type [10-K FY2026, Note 17]. **Components** are building blocks a customer puts inside its own product: laser chips, laser sub-assemblies and telecom line subsystems [10-K FY2026, Item 1]. **Systems** are finished products: transceiver modules, optical circuit switches and industrial lasers [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

## 3. The economics

Lumentum is a factory business, so cost behaves like a factory's. A wafer fab costs about the same to run half full or full, and so do the assembly lines in Thailand. When revenue nearly doubles through the same buildings, each dollar of sales carries less fixed cost and gross margin (revenue minus the cost of making the product) jumps. In FY2026 gross margin rose to 41.7% from 28.0% in FY2025, and "Approximately 54% of the gross margin dollar increase was driven by lower manufacturing costs as a percentage of revenue, primarily due to higher internal factory utilization", 29% by "a mix shift to higher margin products" and 17% by lower acquisition amortization [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. The lever cuts both ways: in FY2024 revenue fell 23% (computed) and gross margin collapsed to 18.5%, "lower revenue" alone costing 12.9 points [10-K FY2024, Item 7; 10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Materials rise with every unit, factories do not; that the factories are the bigger piece is our inference, consistent with the 54% utilization share above, and management's phrase is "our operating model delivers outsized leverage" [Q4 FY2026 call]. Where the money goes: R&D was $356.5 million (11.8% of revenue) and selling and administration $363.2 million in FY2026, both growing far slower than revenue, which is why operating margin rose faster than gross margin [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. Employees, 87% in manufacturing (computed), nearly doubled in two years; labour does scale with volume [10-K FY2026, Item 1].

Capital intensity is the price of the model. Capex rose from 5.3% of revenue in FY2022 to 15.0% in FY2026, 3.5 times depreciation, the yearly charge for wear on existing plant and equipment (computed): the company is building far faster than its plant wears out, and $181.4 million more equipment was delivered but unpaid at year-end, against $43.4 million a year earlier [10-K FY2026, Item 8]. Purchase obligations, binding orders mostly for materials due within a year, rose from $837.6 million to $2.4 billion [10-K FY2025, Note 16; 10-K FY2026, Note 16]. Customers help pay: "we have secured multiple long-term customer agreements that helped offset our planned capital expenditures" [Q4 FY2026 call]. There is no capex guidance; the balance sheet shows Thailand's plant rising from $218.6 million to $450.6 million and Japan's from $144.3 million to $232.1 million in a year [10-K FY2026, Note 17].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Laser know-how plus owned capacity, while lasers are scarce.** The core skill is making indium phosphide laser chips in volume with very consistent performance: "We have a distinctive ability to deliver at scale to a very tight set of specifications, which enables superior yields in transceiver manufacturing", and so "we are able to command a nice price premium" [Q4 FY2026 call]. Shortage reinforces this: EMLs ship "behind customer demand", pump lasers are "effectively sold out for the foreseeable future", high-power lasers are "very much further behind" [Q4 FY2026 call]. A customer cannot leave a supplier it cannot replace. Sign of weakening: gross margin falling while revenue still grows, or pricing talk vanishing from calls; the 10-K already warns of "commoditization" (products becoming interchangeable, so only price matters) from "predominantly Asia-based competitors" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A].

**Qualification and long-term agreements.** Customers design a laser into a product, and the company claims to be "first to market in many instances ahead of larger competitors, giving us a market share advantage that we should be able to maintain through the cycle" [Q4 FY2026 call]. In pump lasers it claims "somewhere between 70%, 80% share" under mostly "3-year arrangements" that "have pricing built into them" and, per the CEO, "in most instances, I think they're take-or-pay" (the customer pays whether or not it takes the product), which his colleague did not confirm [Q4 FY2026 call]. Sign of weakening: agreements not renewed, or the 10-K line that most customers have no "volume or long-term purchase commitments" unchanged as they roll off [10-K FY2026, Item 1A].

**The only merchant OCS supplier.** "we're really the only merchant supplier today of OCS", meaning the only one selling to all comers rather than building for itself, backed by a "multi-year, multi-billion-dollar purchase agreement", though the main customer keeps an in-house version: "they'll continue to use an internal version" [Q4 FY2026 call; Q4 FY2026 slides, p.3]. Sign of weakening: a second merchant supplier, or the promised crossover to "#1 supplier" at that customer in "early '27" not happening [Q4 FY2026 call].

Caveat: no customer is named anywhere, and NVIDIA's $2.0 billion is a signal of relevance, not a supply contract; the 10-K discloses no commercial agreement with NVIDIA [10-K FY2026, Note 14].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked from most to least plausible.

**1. Two customers are 42% of revenue.** The largest was 26.6% of FY2026 revenue and the second 15.0%; the largest owed 30.4% of receivables (money customers owed Lumentum) at year-end [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Note 17]. Since the nine-month figure was 24% and the full year 26.6%, the largest customer was roughly 32% of Q4 alone, about 31%–33% given rounding, assuming the 10-K's largest customer is the same company as the 10-Q's (computed, approximate) [10-Q Q3 FY2026, Note 15; 10-K FY2026, Item 1]. Orders are mostly cancellable; customers "may alter their purchasing behavior with little or no notice" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: the concentration note in every 10-Q.

No customer is named; whether the 28.7% customer of FY2022 was Huawei is not disclosed.

**2. Hyperscaler spending slows.** Growth comes from "AI and cloud customers", and our inference is that they are now most of revenue; the share is not disclosed, and the nearest proxy is the old Cloud & Networking segment at 85.8% of FY2025 revenue, which also held telecom [10-K FY2026, Item 1; 10-K FY2025, Note 18]. In FY2024 customers "brought down inventories as supply chain constraints eased", revenue fell 23% and operating margin hit (31.9)% [10-K FY2026, Item 7]. With capex at 15% of revenue and $2.4 billion of purchase obligations, a repeat would hit harder [10-K FY2026, Note 16]. Early warning: inventory rising faster than revenue (up "$59 million sequentially", meaning versus the prior quarter, in Q4), purchase obligations flattening, gross margin falling [Q4 FY2026 call; Q4 FY2026 release; 10-Q Q3 FY2026, Item 1].

**3. Competitors or customers make their own lasers or parts.** On Chinese indium phosphide fabs: "We obviously haven't seen any impact on our numbers as yet" and "some of these Chinese laser suppliers are not delivering in the market today" [Q4 FY2026 call]. The 10-K's warning is wider: export controls "may cause those customers to seek domestic alternatives to our products, including developing alternatives internally" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Lumentum also sells chips to transceiver makers while competing with them in finished transceivers, and the 10-K warns that "our competitors may seek to vertically integrate by buying suppliers that also supply products or components to us" [10-K FY2026, Item 1; Item 1A]. Early warning: laser price declines, the "price premium" language fading, a hyperscaler naming another laser source.

**4. Export controls and China.** Huawei revenue is gone ("we may never recover this demand"), and the company holds subpoenas from the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) and the Department of Justice (DOJ) over past Huawei shipments: "We are unable to predict the likely outcome of these matters" [10-K FY2026, Item 1A; Note 16]. China restricts exports of "indium, gallium, germanium", the chips' raw material, and US tariffs may hit goods from "China and Thailand", where the factories are [10-K FY2026, Item 1A]. Early warning: a BIS or DOJ outcome; tariff wording in the 10-Q.

**5. One site fails.** Thailand holds 1.17 million square feet of owned factories and 39% of net plant (computed); "For some of the components and finished good products, we are the sole manufacturer", and moving "would be costly and require a long period of time" [10-K FY2026, Item 2; Note 17; Item 1A]. The laser chips come from two Japan fabs; Greensboro, bought March 2026, ships "first revenue" only in "early 2028" [Q4 FY2026 call]. Early warning: none in the filings.

**6. The technology shifts under the transceiver.** Co-packaged optics (CPO) puts optics on the processor package instead of in a pluggable transceiver, shrinking the market the Systems business sells into; management's answer is that CPO needs its high-power lasers and "continues to be viewed as the natural end state" [Q4 FY2026 call]. Early warning: high-power laser revenue missing the "$50 million range by the end of the calendar year" (transcript figure, not confirmed elsewhere), or a CPO design announced without Lumentum lasers [Q4 FY2026 call].

**7. Dilution keeps going.** Note principal (the amount borrowed) is settled in cash and the excess in shares; $757.8 million had been called by August 14, 2026, and all notes are classified as current because holders can convert at will [10-K FY2026, Note 10]. Shares went from 69.8 million in June 2025 to 89.7 million in August 2026, and the 2032 notes ($1,265.0 million, conversion price $187.77) are next [10-K FY2026, Note 10; cover]. Early warning: the share count on each 10-Q cover.

## Glossary

- **Transceiver** — the small device at each end of a fiber that converts electrical signals into laser light and back; Lumentum sells the laser chips inside them and also makes finished ones.
- **EML** — electro-absorption modulated laser; a laser chip that both emits light and switches it on and off at very high speed, the light source inside most high-speed transceivers today.
- **CW laser** — continuous-wave laser; a chip that emits a steady beam which a separate silicon chip then switches, the light source for "silicon photonics" transceivers (ones whose light-handling parts are built on a silicon chip).
- **OCS** — optical circuit switch; a box that redirects light beams between fibers with mirrors, so a data center's connections can be rearranged without converting the light to electricity.
- **CPO / NPO** — co-packaged optics puts the optical parts on the same package as the processor; near-packaged optics puts them on the board right next to it; both replace pluggable transceivers and need external laser light sources.
- **ELS** — external light source; a module holding high-power lasers that feeds light to co-packaged or near-packaged optics.
- **Indium phosphide** — the semiconductor material on which Lumentum's laser chips are grown; its wafer fabs and raw-material supply are built around it.
- **Pump laser** — a laser that powers the amplifiers that boost optical signals on long fiber links, including between data centers.
- **800G / 1.6T / 200G lane** — transceiver speeds in bits per second (800 gigabits, 1.6 terabits); a "lane" is one of the parallel channels inside, and moving from 100G to 200G lanes doubles each channel's speed.
- **Hyperscaler** — one of the handful of giant cloud companies that build data centers at enormous scale.
- **Convertible note** — a loan that the lender may swap for a fixed number of shares instead of being repaid; when the stock is far above the swap price, the note behaves like shares.
- **GAAP / non-GAAP** — GAAP is the official accounting standard; non-GAAP is the company's adjusted version that removes stock pay, acquisition amortization and one-off items such as the debt-extinguishment loss.
- **Capex** — capital expenditure; cash spent on factories and equipment rather than day-to-day costs.
