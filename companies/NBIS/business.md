# Nebius Group N.V. — The Business
_As of Q2 2026 (quarter ended June 30, 2026). Written 2026-09-07._

## 1. What they do

Nebius rents out computing power for artificial intelligence. It buys GPUs (the chips that do the arithmetic behind AI models), installs them by the thousand in data centers it owns or leases, writes the software that lets many customers share them, and charges for the hours or months they are used [6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Overview; 20-F FY2025, Item 4]. The customers are companies building AI, from "large enterprises, established software vendors, scaled AI companies and startups to research labs", plus two hyperscalers (giant cloud companies), Microsoft and Meta, that rent whole dedicated clusters for years at a time [20-F FY2025, Item 4]. They pay because they need more GPU capacity than they can buy and house themselves, and because there is not enough of it: "we sold out of capacity because, as fast as we bring capacity online, we can sell it" [Q2 2026 call]. Nebius calls itself a "full-stack AI cloud": own racks and servers, own cloud software, and Token Factory, a service billed per token (a piece of text a model processes) rather than per GPU-hour [20-F FY2025, Item 4].

The group reports three segments: Nebius AI cloud, "approximately 98% of total group revenue" in Q2 2026; Avride, self-driving cars and delivery robots; and TripleTen, an online coding school, which management calls "our non-core businesses" [Q2 2026 letter, p.9; p.8; 20-F FY2025, Item 4]. It also holds minority stakes in ClickHouse, a database company spun off in 2021, and Toloka, an AI-data company it stopped controlling in May 2025 [20-F FY2025, Item 4].

This is the former Yandex N.V., whose founders launched the Yandex search engine in 1997 and listed on Nasdaq in 2011; on February 28, 2022 Nasdaq halted trading in Yandex and other companies with material operations in Russia [20-F FY2023, Item 4]. In 2024 it sold all its Russian businesses to a Russian buyer, "Consortium First", at a valuation of RUB 475 billion [20-F FY2024, Item 10]. Those businesses had been "more than 95% of the Group's consolidated revenues in 2023"; the closings on May 17 and July 12, 2024 brought $2.6 billion of cash and 162.5 million of the company's own Class A shares, which went into treasury (shares the company holds itself) [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1]. The company became Nebius Group in August 2024 and trading resumed on October 21, 2024 as NBIS [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1; 20-F FY2024, Item 4]. Since then it has raised money almost continuously (details in §7) [20-F FY2025, Item 5].

Nebius AI cloud sells GPU capacity two ways: "pay-as-you-go", billed monthly for what was used, and "reserved capacity" contracts, where a customer books a cluster for a period and revenue is recognized (counted) evenly over it [6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Components of Results of Operations; 20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1]. Management describes three kinds of deal: short contracts "typically between three and six months", "priced at a significant premium"; mid-term contracts of one to three years with AI companies, "our core business"; and long-term contracts with investment-grade customers (customers with top credit ratings) "which help us finance our buildout faster and more efficiently" [Q2 2026 letter, p.3]. Pricing is per megawatt (MW) of data-center power: Q2 mid-term deals carried "a yield of $20-25 million per megawatt" of annual revenue, short-term deals are being negotiated in "the $40-50 million per MW range", and the letter's chart labels the "2026 base" at about $12 million [Q2 2026 letter, p.2-3]. "Roughly 70% of deals closed included customer prepayments", covering "50-60% of the associated capex" [Q2 2026 letter, p.3-4].

The two hyperscaler contracts are the largest; their terms are in the filings. Microsoft (September 2025) rents dedicated clusters at the Vineland, New Jersey data center over five years, delivered "in nine tranches during 2025 and 2026" (tranche: a batch delivered on its own schedule), for fees "estimated to be up to $17,392.9" million payable "irrespective of actual utilization of the GPU capacity", including "aggregate upfront payments of approximately $6,958.1" million [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1]. Meta signed a five-year order of "approximately $2,880.7" million in November 2025 and, in March 2026, a second agreement: $12 billion of clusters "with deployments in tranches starting early 2027", plus an order under which Meta must buy any capacity in specified clusters that Nebius fails to sell to others, "up to $15 billion" [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1; 6-K Q1 2026 FS, Note 1]. Contracted work not yet delivered (RPO) was $37,490.6 million at June 30, 2026, all in the AI-cloud business, 36% of it due within two years [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 4]. Revenue by contract type, customer or product is not disclosed [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 15].

Avride earned $1.0 million in Q2 2026 and is partly funded by outside investors; TripleTen sells coding boot camps to individuals and its revenue fell 19% [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 15; Note 6; 6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Revenues].

## 3. The economics

Think of a hotel that must buy a new building every couple of years. Almost all of Nebius's cost is fixed once a cluster is switched on: the GPUs are depreciated (their price spread as an expense) over five years, data-center leases and power contracts run for up to twelve years, and the engineers are salaried [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 1; Note 8; Note 11]. Selling one more GPU-hour costs little beyond electricity, so profit depends on how full the clusters are and on the price per MW; the CFO credits Q2 to "capacity added in Q1, higher utilization and high-margin revenue" [Q2 2026 call].

"Cost of revenues" here is mostly rent and power: "costs of operation and co-location of data center facilities, electricity, utility and maintenance costs in data centers, personnel costs" (co-location: renting space and power in someone else's data center) [6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Components of Results of Operations]. It excludes depreciation and amortization (D&A; amortization is the same spreading of cost for purchased software and similar assets), which is its own line, so Nebius reports no gross profit; the table shows two computed versions, before D&A (flattering) and after all D&A (slightly harsh, since some depreciation relates to offices and acquired software) [6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Cost of revenues; 6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 7]. Depreciation was the largest cost line in Q2 2026 at $259.7 million, 45% of revenue [Q2 2026 release]. Two accounting choices matter. In January 2026 the company lengthened the assumed life of servers "from four to five years", cutting Q2 depreciation by $43.0 million, while admitting that "our estimates of the useful lives of such assets may be subject to change" [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 1; 20-F FY2025, Item 3]. And the preferred profit measure, adjusted EBITDA, removes depreciation entirely, along with stock pay and deal costs; its definition also changed between the FY2025 20-F ("certain SBC", "one-off restructuring and other expenses") and the 2026 6-Ks (all share-based compensation, "acquisition and other corporate transaction-related costs") [20-F FY2025, Item 5; 6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Adjusted EBITDA]. For a business whose main cost is GPUs wearing out, adjusted EBITDA measures pricing and utilization, not profit.

The improvement is real: cost of revenues fell from 29% of revenue in Q2 2025 to 23% in Q2 2026 "primarily reflecting operating leverage as we scaled capacity" (operating leverage: fixed costs spread over more revenue), and the AI-cloud segment's adjusted EBITDA margin reached 49.7% [Q2 2026 letter, p.10].

An "asset-light" model announced in Q2, where partners "finance, build and operate the facilities", aims to grow without the capex; it has no disclosed revenue yet [Q2 2026 call].

## 4. How profitable, really

Not yet, in cash. Operating cash flow (cash from running the business, before investment) looks spectacular, $4.5 billion in H1 2026 on $1.0 billion of revenue, but $4.4 billion of it was "advance payments received from customers", booked as deferred revenue and owed back as service over "one to five calendar years" [6-K Q2 2026 MD&A, Cash Flows; 6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 4]. Prepayments are customer financing, not profit; the accounting even books an interest charge on them [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1]. After capex the business consumed $3.6 billion in H1 2026 alone (computed) [Q2 2026 release].

## 5. Why customers don't leave

**Contracts, not loyalty.** Microsoft and Meta pay take-or-pay, "irrespective of actual utilization"; the $37.5 billion of RPO is "non-cancelable"; and prepaid customers do not walk away [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1; 6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 4]. Sign of weakening: RPO growing slower than revenue, or deferred revenue shrinking quarter on quarter.

**Scarcity.** Today the customer needs Nebius more than the reverse: "in a market where we have several buyers for every GPU, we let the market tell us directly", and pricing on older GPUs rose "more than 30%" versus Q1 [Q2 2026 call; Q2 2026 letter, p.4]. This is a cycle, not a moat; the 20-F itself warns of "excess industry capacity" and "pricing pressure in respect of capacity for older generations of GPUs" [20-F FY2025, Item 3]. Sign of weakening: revenue per MW on new deals falling, auction prices dropping, or the "sold out" language disappearing.

**Software and speed.** Management's claim is that the full stack and being "one of the first-to-deploy the latest generation of NVIDIA GPU chips" win competitive deals: all four Q2 landmark customers "already had an existing supplier, in some cases, hyperscaler" [20-F FY2025, Item 4; Q2 2026 call]. That cuts both ways, since customers who switched to Nebius can switch again; named competitors are CoreWeave, Crusoe and Lambda, plus AWS, Google Cloud, Azure and Oracle [20-F FY2025, Item 3]. Sign of weakening: renewals are not disclosed, so watch whether the largest customers' shares of revenue in Note 4 hold year to year.

The honest limit: a GPU-hour on Nebius is the same GPU-hour on a rival's cloud, and the 20-F says "we have limited experience in delivering large customer contracts" [20-F FY2025, Item 3].

## 6. What could break it

Ranked from most to least plausible.

**1. Financing dries up.** The 20-F: "our core business is capital-intensive and currently not profitable, and our ability to continue to operate and to grow will depend in large part on our ability to raise additional equity or debt financing" [20-F FY2025, Item 3]. Capex guidance of $20–25 billion for 2026 compares with $8.0 billion of cash and $8.5 billion of convertible debt at June 30, 2026, plus "over $9 billion" of prepayments expected during 2026 [Q2 2026 call; Q2 2026 release; Q2 2026 letter, p.10]. Early warning: cash falling faster than capex explains, ATM sales at lower prices, a high-coupon (high-interest) convertible, or prepayment coverage of capex slipping below Q2's "50-60%".

**2. GPU prices fall or demand shifts.** The margin story rests on $20–25 million per MW deals, yet the 20-F flags "AI models that require less computation power than earlier models", "excess industry capacity" and price pressure on older GPUs [20-F FY2025, Item 3]. Early warning: revenue per MW drifting back toward the $12 million "2026 base", weaker utilization language, a shortened useful life or an impairment charge (a write-off of equipment judged worth less than its book value).

**3. Concentration and contract failure.** Three customers were 59% of Q2 2026 revenue, and Microsoft's contract of up to $17.4 billion equals about 46% of RPO (computed; the remaining share is lower since part has been earned) [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 4; 20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1]. Microsoft may "terminate individual tranches" after "specified delivery delays or repeated failure to meet availability requirements" [20-F FY2025, F-pages, Note 1].

Early warning: the Note 4 percentages, the letter's sentence on tranches delivered, any mention of service credits (refunds owed when service falls short).

**4. The build slips.** The 5 GW target rests on $12.1 billion of data-center leases signed but not yet started, $5.3 billion of power commitments (computed total), and a New Jersey site whose local hearing, in an analyst's words, adjourned "without a vote" [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 8; Note 11; Q2 2026 call]. Early warning: connected power against the 800 MW to 1 GW year-end target, "assets not yet in use" growing faster than assets in service, the Vineland approval.

**5. One supplier.** "We currently rely on Nvidia for the GPU chips we use", and three suppliers accounted for the concentrated share of H1 2026 capex [20-F FY2025, Item 3; 6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 4]. Early warning: Vera Rubin (the next NVIDIA generation) arriving later than "late this year or early next year" [Q2 2026 call].

**6. Books and controls.** Two material weaknesses in internal control (serious gaps in the checks behind the accounts) were unremediated at December 31, 2025, one over "depreciation start dates" on fixed assets, the estimate that drives reported margins [20-F FY2025, Item 15]. Early warning: Item 15 of the next 20-F.

**7. Legacy.** The Russian past "could create challenges for our businesses, including more protracted client on-boarding", and the Dutch tax authorities "may challenge our assessment" of the tax on the divestment shares [20-F FY2025, Item 3; 20-F FY2024, Item 3].

## 7. Who runs it and what they do with the cash

Arkady Volozh, 62, is "the principal founder", ran the company from 2000 to 2022, and became CEO of the renamed group in August 2024 [20-F FY2025, Item 6]. Co-founders Roman Chernin (Chief Business Officer, who leads Token Factory) and Andrey Korolenko (Chief Product and Infrastructure Officer, formerly head of infrastructure at Yandex) run product and construction [20-F FY2025, Item 6]. Recent hires: CFO Maria del Dado Alonso Sanchez since June 2025 and Chief Revenue Officer Marc Boroditsky, formerly of Cloudflare, since May 2025 [20-F FY2025, Item 6]. Chairman John Boynton has been a director since 2000 and is "a founding shareholder of Yandex" [20-F FY2025, Item 6]. The record: this team built Yandex, lost it to the forced sale, and since relisting has taken revenue from $91.5 million a year to $582.3 million a quarter [20-F FY2025, Item 5; Q2 2026 release].

There are no buybacks (buying back its own shares) and no dividends: "there have been no purchases of equity securities" in 2025 and "we do not have any present plan to pay cash dividends" [20-F FY2025, Item 16E; Item 8]. Cash goes to GPUs, data centers and small technology acquisitions ($251.6 million of cash plus about 3.8 million shares, computed as 1.4 million of purchase consideration and 2.4 million to Eigen AI employees, in H1 2026 for Tavily, ClarifAI and Eigen AI) [6-K Q2 2026 FS, Note 3; cash flow].

## Glossary

- **GPU** — graphics processing unit; the chip that does the parallel arithmetic AI models need, and the main thing Nebius buys and rents out.
- **Hyperscaler** — one of the handful of giant cloud companies (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta); here, a customer that rents whole dedicated clusters.
- **Neolab** — management's term for a newer AI research lab that trains its own large models; a target customer type.
- **Training / inference** — training is building an AI model from data; inference is running the finished model to answer requests. Both consume GPU time.
- **Agentic AI / agents** — software that uses an AI model to carry out multi-step tasks on its own (search, coding, customer support); each task triggers many model calls, so it consumes a lot of inference.
- **Token** — a piece of text (roughly a word fragment) a model reads or writes; Token Factory charges per token instead of per GPU-hour.
- **MW / GW** — megawatt / gigawatt; the electrical power a data center can feed to equipment, used by the company as its measure of capacity and as the unit for pricing deals.
- **ARR** — annualized run-rate revenue; the company's definition is AI-cloud revenue in the last month of the quarter multiplied by 12.
- **RPO** — remaining performance obligations; contracted, non-cancelable work not yet delivered, the closest thing to a backlog in the filings.
- **Deferred revenue** — cash customers have paid in advance for service not yet delivered; a liability that turns into revenue over time.
- **Adjusted EBITDA** — profit before depreciation, interest, tax, stock pay, deal costs and paper gains; the company's preferred measure. It ignores the cost of GPUs wearing out.
- **Capex** — capital expenditure; cash spent on GPUs, servers, land and buildings, shown as "purchases of property and equipment and intangible assets".
- **Convertible notes** — bonds that the holder can swap for shares at a set price; cheap interest now, dilution later (each existing share owns a smaller slice) if the share price rises.
- **ATM program** — at-the-market program; a standing arrangement to sell new shares into the market gradually at prevailing prices.
- **Take-or-pay** — a contract where the customer pays for the reserved capacity whether or not it uses it (the filings say "irrespective of actual utilization").
- **Contracted / connected power** — contracted power is the data-center electricity the company has signed agreements to obtain; connected power is the part already hooked up and able to run equipment. Both are management's own capacity measures, reported in the shareholder letters.
- **GAAP** — US generally accepted accounting principles, the standard rules the audited statements follow; "adjusted" figures start from GAAP and remove items management chooses to exclude.
